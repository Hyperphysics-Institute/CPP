## Fundamental Entities

### Conscious Point (CP)

The irreducible building block of CPP reality. A CP has: (a) definite polarity (positive or negative, electric-type or quark-type), (b) position on the 600-cell lattice, (c) the capacity to perceive its local environment (SSV field), and (d) the capacity to respond according to deterministic rules (the PCD cycle). CPs are not particles — they are the substrate from which particles are built.

### Dipole Pair (DP)

A bound pair of opposite-polarity CPs occupying adjacent lattice sites. DPs are the "atoms" of the vacuum — the DP Sea fills all of space. Types: eDP (electric), qDP (quark), hDP (hybrid). A DP is electrically and colour-neutral. The binding energy of a DP is the vacuum energy density.

### DP Sea (Dipole Sea)

The background medium of CPP — all lattice sites occupied by DPs in their ground state. The DP Sea plays the role of the vacuum in quantum field theory. It transmits all forces, mediates all interactions, and produces all quantum effects. Perturbations of the DP Sea are what we observe as photons, gluons, and other gauge bosons.

### DI-bit (Displacement Increment)

The fundamental quantum of energy/information transfer. When a CP is displaced from its equilibrium position, the displacement propagates as a DI-bit to the neighbouring CP. DI-bits carry phase, amplitude, and polarisation. The DI-bit hopping amplitude determines the Schrödinger equation (QM-1).

### Nexus

The global consistency constraint that enforces physical law at each Absolute Moment. The Nexus ensures that all CPs act consistently — it is the mechanism by which the universe computes its next state. In CPP's theological framework, the Nexus corresponds to God's sustaining action.

## Lattice Geometry

### 600-Cell Polytope

The regular 4-dimensional polytope with V=120 vertices, E=720 edges, F=1200 triangular faces, and C=600 tetrahedral cells. Coordination number z=12 (each vertex has 12 nearest neighbours). Edge length in circumradius units: l = 1/φ. Symmetry group: the binary icosahedral group 2I (order 120). The 600-cell is the fundamental lattice of CPP — AXIM-2.

### Grid Point

A vertex of the 600-cell lattice. Each Grid Point is occupied by a CP. The lattice is not embedded in pre-existing space — the lattice IS space. The spacing between Grid Points is the Planck length l_P ≈ 1.616 × 10⁻³⁵ m.

### Absolute Moment

The fundamental unit of time in CPP. One tick of the cosmic clock during which every CP executes one PCD cycle. Duration: the Planck time t_P ≈ 5.391 × 10⁻⁴⁴ s. Time in CPP is discrete and absolute (not relative), but relativistic time dilation is reproduced through the PSR mechanism.

### Voronoi Cell

The region of space "owned" by each lattice vertex. When SSV_abs increases (due to kinetic energy or gravitational potential), the Voronoi cell volume shrinks, reducing the PSR and producing relativistic effects (SR-1).

### Golden Ratio (φ)

φ = (1+√5)/2 ≈ 1.6180. Appears as: the edge-to-circumradius ratio of the 600-cell (l = 1/φ), in 4 of the 9 adjacency eigenvalues, as the propagation efficiency η = 1/φ, and in the Weinberg angle sin²θ_W = 3/(8φ) and strong coupling α_s = 5/(8φ). Not a free parameter — a geometric consequence of the 600-cell.

## Cage Structures and Particles

### Tetrahedral Cage

The fundamental particle structure in CPP. A tetrahedron (K₄) formed by 4 CPs at lattice vertices, connected by 6 qDP chain bonds. The cage traps energy through resonant oscillation of its bonds, and the trapped energy is what we measure as mass.

### K₃ Face

The triangular face of the tetrahedral cage. The K₃ adjacency matrix has eigenvalues +2 (bonding, multiplicity 1) and −1 (antibonding, multiplicity 2). The eigenvalue ratio gives the Koide ratio K = 2/3, and the Koide phase comes from the isotropic electroweak shift on this face.

### Hybrid Tetrahedron

The tetrahedral scaffold upon which baryons are built. Composed of two hDPs (hybrid Dipole Pairs, each a qCP + eCP). Three of its four vertices bond to quarks; the fourth (open) vertex is the nuclear bonding site for inter-nucleon forces.

### qDP Chain (Gluon Bond)

A string of qDPs connecting two cage vertices. This is the CPP implementation of the gluon flux tube. The chain has a restoring force that increases with distance up to a maximum (confinement region), then decreases as bonds fray — eventually breaking to produce new quark-antiquark pairs.

### Lepton Cage

A tetrahedral cage hosting an electron, muon, or tau. The mass spectrum is determined by the K₃ eigenvalue structure perturbed by the electroweak isotropic shift ε = +3/(52φ).

### Quark Cage

A tetrahedral cage hosting a charm, bottom, or top quark. Three quark cages bond to the hybrid tetrahedron to form a baryon. The mass spectrum is determined by the K₃ eigenvalue structure perturbed by the combined EW+colour shift ε = −27/(52φ).

## Forces and Interactions

### Electric Interaction (Edge Modes)

The force between electrically charged particles. In CPP, this corresponds to abelian (U(1)) edge modes — closed walks of length 2 on the 600-cell graph. A DI-bit hops along an edge and returns. The interaction is linear, reversible, and commutative. Push-pull, attract/repel. Strength governed by sin²θ_W = 3/(8φ).

### Colour Interaction (Face Modes)

The force between colour-charged quarks. In CPP, this corresponds to non-abelian (SU(3)) face modes — displacement pulses circulating on the K₃ triangular loops of the tetrahedral cage. The 8 standing-wave patterns correspond to the 8 Gell-Mann generators. Non-commutative because each pulse changes the vertex SSV_abs. Always attractive. Strength governed by α_s = 5/(8φ).

### Weak Interaction (Face Circulation, SU(2))

The force responsible for beta decay. In CPP, this corresponds to the SU(2) component of the face-mode spectrum — closed walks of length 3 on the 600-cell. The face modes are the non-abelian propagation channel with efficiency η = 1 (circumradius scale).

### SSV Gradient Force (Gravity)

In CPP, gravity is not a separate force — it is the SSV gradient produced by mass-energy. Massive particles increase SSV_abs in their vicinity, which alters the PSR of nearby particles, producing apparent attraction. Derived in SR-1.

### Nuclear Force (Open-Vertex Bonding)

The force between nucleons. In CPP, the open (fourth) vertex of the baryon's hybrid tetrahedron bonds to the open vertex of another baryon. The electrical polarity is correct for attraction, and the colour force between apices is inherently attractive. This is the CPP mechanism for nuclear binding.

## Coupling Constants and Mixing Angles

### Weinberg Angle (sin²θ_W)

The fraction of vacuum disturbances that propagate as photons. CPP derivation: sin²θ_W = η × Tr(A²)/N = (1/φ)(1440/3840) = 3/(8φ) ≈ 0.2318. PDG: 0.2312 (0.24%). Derived in SM-6 with zero free parameters. NOT the SM definition g'²/(g²+g'²).

### Strong Coupling Constant (α_s)

The fraction of vacuum disturbances that propagate as colour (face-mode) excitations. CPP derivation: α_s = η × [Tr(A³)/3]/N = (1/φ)(2400/3840) = 5/(8φ) ≈ 0.386. This is the bare lattice value; running to α_s(M_Z) = 0.118 is an open problem. Derived in SM-7.

### Mode Complementarity Sum Rule

sin²θ_W + α_s = 3/(8φ) + 5/(8φ) = 8/(8φ) = 1/φ ≈ 0.618. The EW and colour sectors exhaust the metric-weighted mode capacity of the lattice. NOT a GUT unification — a topological mode budget.

### Coupling Ratio

α_s/sin²θ_W = F/E = 1200/720 = 5/3. A topological invariant of the 600-cell. The colour sector has 5/3 times as many modes as the electromagnetic sector.

## Mass Generation

### Koide Ratio (K)

K = (Σm_i)/(Σ√m_i)² = 2/3, satisfied to 11 ppm for charged leptons. In CPP: K = λ₊/(λ₊ + |λ₋|) = 2/(2+1) = 2/3, from the K₃ eigenvalue ratio. Proved in SM-3.

### Koide Phase (θ)

The angle in the Koide parametrisation √m_i = (S/3)(1 + √2 cos(θ + 2πi/3)). Determines individual masses within a generation. For leptons: θ = 132.731° from cos θ = −(2/3)(1 + 3/(104φ)). For heavy quarks: θ = 124.035° from cos θ = −(2/3)(1 − 27/(104φ)).

### Isotropic Shift (ε)

The perturbation δH = ε·I₃ on a K₃ face from the gauge sector. For leptons: ε = +2sin²θ_W/(z+1) = +3/(52φ) (EW only, 2 internal bonds). For quarks: ε = (2sin²θ_W − 12α_s)/(z+1) = −27/(52φ) (EW on 2 bonds minus colour on 12 bonds). The shift preserves C₃ symmetry but changes the eigenvalue ratio.

### Bond-Count Asymmetry

EW modes couple to the 2 internal K₃ bonds per vertex. Colour modes couple to all z=12 bonds per vertex. This asymmetry is the structural origin of the different ε values for leptons and quarks. Formalised as Axioms A8 (Edge Locality) and A9 (Face Saturation).

## Quantum Mechanics Concepts

### Zitterbewegung (ZBW)

The rapid oscillation of a CP between partner DPs at the Compton frequency ω = mc²/ℏ. During ZBW, the CP alternately bonds with the DP to its left and right, causing its effective position to jitter. This jitter produces the apparent wavelength of quantum mechanics.

### Partner Switching

During ZBW, a CP switches between DP partners. The switching rate is deterministic but appears random due to the complexity of the local SSV field. This is the CPP origin of quantum randomness — not ontological indeterminacy but practical unpredictability.

### Born Rule

In CPP, the Born rule (P = |ψ|²) emerges from the DI-bit interference pattern. The probability of detecting a particle at a point is proportional to the squared amplitude of the DI-bit field at that point, because detection requires a threshold interaction that depends on amplitude² (QM-2).

### Bell Violation (S = 2√2)

CPP reproduces the quantum Bell violation because the Nexus enforces global consistency. Entangled particles share a common determination at the Nexus level — not through hidden variables carried by the particles, but through the Nexus's global computation. This is superdeterminism without fine-tuning (QM-3, SD series).

## Relativistic Concepts

### Planck Sphere Radius (PSR)

The effective displacement a CP can achieve per Absolute Moment. In the rest frame, PSR = l_P (Planck length). When SSV_abs increases (due to kinetic energy or gravity), the Voronoi cell volume shrinks, reducing PSR. The formula PSR = l_P(1 − kE/V)^(1/3) reproduces the Lorentz factor γ at accessible energies (SR-1).

### Lattice Saturation

As kinetic energy increases, more energy is stored as ΔSSV in the surrounding lattice, reducing the Voronoi cell volume available for CP displacement. This is the CPP mechanism for the speed-of-light limit: at v = c, all Voronoi volume is consumed and PSR → 0.

## Spectral Trace Identities

### Tr(A²) = 2E = 1440

Counts closed walks of length 2 on the 600-cell (DI-bit hops to a neighbour and returns). These are the abelian edge modes — the U(1) propagation channels.

### Tr(A³) = 6F = 7200

Counts closed walks of length 3 (oriented triangular circulations). Division by 3 removes cyclic overcounting: Tr(A³)/3 = 2F = 2400 face modes — the non-abelian propagation channels.

### N = Tr(A²) + Tr(A³)/3 = 3840

The total lattice mode capacity — a topological invariant. This is the denominator in the Weinberg angle and strong coupling formulas. It does not depend on the metric.

**Schlegel diagram (and Schlegel-style projection)** — A planar embedding of a 3-polytope obtained by projecting the polytope through one face onto the opposite face's plane. The defining property is that the projection has no edge crossings — the result is a straight-line planar drawing of the polytope's 1-skeleton. By Steinitz's theorem, every 3-polytope has a Schlegel diagram (since the 1-skeleton is planar). In SS-9 Figure 1, panels (a) tetrahedron, (b) triangular bipyramid, (d) pentagonal bipyramid, and (h) icosahedron are accurate Schlegel diagrams; panel (c) octahedron uses a "Schlegel-style projection" with one rendering crossing in the square+two-apexes layout (the 1-skeleton is preserved exactly even though the rendered drawing crosses); panels (e) snub disphenoid, (f) triaugmented triangular prism, and (g) gyroelongated square bipyramid use "simplified topological schematics" that preserve vertex count, edge count, and vertex degree distribution but use approximate edge structure (these polytopes are difficult to embed cleanly in 2D without crossings). The distinction matters in figure captions: misnaming "Schlegel diagram" for a layout with crossings is technically incorrect. Visual edge crossings reflect the projection layout, not graph intersections — every FvdW deltahedron is a planar 3-polytope by Steinitz. [First appeared: SS-9 v0.8 figure (term used in caption); refined to "Schlegel-style projection" with rendering-crossing distinction at SS-9 v0.9 per ChatGPT d.7 review]

**Steinitz's theorem** — The fundamental theorem characterizing 3-polytope realizability (Ernst Steinitz, 1922): a graph $G$ is the 1-skeleton (vertex–edge graph) of some convex 3-polytope if and only if $G$ is a 3-vertex-connected planar graph. Equivalently, the simple 3-vertex-connected planar graphs are exactly the polyhedral graphs. Used in SS-9 Lemma B$'$ Step 5 to identify the alpha-cluster contact graph $G$ as the 1-skeleton of a convex 3-polytope from the conditions $G$ is connected (Lemma A), $|E| = 3N_\alpha - 6$ (Lemma C combined with Euler's formula), $G$ is planar and triangulated (C7 + Lemma A), and 3-non-degeneracy (Sub-Lemma 2.2). The combinatorial polytope structure thus established is then identified with the unique FvdW deltahedron at each $N_\alpha$ via the FvdW classification (clause (iv) of Theorem 6.1). Steinitz's theorem provides the bridge from graph-theoretic content (Lemmas A, B$'$, C, sub-lemmas) to polytope content (Theorem clause (iv)) that makes the SS-9 conditional theorem possible. The geometric realization at uniform edge length $R_{\alpha\alpha}$ requires hypothesis C8 (FvdW centroid-realizability) — Steinitz alone gives the abstract polytope; C8 supplies the metric. [First appeared: SS-9 v0.1 working draft Lemma B$'$; cited explicitly at SS-9 v0.5 Theorem clause (iv) rework per ChatGPT d.1 review where the abstract-vs-metric distinction was made explicit]
