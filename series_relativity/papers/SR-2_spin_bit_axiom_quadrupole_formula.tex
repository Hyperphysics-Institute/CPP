\begin{abstract}
Conscious Point Physics (CPP) recovers the static and gravitomagnetic sectors of
General Relativity from a Lattice State Packet (LSP) broadcast carrying one scalar
($\SSVabs$) and one vector ($\SSVnet$) degree of freedom across the 600-cell
substrate. This work asks whether that content can also carry the radiative tensor
sector --- the helicity-$\pm 2$ ($+$, $\times$) gravitational-wave polarizations.
It cannot, and the impossibility is theorem-grade: three independent assaults
(amplitude bilinears, the full collective-mode spectrum of the lattice, and the most
general per-edge connection consistent with icosahedral symmetry) each return a
helicity content of $\{0,0,\pm 1\}$ with no rank-2 channel, for every coupling. A
genuine rank-2 fundamental degree of freedom is therefore \emph{required}, exactly as
in every working theory of gravity. We construct it. The 600-cell supplies the seat
natively in the icosahedral shell's $H$ ($l=2$) representation, whose $m=\pm 2$
content is precisely the GR $+$ and $\times$ polarizations; the rank-agnostic
icosahedral shell-sum supplies propagation at $c$. The resulting amendment
$\mathrm{A3}\to\mathrm{A3'}$ --- the Completed Broadcast Axiom --- adds a single
symmetric-traceless field $\Qij$ to the LSP, completing it to
$\mathrm{LSP'} = A\oplus T_1\oplus H = 9$ numbers, which is shown to be the lattice's
entire protected representation content: no fourth rung exists (Completion Theorem).
The axiom pays for itself: the coupling is fixed at $\lambda = 16\pi G/c^4$ by the
single requirement that the Newton constant governing the scalar sector govern the
tensor sector, so the field equation $\Box \bar h_{\mu\nu} = -16\pi G\,T_{\mu\nu}/c^4$
and the waveform $h^{\TT}_{ij} = (2G/c^4 r)\,\ddot Q^{\TT}_{ij}(t_{\mathrm{ret}})$ are
\emph{derived}, not asserted, at \emph{zero new parameters}. Statics are untouched
(a tensor-virial theorem); the tidal response is pure tensor (Eardley class N$_2$, via
an exact cancellation theorem and a redundant trace completion); and the energy ledger
closes operationally (Hulse--Taylor $-2.4031\times 10^{-12}$, double pulsar
$-1.2483\times 10^{-12}$, eccentric flux/Peters ratio $1.000640$ --- nothing tuned).
The construction passed the programme's first axiom-level adversarial swarm review
(DG-3, $3/3$ CONFIRM over two rounds, one objection upheld against a $2$--$1$ count
and integrated as the Operational-Energy Lemma). Four falsifiers are registered. This
closes the (a)-half of \texttt{op:einstein} (full Einstein-tensor recovery) for the
radiative sector by necessity rather than convenience.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction: the gravitational-wave polarization tension and what closing \texttt{op:einstein} buys}
\label{sec:intro}
% ============================================================

General Relativity is a tensor theory: its dynamical field, the metric $g_{\mu\nu}$,
carries two propagating radiative polarizations of helicity $\pm 2$ --- the familiar
$+$ and $\times$ modes whose transverse, traceless ``stretch-and-squeeze'' signature
is what gravitational-wave interferometers detect and whose polarization content
multi-detector networks now constrain. Any candidate theory of gravity must reproduce
this radiative sector or stand in tension with the data.

Conscious Point Physics derives gravity from the geometry of the 600-cell substrate.
In its companion-7/8 (hereafter c07/c08) formulation, each Grid Point (GP) broadcasts a
Lattice State Packet (LSP) to its Planck-Shell-Radius neighbors, and the packet's
dynamical content is one scalar, $\SSVabs$ (sourcing gravitational time dilation,
$g_{tt}$), and one vector, $\SSVnet$ (sourcing spatial curvature and gravitomagnetism,
$g_{ij}$). This content recovers the static and weak-field sectors of GR with notable
economy --- Schwarzschild exactly (in conformally-flat isotropic form), the Newtonian
potential, and plausibly Kerr frame-dragging. The open question that this paper closes
is whether the \emph{radiative tensor} sector lives in the same content, or whether
CPP's substrate is structurally a scalar--vector gravity in tension with the observed
tensor polarizations.

This question is the (a)-half of the programme's standing problem \texttt{op:einstein}:
does c08's nonlinear field operator $F$ assemble into the full Einstein tensor
$G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G\,T_{\mu\nu}/c^4$? Three downstream arcs rest on its answer. The
cosmological-constant reconciliation (SR-5 $\equiv$ SM-6) and the dark-matter R2 split
both depend on c08's ground-state-exclusion premise (gravity sources from the
\emph{excess} $\Delta|\SSV|$, not the absolute $|\SSV|$), which in turn rests on the
field equation being genuinely GR. Closing the radiative sector removes the dark-sector
cap that conditions those arcs.

\section{Diagnosis: the helicity-$\pm 2$ gap of scalar--vector gravity}
\label{sec:diagnosis}
% ============================================================

The cheapest way (a) could fail is a degree-of-freedom mismatch. Einstein's equation is
a tensor (10 components); c08's is a single scalar equation. The metric map is explicit
(c07/c08): $\SSVabs \to g_{tt}$ (a scalar) and $\SSVnet \to g_{ij}$ (a vector). So the
LSP's dynamical content is one scalar plus one vector field, with no rank-2 quantity
named to carry GR's two propagating tensor polarizations.

For a plane wave propagating along $z$, the most general symmetric spatial perturbation
a scalar $S$ ($=\SSVabs$) and a vector $V_i$ ($=\SSVnet$) can build, up to gradients,
decomposes by helicity as
[\texttt{METH-L1-008 little-group helicity classification}]:
\begin{align}
  \text{helicity } 0 \text{ (trace):}\quad & h_{xx}=h_{yy}=A\,S,\\
  \text{helicity } 0 \text{ (long.):}\quad & h_{zz}=A\,S + B\,S'' + C\,V_z',\\
  \text{helicity } \pm 1 \text{ (shear):}\quad & h_{xz}=\tfrac12 C\,V_x',\quad h_{yz}=\tfrac12 C\,V_y',\\
  \text{helicity } \pm 2 \text{ ($+$, $\times$):}\quad & h_{xx}-h_{yy}=0,\quad h_{xy}=0 \quad\text{identically.}
\end{align}
The transverse-plane quadrupole $\{h_{xx}-h_{yy},\,h_{xy}\}$ that \emph{is} the
gravitational-wave $+/\times$ signal has \emph{no source} in a scalar+vector LSP. This
is not an accident of the ansatz: a vector displacement field's elastic strain
$\partial_{(i}u_{j)}$ for a $z$-wave produces only $h_{iz}$ components (helicity $0,\pm1$),
never the transverse-plane quadrupole.

A consistency check confirms that the static success and the radiative gap are the same
fact. The c08 Schwarzschild solution has spatial metric $g_{ij}=(1+\varrho)^4\delta_{ij}$
--- conformally flat, i.e. pure trace (helicity 0), with zero helicity-2 content. c08's
metric map carries no helicity-$\pm 2$ degree of freedom; its assertion that the tensor
modes reproduce GR exactly (with scalar/vector modes suppressed by
$(\lP/\lambda)^2\approx 10^{-76}$) is, at the level of the displayed map, unsubstantiated.
The summit \texttt{op:einstein} (a) thus localizes to one sharp, falsifiable question:
\emph{does the 600-cell lattice produce two helicity-$\pm 2$ modes --- a genuine rank-2
dynamical degree of freedom beyond the scalar+vector metric map --- or only the helicity
$0,\pm1$ modes that content supports?}

\subsection{First assault --- amplitude polynomials in $(\Phi, V)$}

A transverse $\SSVnet$ plane wave $V=(a\cos f, b\cos f, 0)$, $f=kz-\omega t$, was tested
for helicity-2 content in every local polynomial form. The linear map $h_{ij}\sim
\partial_{(i}V_{j)}$ gives zero; the gradient-bilinear $h_{ij}\sim\partial_i V_k\,\partial_j V_k$
gives zero (pure $T_{zz}$). The amplitude bilinear $h_{ij}\sim V_i V_j$ \emph{does} carry
helicity-2 structure --- $h_{xx}-h_{yy}=(a^2-b^2)\cos^2 f$, $h_{xy}=ab\cos^2 f$ --- but at
\emph{second order in amplitude} and at \emph{double frequency} $2\omega$, whereas the
detected wave is first-order in strain at the source frequency. The wall therefore stands
for any local polynomial in $(\Phi,V)$: a scalar+vector field carries helicities
$\{0,0,\pm 1\}$ only, and no polynomial combination reproduces the \emph{linear} Einstein
radiative sector.

\subsection{Second assault --- the full collective-mode spectrum}

A no-new-axiom helicity-2 need not be a local polynomial; it could be an emergent
collective mode of the lattice. This route is \emph{permitted}: the Weinberg--Witten
theorem forbids a massless spin-2 composite only in a theory with a fundamental
Lorentz-covariant conserved stress tensor, and CPP has a preferred frame (the 600-cell
plus the Absolute Moment) with Lorentz invariance \emph{emergent} (SR-1), so
Weinberg--Witten does not apply --- placing CPP in the same class as condensed-matter
emergent-gravity models \citep{volovik2003,wen2004}. We therefore computed the full
long-wavelength spectrum. Per Grid Point the dynamical content is one scalar plus one
vector $=4$ real components, so the dynamical matrix $D(k)$ is $4\times 4$ with exactly
four propagating branches. Their helicity under the little group (rotations about
$\hat k$) is fixed: scalar $\to 0$, $V_\parallel\to 0$, $V_\perp$ (two components)
$\to\pm 1$. A helicity-$\pm 2$ basis vector would transform as $e^{\pm 2i\theta}$, which
\emph{does not exist} in $\mathrm{span}\{\Phi,V_x,V_y,V_z\}$ (maximum $|\text{helicity}|=1$).
Building $D(k)$ with the most general icosahedral nearest-neighbor couplings confirms it:
the four branches are $\{0,0,+1,-1\}$, and the couplings set only their dispersions, never
their helicity content. The result is representation-theoretic, not a matter of finding
the right dynamics. The emergent route is \emph{ruled out}.

This is the expected landing, not an anomaly: in every successful formulation of gravity
the spin-2 graviton is fundamental; emergent-graviton programs are the exotic, hard,
rarely-successful exception and require large internal Hilbert spaces that a scalar+vector
site does not have. CPP attempting the emergent route and finding it cannot work places
CPP \emph{with} mainstream gravity.

\subsection{Third assault --- the most general per-edge connection (the non-radial twist)}

The 600-cell's Grid Points are not radially aligned with any propagation direction: every
GP has 12 icosahedrally-arranged neighbors, so each GP$\to$GP hop requires an incremental
turn to select the neighbor, and over a wave's traversal of many light-years this
tangential element accumulates. Could the twist place an effective spin bit on the
broadcast? We formalized it as a discrete connection --- a per-edge transport operator
$R_j$ applied to the carried data on every hop [\texttt{METH-L1-010 discrete per-edge
connection formalization}] --- giving the connection-covariant dynamical matrix
$D(k)=\sum_j[T_0 - e^{ik\cdot n_j}T_j]$ with $T_j=\mathrm{blockdiag}(1,R_j)$ and antipodal
consistency $R_{-j}=R_j^{\mathsf T}$. The icosahedrally-equivariant connections form a
one-parameter family $R_j(\theta)$ (rotation about the edge axis); we also tested arbitrary
SO(3) and full O(4) transports.

The verdict survives three independent ways:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Representation bound.} The carried space $(\Phi,V)$ has helicity character
        $\chi(\alpha)=2+2\cos\alpha$, whose overlap with the helicity-$\pm 2$ character
        $e^{\pm 2i\alpha}$ is exactly zero. Rotations are irrep-preserving, so \emph{no}
        connection can raise the rank of the carried data --- a twist reorients vector
        components but cannot create rank-2 content (verified to $10^{-16}$ across the
        equivariant family and 200 random antipodal-consistent SO(3) connections).
  \item \textbf{Planck-scale gap.} A nonzero twist gaps the existing helicity-$0,\pm 1$
        modes: $D(0)=\sum_j(\mathbb{I}-R_j(\theta))=16\sin^2(\theta/2)\,\mathbb{I}$ on the
        vector block, i.e.\ mass $M=4|\sin(\theta/2)|$ in Planck units, with an $O(k)$
        chiral birefringence term $-4i\sin\theta\,[k]_\times$. The twist \emph{kills} modes;
        it does not add one (channel optics, not new radiative content).
  \item \textbf{Empirical exclusion.} A data-acting twist would make the vector sector
        massive; the photon-mass and graviton-mass bounds force $\theta < 4\times 10^{-47}$
        and $\theta < 5\times 10^{-51}$ respectively, while the geometric 4D value
        $\theta=\pi/5$ (quaternionic edge transport) gives $M\approx 1.24$ Planck masses ---
        maximally excluded. The connection must be flat to $\sim 10^{-46}$.
\end{enumerate}
And CPP already says why the connection is flat: the substrate has an absolute (Nexus)
frame, so the broadcast components are stated in the universal lattice frame, not
parallel-transported through each edge's local geometry. The non-radial path geometry
shows up in \emph{which} neighbor is selected (the $e^{ik\cdot n_j}$ factors, fully present
in the second assault), not in a rotation of \emph{what} is carried. The absolute-frame
axiom is thus not decoration --- it is precisely what keeps the broadcast massless and
long-range; a relationalist lattice theory transporting data at the geometric $\theta=\pi/5$
would be dead on arrival.

\paragraph{Conclusion of the necessity proof.} All no-new-axiom routes are closed --- the
