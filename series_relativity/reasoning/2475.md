## The error

H.1 Model 3 computes the exact 4D hyperspherical cap. Published:
```
V_cap/V_4 = 4*sqrt(2f)/(3pi) + O(f^{3/2})   ->  eps_cap ~ sqrt(2f)/(3pi) ~ f^{1/2}
```
Correct (verified 50 dps, `code/2475_h1_cap_exponent_verification.py`, 8/8):
```
V_cap/V_4 = 8*(2f)^{5/2}/(15pi) + O(f^{7/2})  ->  eps_cap ~ 0.2401 * f^{5/2}
```
Measured exponent **2.4999999990**. My closed form matches the exact integral to
**ratio 1.0** at f = 1e-10; the published formula is off by **twenty orders of magnitude**
there. The general fact: a cap of height h on a d-ball has volume ∝ h^{(d+1)/2}; for
d = 4 that is h^{5/2}. The published expansion missed that the leading √(2f) terms
*cancel* to fourth order.

## Why it is worse than an arithmetic slip

The Theorem states: *"ε_geom = O(f^n) with **n ≤ 1** at small f. Since γ_SR − 1 = O(f²),
no such model recovers the Lorentz factor."*
Its proof: *"any simply connected excluded region of height h = fR in a 4D hypersphere
has volume [~f^{1/2}]."*

- The proof's **premise is the arithmetic error** → the proof is void.
- The theorem's **claim is refuted by its own Model 3** (n = 5/2 > 1).
- **The theorem is false as stated.**

## What survives, and what reopens

**Survives:** the three models give n ∈ {1, 1, 5/2}; none equals 2; none recovers γ. That
is three worked eliminations — a **Proposition**, not a class theorem. Demoted.

**Reopens — and this is the important part:** the corrected exponents **BRACKET** the
target, 1 < 2 < 5/2. The paper's *only* class-coverage argument was "all n ≤ 1 < 2."
That is gone. The exponent is set by how the excluded volume scales: corridor = f¹ by
fiat; cap = f^{5/2} by geometry. **Nothing now excludes an exclusion region with
V_excl ∝ f², which by V ∝ r⁴ gives ε ∝ f² — exactly γ_SR − 1.**

**The erroneous theorem was closing a route that may in fact be open.** Registered
`OPEN-SR-H1-CLASS` as the cheapest live attack on `OPEN-SR-EPSILON`.

Note the direction of the error: Model 3 fails by giving too *little* strain (f^{5/2} <
f²), not too much as published. The models straddle the target instead of lying under it.
