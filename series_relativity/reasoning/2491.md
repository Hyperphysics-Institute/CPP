## The starting frame

The handover's attack line: the postulate-available structure for a uniformly
moving aggregate is ê_motion plus the universal τ̂; the per-Moment displacement
4-vector lies in their span; can that observation be promoted to "exactly one
2-plane is postulate-distinguished"? The pre-logged K3′ risk: privileging the
drift-spanned plane because the tube needs it. So the first structural decision,
made before any derivation: the uniqueness argument must ENUMERATE, not construct-
and-declare — run over all candidate subspaces on postulate criteria and let the
answer fall out. And a second decision immediately after: enumerate over ALL
dimensions k, not just k = 2 — because asking only "which 2-plane" smuggles the
answer's dimensionality in as an input. If the postulates also distinguished a
unique line or a unique 3-space, the panel should know; if they distinguish a
unique object ONLY at k = 2, that is a result, and it is checkable.

## The inventory step

What does motion make available? Only ê_motion — that is what "uniformly moving"
means and nothing else changes. τ̂ is universal (c01's text is unambiguous:
"exactly l_P in the timelike direction once per universal tick,
frame-independently"; SR-1 restates it as the τ-orthogonality of the
decomposition). The drift magnitude d = v·t_P was already forced kinematically at
2489 step (i), so Δ = c t_P τ̂ + v t_P ê_motion is on the record forward. The
arena is the ball (M4), so the arena contributes no directions. Internal structure
is aggregate-particular and survives at v = 0 where d = 0 — not motion-sourced.
Writing the inventory this way, the K4 question surfaced: does SAYING "internal
structure is not motion-sourced" constitute evaluating candidates (b)/(c)? Ruling
made and recorded: no — it is a statement about what is in the data inventory, not
a construction or test of those planes as mechanisms; neither plane is built,
neither is scored. K4 intact. Logged in the session entry explicitly so the panel
can audit the distinction.

## The invariant-theory core, and the "too good?" moment

Stabilizer of {τ̂, ê_motion} in the arena symmetry = SO(2) about ê_motion. Rep
decomposition trivial ⊕ trivial ⊕ rotation. Invariant 2-planes: Π and P⊥ only, at
generic angle. Then the k-sweep: k = 1 has a constructible continuum of invariant
lines (all of Π's lines are invariant — the trivial² block sees no rotation at
all); k = 3 the mirror continuum; k = 2 exactly two. The immediate honest reaction,
recorded verbatim: this is suspiciously pretty — k = 2 is the unique dimension
with a finite invariant set, exactly where the tube needs its plane. Is the
prettiness itself a K3′ flag? Applied the test: would a target-ignorant derivation
have run this enumeration? Yes — running over all k is precisely what
target-ignorance looks like; a target-informed derivation would have asked only
about 2-planes. And the K3a counterfactual came out unusually strong: under an
n = 3 target the SAME enumeration says "no unique 3-space exists" — the argument
is not merely target-independent, it is target-LETHAL under the counterfactual.
That settled the "too good" worry: an argument that would kill the campaign under
a different target is not bending to this one.

## The near-miss (logged per K3′ discipline)

First draft of the Π-vs-P⊥ discrimination reached for: "P⊥ is only specifiable
via Π (as its complement), whereas Π is specified directly — so Π is primary."
Checked it and it is FALSE: P⊥ is directly specifiable from the data by
orthogonality (the arena's Euclidean structure is available; SR-1's decomposition
uses it). The argument was abandoned because it was wrong — but the record should
show it was also CONVENIENT, which is exactly the profile K3′ exists to catch. It
was replaced with the containment criterion (Π is the member the data spans; every
datum has identically zero P⊥-projection), and — because the fitting pressure at
this fork is the highest in the whole step (the tube wants Π) — the fallback was
logged as an explicit against-interest caveat rather than argued to zero: if the
panel rejects the positivity criterion, M1 delivers "unique splitting" and the
orientation becomes M2's burden, where the drift-prices-Π-budget claim must be
derived anyway. The step does not need to win that argument here to be honest.

## The v → 0 check

At rest the stabilizer is SO(3), the vector rep is irreducible, no invariant
2-plane exists — the plane dies exactly where d = 0 says nothing needs it. One
subtlety noticed and recorded: Π has no continuous limit as v → 0⁺ (it depends on
the limiting direction), but the exclusion payload shrinks to nothing along every
sequence, so the discontinuity is unobservable. Flagged for M2/M3 to carry
quantitatively rather than resolved here with hand-waving.

## The broken branch

The kill that was live: if the lattice's discrete orientation participated in
displacement kinematics, the stabilizer is generically trivial (or C₂ on special
axes), and under C₂ the R(π) = −1 action on P⊥ makes EVERY mixed plane invariant —
a two-parameter continuum, uniqueness dead. Computed, not asserted (V5). This ties
M1's fate to the same A3′-C2/M4 load-bearing wall M4's caveat already identified,
which is the right shape for the K2 package: one scope question, two consequences.
The C_n (n ≥ 3) case was checked in passing — no invariant lines in P⊥, so the
finite set survives — and deliberately NOT leaned on (the generic discrete
stabilizer is trivial, and the honest counterfactual is the one that kills).
