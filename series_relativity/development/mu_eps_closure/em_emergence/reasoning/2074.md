## The move
Round 4 left one fork: does the deterministic icosahedral quasicrystal reach EXACT isotropy (W1) or only
dense-suppressed (W2)? I went after it with group theory, not more shell-tuning, because the answer is a
symmetry fact, not a numerical accident.

## The decisive realization
A structure with icosahedral point symmetry is isotropic only up to its first anisotropic invariant. For
the icosahedral group that is degree 6 (no l=2, no l=4). So:
- rank-2 tensor isotropic (always),
- rank-4 tensor ISOTROPIC -- this is the isotropic-elasticity theorem for icosahedral QCs; it means
  v_phase is isotropic at O(q^2),
- rank-6 tensor ANISOTROPIC (l=6) -- v_phase anisotropy enters at O(q^4), and is GENERIC + nonzero.
Verified to machine precision (rank-4 deviation ~3e-16, rank-6 ~9e-2). This is the same q^4 I measured in
Rounds 3-4 -- now identified as the l=6 harmonic, not a finite-shell artifact.

The quasicrystal inherits this: aperiodicity removed the BZ (Round 4's periodic obstruction) but NOT the
point-symmetry obstruction. Icosahedral is icosahedral whether periodic or aperiodic, so the l=6/q^4 floor
survives. => W2.

What W1 would need: every anisotropic harmonic to vanish -- a continuum, or a RANDOM (Poisson/causal-set)
set. Verified the Poisson set washes out all harmonics (rank-4, rank-6 -> 0 with N). CPP's substrate is
deterministic, not random, so it doesn't get that route. Clean dichotomy: deterministic icosahedral = W2;
random = W1; CPP is the former.

## Why this closes W1 cleanly (and the §4.1 channels become moot)
W1 fails at the FREE-FIELD DISPERSION level -- the most basic channel. The Round-4 §4.1 further channels
(interactions, mode-mixing, C2 carriage, composition closure) were additional hurdles for a W1 claim; they
don't need separate checking, because you can't have exact-discrete Lorentz when the free dispersion is
already q^4-anisotropic. So Round 5 is genuinely the decider, not just another probe.
