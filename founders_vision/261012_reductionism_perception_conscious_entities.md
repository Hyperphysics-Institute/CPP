## Context

Discussion began with the problem of mapping Thomas's interdisciplinary essays into categories. The difficulty of categorization led Thomas into core CPP philosophy: why every reductionist vector ends at God, how sensation implies an observer, and how identity depends on what the observer brings.

---

## 1. Every Reductionist Vector Ends at God

Isak described watching light reflect off a bush and tracing the experience: photons from the sun → redistributed by objects → interpreted by eyes at different angles. He concluded:

> "I'm not looking at a bush. I'm looking *toward* a bush. The only reason I'm seeing it is because something else is showing it to me."

Extended to CPP: if we're all receiving something from a source, it's not just photons from the sun — it's coming from every direction at once. Clusters of "decided particles" behave together, form DNA, follow rules, repeat as they die and are reborn.

Thomas affirmed this as **reductionism** — tracking any experience back to its source:

> "Everywhere you look, you will find God" — that's the entire CPP project.

The fact that every reductionist vector leads to God is an indicator that God is underneath everything. Having established that, the conclusion is in a sense trivial — hidden, but elementary. The next step: "Is there anything more useful we can do with that knowledge?"

## 2. Senses, Perception & the Observer

Life has senses: hot/cold, red/blue, pain/pleasure — all create particular neural patterns. To perceive a conscious point configuration, you need a perceiver — which implies a conscious-point nature to the observer. There's an "eye" that observes the patterns arising in the brain.

Thomas was careful: he does not know whether the observer *is* a conscious point, but there is certainly a conscious-point nature associated with our perception.

## 3. Conscious Entity Theory

More gestalt view: any pattern with sufficient "critical mass of consciousness" creates its own identity — a **conscious entity**. Any concentration of consciousness has beingness; with space around it, you get distinction.

This is the origin of Thomas's conscious entity concept: it can be applied to anything. There is a beingness associated with any concentration of conscious points together with the diffuse space around it.

## 4. Identity as Relational

A rock is just an "it" until someone frames it: doorstop vs. museum fossil — same object, different identity. **Identity depends on what the observer brings** — if you bring nothing, you won't even notice it.

Thomas concluded that identity is a very important principle, and this is directly connected to why categorizing essays (or anything) is so hard: what a thing *is* depends on who is looking at it.

## 5. Teaching Order for CPP

Open question from the discussion: does one teach CPP bottom-up (quarks/eCPs), top-down (from God), or show simultaneous patterns across levels unified by one principle? Thomas suggested per-essay mind maps as a first step — a central idea with branches — but noted the catch: to mind-map something, you almost have to understand it first.
