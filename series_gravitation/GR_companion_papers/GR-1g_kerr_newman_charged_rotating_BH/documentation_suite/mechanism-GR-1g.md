# Mechanism — GR-1g

In causal order. No postulate beyond GR-1c and GR-1f.

1. **Three sources, three broadcast components.** Mass contributes
   scalar compressive SSV (GR-1c); angular momentum contributes
   azimuthal vector SSV (GR-1f); charge contributes **radial** vector
   SSV (inherited from the Stiffness C companion, where it produces
   Coulomb's law). The Kerr–Newman spacetime is what happens when all
   three source the substrate at once.
2. **The components add in the structure function.** Adding the Coulomb
   SSV contribution to the Kerr Δ gives Δ_KN = r² − 2Mr + a² + Q², with
   Q² = k_e Q_SI² G/c⁴ in geometric units. Charge enters exactly where
   it enters in general relativity, by the same additive route.
3. **Limits are checked rather than assumed.** Q = 0 recovers Kerr;
   a = 0 recovers Reissner–Nordström; both zero recovers Schwarzschild.
   The paper verifies each.
4. **The bound generalizes by the same causality argument.** Requiring
   the total SSV broadcast to remain subluminal at the outer horizon
   gives **M² ≥ a² + Q²** — GR-1f's Kerr bound with the charge term
   included. Extremality remains a causality limit rather than a
   censorship conjecture.
5. **The ring singularity is prevented the same way the point one was.**
   At r = 0, θ = π/2, Σ → 0 and the classical metric diverges. The CP
   Exclusion bound PSR_eff ≥ l_P/2 saturates the lattice at Planck
   density before Σ can vanish, replacing the ring singularity with a
   Planck-density **ring** core.
6. **The evaporation chain inherits.** GR-1e's Hawking-to-remnant
   sequence applies, with the remnant carrying residual angular momentum
   and charge — *if* those are not radiated first. For most
   astrophysical holes charge neutralises rapidly, so the generic
   history reduces to Kerr → Schwarzschild → Planck remnant.

**The load-bearing feature is step 1's additivity.** The paper's claim
is not that a new mechanism handles charged rotation, but that three
already-derived broadcast components superpose without interference
terms — and the check on that claim is step 3's limits plus step 4's
bound, both of which come out matching general relativity's forms
exactly.
