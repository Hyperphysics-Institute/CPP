# FAQ — GR-1g

**Q1. What does this paper add to the arc?**
It closes the black-hole family. Mass, spin, and charge each contribute
their own broadcast component — scalar, azimuthal, radial — and the
three together give the Kerr–Newman geometry, with Reissner–Nordström,
Kerr, and Schwarzschild as verified limits.

**Q2. Why exactly three parameters?**
Because there are three source types and therefore three broadcast
components, and nothing else to broadcast. That is a *reframing* of the
no-hair count rather than a proof — the theory has not shown a fourth
channel is impossible — but it does supply a reason where general
relativity supplies only a result.

**Q3. How does charge enter the metric?**
By adding the Coulomb SSV contribution to the Kerr structure function:
Δ_KN = r² − 2Mr + a² + Q², with Q² = k_e Q_SI² G/c⁴ in geometric units.
Charge enters exactly where it does in general relativity, by the same
additive route.

**Q4. Isn't simply adding the components too easy?**
This is the right objection, and it is where a future review should
start. The Einstein–Maxwell system is nonlinear, and charge does not
generally superpose onto vacuum solutions — Kerr–Newman works out for
reasons internal to that structure. Whether the CPP broadcast components
genuinely superpose without interference terms, or whether agreement is
being obtained by construction, has never been examined externally.

**Q5. Where does the extremality bound come from?**
The same causality argument as GR-1f's Kerr bound, extended to three
components: requiring the total SSV broadcast to stay subluminal at the
outer horizon gives **M² ≥ a² + Q²**. As there, extremality is a
causality limit rather than a censorship conjecture — and as there, the
argument's exposed step is treating a pattern velocity as a
propagation speed.

**Q6. What happens to the ring singularity?**
It doesn't form. At r = 0, θ = π/2 the classical metric diverges as
Σ → 0; the CP Exclusion bound PSR_eff ≥ l_P/2 saturates the lattice at
Planck density first, leaving a Planck-density **ring** core. Worth
noting: this is the *same* untouched rule that removed the point
singularity in GR-1c, now handling a structurally different divergence.

**Q7. Do charged black holes actually matter astrophysically?**
Mostly not, and the paper says so. Charge neutralises rapidly for most
astrophysical holes, so the evaporation chain generically reduces to
Kerr → Schwarzschild → Planck remnant. The result is about completeness
of the family, not about objects in the sky.

**Q8. Does the paper derive the full Kerr–Newman metric from first
principles?**
Not at all orders. The metric is assembled and verified in every
limiting case, but obtaining Σ and Δ_KN from the nonlinear PSR formula
with the total three-component SSV magnitude is `op:allorders` — the
three-component version of GR-1f's open problem, and part of the same
family as `op:einstein`.

**Q9. Are the open problems still open?**
Three of four, genuinely. The fourth — Kerr–Newman superradiance — is
*partially* delivered: GR-1h supplies the generalized threshold
ω < mΩ₊ + qΦ₊, which is the form this item names, but the quantitative
amplification it asks for remains open (that is GR-1h's own open problem
1, for the uncharged case too). This paper's list has aged better than
GR-1f's.

**Q10. Has it been reviewed?**
No. And the inherited support is thinner than the arc's overall record
suggests: the scalar component's *metric* is externally confirmed, but
GR-1c's theorems were never reviewed, and GR-1f — the source of both the
azimuthal component and the bound argument this paper generalizes — has
had no review at all.
