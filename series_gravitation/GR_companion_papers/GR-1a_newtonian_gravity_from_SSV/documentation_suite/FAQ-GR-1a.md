# FAQ — GR-1a

**Q1. Why is gravity always attractive?**
Because its source has no sign. The eDP polarization cloud holds equal
numbers of + and − Conscious Points, so their charge contributions
cancel while their ZBW kinetic energies add — leaving mass-energy as a
positive scalar that contributes compressive SSV in every direction.
With nothing to cancel, gravitational SSV always adds between any two
masses.

**Q2. Then why can't gravity be shielded, when electric fields can?**
Same fact, read differently. Shielding requires opposite charges to
rearrange and cancel the field. There is no opposite sign of mass-energy
to rearrange, so there is nothing available to shield with.

**Q3. Is G predicted, or just re-expressed?**
Derivable, not predicted — and the distinction is worth keeping. G is
identified as ħc/m_P², where m_P is already fixed by the 600-cell
lattice, so the theory has nowhere to insert a free parameter and the
CODATA value comes out. But G was a known number; nothing was foretold.
The claim is about parameter-freedom, not about foresight.

**Q4. Doesn't the hierarchy problem just get relabelled here?**
Partly, and honestly so. The ratio F_grav/F_EM = (m/m_P)²/(e/e_P)² is
pure Planck-unit suppression, so "why is gravity weak?" becomes "why is
the electron ~10²² times lighter than the Planck mass?" — which the
paper answers by pointing at the ZBW assembly hierarchy rather than at a
tuned constant. A reader who thinks that moves the question rather than
closing it is not being unfair; what changes is that the smallness is no
longer a free choice.

**Q5. How can inertial and gravitational mass be identical *by
construction*?**
Because they are the same quantity. E_pol = mc², the compressive ZBW
polarization energy of the eDP cloud, is what resists acceleration
through the stiffness C *and* what sources the gravitational SSV field.
GR takes their equality as a principle abstracted from experiment; here
there are not two things to be equal.

**Q6. Does the equivalence principle come out too?**
At Newtonian order, yes, and without extra postulates. A test particle
in free fall moves along the local SSV gradient, which contracts its
PSR_eff exactly as acceleration does in flat space — so no local
measurement distinguishes them. The full-strength statement belongs to
the strong-field companions.

**Q7. Where does the inverse-square law come from?**
It is inherited, not re-derived. The Stiffness C companion established
that a Grid Point's fixed per-tick SSV quantum, spread over a growing
spherical shell, gives ΔSSV ∝ 1/r². Gravity uses the identical
geometry — the paper's contribution is the source, not the falloff.

**Q8. Can this paper handle light bending?**
No, and that limitation is structural rather than incidental. A
scalar-only treatment sources clock rate but has no direction in it, and
gives half the observed deflection (0.875″ against 1.75″). GR-1b's
extension of the broadcast to carry the net SSV *vector* was forced by
exactly this shortfall. GR-1a is the arc's scalar ground floor, and the
factor of two is the observable that demanded the next paper.

**Q9. Has this paper been peer-reviewed by the AI panel?**
Not at paper level — it predates the CONV protocol's application to the
gravitational companions, and no dedicated round exists for it. Its
central relation k·Δ|SSV| = GM/rc² was, however, independently
re-derived from the messenger census in the field-equation programme
(GR-1j, T-1), which is the strongest indirect check it has. The
no-polarity proposition and the Q_grav normalization remain
unreviewed — a future round would start there.

**Q10. What's the weakest step?**
The "by analogy" construction of Q_grav from Q_EM in §3. Everything
downstream — G, the hierarchy ratio, the force law — depends on that
normalization being the right one rather than a plausible one. The paper
states the analogy openly; it does not derive the gravitational quantum
from the census the way GR-1j later derives the field equation.
