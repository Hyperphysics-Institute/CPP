# Philosophy — GR-1e

**Ruling out your own explanation.** The paper's best moment is a
negative result about itself. The natural story for why evaporation
stops is thermal runaway — the temperature climbs toward Planck and
something gives. The paper computes it: at termination T_Haw is 4% of
the Planck temperature. The thermal mechanism does not fire, and with a
factor of ~25 to spare. What binds instead is geometric, and the
argument is only convincing *because* the obvious alternative was
checked and eliminated. Most papers arguing for a cutoff do not do this.

**A remnant that is held up rather than left over.** There is a weak and
a strong version of "the evaporation stops." The weak one: the
calculation breaks down, so we don't know what happens, and something
presumably remains. The strong one: a specific balance of forces holds a
specific object at a specific radius. This paper claims the strong
version — gravitational SSV compression against CP Exclusion repulsion,
a self-sustaining fixed point at PSR_eff = l_P/2, requiring no external
pressure. Whether it is right is another matter, but it is a real claim
with a mechanism, not a shrug at a breakdown.

**Reporting the number that hurts.** On the information paradox, the
paper computes the remnant's capacity as order k_B and states outright
that this is *far below* the original Bekenstein–Hawking entropy — then
concludes that the remnant cannot resolve the paradox by itself and the
answer must involve the whole radiation train. Remnant scenarios are
often sold as paradox solutions. This one arrives at a remnant and then
declines the sale, which is the harder and more useful thing to publish.

**Discreteness as the terminating structure.** The arc's refrain is that
lattice grain is invisible: (l_P/r)² at every tested scale. GR-1d found
the one region where that fails outward (the near-horizon cavity); this
paper finds where it fails inward. When the whole object is two lattice
cells across, the continuum has nothing left to approximate. Both papers
are the same observation from opposite directions — discreteness matters
exactly where the smoothing runs out — and both are the places CPP could
be caught.

**An unexploited flag, recorded rather than mined.** CONV-027 minted a
note that the census speed drops toward ~0.29c near the exclusion
radius, and flagged it for this lane specifically. It has never been
folded in here. The suite records it as a forward pointer rather than
speculating about its effect, because guessing at consequences inside a
documentation pass is how documentation starts inventing physics.
