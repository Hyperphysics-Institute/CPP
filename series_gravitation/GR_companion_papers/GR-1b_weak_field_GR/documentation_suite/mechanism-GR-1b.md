# Mechanism — GR-1b

In causal order, and starting from where GR-1a stopped:

1. **The scalar theory is not enough.** GR-1a's |SSV|_abs sources clock
   rate and reproduces Newton, but a scalar field has no direction in
   it: it cannot curve space, and it deflects starlight by 0.875″
   against a measured 1.75″. The shortfall is a factor of two, and it is
   not adjustable.
2. **The broadcast is given a second component.** The DI-bit broadcast
   is extended to carry the net vector **SSV**_net alongside the scalar
   |SSV|_abs. The pair is the Lattice State Packet, constructed
   explicitly from the eDP cloud's compressive polarization energy:
   |SSV|_abs = E_pol/V₀.
3. **The two channels source different parts of the metric.**
   |SSV|_abs → g_tt (time curvature); **SSV**_net → g_ij (spatial
   curvature). Holographic summation over the broadcast reconstructs the
   weak-field isotropic Schwarzschild metric with no free parameters.
4. **The factor of two falls out as an equality, not a fit.** In the
   weak field the temporal and spatial contributions to null deflection
   are *equal*, giving α = 4GM/c²b = 1.75″. The equality is structural:
   nothing was tuned to produce it, and no scalar-only variant of CPP
   can reach it.
5. **Geodesics come from the selection rule.** Motion follows the
   12-edge maximum-gradient rule of the Absolute Moment companion; its
   continuum limit is geodesic motion, and the equivalence principle
   follows without additional postulates.
6. **The field equations appear as a self-consistency requirement.** The
   metric reconstructed from the LSP summation must equal the metric
   that governs LSP propagation. In the weak field this is equivalent to
   the linearised Einstein equations — and the paper stops there,
   labelling the full nonlinear case a *strong conjecture* requiring a
   convergence proof it does not attempt.
7. **Waves and horizons.** Gravitational waves are propagating lattice
   perturbations with both channels jointly perturbed in
   transverse-traceless gauge, travelling at c. The GP Exclusion rule
   produces an effective horizon. Z₀ = √(μ₀/ε₀) is derived from lattice
   parameters as a side result.

**The load-bearing novelty is step 4's equality.** The two-component
extension would be unfalsifiable decoration if the split were free; what
makes it a real claim is that the weak-field shares come out *equal*,
reproducing the measured deflection exactly, with the extension having
been forced by that same observable rather than fitted after it.
