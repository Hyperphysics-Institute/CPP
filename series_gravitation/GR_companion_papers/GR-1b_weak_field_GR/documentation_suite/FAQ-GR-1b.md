# FAQ — GR-1b

**Q1. What does this paper add that GR-1a didn't have?**
A direction. GR-1a's scalar |SSV|_abs sources clock rate and reproduces
Newton, but a scalar has no orientation and cannot curve space. This
paper extends the broadcast to carry the net vector **SSV**_net, which
sources g_ij — and that second channel is what makes the weak-field
metric, the correct deflection, and gravitational waves possible.

**Q2. Isn't adding a second component just adding a free parameter to
fit the data?**
It would be, if the split were adjustable. It isn't: in the weak field
the temporal and spatial contributions to deflection come out *equal*,
which is what produces 1.75″ rather than some tunable value between
0.875″ and 1.75″. The extension was forced by the observation, and then
had to survive every subsequent paper in the arc without further
adjustment.

**Q3. So is the factor of two a prediction?**
Not in the foresight sense — Eddington measured it in 1919. The paper
calls it a retroactive prediction, which is fair if read carefully: the
structure was chosen because nothing scalar could reach the number, and
the structure's *other* consequences were not chosen. The claim worth
defending is that no scalar-only variant of CPP can reproduce the
measurement.

**Q4. Does this paper derive Einstein's field equations?**
The linearised ones, as the weak-field form of the self-consistency
condition — the requirement that the metric reconstructed from the LSP
summation equal the metric governing LSP propagation. The *full
nonlinear* equations are explicitly labelled a **strong conjecture**,
requiring a proof that the discrete lattice sum converges to smooth
Riemannian geometry, and are deferred. GR-1j has since derived a general
field equation from the messenger census by a different route, but full
rank-2 Einstein equivalence remains open at `op:einstein`.

**Q5. Where do gravitational waves come from here?**
Propagating perturbations of the lattice geometry sourced by
time-varying mass-energy quadrupoles, with both LSP channels jointly
perturbed in transverse-traceless gauge. They travel at c. The LIGO
observations therefore confirm, in this reading, that the **SSV**_net
component propagates at c and carries energy.

**Q6. Why does the impedance of free space appear in a gravity paper?**
Because it is derivable from the same lattice parameters, and it closed
an open problem inherited from the ZDC Chaining companion. It is a side
result, honestly presented as one.

**Q8. Are the paper's Open Problems still open?**
Two of six are not. Item (2), the exact Schwarzschild solution with a
non-singular Planck-scale interior, was delivered by GR-1c (Theorem 1;
r_core = r_S/2). Item (3), the Kerr metric, was delivered by GR-1f, and
GR-1g extended it to Kerr–Newman. Item (1) remains open as stated,
though substantially advanced by GR-1j. Item (5), the cosmological
constant, has been *downgraded* rather than advanced. The full current
status is tabulated in `phenomena-GR-1b.md`; the paper's own list has
not been edited, and correcting it is scoped to the founder as a
proposed pass across the legacy companions.

**Q9. What happened with the cosmological-constant claim?**
It was withdrawn and labelled. Under OBL-CAL-LABEL (V3.3, Patch 3204),
the residual DP-Sea SSV is a candidate *mechanism* only; the *magnitude*
of Λ is ruled not derivable from first principles at current knowledge;
d_s^emp = 4.636 is cosmology-calibrated and never predicted; and the
earlier "zero-parameter mechanism brackets the observed Λ" language was
withdrawn at CONV-020. The label rides inside the paper rather than
living only in a registry.

**Q10. What's the weakest step?**
The holographic LSP summation by which the metric is reconstructed. It
delivers the right weak-field form, but it has never been externally
reviewed, and the equal-shares result — on which the entire
factor-of-two argument depends — is asserted from that construction
rather than derived from the census the way GR-1j later derives its
statics.
