# Philosophy — GR-1h

**A mechanism reading that costs nothing and buys clarity.** Standard
treatments obtain superradiance by scattering waves off the Kerr metric.
Here it reduces to a comparison of two rates: a wave's phase velocity
against the rotation rate of an actual broadcast field. The resulting
condition is the standard one, ω < mΩ₊ — no new number, no discrepancy
to explain. That is the arc's recurring pattern at its most modest and
most defensible: same physics, transparent mechanism, no claim of
novelty where none exists.

**Recovering a famous number and saying what it *is*.** The 29.3%
maximum extraction is textbook. What CPP adds is an identification: it
is the fraction of rest-mass energy stored in the azimuthal SSV at
extremal spin. Whether one finds this illuminating or merely
re-descriptive is a fair question, and the paper does not oversell it.
The honest position is that a mechanism reading of a known quantity is
worth something when the mechanism is independently constrained, and
worth little when it is not.

**Predictions that are real but not distinguishing.** The boson-cloud
gravitational waves and the Regge gap are genuine, testable, and already
being searched for — by people who arrived at them from standard
ultralight-boson physics. CPP reproduces them; it does not own them. The
suite states this plainly because the alternative — listing them as
"CPP predictions" without qualification — would be the kind of quiet
inflation the arc's claim discipline exists to prevent.

**One place where the theory could actually differ, and it is
unresolved.** Open problem 2 asks whether the Planck core reflects
bosonic waves as it does gravitational ones. If it does, the cavity
between barrier and core would enhance the instability beyond the GR
prediction — a "Planck-core bomb." That is the only genuinely
distinguishing signature in the paper, and it is registered as a
question rather than advertised as a result. Restraint of exactly the
kind that makes the rest of the arc's claims credible.

**A bottleneck, seen from a second angle.** Three of this paper's four
open problems turn on Planck-core reflectivity — the same quantity GR-1d
needs to convert its parameter-free echo *delay* into an *amplitude*.
Two independent papers, arriving from different directions, stall on one
uncomputed number that lives behind `op:einstein`. That is worth
noticing as programme structure rather than as two separate gaps: the
strong-field interior is not merely one open problem among many; it is
the shared blocker for the arc's two most observationally live results.
