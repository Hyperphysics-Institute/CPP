# FAQ — GR-1h

**Q1. What is superradiance, in this picture?**
A rate comparison. A spinning hole's azimuthal SSV field rotates at
Ω₊ = ac/(r₊² + a²). A bosonic wave with frequency ω and azimuthal number
m has phase velocity ω/m in that direction. When the wave lags the field
— ω < mΩ₊ — the field does work on it and the wave is amplified.

**Q2. Is that different from the standard derivation?**
Same condition, different route. Standard treatments scatter waves off
the Kerr metric; here the threshold falls out of comparing a wave's
phase velocity to the rotation rate of an actual broadcast. There is no
new number and no discrepancy — the contribution is the mechanism, not
the result.

**Q3. Where does 29.3% come from?**
It is the standard maximum extraction, (1 − 1/√2)Mc², from taking an
extremal hole (a = M) to a non-rotating one. What CPP adds is an
identification: that energy is what was stored in the azimuthal SSV at
extremal spin, and the irreducible mass is the Schwarzschild hole left
once it is gone.

**Q4. Are the boson-cloud predictions CPP predictions?**
They are real predictions and they are **not distinguishing**. The
continuous gravitational waves at f_GW ≈ 2μc²/h and the Regge gap are
consequences of superradiance itself, and are already being searched for
by people who arrived at them through standard ultralight-boson physics.
CPP reproduces them. Listing them as "CPP predictions" without that
qualifier would be inflation.

**Q5. Is there anything here that *would* distinguish CPP?**
One thing, and it is unresolved: open problem 2. GR-1c's Planck core is
reflective for gravitational perturbations. If it also reflects scalar
and vector bosonic waves, the cavity between the potential barrier and
the core would enhance the superradiant instability beyond the GR
prediction — the "Planck-core bomb." That is registered as a question,
not claimed as a result.

**Q6. What boson masses are in play?**
Cloud growth is efficient for α = GMμ/ħc ~ 0.1–0.5, giving
μc² ≈ α × 1.3×10⁻¹² eV around stellar-mass holes — so
μ ~ 10⁻¹³–10⁻¹² eV puts f_GW in the LIGO O4 band.

**Q7. What is the Regge gap and why does it matter?**
Superradiance spins holes down until the threshold closes, so the
population should show a depleted region in the (M, a/M) plane for boson
masses in the growth window. It matters because it can be tested against
*existing* black-hole spin measurements — no new instrument required.

**Q8. Can you predict how strong the amplification is?**
No. The reflection coefficient |R|² − 1 requires solving the radial
Teukolsky equation with CPP boundary conditions at r₀ = r₊ + l_P, and
the CPP core modifies the near-horizon boundary condition in a way that
may change amplification relative to GR. Registered as open problem 1.

**Q9. Are the open problems still open?**
All four, genuinely — and three of them turn on the **same uncomputed
quantity**: Planck-core reflectivity. That is also what GR-1d needs to
turn its parameter-free echo delay into an amplitude. Two papers, two
directions, one shared blocker sitting behind `op:einstein`.

**Q10. Has this paper been reviewed?**
No. And its foundation is unreviewed too: everything follows from
GR-1f's azimuthal SSV, and GR-1f has never had a round. Note that the
agreement with textbook Kerr results is not evidence about CPP — it
shows the mechanism reading reproduces known physics, which is weaker
and different. A future round should examine this paper together with
GR-1f, since both rest on the same class of identification and one
objection would land on both.
