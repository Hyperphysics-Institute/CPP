# Mechanism — GR-1h

In causal order. No postulate beyond GR-1f.

1. **The horizon rotates because the broadcast does.** GR-1f gave the
   spinning source an azimuthal SSV component. Its angular frequency at
   the outer horizon is Ω₊ = ac/(r₊² + a²) — in CPP this is not an
   abstract property of a metric but the literal rotation rate of a
   physical field.
2. **A lagging wave is dragged.** A bosonic wave of frequency ω and
   azimuthal number m has phase velocity ω/m in the rotational
   direction. When ω/m < Ω₊ — that is, **ω < mΩ₊** — the wave lags the
   rotating SSV field, and the field does work on it. That is
   superradiance, with the amplification condition falling out of a
   simple comparison of rates.
3. **Charge adds a second lag.** For a charged hole the electrostatic
   potential at the horizon contributes, giving ω < mΩ₊ + qΦ₊ — the same
   argument with a second channel.
4. **What is extracted is rotational SSV energy.** The irreducible mass
   is the Schwarzschild hole left after all azimuthal SSV energy is
   removed. Going from extremal (a = M) to non-rotating gives
   ΔE_max = (1 − 1/√2)Mc² ≈ 29.3% — which, read through CPP, is *the
   fraction of rest-mass energy stored in the azimuthal SSV at extremal
   spin.*
5. **Bound states grow into clouds.** If ultralight bosons exist, they
   occupy bound states around the hole, and every mode satisfying the
   threshold grows. Efficiency peaks at α = GMμ/ħc ~ 0.1–0.5, giving
   μc² ≈ α × 1.3×10⁻¹² eV for stellar-mass holes.
6. **Clouds radiate, and the hole spins down.** Cloud annihilation
   produces continuous gravitational waves at f_GW ≈ 2μc²/h — in the
   LIGO band for μ ~ 10⁻¹³–10⁻¹² eV. Meanwhile the extracted angular
   momentum reduces a, until the threshold closes.
7. **The spin-down is visible in a population.** Holes should be
   depleted in the region of the (M, a/M) Regge plane where α sits in
   the growth window — the "Regge gap," testable against existing spin
   measurements without waiting for a new instrument.

**The load-bearing move is step 1→2.** Standard treatments derive
superradiance from wave scattering on the Kerr metric; here it reduces
to a comparison between a wave's phase velocity and the rotation rate of
an actual field. The physics is the same, but the mechanism is
transparent — which is the arc's usual claim, and here it costs nothing
because the resulting condition is the standard one.
