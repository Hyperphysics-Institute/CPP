# Mechanism — GR-1f

In causal order. No postulate beyond GR-1c.

1. **A rotating source broadcasts asymmetrically.** The same 12-edge
   selection rule that generates the Schwarzschild scalar SSV, applied
   to a source with angular momentum J, produces an azimuthal component:
   k(SSV_net)_φ = GJ sin²θ/(c²r³).
2. **The azimuthal component maps to an off-diagonal metric term.** Via
   the LSP metric mapping, it gives g_tφ = −2GJ sin²θ/(c²r) — exactly
   the Lense–Thirring frame-dragging term in the weak field, confirmed
   by Gravity Probe B to 0.3%.
3. **The scalar backbone is unchanged.** The Schwarzschild content
   inherited from GR-1c is untouched; rotation adds a channel rather
   than modifying the existing one. Setting J = 0 recovers Schwarzschild
   exactly.
4. **The broadcast has a speed limit, and it binds.** Every LSP
   broadcast propagates at exactly c, and the Absolute Moment tick is
   the minimum time interval. The azimuthal pattern velocity at the
   outer horizon is v_φ = ac/r₊ = ac/(M + √(M² − a²)).
5. **Subluminality *is* the Kerr bound.** Requiring v_φ ≤ c reduces
   algebraically to a² ≤ M², i.e. **J ≤ GM²/c**. A hole spinning faster
   cannot form — not because a naked singularity would be unseemly, but
   because its own broadcast would have to outrun light on the lattice.
6. **The ergosphere is where the drag reaches c.** The region in which
   the azimuthal drag velocity equals c is exactly the region where no
   observer can remain static — the ergosphere, with the Penrose process
   following classically.
7. **The full metric is consistent, not derived.** The complete
   Boyer–Lindquist Kerr form is shown consistent with the CPP mechanism
   at all orders; deriving Σ = r² + a²cos²θ and Δ = r² − 2Mr + a² *from
   the CPP field equation* is registered as `op:allorders`.

**The load-bearing novelty is step 5.** In general relativity the Kerr
bound is a consequence of cosmic censorship — a conjecture about what
nature declines to expose. Here it is a causality constraint on a
broadcast in a lattice with a fixed tick, and it comes out as a theorem.
Converting a conjecture into a theorem is the strongest kind of claim a
substrate theory can make, and correspondingly the one most worth
attacking.
