# Reviews — GR-1f

**Review basis: NONE.** No dedicated five-seat CONV round exists for
GR-1f, and no adjudication record.

**Inherited and indirect support:**

- The **scalar backbone** is GR-1c's exact Schwarzschild solution, which
  was independently confirmed to machine precision (3.3×10⁻¹⁶) within
  CONV-029's clearance of GR-1i. The J = 0 limit is therefore on firm
  ground.
- The **Lense–Thirring term** matched against Gravity Probe B (0.3%) is
  standard weak-field physics and is not in dispute; but it is
  *inherited* agreement, not a test of this paper's mechanism.
- **GR-1j's T-2** proves the isotropic solution is the unique static
  spherically symmetric vacuum exterior. That is the *non-rotating*
  case, and it says nothing about the Kerr sector.

**What has NOT been reviewed:** the azimuthal SSV expression
k(SSV_net)_φ = GJ sin²θ/(c²r³); the LSP mapping that turns it into
g_tφ; **the Kerr bound theorem** (the paper's headline claim); the
ergosphere argument; and the all-orders consistency demonstration for
the Boyer–Lindquist form.

**Where a future round should start: the Kerr bound.** The argument
identifies the azimuthal SSV's "pattern velocity" v_φ = ac/r₊ with a
physical propagation speed subject to subluminality. Whether a *pattern*
velocity is the right object to constrain — as opposed to a group or
signal velocity — is precisely the sort of question a panel exists to
press, and it carries the whole theorem. If it survives, the programme
has converted cosmic censorship into a theorem; if it does not, the
bound reverts to being inherited from GR.

**A cross-arc note:** CONV-027's NOTE-GR-CSTAR-STRONGFIELD (census speed
c_* → ~0.29c near the exclusion radius) was flagged for the GR-1d/GR-1e
lane, but it bears on this paper too — a reduced propagation speed near
the horizon is directly relevant to a bound derived from a
near-horizon velocity reaching c. Recorded here as an open question, not
an objection; nobody has worked it through.
