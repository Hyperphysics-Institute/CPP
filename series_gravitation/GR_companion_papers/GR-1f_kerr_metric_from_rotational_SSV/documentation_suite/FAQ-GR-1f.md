# FAQ — GR-1f

**Q1. Where does frame dragging come from?**
From the broadcast acquiring a direction of rotation. A source with
angular momentum J produces an azimuthal component
k(SSV_net)_φ = GJ sin²θ/(c²r³) via the same 12-edge selection rule that
generates the Schwarzschild scalar; the LSP mapping turns it into the
off-diagonal metric term g_tφ = −2GJ sin²θ/(c²r), which is exactly
Lense–Thirring in the weak field.

**Q2. Does the paper derive the Kerr metric?**
It derives the frame-dragging term and shows the full Boyer–Lindquist
form is *consistent* with the CPP mechanism at all orders. Deriving the
structure functions Σ = r² + a²cos²θ and Δ = r² − 2Mr + a² from the CPP
field equation is registered as `op:allorders` and has not been done.
The distinction is the paper's, not a later gloss.

**Q3. What is the Kerr bound result, and why does it matter?**
Requiring the azimuthal broadcast to stay subluminal — v_φ = ac/r₊ ≤ c —
reduces algebraically to a ≤ M, i.e. **J ≤ GM²/c**. That is the Kerr
bound. It matters because in GR the same bound follows from cosmic
censorship, an open conjecture; here it is a causality constraint on a
lattice with a fixed tick. **Cosmic censorship becomes a theorem.**

**Q4. Isn't that too strong a claim?**
It is the strongest claim in the paper and the least examined. The step
worth pressing: it treats the azimuthal SSV's *pattern* velocity as a
physical propagation speed subject to subluminality. Whether a pattern
velocity is the right object to constrain — rather than a group or
signal velocity — carries the entire theorem, and no external reviewer
has yet asked. See `reviews-GR-1f.md`.

**Q5. What is the ergosphere here?**
The region where the azimuthal SSV drag velocity reaches c, which is
exactly the region where no observer can remain static. The Penrose
process follows classically; its wave counterpart is superradiance
(GR-1h).

**Q6. Does anything change in the non-rotating limit?**
No — J = 0 recovers Schwarzschild exactly, not asymptotically. Rotation
adds a channel; it does not modify the scalar backbone.

**Q7. Has this been reviewed?**
No dedicated round exists. The Lense–Thirring agreement with Gravity
Probe B is standard weak-field physics and can create a misleading
impression of coverage: it is *inherited* agreement, not a test of this
paper's mechanism. The novel content — the azimuthal SSV expression, the
LSP mapping, the Kerr bound theorem, the ergosphere argument — is
unexamined.

**Q8. Are the paper's open problems still open?**
Two of four are not. The Kerr–Newman extension was delivered by GR-1g;
superradiance by GR-1h — both sibling companions carrying the same March
2026 date, written in immediate succession, with this paper's list never
updated afterward. Kerr echoes remain open (GR-1d covers Schwarzschild
and registers the Kerr case as its own open problem), and `op:allorders`
remains correctly open. The `.tex` has not been edited; see
`phenomena-GR-1f.md`.

**Q9. Does the strong-field wave-speed note affect the Kerr bound?**
Nobody knows, and it is a fair question. CONV-027 minted
NOTE-GR-CSTAR-STRONGFIELD — the census speed dropping to ~0.29c near the
exclusion radius — and flagged it for the GR-1d/GR-1e lane. But a bound
derived from a *near-horizon velocity reaching c* is plainly the kind of
result a near-horizon speed reduction could bear on. Recorded as an open
question, not an objection.

**Q10. What's the weakest step?**
The identification of the pattern velocity with a subluminality-constrained
propagation speed (Q4), followed by the LSP off-diagonal mapping. Both
are load-bearing, neither is reviewed, and the second has no captured
reasoning behind it.
