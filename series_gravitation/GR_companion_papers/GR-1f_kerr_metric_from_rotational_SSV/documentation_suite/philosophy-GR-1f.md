# Philosophy — GR-1f

**A conjecture becomes a theorem, and that is exactly where to push.**
In general relativity the Kerr bound follows from cosmic censorship — a
conjecture, undecided after half a century, about whether nature permits
naked singularities. Here it drops out of causality: the azimuthal
broadcast cannot exceed c on a lattice with a minimum tick, and the
algebra reduces to a ≤ M. That is the most ambitious kind of claim an
emergent theory can make — not reproducing a result but *changing its
epistemic status*. It is also, for the same reason, the claim most
deserving of hostile attention, and nobody outside the programme has yet
given it any.

**Consistency is not derivation, and the paper says which is which.**
The full Boyer–Lindquist metric is shown *consistent* with the CPP
mechanism at all orders; deriving Σ and Δ from the field equation is
registered as open. That distinction is the same one GR-1's ledger drew
between recovering solutions and deriving equations, applied here to a
single metric. A looser paper would have written "we derive the Kerr
metric" and let the reader discover the qualifier in the open-problems
section.

**One channel added, none modified.** Rotation does not alter the
scalar backbone; it adds an azimuthal component alongside it, and J = 0
recovers Schwarzschild exactly. The arc's recurring structural claim —
one mechanism, extended rather than patched — holds here as it does
between the weak and strong field. What makes it checkable is that the
limit is exact, not asymptotic.

**Batch-shipped, and never cross-referenced.** Two of this paper's four
open problems were solved by companions dated the same week. That is a
different failure from the one the earlier suites found: GR-1b and
GR-1c were overtaken by work done months later, whereas this paper's
siblings landed almost immediately and nobody went back to update the
list. It is a small thing and an instructive one — the papers were
written as a batch, and a batch needs a closing pass that the programme
did not have in March 2026.

**The unreviewed centre.** The Kerr bound theorem, the ergosphere
argument, and the LSP mapping that produces g_tφ have never been
examined by anyone outside the programme. The Lense–Thirring number is
solid because it is standard weak-field physics matched against Gravity
Probe B, and that solidity can create a false impression of coverage.
The novel part — the causality derivation — is the unexamined part.
