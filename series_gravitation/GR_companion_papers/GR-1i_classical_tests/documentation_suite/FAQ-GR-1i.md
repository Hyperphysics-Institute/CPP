# FAQ — GR-1i

**Q1. If every number equals the GR value, why write the paper?**
Entry-criterion compliance. A reader asking "does this theory pass the
classical tests?" is entitled to find the derivations in one citable
place rather than being told the metric is Schwarzschild and left to do
the geodesics themselves. The paper also makes explicit what the tests
*do* discriminate — CPP from Newton — which a bare assertion of metric
equivalence would leave unstated.

**Q2. Does anything here count as a prediction?**
No, and the Swarm-Validation section says so. Because the metric is
exactly Schwarzschild, every value is the GR value by construction;
counting them as confirmations would be double-counting against results
already claimed by GR-1c. One falsifiable feature is registered
*unminted*: achromatic bending.

**Q3. So is CPP just general relativity in different notation?**
No — this is the misreading the claim discipline exists to pre-empt. CPP
*reproduces* GR's classical tests; it does not *share* GR's theoretical
basis. Underneath is a rigid Grid Point lattice, compressed-DP
aggregates, and a Planck-sphere response; the agreement is a derived
consequence of that machinery, not a reformulation of Einstein's. The
theories are structurally different where the substrate becomes visible.

**Q4. Where does the factor of two in light deflection come from?**
Half from time curvature (the scalar |SSV|_abs channel, which alone
gives the Newtonian 0.875″) and half from spatial curvature (the vector
**SSV**_net channel). In CPP this split is structural rather than
accidental: GR-1b's extension of the LSP broadcast to carry the vector
component was forced by exactly this observable. The test is therefore
the arc's sharpest discriminant — it separates CPP from Newton *and*
from any scalar-broadcast-only variant of CPP itself.

**Q5. What is physically bending the light, if space isn't curving?**
Refraction. PSR shrinkage makes the Sea effectively denser near the
mass, giving a graded index n(r) ≈ 1 + 2GM/c²r; wavefronts advance more
slowly on the sun-side and the ray turns. Shapiro delay is the same
slowing integrated along the path. Redshift is the clock-rate gradient
directly.

**Q6. Why is Mercury's precession singled out as different?**
It is the only one of the four sensitive beyond first order in ρ. The
∝u² term in the timelike Binet equation exists because the PSR response
is used *unexpanded* — so perihelion precession is the first observable
ever recorded that sees the substrate response as a full constitutive
law rather than a linearization.

**Q7. You quote GM_⊙ that differs from the IAU value. Why?**
Deliberately, for provenance matching: the paper's inputs ARE the verify
script's inputs, so a reader running the script gets the paper's

entry is tabulated — notably the perihelion figure moves from 42.99 to
42.98″/cy, dead-centre of the observed 42.98 ± 0.04. Every shift is far
inside the observational uncertainty, and a reader preferring the IAU
value may rescale throughout.

**Q8. The Shapiro number is a leading-log estimate. Isn't that weak
against Cassini's precision?**
It would be, if it were being offered as a γ test — which is why the
panel had it relabelled. Cassini compliance here is *structural*:
β = γ = 1 identically, because the metric is exactly Schwarzschild. The
leading-log figure is a coarse consistency check, nothing more, and the
paper now says so.

**Q9. The numeric integration agrees with the closed forms. Doesn't

No — it confirms the implementation. Both routes instantiate the same
Schwarzschild geodesics, so the agreement tests the code, not the
theory. The paper labels it an implementation cross-check, which is why
it does not appear anywhere in the predictions accounting.

**Q10. What would falsify this paper?**

frequency-independent, so achromaticity is not optional). A measurement
isolating the spatial-curvature contribution to deflection and finding

Below those, everything inherits the arc's standing conditional: if the
