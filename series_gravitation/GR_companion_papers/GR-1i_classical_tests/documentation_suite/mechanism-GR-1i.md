# Mechanism — GR-1i

The paper's mathematics is standard geodesic integration; its *physics*
is what the substrate is doing during each test. In causal order:

1. **The mass loads the Sea.** Polarization energy from the source
   loads the Dipole Sea with compressive stress, broadcast outward as
   shells of LSP content.
2. **The substrate responds by shrinking.** PSR_eff = l_P/(1+k·Δ|SSV|):
   Planck spheres nearer the mass are smaller, so more processing steps
   fit per unit distance. The Sea is effectively *denser* there.
3. **Density becomes optics and clock rate.** Clocks — bound ZBW cycles
   — tick slower in the denser region. Light, advancing one Planck
   sphere per Moment, has a lower coordinate speed: to first order
   c(r) ≈ c(1 − 2GM/c²r), so the gravitating Sea is a graded-index
   medium with n(r) ≈ 1 + 2GM/c²r.
4. **Each test reads a different consequence of the same gradient:**
   - *Redshift* is the clock-rate gradient directly — the same
     oscillator runs slower deep in the stressed Sea. Pure scalar
     physics.
   - *Deflection* is refraction: wavefronts advance more slowly on the
     sun-side, turning the ray. Exactly half of n−1 comes from the
     slowed clock (scalar channel) and half from the compressed spatial
     lattice (vector channel). The index is frequency-independent, so
     the bending is achromatic.
   - *Shapiro delay* is the same slowed propagation integrated along the
     path: the pulse spends extra coordinate time in the dense region,
     and a distant clock registers the accumulated lateness.
   - *Perihelion precession* is the orbit-scale signature of the
     *unexpanded* nonlinearity: the effective potential acquires the
     ∝u² term, the apsides no longer close, and the ellipse swivels.
5. **What is visible and what is smoothed.** At Mercury's orbit the
   discreteness parameter (l_P/r)² is ~10⁻⁹⁰ — nothing in these tests
   can see a Planck sphere. What survives smoothing is *functional
   form*: the specific u² correction is the fingerprint of the PSR
   formula, and the equal scalar/vector split is the fingerprint of the
   two-component broadcast. Structure, not granularity.

**The load-bearing point is step 4's deflection entry.** The two-channel
split is not fitted to the observation; GR-1b's extension of the LSP
broadcast to carry **SSV**_net was *forced* by exactly this observable.
That makes the factor of two the arc's sharpest internal discriminant —
it rules out scalar-only variants of CPP, not merely Newton.
