# Patch 3619 — reasoning fragment (verbatim, Tier 4)

The founder asked the right question: predicting something different
from general relativity seems risky; can we check the number against
GR, or are we confident? I was not confident, and the way to find out
was to compute what a black hole IS in the language of the map.

A horizon is a wall law too: take the solution that is ingoing at the
horizon, carry it out to 2.734 M, and read its log-derivative there.
My first attempt — "ingoing at the wall" — was wrong by twenty and
sixty percent, because a wall at 2.7 M is not the horizon and the
potential between them matters; corrected within the patch. Done
properly, the horizon-equivalent wall reproduces the Kerr quasinormal
mode to under a percent in frequency and about one in damping. The
machinery is validated against GR at the level the ringdown tests
need.

And the black hole's impedance is minus 0.032 plus 0.039 i. The real
part is the number the ringdown calibration produced yesterday for a
lossless wall, minus 0.025 plus or minus 0.005. The calibration
recovered the horizon's real impedance without being told to. The
imaginary part is absorption, which a lossless wall lacks, and the
eighteen percent deficit is exactly the difference between a lossless
wall and a horizon at the same real impedance. Walk from one to the
other and the residual goes from eighteen percent to zero, every step
inside GW150914's box.

So the honest answer: the eighteen percent is not a prediction, it is
the lossless extreme. The prediction is that the ringdown is shorter-
lived than Kerr's by between zero and eighteen percent, depending on
how much of the wave the surface absorbs into the core relative to a
horizon, which absorbs everything. The theory's wall absorbs — the
June axiom sends the traceless content into the core — so the theory
sits on the path, and where it sits is the junction computation. A
surface that absorbs as a horizon does is invisible in the ringdown.

The risk is smaller than the founder feared and differently placed.
The theory cannot predict a longer-lived ringdown than Kerr's, and it
cannot predict a real impedance away from the horizon's; the data show
neither. What it does predict is a residual bounded above by eighteen
percent, and the map now has its most important marked point: the
black hole itself.
