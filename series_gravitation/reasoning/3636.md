# Patch 3636 — reasoning fragment (verbatim, Tier 4)

Thomas said the view might come from continuing the derivation, and it
did, in one step I should have taken a patch ago. The corpus already
says the register is the lapse and the cap is lapse ½, and it already
says matter obeys Einstein's equations, whose static limit is that the
pressure gradient is the lapse gradient. A pinned register is a
uniform lapse; a uniform lapse is no pressure gradient. Reading (b)
needed a force that isn't the lapse gradient, and the corpus has none.
So reading (a) is a theorem of two ratified rulings, and the postulate
of 3635 retires into it.

Then solar masses. I transcribed SLy and APR4 as piecewise polytropes
from memory — flagged — and got the textbook maxima, 2.05 and 2.19, at
ten kilometres, so the transcription is sound. The threshold star,
where the central lapse hits ½, is 1.78 solar masses for both. That
number is the lapse, not the EOS, which is why it is the same for the
two. PSR J0740 is 2.08 ± 0.07. Four sigma.

I expected that to be the end of the ordinary branch and a
disconnected exotic branch somewhere above, as 3635 had it. The
realistic EOS showed otherwise: the flat-core branch begins at the
threshold star — there is a member at 1.82 with a three-kilometre
core — and continues with the core growing and the core pressure
falling, up to about 2.9 solar masses with the core most of the star.
The polytrope scan had simply missed the join. So the theory's
sequence is one sequence: TOV to 1.78, then a flat core that grows.
Every pulsar heavier than 1.78 is a flat-core star, and a 2.08 one is
eleven kilometres across, against NICER's 12.4 ± 1.

All of that hangs on one thing I have not done: whether the flat-core
branch is stable. Mass rises as the core pressure falls, which in a
TOV star is the signature of instability, but this is not a TOV star
and the turning-point argument does not transfer. If the branch is
unstable, the theory as ruled is contradicted by J0740 at four sigma.
If it is stable, the theory predicts a knee at 1.78, small radii above
it, and a maximum near 2.9. Either way the next patch is the radial
modes, and I would rather compute them than argue about them.
