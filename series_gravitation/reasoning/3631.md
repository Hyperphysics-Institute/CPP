# Patch 3631 — reasoning fragment (verbatim, Tier 4)

I went in expecting to compute a stress and came out with something
better: the datum is not a stress at all.

First the bookkeeping, which I did symbolically end to end because the
last round caught a recalled sign. Hinderer's convention has the minus
signs on g_rr and the angular part; with them the (θθ)−(φφ) equation
closes on H0 = H2, the (r,θ) equation gives 3624's K′, the (θθ) with K′
eliminated gives 3624's algebraic K, and the (tt) with K eliminated is
the master ODE up to a factor with no H in it. The decaying solution
falls out of reduction of order in closed form, with a log, and there is
no integration anywhere. Then Israel: the background shell has σ =
1/(8πR), P = σ/4, rest mass 4M/3 — all recovered from the extrinsic
curvature jump, so the machine is right on the case we know.

Then the first junction, and this is where 3624 went wrong in a way I
had not seen. Continuity of g_tt is the lapse pin; it fixes the
exterior displacement ξ. Continuity of g_θθ does not give K(R) = 0. It
gives ξ_in = ξ − RK/2 — it defines where the surface sits in the flat
interior. The "displacement terms cancel" in 3624 only if you set ξ_in
equal to ξ, which is a statement about two coordinate systems, not
about physics. So K(R) = 0 was an assumption, and the panel's YES 4–1
was exactly right: the exterior's decaying amplitude λ is left free by
the first junction, and the second junction is where it lives.

The second junction, written out, is three numbers — δσ, δP, Π — all
affine in λ. Two of them barely depend on λ at all: the shear and the
isotropic pressure are set by the tide to better than a part in a
thousand, and shell conservation with a pinned lapse forces δP = 2Π/R²
identically (it needs the field equations to hold, so it is Bianchi,
not a tautology). So the surface must carry shear — a fluid shell is
out — and the whole datum collapses to one number, δσ: what the count
per proper area does under a tide. That is a cleaner thing to have to
answer than "the shell's constitutive law."

And the Love number runs over both signs with it. K(R) = 0 gives
−0.080, a rigid surface −0.049, fixed count density +0.012. The
magnitude the panel called robust is robust. The sign I called the
theory's in 3626 was the K(R) = 0 closure's. I am holding V2.2's sign
rather than correcting it, because none of these three closures is the
register's.

The register's closure is the census. The corpus never said the surface
was matter; it said M is the count at cap, the register is the 1/r
census of the count, the surface is the level set v = 2/3, and the
inside is uniformly at cap. Put those together under a tide and you
have a uniform-count region whose boundary is the level set of its own
field plus the tide — and the induced exterior field is just the census
of that. No stress law. GR's S_ab is bookkeeping, the way σ was. In the
Newtonian limit that problem is Kelvin's: k₂ = 3/2, positive, order
one. At C = 0.375 it will be a different number and I will not guess
the sign; the relativistic census with the corpus's own v = M/r_iso law
is one patch and it is the next one.

What I want from Thomas is one picture: under the tide, is the count
still uniform at cap right out to the moved level set, or does it pile
up on the bulge? The derivation assumes the former, which is what 3375
says, but the sign of the Love number now turns on it.
