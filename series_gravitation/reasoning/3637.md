# Patch 3637 — reasoning fragment (verbatim, Tier 4)

The pulsation problem for a star with a flat core and a level-set
boundary is a new one and I did not want to invent its dynamics in a
turn. So I asked what statics can decide, and it decides more than I
expected.

The combined sequence — TOV up to the threshold, then the flat core
growing — has mass and baryon mass rising together with no turning
point all the way to the branch maximum. The stars are bound, and the
radius does something GR never does on its way to the maximum mass:
it turns around and rises. That is the signature, if the branch is
real: a knee at 1.78 solar masses with bigger stars above it.

Then the variational test. At fixed baryon number I let the core
radius float with the envelope in hydrostatic balance, and the
gravitational mass is not stationary at the register radius — it rises
with the core, for every member. The star would rather shrink its core,
through states with central lapse below one half, which the register
forbids. So the flat-core star is a constrained equilibrium. The cap
holds it, about five hundredths of a solar mass above the GR
configuration of the same baryon number at J0740's mass. The
turning-point theorem cannot be applied in the unconstrained sense; on
the constraint manifold it applies, there is no cusp, and stability is
inherited from below the threshold.

So the whole question has become one word about the cap: rigid or
soft. The corpus already answered it four days ago — R-FLOOR-REGISTER
says the floor is a saturation limit, which is rigid. Under that
ruling the branch is stable, the theory passes J0740 with an
eleven-kilometre flat-core star, and it predicts something GR does
not: radii rising with mass above 1.78. I am asking Thomas to confirm
the picture in one line, because the arc now hangs on it, and I have
not done the dynamical modes.
