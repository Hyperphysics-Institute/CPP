# Patch 3639 — reasoning fragment (verbatim, Tier 4)

Thomas gave me a spring: every GP holding a CP at its PSR, the CP
moving under collisions but anchored, the ringing passing inward, a
stiffness short of full displacement. That is the elastic reading, and
it puts the arc back on static ground with one new number.

I wrote the number as the slope of the register above the cap —
register = cap + χ(demand − cap) — because that is the least the
picture requires, and because the corpus's own PSR law turns out to
have room for it: the log law is Schwarzschild's isotropic lapse and
runs smoothly past v = 2/3 all the way to a horizon at v = 2. The
floor was a clamp we added. So χ = 1 is the law with no clamp, χ = 0
is the clamp, and the founder's spring is the third-order question
3390 left open, now with a physical meaning.

The first rung was cheap and clean. The surface does not move with χ,
because the demand outside is the census and the census does not care
what the register does inside. So GR-2's surface at 8M/3 and its
ringdown boundary stand for every χ. What moves is the interior: the
lapse dips to a third at the centre at χ = 1, no horizon anywhere, and
the light-crossing of the cavity lengthens by up to fifty-six percent.
That turns the echo prediction from a number into a function — 0.70
milliseconds times T(χ)/T(0) — and an observed delay would read χ off.
And the load I found in 3638 is exactly what the spring absorbs: at
χ = 1 the interior mass function carries all of M and there is no
shell left to stress.

What I think of the picture: it is the right kind. It makes the
R-core static, keeps the Love numbers, dissolves the flat-core
exotica into an interpolation, and it does all that with a parameter
that the theory should be able to derive rather than fit — the
anchoring of a CP at its PSR under excess demand is a PCD statement,
not a free constant. If it can be derived, the echo delay is a
zero-parameter number again, just not 0.70 ms.
