# Patch 3638 — reasoning fragment (verbatim, Tier 4)

I read the ruling the wrong way round and Thomas corrected it in one
sentence: the saturated register yields under load. So the fork of
3637 closes on the side I had not built for, and I owe the corpus the
consequences before anything else.

First the easy ones. The flat-core stars carried a load — that was the
finding of 3637, the mass gradient at fixed baryon number pointing
toward compression — and a cap that yields does not hold a load. So the
branch is not static, the knee at 1.78 is not a mass limit, the rising
radii and the 2.9-solar-mass maximum go, and with them the four-sigma
tension with J0740: a core that yields to its demand is a GR core, and
a GR star at 2.08 solar masses is nobody's problem. The saturation
threshold of 3634 stays — the cores of the heaviest stars do reach the
cap — and the flat-core theorem stays as what an unloaded saturated
region would be.

Then the one that matters. The R-core's own surface is loaded. Israel
says it must carry a tangential stress of a quarter of its energy
density or fall in; that was in 3631 as bookkeeping and I did not read
it as a load until today. 3390 had already found the surface unstable
and offered compliance as the way out; the founder has now chosen
compliance. But compliance comes in two kinds, and only one of them
leaves anything static: a stiffness lets a loaded surface settle
displaced; a creep lets it keep going. The corpus's one-Moment-delay
compliance is a rate law, which is creep unless a restoring term is
there. The Love numbers I computed this week assumed a settled,
displaced level set, which is the elastic reading. So the whole arc —
the static R-core, the echo cavity, the Love numbers, and the heavy
neutron stars — now waits on one picture: does the loaded register
settle or keep moving?

I put the two loads side by side so the law, when it comes, has
something to be tested against: the R-core needs a quarter of its
surface energy in stress; a 2.08-solar-mass star's cap was holding
forty-three percent of the core pressure. Whatever stiffness holds the
first must not stiffen the second into the flat-core star that J0740's
radius does not want. That is a window, and it is computable the day
the law has a form.
