## The re-identification frame

The chapter's structural problem: five papers, all written pre-RCORE,
now telling a horizonless story. Options were (a) narrate the zoo as
shipped and correct at the end, or (b) narrate post-correction
throughout. Chose (b) with the field-guide metaphor doing the work in
one line — "it didn't discard the entries, it re-identified the
animal" — because chapter 2 already taught the correction as drama;
re-staging it would be repetition, while the field-guide frame lets
each entry carry its own before/after in a clause.

## The cliffhanger

The ergoregion question closes the chapter because it is genuinely
open and genuinely load-bearing — and the placement rule is stated as
programme character: "filed where a hostile reader would look,
because that is where this programme files its weak points." Both
forks are given fairly (kills-or-forces-deeper vs
slow/saturating/drained), with the possible drain honestly tied to
the very emission unknown the remnant entry just declared — the two
blanks are connected, and saying so is better pedagogy than
pretending the cliffhanger stands alone. The student sentence ("Can a
spinning mirror the size of a city...") is the takeaway formulation.
