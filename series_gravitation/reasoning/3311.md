## Design decisions

1. **Entry-lesson duty.** Chapter 5 could assume the census; chapter 1
   must build it. The postal-service image carries the whole load:
   broadcast, shell dilution (planted early so 1/r² reads as "a fact
   about spheres," defusing the usual mysticism), and conservation
   ("never loses a letter") — which chapter 5 then inherits for
   Birkhoff.
2. **The ethical core is the Q_grav asterisk**, per the plan's CHS
   mandate. The W-B pass had already identified the "by analogy"
   normalization as GR-1a's never-reviewed load-bearing claim AND
   recorded the independent T-1 re-derivation as stronger-than-a-round;
   the chapter turns that registry fact into pedagogy: label your
   scaffolding, because honest scaffolding is what makes later
   confirmation mean something.
3. **The factor of two gets the climax slot.** It is the arc's best
   teachable falsifier: equal potentials BY CONSTRUCTION (GR-1b's
   two-channel census, rem:equal), so a single-channel rival predicts
   0.875″ and dies in 1919. "How many columns the lattice's ledger
   has" makes the eclipse a census measurement — the kind of
   re-description the anthology exists for.
4. **Post-RCORE honesty in the trailer.** Written after the horizonless
   result, the closing names "strange solid-surfaced objects" rather
   than promising black-hole interiors the arc no longer believes in.
   Chapter 2's title ("The Black Hole That Was Already There") now has
   a second, unplanned meaning — the surface was already outside the
   horizon — noted here for the chapter-2 writer.
5. **Claim discipline.** No numbers beyond shipped ones (0.875/1.75″,
   ~45 μs/day GPS, 4GM/Rc²); the "exact Schwarzschild" claim is
   trailed, not asserted, since it is chapter 2's cargo; the
   chase-vs-commit closing states the parent paper's thesis without
   overclaiming (what the formula "must still survive" is listed,
   including the classical tests "measured to modern precision").
