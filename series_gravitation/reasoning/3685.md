# Patch 3685 — reasoning fragment (verbatim, Tier 4)

Thomas read the last patch as not a success and asked me to keep
going, which was the right read: a row that had passed "by
construction" had become a row that passed only if a number nobody
had computed came out small. The register's stiffness is not in the
corpus, and I could not invent it. What I could do was bound the
number from above with a body everyone agrees is the softest thing
that still pushes back — an incompressible fluid at the same
compactness — because a spring that resists at least as hard as
pressure deforms at most as much as that fluid does.

The relativistic Love number of such a body at the R-core's
compactness is 0.019. The row needed less than 0.39. That is a
twenty-fold margin, and it holds for every register the founder's
own ruling admits. What fails is putty: a register with less
restoring force than a fluid, which is precisely what the budget
law's trace lock turned out to be when its Love number came back at
eight. So the correction I made yesterday was real, and the answer
to it was a bound rather than a law. The panel should see both.

I checked the machinery against the one number I could not be wrong
about — three quarters in the Newtonian limit — and it came in at
0.7475 at a thousandth of a compactness, with the one-percent miss
at a hundredth being the first relativistic correction, not a bug.
