## Note design

The distinction held throughout is DELIVERED-INPUT vs CLOSED. RCORE
delivered the reflectivity these problems named as their blocker; it
did not solve the problems (Teukolsky on a Kerr surface that doesn't
yet exist; scalar/vector wall conditions; saturation on the new
geometry). Writing "closed" would be the Rule-2 upgrade. Each note
therefore names what arrived, what changed under it, and what remains
— OP-1 "still open as posed," OP-3 "unposed against the amended
geometry."

The OP-2 note carries the arc's best re-framing: the "Planck-core
bomb" becomes the ergoregion instability of a horizonless spinning
perfect reflector — a known, studied problem class — and is flagged
load-bearing, because if the instability is real and fast, rapidly
spinning CPP objects are in tension with observed BH spins. That is a
falsifier-shaped statement and belongs in the open problem where a
hostile reader will find it, not buried in a frontier file.
