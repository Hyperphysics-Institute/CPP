# Patch 3644 — reasoning fragment (verbatim, Tier 4)

Row 7 asked for the reflectivity from the returned-bit fraction. The
fraction itself is trivial to write down and it says something sharp:
zero at the surface. Under the budget law the surface returns nothing;
whatever the wave does, it does inside. So the question is not "what is
the reflectivity" but "what does a transparent surface with any interior
fate look like to the ringdown" — and the ringdown is the one empiric
PD-007 has already pinned (row 1). That reframing is the whole patch.

I expected one of the transparent readings to pass. Absorbing at some
depth inside the graded core should mimic a horizon, I thought, because
a horizon is an absorber at depth. It does not. Every transparent
reading — lossless, absorb at the surface, absorb at any depth from 3%
to 80% of the core — damps the mode about 30% too fast, at a = 0 and
at Kerr, even where the frequency is matched to 1%. The number that
explains it is the horizon-equivalent wall at 8M/3: at the QNM
frequency its admittance is 0.31 of the exterior's, which means the
black hole itself, seen from where the R-core's surface sits, reflects
half the amplitude coherently. A wall inside the potential barrier's
inner flank cannot be transparent and still ring like a black hole; the
missing potential between 8M/3 and 2M is, to the ringdown, a partial
mirror.

That is not a new idea in the corpus. It is R-CAP-SPRING: the founder
said the register is anchored with a stiffness that prevents full
displacement, and 3639 §4 read that as χ strictly less than one. The
budget law I adopted in 3641 gives χ = cap/v, which is exactly one at
the surface — I dropped the founder's stiffness in the name of the
least-assumption extension, and row 7 is the ledger doing its job. So
the calibration is named: one number, the interior's wave impedance
relative to the exterior's. I pinned it by the a = 0 ℓ = 2 horizon
point, then tested it cold on ℓ = 3 and it landed within 1.4%. That is a
real test and it passed; I did not touch s after the pin.

Kerr is where I have to be careful with myself. The pinned law is just
outside GW150914's box there, and I found that no pure-imaginary law of
any s gets inside it. Before calling that a failure I checked what the
horizon-equivalent law looks like on the SN function at real frequency:
it has a real part of +0.063, where the Zerilli one at a = 0 has
+0.008. A pure-imaginary β is a local absorber on Zerilli and is not one
on SN — the transformation mixes the function and its derivative — so
the Kerr number is a test of the dictionary, not of s. 3619 hit the
same wall from the other side ("ingoing at the wall" wrong by 21%). I
have scored the Kerr test as owed to KERRWALL-1 and said why, rather
than either claiming a pass or booking a fail on a comparison I do not
trust.

The consequence I did not want: PRED-O-39's echo. The only reading that
returns everything coherently after one crossing is the one the
ringdown excludes by 23% in frequency. Under every reading that rings
like a black hole, half the amplitude comes back promptly and the echo's
amplitude is capped at about 0.3 of the ringdown times the core's
coherent return — which the damping already says is small. The 0.95 ms
is still the cavity time, but the extension now predicts a faint echo
or none. That has to go into V2.3 as written, and row 5 has to be
re-cut.

Under 3641's rule 5 a failed row is a failed extension unless the
founder pins the calibration explicitly. This is the one governance
act PD-007 reserved to Thomas, so I am not enacting the stiffness
myself: I am putting the pin in front of him with both sides computed.
Row 6 waits on it, because the Love number as a spring deflection is
the same stiffness in the static limit.
