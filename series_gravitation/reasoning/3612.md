# Patch 3612 — reasoning fragment (verbatim, Tier 4)

The founder said: choose what the empirics support; calibrate only if
you must; empirics are the arbiter. Applied, and it splits the question
cleanly in two.

The exterior is decided by an observation nobody in this arc had
invoked: the ringdown. If CPP's near-zone dynamics were a component-
wise scalar relay of the packet, the fundamental mode of a black hole
would sit thirty percent above where general relativity puts it, and
the detected ringdowns sit where general relativity puts them to about
ten percent. So the exterior perturbation equations are Einstein's,
and the arc's use of them since July stands on empirical ground rather
than on a borrowed assumption. That is worth more than it cost.

The wall is not decided by any observation, because the observation
that would decide it — the echo — has not been made. The searches give
upper limits. So the residual gauge at the wall, which 3611 showed
moves the register content by a factor of three, becomes the arc's one
calibration parameter, bounded by the null results and fixed by the
first detection. The founder said to avoid that if I can, and the way
to avoid it is a derivation — the packet's own relay at the wall, whose
gauge-invariant content the ringdown now says must be Einstein's and
whose gauge content is what CPP would supply. I have named that item
and put it in parallel with the family computation, so that if the
derivation lands inside the family the calibration retires.

I have not made the residual disappear by argument, having done that
twice already this month. The line set on file is one member of a
family, and the paper will say so at its next version. The first CPP
parameter to be fixed by a gravitational-wave observation, if it comes
to that, is a boundary condition at a black hole's surface — which is
at least the right kind of thing to learn from a black hole.
