# Patch 3676 — reasoning fragment (verbatim, Tier 4)

The falsifier needed a number I did not have, so I went and read for
it rather than reach for what I half-remembered. Three things came
back. The field's own formula for the Planckian delay, with the
Planck-floor point I had used, gives 749 M against my 746 — the same
number by a different road, which is the kind of agreement I trust.
Uchikata's group searched thirty-four O3 events with two echo models
across windows that hold my delay for remnants of this mass, and
found p-values that look like noise; and their own injections show
that a train at thirty percent of the merger's signal would have
lit the distribution up at a millionth. And the single-event bound on
GW150914 is fifteen percent of the merger.

The alternative to SEA — that the untaken bits come back carrying the
wave — would put the first return at about twenty-nine percent of the
ringdown, using the barrier transmission the lane computed itself two
sessions ago. That is at the level the searches see. So the
alternative is disfavored at the population level, and I have been
careful not to write "excluded": to exclude it per event I need the
ringdown-to-merger ratio for the loud ones, and I have not computed
that. The null itself, which is what SEA predicts, is consistent with
everything published, including the O4 search that used GW250114.

The Kerr seat I thought I owed turned out to be already in the
record: 3668's third test is the horizon-equivalent wall at spin
0.68 giving Leaver's Kerr mode, and under SEA that wall is the whole
story. I pointed to it rather than re-running six minutes of code to
learn what I already knew.

Three seats are in. The review economy says a panel goes out on a win,
and a falsifier scored against real data is a win. I drafted the one
question the panel should answer — whether the partition is open or
forced by the axioms — and left the dispatch for the next act so
Thomas can see the shape of it before it goes.
