The founder left the decision to me with a criterion: run the round
if it helps the next derivations. It does, and here is why I said yes.
The next three things — the fixed-compactness claim against the
neutron stars, the spinning Love numbers, the core's conversion
mechanism — all sit on the static picture I built this week: a rigid
interior at cap, a surface that is a level set, one junction condition
kept and one dropped. If that picture has a free parameter hiding in
the dropped junction condition, or if the rigid interior contradicts
the founder's own picture of what a tide does to the Sea, I would
rather learn it before I compute a mass-radius relation on top of it.

I have written the package to break the static picture, with the
weakest point first: I kept the induced-metric condition and let the
shell's stress absorb the extrinsic-curvature jump, on the assertion
that the register cap sets the shell's stress. A seat who knows the
junction formalism can say whether that is legitimate or a parameter
in disguise. And I have put the fixed-compactness claim in as a
question rather than a result — it is the arc's strongest exposure, a
statement about every compact object that saturates, and I do not
know what in the corpus decides whether a body saturates at all.

The key has no rounding ties this time. Each value is checked to sit
away from one.
