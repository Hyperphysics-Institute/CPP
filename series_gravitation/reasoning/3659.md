The handover asked for a Leaver solver for the overtone. I looked at what actually fails in the
existing instrument: not the equation, the direction. On the real axis the outgoing solution
shrinks going inward while its partner grows, by e^{24} at the overtone. That is a statement
about the phase of ω r*, and a phase can be rotated: along a ray in complex r* tilted past
arctan(|ω_I|/ω_R), the roles reverse and inward integration becomes the stable direction. The
horizon side reflects the same way. It costs one extra state (complex r) and nothing else, and it
is exactly the same equation the lane has validated for two months, so it carries less recall risk
than a Leaver series I would write from memory. I validated before using it: the fundamental
against the real-axis machinery, the overtone against Leaver's five-figure number, and the answer
against the ray angle and the far end. All held.

Then the data, before scoring: GW250114's overtone test is δf = 0.1 ± 0.3 in the log with the
damping unconstrained. That is a ±30% frequency box and no damping box. The hypothesis's overtone
is inside it — and 12% and 25% from GR. I wrote both. Calling this member "descriptive" without
qualification would be true by the letter of rule 6 and misleading in substance; calling it a
failure would be scoring against GR rather than against the phenomenon, which is not the rule
either. So the record says: inside the data box, far from GR, the group's weakest, and it fails
the first overtone measurement that constrains damping to 25%.
