# Patch 3608 — reasoning fragment (verbatim, Tier 4)

The founder asked "will this work?" about a story in which direction
comes back into the theory through the DI-bits' addresses and shows up
in the direction and magnitude of SSV_net. I tested the story rather
than the intention.

It does not work as told, and the reason is a blind spot rather than
an error. A vector sum of arrival directions cannot see a census that
comes equally from +x and -x: the net vector is zero, the same as for
an isotropic census. But that pattern — more along one axis than
across it, with no net direction — is exactly what a gravitational
wave is at a grid point. So "SSV_net embeds theta and phi" is true and
insufficient: it embeds the first moment, and the wave lives in the
second.

The fix is one more sum over the same addresses. The register already
keeps the zeroth moment (the count) and the first (the vector sum).
Keep the second — the alignment tensor of the arrival directions — and
it sees the squeeze: for the +x/-x census it is diag(2/3, -1/3, -1/3),
traceless, symmetric, rank two. The perception radius then depends on
direction, shrinking most along the axis the excess census came from,
and that is the founder's original anisotropic sphere, now derived from
the DI-bits instead of assumed beside them. Nothing new arrives at the
GP; one more moment is kept.

Then I checked the objection I expected the founder to have: won't a
directional radius break GR-1? It cannot. For a static mass,
Schwarzschild in areal coordinates needs the split (u/3, u) between
isotropic and anisotropic; in isotropic coordinates it needs (u, 0).
Same geometry. Statics fixes only the combination, and the rank-2
coefficient is the lattice coordinatization, which the wave fixes. The
isotropic simplification chose zero, which statics allows and
radiation does not.

So the answer is: not as stated, yes with one addition, and what
decides it is the third item in the charter — whether the relay of
the second moment delivers GR's amplitude and luminosity with a
coefficient that also passes the scalar bound and the statics
re-check. That is a derivation with numbers, not a picture. I have
asked for the ruling that lets it start: may the register keep the
second moment, and may the payload carry it.
