# Patch 3640 — reasoning fragment (verbatim, Tier 4)

Thomas asked the question the arc had been walking around: does any
of this follow from the PCD story, or did we add a rule inside the
black hole? I think it follows, from one thing we already say and
have never used — a CP does one cycle per Moment, and a census in one
Moment is a finite count.

If the count can be at most K, then over-supply has to be handled, and
the rule that assumes the least is that the CP takes K bits without
choosing. That caps the absolute count, which is the clock, and scales
the net vector by K over D, which is the spring. The bits not taken
are not destroyed — they sit in the sea and go back out, which is the
mirror and the momentum Thomas wanted conserved. Three effects, one
sentence, no constant.

I wrote it on the register and it is a clean formula, twice the cap
minus cap squared over the demand, and its first surprise is that it
joins the exterior with a continuous derivative. That means no shell.
The surface stress I had spent 3638 worrying about was the clamp's
doing; the budget law has none. The second surprise is that the
interior is not flat — the lapse goes from a half at the surface to
five thirteenths at the centre — so the founder's picture of a
constant PSR inside is not quite what the law says; the register
keeps recording, slower. And the echo cavity comes out 1.36 times the
flat one with nothing to fit: 0.95 milliseconds at 62 solar masses.

What I cannot settle from here is whether the clock and the force
saturate together. The rule caps the scalar and scales the vector by
the same factor; if that is what the mechanics do, then inside a
heavy neutron star gravity and pressure scale alike and the star is
Einstein's with a slower clock, and the flat-core exotica never arise.
But inside the R-core, where nothing pushes back, a scaled pull is
still a pull, and the interior would need to be held by something the
rule does not yet say — the c/2 the founder keeps mentioning, or the
lattice at the centre. So I asked one question, the one the whole
thing turns on: is the displacement computed from the bits that
arrive, or from the bits that get processed?
