# Reasoning — 3668

The handover's single next act was KERRWALL-1b, literature-bound: build the even-type Kerr master
variable from a source, not memory, and validate at a = 0 before any Kerr number. The gate was the
first thing: full-history clone (my first clone was --depth 50, which the bootup forbids for exactly
the reason it gives, so I unshallowed before reading anything else), GR.md header 3667/3668,
next_id.py agreeing, grep for KERRWALL-1b and 3668 clean, no parallel windows.

For the source I did not want a paper that merely mentions the Chandrasekhar–Detweiler transformation;
I wanted the potential written out with its sign conventions stated and its relation to the angular
eigenvalue and the tortoise coordinate made explicit, so that every symbol could be mapped onto 3359's
conventions without interpretation. Hatsuda & Kimura (arXiv:2006.15496) is that: eqs. (12)–(17) give
V_CD in closed form, footnote 8 pins their λ to Berti–Cardoso–Casals's A with s = −2 (which is 3359's
lam exactly), and their App. A gives the QNM boundary behaviour in the same e^{−iωt} convention. The
attempt to fetch a second transcription for cross-checking was rate-limited; I decided the numerical
validation is the stronger check anyway — a wrong sign on am/σ or a wrong κ₂ is not a small error, it
is a different potential and it cannot be isospectral with Teukolsky. So the plan was: pointwise
reduction at a = 0 (both parities, two ℓ), isospectrality of the two parities at a = 0 against
Leaver's Schwarzschild QNM, isospectrality at Kerr against 3358's Leaver radial continued fraction —
and only then any wall number.

The first run had three failures, and rule 4 says locate before touching the criterion. Two were the
same cause: the horizon start at r₊ + 1e−3, which 3657 also used. At complex ω the ingoing solution
decays outward and the outgoing one grows, so integrating outward from a coarse start amplifies the
contamination; at 1e−6 both parities hit Leaver to 2e−5 at a = 0 and the two b₂ = −3α² potentials hit
Leaver at χ = 0.68 to 3e−5 and 6e−5. That convergence is what tells me the transcription is right. The
b₂ = +3α² combinations do not converge (3.6e−3 and 4e−2 regardless of start), so they are not
isospectral, and Hatsuda–Kimura's footnote saying "the plus signs give Zerilli" cannot mean both plus.
I let the numerics fix the sign rather than re-reading the footnote until it agreed with me; the record
says which combination is isospectral and which is not.

The third failure was the one worth the session. T4 asked the Z⁺ horizon law at 8M/3 at real ω to
match 3644's Zerilli point +0.008 − 0.116i and got +0.116 − 0.185i. Since T1 had shown Z⁺ at a = 0
IS Zerilli pointwise, the discrepancy could not be in the potential; it had to be in the reference. I
read 3644's script: `bh = beta_horizon(wGR)` with wGR complex. So the "near-pure-imaginary Zerilli
horizon law" is a complex-ω number, and the pin s = 3.218 was defined there. 3657's test (1b) then
compared Regge–Wheeler at REAL ω against that complex-ω Zerilli value and concluded the parities
differ at a = 0. At matched ω they do not: at the pole both are near-imaginary (Z⁺ Re +0.009, Z⁻ Re
−0.002), at real ω both are real-heavy (Re +0.116 and +0.105). I had written the "even variable is
special" premise into 3657 myself, and it was an ω mismatch. This does not change any number in 3657's
Kerr test, and it does not change the count, but it removes the reason KERRWALL-1b was ever expected
to differ — which is consistent with the a = 0 group being parity-robust, the one thing 3657 §3 got
right about the parities.

The test itself was then almost anticlimactic. On Z⁺, with s unchanged, the surface-frame line is
−27% / −32% out of both boxes; Ω_H −23% / −20%; Ω = 0 −29% / −37%; the s-scan never enters. The even
variable moves the miss around — frequency worse than SN's, damping better — and does not close it. The
horizon seen from the wall in Z⁺ is again a complex reflector, 0.77 at −13° in the surface frame
against SN's 0.71 at −17°, so the requirement 3657 recorded is robust in kind across the two
realizations and basis-dependent in number, exactly as CONV-042's GPT item 5 said it would be. I have
not written "fails on every Kerr realization"; two were tested. I have written "fails at Kerr on both
realizations tested", which is what CONV-042 said would become admissible once KERRWALL-1b ran.

Two things I chose not to do. I did not refit or re-pin s at 3.235 even though the converged start
moves the pin's defining number by 0.5%; the pin is frozen and rule 1 does not have a "small
correction" clause. And I did not dispatch a panel: closing an owed door with a failing member is
neither a win nor a stuck point under the review-economy triggers; it goes in the ledger and the paper
restatement. The CD instrument itself is the reusable residue — closed-form potential, no dictionary
term, tighter than SN against Leaver — and it is the natural carrier for the complex ray on the (2,+1)
comparator that sits two items down the queue.
