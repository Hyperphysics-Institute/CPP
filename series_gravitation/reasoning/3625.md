The sign question first. Could the interior's shape register respond
to a static tide and flip the Love number? Its static solutions
regular at the centre are two: a uniform strain, which has no
curvature and is pure gauge — the ellipsoidal deformation the moving
surface already is — and a quadratic one, which is harmonic but
curved, and curvature in a static interior must be held up by stress.
A core at the register cap has none to give: the count is capped, the
medium uniform, the tide moves the surface and leaves the inside at
cap. So in statics the June axiom's interior is the rigid one, and the
Love number I computed yesterday, minus 0.08, is the theory's, sign
and all. The bulge is where the register is fed, and the field of that
bulge opposes the tide at the order that defines the number. Thin-shell
objects in the literature do the same.

Then the magnetic one. I derived the static axial equation from the
Ricci tensor and got the standard form, imposed the odd sector's
continuity into the core — the vector register is uncapped and the
lapse is continuous at the surface by the surface's own definition —
and compared the asymptotic tail against the horizon-regular
solution's. The difference is the response: about plus 0.03, with a
normalization I have flagged because the axial convention varies by
author and I will not guess a factor. A black hole has zero.

So the R-core now carries two static numbers a next-generation detector
could read as "surface": an electric deformability of about minus
seven and a magnetic one of the same order and opposite sign. Neither
has a parameter in it. That completes the first row of the list I
wrote when the founder asked "what now." The spin dependence and the
exact axial convention are owed; the physics is in.
