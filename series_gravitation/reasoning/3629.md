# Patch 3629 — reasoning fragment (verbatim, Tier 4)

Five returns, and the round did what it was built to do.

The datum I had hidden is named: the shell's stress response, the
second junction condition, which I had waved through with "adjusts to
maintain the cap." Four seats said that is an assertion, and they are
right; the Love numbers are the K(R) = 0 member of a family until the
register supplies the shell's stress. The paper says so now.

Two objections I answered by computing rather than arguing. GPT said
vacuum tidal curvature needs no matter, so my "needs stress" was false.
The vacuum tide inside a flat region is a lapse and a conformal factor,
both pinned at the cap; the traceless tensor I had in mind has nonzero
Ricci — it is not vacuum, and it does need stress. The rigid interior
stands, in that corrected form. And GPT said my magnetic number came
from subtracting numbers of order a billion, which it did, and which I
had not noticed; it did not even converge in the extraction radius. The
static axial equation turns out to have an exact horizon-regular
solution, r squared times r minus 2M, with no decaying tail at all —
the black hole's magnetic Love number is zero exactly — and with that
and reduction of order the number comes out clean: plus 0.028, near
the old estimate by luck rather than method.

And the exposure I feared most closed by a calculation every seat
asked for. Which bodies saturate? At uniform density the register
reaches the cap at the centre only past M over R of 0.44 — the
Buchdahl bound. Neutron stars sit at 0.15 to 0.30. They do not
saturate; the fixed compactness is a claim about collapsed objects; and
the theory acquires a sentence it did not have this morning: in CPP,
collapse past Buchdahl is register saturation.

Grok found y is exactly minus thirty-one thirds, and it is; the Love
number is analytic in the surface radius. DeepSeek read the key off a
two-decimal print, and the key caught it, as it did in July. V2.2 is in
the paper, fourteen pages, clean.
