# Patch 3614 — reasoning fragment (verbatim, Tier 4)

Two steps, and the first one changed what I thought the null results
could say.

For a lossless wall the impedance does not enter the size of the first
echo at all. The wave goes in through the barrier, reflects entirely,
and comes back out through the barrier: the first echo is the barrier's
transmission squared, and at the ringdown frequency that is forty-four
percent. So the LIGO searches, against a lossless wall, are not
measuring the wall's stiffness; they are asking whether a wall is there.
If their amplitude limits at the loudest events are below about forty
percent — and my recollection is that they are of that order, which is
not good enough to draw the region, so I have asked for the numbers —
then every lossless wall the arc has considered is disfavoured at
those events, including the shipped hard wall and the free-surface
law. What survives is a lossy wall, and the theory already has one:
the two-channel wall of A3-prime, in which the traceless content goes
into the core and comes back late. I did not put that there to fit the
data. It was the consequence of the June axiom, and the data, read this
way, point at it.

The second step was mechanical and it worked: the Kerr line at the
flagship spin over the same impedances, using the ladder that already
took any Robin law. A family from 122 to 188 hertz, with the shipped
191 at the top edge as the hard wall at the old surface. The 193 I
reported as "indicative" was a frequency-dependent law and is not on
this map; the map does not need it.

What the paper's next version should be is now clear: two maps, the
barrier's transmission, the calibration named as an impedance, and
one sentence the arc could not have written a month ago — that a
lossless surface predicts an echo the searches should have seen, and
the theory's own surface is not lossless.
