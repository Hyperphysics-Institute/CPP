# Patch 3607 — reasoning fragment (verbatim, Tier 4)

Two things in the founder's message, and the first reorders the week.

The perception sphere was not originally a sphere. His conception,
presented to Grok a year ago, had azimuth and elevation dependence in
the perception radius, and the rewrite — Sonnet's, then mine — replaced
it with one number per grid point and built the gravitational papers
on that. He is right that the simplification was invisible in every
test the papers passed, and I can now say why: for a single static
mass the census arrives radially, a directional radius would differ
radially from tangentially, and that is the difference between
isotropic and Schwarzschild coordinates — the same geometry twice.
Statics cannot tell them apart. They part company only when the
anisotropy has dynamics of its own, and that is the gravitational
wave, which is the one thing the papers never computed from the
theory. So what I called yesterday a "CPP-native candidate" is his
original idea, older than the corpus, and restoring it is not the rule
he forbade. It is undoing a simplification I made. I searched the
retained corpus for the original directional formulation and it is not
there; the Grok-era notes carry the isotropic law plus a vector cross
term. He will have to restate it.

The second thing was a hope, and it deserved a computation rather
than a repetition of yesterday's argument. Could the spherical PSR,
with the full retarded superposition of two moving sources — changing
distances, changing delays, everything he named — produce the tensor
effect at second order? I built the retarded field of two orbiting
point sources and took its Hessian at ten, thirty and a hundred
wavelengths. The stretch-and-squeeze part falls as one over r squared
relative to the breathing part, at every distance, tracking (lambda
over two pi r) squared; at a detector it is ten to the minus
thirty-four of the signal. The frequency comes out at twice the
orbital rate, as he said. Nonlinearity is ten to the minus forty-two.
Complexity does not change rank. And I found a second discriminator on
the way: the scalar radiates nothing along the orbital axis, where GR
radiates most — face-on binaries are LIGO's loudest.

So the answer to his hope is no, twice over, and the answer to his
worry is better than he feared: the theory does not need a new idea,
it needs his old one back. I have asked him for it in the form the
relay derivation can start from: for a census that arrives unevenly,
what did the original theory say the radius does in each direction.
