# Patch 3388 — reasoning fragment (verbatim, Tier 4)

The founder said: we got to the end of the theory without adding a
rule, and now you want one for Mercury. He is right that "heavier
registers send heavier messages" is a rule — it would change what a
DI-bit carries, and AP-4 says the payload is a fixed snapshot and the
register is a count. Withdrawn. I should have checked the axiom before
naming the mechanism, not after.

Then I asked what the bare theory does at second order, given only
what it has: a DI-bit hops one PSR per Moment, and the PSR is shorter
where the register is larger. Those two facts already make the census
enhance itself. A conserved flux crossing a region of short hops is
deposited more densely there, and the register is a deposit count. So
the field shortens its own messengers' strides and thereby counts them
more often. Gravity gravitates, in the theory as written, through the
medium and not through the message. No rule.

The coefficient of that enhancement is an exponent, and the exponent
decides everything. Zero — the corpus's linear register — gives five
sixths of Mercury. One — the naive deposit count — gives seven sixths.
One half gives Mercury exactly, and one half is also exactly the value
at which the founder's clock mechanism reproduces the log-lapse the
charter had imposed. So the whole question "does the bare theory
compute Mercury" has collapsed to one number that the relay recursion
must return. I have not computed it. It is the T-1 kernel with a
variable hop, and it is the next real deliverable. If it comes back
one half, the log-lapse, Mercury, the clock mechanism and the surface
location all follow from AP-4 and the PSR law. If it comes back
anything else, the bare theory fails Mercury at second order and we
will know it honestly.

On the near and far side of Mercury: the relativistic precession is a
property of the orbit of a point, not of the planet's extent; tides on
Mercury are ten billion times too small, and the Sun's oblateness is
already in the number. But the founder's nose for a missing
differential effect was pointing at the right thing in the wrong
place. The differential effect is in the messengers crossing the
orbit, not in the planet.
