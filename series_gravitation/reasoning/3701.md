# Patch 3701 — reasoning fragment (verbatim, Tier 4)

The reviewers wanted lockstep stated because I had used it without
proving it, and I expected the proof to be hard. It was not, once I
asked the right question: what in a census can tell a positive charge
from a negative one? Only the first moment of the arriving field
vectors. A gravitational wave has none — it is the second moment, by
the third axiom's own decomposition — and on a symmetric shell it
pushes nothing at all; it only writes itself into the register. So
both halves of a dipole go where the count sends them, together. That
is lockstep, for every wave the black hole absorbs and for the static
state, and it is a theorem now.

What the same argument gave up is more interesting than what it
proved. Light has a first moment, and the copy separates the pair on
purpose — that is how the founder said EM energy is held. So a black
hole's surface cannot glow thermally, but it might shine back
coherently, like a mirror rather than a stove. Nobody has asked what
this surface's albedo is. I have written it down as the next thing to
compute, and I think it is the first genuinely new question this
lane has produced in a week.
