# Patch 3397 — reasoning fragment (verbatim, Tier 4)

The founder answered the junction question in one sentence — every
DI-bit that reaches the surface counts, from either side — and then
asked a question I should have asked myself a week ago: why would a
wave bounce off the centre?

It doesn't. I had been writing "returns from the centre" and "reflects
at the centre" since 3374, and the model I actually used (3384) never
had anything at the centre to reflect from: it had the regular
solution, x j_l(x), which is the statement that an l = 2 pattern
cannot be crammed into a point. I computed where the wave actually
turns: at the centrifugal radius, 0.98 M, two-thirds of the way in,
with an amplitude at the origin fifteen orders of magnitude below its
peak. The inward DI-bit stream curves around and comes out. Nothing
bounces; AP-4 says nothing is lost. Sound in a sphere. The phrase was
loose and the physics was fine, and the founder's question is what
made me check that those were two different things.

His ruling does real work. At 3396 the two-channel picture had an
energy leak: the level set alone gave unit reflection while the vector
channel carried energy in. Now the returning vector wave loads the
surface register — a vector arrival adds its magnitude to SSV_abs,
which is AP-4's count rule — so the level set moves with the interior
wave too, and the two channels are coupled through one register. Two
unknowns, two conditions, no leak. That is a junction.

What I still cannot do is compute it, for one reason I have already
confessed: I do not have a shift reconstruction I trust. That is the
next thing to source. The numbers I can give say the core at this
frequency is a single turn-around, not a cavity — shorter than half an
interior wavelength — so the open channel should add a delayed
component to the even lines rather than a second line. I expect the
sharp model poles to broaden. I have written that as an expectation.
