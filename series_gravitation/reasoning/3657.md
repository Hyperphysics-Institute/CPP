# Reasoning — 3657

The handover named one act: the SN ↔ local-wave dictionary at the Kerr wall. I looked for what the
SN family itself offers before reaching for the literature. The SN equation has a first-derivative
term F X′; that is the whole reason a pure-imaginary log-derivative on X is not a local absorber.
The standard move for a first-derivative term is the exponential substitution, and here it is not
just standard but exact and closed: F dr* is d ln η, so Y = X/√η is Schrödinger-form and the
law transports by F/2. At a = 0 it is the identity, which is the right sanity check — the dictionary
must vanish where 3644's Zerilli-vs-SN puzzle vanishes. I checked the identity pointwise with
3359's own η rather than trusting my algebra.

I expected F/2 to be real-dominated and to carry 3644's "+0.063". It does not: it is
+0.011 − 0.084 i. I wrote that check as an expectation and it failed; I rewrote it as a finding
rather than loosening it, because the failed expectation is the result. The real part of the Kerr
horizon law is in the local wave, and the gauge factor mostly adds absorption. So the Kerr member,
which was already outside the box in 3644's form, moves further out under the dictionary.

Before scoring Kerr I went back to a = 0 on the odd variable, because the hypothesis was pinned on
Zerilli and the SN family's a = 0 member is Regge–Wheeler. The horizon law on RW at real ω has a
real part twelve times Zerilli's — the "near-pure-imaginary horizon" is a Zerilli property. Yet
the hypothesis with the Zerilli-pinned s lands ℓ = 2 and ℓ = 3 on RW inside the box. That is
parity-robustness of the a = 0 group, which I recorded as such and not as new members; the mode
is the same mode.

Then the honest question about the Kerr failure: is it the number or the form? An s-scan from 1.5
to 40 in both frames never enters the box, so it is the form. I computed what the Kerr ringdown
actually asks of the wall in the local-wave variable — the horizon's reflection seen from 8M/3 —
and it is 0.71 at −17° in the surface's frame against 0.53 at −3° at a = 0. One real number
described four members because at a = 0 the horizon is a real step to three degrees; at Kerr it
is a complex one. I recorded that as the requirement, the way row 7 recorded its requirement, and
did not try a complex-impedance refit: rule 6 says a failed member is a failed member.

The one door I left open and did not walk through is the even-type Kerr variable
(Chandrasekhar–Detweiler). Building it from memory is the 3359 §1 recall risk in its worst form —
a complex ω-dependent potential that gives plausible wrong poles — and it needs the a = 0 parity
map as a validation anchor first. I registered it as KERRWALL-1b, literature-bound, and said
plainly that the parity-robustness result gives little reason to expect it to change the verdict.
