# Patch 3602 — reasoning fragment (verbatim, Tier 4)

The founder answered the detector question with a picture: the wave
is a longitudinal density wave in the count, homogeneous sideways; the
transverse arm sits uniformly in the crest and shortens; the
longitudinal arm averages over the crest and shortens less; the
difference is the signal. I computed what that picture predicts before
saying anything, because the founder deserves the arithmetic and not
my reaction.

The arithmetic is unkind to it. A four-kilometre arm in a
three-thousand-kilometre wave averages almost nothing away: the
differential is the scalar strain times (kL)^2/6, about a hundred-
thousandth. A source overhead — both arms transverse — gives exactly
zero, where general relativity gives its maximum. And to produce the
10^-21 the detectors measure, the count would have to swing by 10^-16,
a common-mode strain any non-cancelling instrument would see. The
polarization content is scalar; the multi-detector tests favour tensor.
I have stated the direction of those tests from memory and not their
numbers.

I do not think this is fatal, and the reason is in the founder's own
words from two days ago: the sideways part of a wave shows up as the
DP Sea's E and B transverse polarizations. A transverse polarization
of the sea is exactly what can carry a plus-and-cross strain with no
change in the count — and general relativity says a vacuum wave has
no trace, no density modulation at all. The two halves fit only one
way: far from mass the gravitational wave is the sea's transverse
polarization, and the count is untouched. The density-wave picture
describes a breathing mode that GR forbids in vacuum and the detectors
do not see. That reconciliation is consistent with the whole arc —
near the surface the register sees the mode's trace part, a near-zone
gauge quantity, and the level set constrains it; far away the wave is
transverse-traceless and the register is blind.

What it demands is the rank-2 sector the corpus has deferred since
July: the sea's transverse polarizations obeying a wave equation that
reduces to GR's in vacuum, producing the tensor patterns at a detector,
carrying no count. Until that is written, the exterior equations the
arc uses are borrowed from GR on the strength of FE-1, and nothing
shipped says otherwise.

The question back is the one that decides it: does a vacuum wave in
CPP modulate the count at all. If no, the founder's 3600 picture is the
theory's gravitational wave and his 3602 picture is withdrawn for it.
If yes, CPP predicts a mode the detectors should have seen, and the
record carries that as an exposure. I would rather write that sentence
than not.
