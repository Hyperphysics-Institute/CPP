# Patch 3383 — reasoning fragment (verbatim, Tier 4)

The two seats that ran the numbers said: do the poles. I did the
poles. They were both right and I was wrong in an instructive way.

The -13.4%, identical for both l to three figures, was an artifact —
of the Dirichlet side, not the derived side. The 3379 centroids for the
derived wall were the pole real parts to three figures. What the half-
maximum centroid mislocated was the Dirichlet reference, a broad low-Q
hump where a centroid has nothing sharp to hold onto. The true
displacements are -7.5% and -10.7%. Not identical. The "structural
identity" I had recorded as unexplained is gone, and good riddance.

But the other thing the poles say is stronger than what I had claimed.
Q goes from 1.7 to 25 for l = 2 and from 3.2 to 92 for l = 3. The
derived wall turns a hump into a line. "Near-trapped" was the word I
was told not to use until the poles were in; the poles are in, and it
is the right word.

The crossing-at-barrier-top regularity survived and sharpened. At
Buchdahl the Neumann crossing of beta_l sits on the barrier top to 7%
for l = 2, 3, 4, tightening with l. Move the wall to 2.5M or 3M and it
drifts to 20-40% off. So it is not a property of the trace law in
general; it is a property of the trace law AT the Buchdahl wall — the
radius where the lapse is 1/3, the interior pressure diverges, and the
register saturation is extremal. I do not know why. I have written it
as a fact and not as insight.

One thing I found by moving the wall that I had not looked for: the
Robin coefficient diverges at 2.38M. There, and only there, the trace
condition is exact Dirichlet on the Zerilli function. The X = 0 the arc
carried for two months would have been the derived even-sector wall
for a surface six percent outside the one the arc derived. Close, and
not the same.

The Dirichlet l = 2 pole came out 0.44506 - 0.13442i — the number 3356
found in July with the same machinery, to five figures. That is the
reason to reuse machinery instead of rewriting it.
