# Patch 3385 — reasoning fragment (verbatim, Tier 4)

The founder said the question is open, offered a precedent, and asked
whether the echo could be a leg of the triangle. Before answering I
asked what else in the corpus already constrains the census-speed map,
and the answer was sitting in GR-1i: the Shapiro delay. It is measured
to a few parts in a hundred thousand, and its weak-field coefficient
is 2. The pure GP-hop-per-universal-Moment map gives 1. So that map,
the one I called "the CPP map" at 3384 and priced as J = 2, was never
admissible. The hop must be GP-counted AND its rate must be slowed by
the lapse. GR-1i had said this in words — "clocks tick slower, light
has a lower coordinate speed" — and then used GR's number. Now it is a
derivation with a mechanism: a loaded GP takes more Moments to cycle
before it re-emits.

With the ratified log-lapse that gives J = 6 at the wall. GR gives 6.75.
Twelve and a half percent is the strong-field departure of CPP from
general relativity, and I have a number for it for the first time.

The precedent the founder offered — saltatory sub-Moment hops along
edges — is the other half: it is why the PSR shrink is a spatial effect
(the conformal factor) and not a clock effect. The two halves together
are the map. What remains open is not GP-counted-or-metric; that is
answered. What remains open is WHICH lapse the substrate implements at
strong field, and the echo's second family measures it: the spacing is
pi over J, and the three admissible dictionaries give 0.52, 0.47 and
0.79. That is the leg he asked for, and it is a measurement of how
many Moments a full register takes to process.

I reran the axial line across the admissible J. It moves down one to
five percent, monotonically; the second family is in band for all of
them. So the second family is a prediction and its spacing is the
observable. I have written the mechanism as a question to the founder
rather than minting it, because "a full register takes three Moments
to cycle" is a picture and he may see it differently.
