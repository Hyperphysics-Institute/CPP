The model is 3375 read inside a star. Where the register is pinned the
lattice is flat at lapse ½; no gravity there, so the core sits at
uniform pressure and density; its count shows up to the envelope as
m(r_c), exactly the shell bookkeeping of the R-core; outside is TOV
matter; and the core boundary is where the envelope's lapse is ½. My
first search failed and taught me the physics: N(r_c) only goes DOWN as
the core grows, because a flat core at the boundary density carries
more count than TOV's compressed interior and deepens the lapse above
it. So a star whose TOV centre has passed lapse ½ cannot grow a
consistent flat core at all. The ordinary sequence just ends at the
threshold. For Γ = 3 that is 15% below GR's maximum mass — the kind of
number the pulsar timing people can argue with.

The second thing I did not expect: at LOWER core pressures the
register condition has a second solution, a star that is mostly flat
core with a thin TOV skin. The branch is narrow, the masses are large
— up to 2.4× GR's maximum for the soft EOS — and the compactness runs
up toward 0.375, which is the R-core. This is the road between a
neutron star and the R-core, drawn by the postulate. Whether anything
sits on it depends on stability, which a turning-point argument cannot
settle for a disconnected branch, and I have not done the radial
modes.
