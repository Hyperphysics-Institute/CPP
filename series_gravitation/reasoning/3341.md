CONV-035 dispatch. The design problem was that this round is partly
about me, and a package written by the party under examination can
soften the examination without meaning to. Three deliberate moves
against that:

1. §0 states the failure FIRST, in plain language, before any
   finding. The panel reads "you ratified a claim that is now known
   to be over-broad, and the worker found it, not the panel" before
   it reads anything I would like it to be impressed by. The two
   strengthening results are introduced with an explicit instruction
   to weigh them on their own merits and NOT as compensation —
   because that is exactly the trade a reader might otherwise make
   unconsciously, and I would benefit from it.

2. Q6 asks the panel to rule on my process call, and the binding
   rule says an IMPROPER majority establishes a standing rule that
   binds me in future. That is real jeopardy, not a courtesy
   question. I think enacting-on-discovery was right — leaving a
   known-over-broad claim live while a round runs is the worse error
   — but I have an obvious interest in that conclusion, so the panel
   gets to set the rule. The GPT seat is explicitly steered to rule
   hard on it, since it is the seat most likely to say I should have
   waited.

3. Q8 is a SCOPE AUDIT with adoption regardless of disposition: the
   panel is asked to hunt OTHER claims of mine carrying a quantifier
   their computation does not support. If the ell<=3 failure is a
   class rather than an incident, I want the class enumerated by
   someone other than me, and I want the items to become work even
   if the round otherwise clears.
