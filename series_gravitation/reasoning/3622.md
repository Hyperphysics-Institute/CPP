V2.0 is in the paper: thirteen pages, compiles clean, the map, the
ringdown band, the bracket, the inference that the core absorbs, the
falsifiers, and the sentence that the hard wall this paper shipped in
August is excluded by a measurement made in 2015. The register is
rewritten. The ledger moves. Nothing in the black-hole section now
rests on an assumption the founder did not rule or the data did not fix.

The founder's question is the right next one: the black hole absorbs
and is not a point, as CPP says; where might the data show that? I
have ranked the places. The ringdown is where the distinction is
bounded, not found. The shadow is identical to GR's, because the
photon sphere sits outside the surface. The tidal Love number is the
cleanest: a general-relativistic black hole has exactly zero, a
surface at 1.33 Schwarzschild radii has a finite static response to a
companion's tide, and that number enters the inspiral in a way the
next generation of detectors can read. It is static, gauge-invariant,
and computable with machinery the arc already has — the first thing
to do. Then the mass-radius relation, which is the strangest zero-
parameter prediction the theory has: every R-core, whatever its mass,
has compactness 0.375. And the surface-luminosity limits from the
Event Horizon Telescope, which are a test of CPP or a pass depending
on one picture I have asked the founder for: what the substrate does
with the energy the core absorbs.

The arc began in July aiming at an echo. It ends V2.0 with a black
hole that rings like Einstein's and differs from Einstein's in what it
is made of, and the observables that follow from what it is made of —
its tidal response, its fixed compactness, the fate of what it
swallows. That is a better place to be than an echo.
