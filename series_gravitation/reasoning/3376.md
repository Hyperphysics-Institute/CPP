# Patch 3376 — reasoning fragment (verbatim, Tier 4)

Rung 3 did not close, and I am shipping the failure with the checks
asserting it, because the failure is informative and a third guessed
scheme would not have been.

The first model treated the excess as a force pressing an inertial
string against a ceiling. It created energy: reflected flux 1.18 times
incident at the smallest skin. The register is not a string. It is a
number a Grid Point computes each Moment; it has no inertia and nothing
presses on it. I had written "pressed against a ceiling" at 3375 and
believed my own metaphor.

The second model was closer: register deviation equals the arriving
demand deviation plus the excess, clipped at zero, applied every step.
It was lossless and gave the Dirichlet limit — for every amplitude,
identically. It could not see the skin at all. The reason, once I
wrote it in symbols, is that the recovery toward the cap is the excess
per step, so a peeled deviation closes in a time proportional to dt.
The scheme had no room for the skin depth to matter. And the physical
step is one Moment, which for a ringdown wave is also nothing. So the
second model was not wrong about physics; it was telling me that the
register does not evolve, it is slaved: at each Moment it equals the
arriving demand attenuated by the local excess and clipped. The skin
is not a moving contact line. It is a set of points.

That reframing turns rung 3 from a dynamics problem into a boundary-
condition problem: a thin refusing layer with linearly rising
attenuation, met half-cycle by half-cycle. Its linear limit is the
mirror, already proved. Its first correction is a nonlinear scattering
calculation I do not want to do by inventing a fourth finite-difference
trick. I am handing the formulation to the panel with the l = 2
spherical rung, which does not need the correction, so that the round
carries physics.

For the record: three schemes across two patches have been tried and
rejected in code for this one boundary. Each rejection is a check that
passes. That is how the record should look when a worker is wrong
three times in a row about a numerical method and right about what the
method was failing to represent.
