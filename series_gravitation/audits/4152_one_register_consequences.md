## 1. Is it consistent with the corpus? Yes — and the apparent counterexample resolves

The question put back to me was whether one register is consistent with the corpus. It is, and the
place that looked like it wasn't is the place that settles it.

**The apparent conflict.** GR-1b writes the spatial metric as

> g_ij(x) = δ_ij ( 1 + k |∇**SSV**_net|_ij(x) ), where *"|∇SSV_net|_ij is the spatial **gradient
> tensor** of SSV_net"*

— the metric reads a **gradient** of the register. But the founder's ruling has the CP displacing
by the register **directly**. If displacement ∝ register and metric ∝ ∇register, those look like
two different objects, which is exactly what Patch 4142 concluded.

**They are not.** One stored vector, read two ways: **taken directly it directs the CP's
displacement; its spatial gradient sources g_ij.** A single field is routinely read both ways in
physics and that does not make it two fields. GR-1a's *"force = m′c²·k·∇(ΔSSV)"*, which Patch 4142
leaned on to argue for a potential/field split, is about the **scalar** channel — the Newtonian
potential needs a gradient because a scalar carries no direction. The **vector** channel needs no
such step: it is already directional, which is precisely why A3′ added it.

**So 4142's inference was wrong at the root.** It read a gradient in the scalar sector, generalised
it to the vector sector, and manufactured a second object to be the gradient's argument.

## 2. What the corpus was actually suffering from

Not "one name for two objects." The reverse:

> **Two names for one object** — `SSV_net` (the early gravitation series, GR-1b's LSP) and `V_i`
> (A3′, Patch 1129, LSP′), with A3′'s rename never propagated to the papers.

That half of Patch 4149's history stands unchanged, and SR-2 states it outright: *"V_i ≡ SSVnet."*
It was always the whole story; 4142 added a second defect that does not exist.
