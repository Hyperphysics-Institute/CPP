So the question becomes precise: **what is in the register that relays?**

## §2 The rank of the ratified register

## §3 Why rank 1 cannot carry a gravitational wave (proven)

Take any transverse plane vector wave `ξ = (A, B, 0)(z − ct)`. Its strain `e_ij = (∂_iξ_j + ∂_jξ_i)/2` has **`e_xx − e_yy ≡ 0` and `e_xy ≡ 0`**: no `+`, no `×`. Its only strain is the cross-shear `e_xz, e_yz` — the pattern of an elastic S-wave or of the magnetic part of light — which a ring of test masses in the transverse plane does not see as stretch-one-way/squeeze-the-other. The `+` pattern `e_xx = −e_yy` is realised only by a displacement *growing with transverse position* (`ξ_x ∝ x`, `ξ_y ∝ −y`): it is a **field acting at each point**, not a bounded displacement passed point to point. That is what "spin-2" means operationally, and no relay of a vector — however the CPs respond to it — produces it.

Therefore: **under the ratified register, CPP relays scalar and vector waves and has no gravitational wave a detector could see.** This is not a failure of the relay; it is the rank of what the relay carries.

A rank-2 local state that relays. Length along `x` set by `PSR_xx`, along `y` by `PSR_yy`: a test-mass ring stretches and squeezes exactly as LIGO's arms do. In the founder's E/B language: the DP Sea's separation polarization with a quadrupolar angular pattern *is* an anisotropic PSR. In GR's language: `PSR_ij` is the spatial metric `g_ij`, of which the isotropic `ψ⁴δ_ij` (3378) is the rank-0 part the register has been keeping.

This is not a new rule in the sense the founder forbids: the census is already directional, the register already computes moments of it (the count is the zeroth, the vector sum the first); keeping the *second* moment is a statement about what the GP computes, and the founder has said "the real question is what the CPs do in response to the SSV_abs and SSV_net they receive" — the question is whether the response has a rank-2 part.
