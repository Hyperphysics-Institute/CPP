## §0 Does a neutron star meet the criteria? Yes — the heaviest ones, at their centres, already in the record
The cap is a *central-lapse* condition, N_c = ½ (3634): every stable TOV sequence crosses it before its maximum mass. On realistic EOS (SLy, APR4) the ordinary branch ends at **M_thr = 1.78 M☉** (3636); PSR J0740+6620 at 2.08 ± 0.07 M☉ is 4σ past it, so under the derived reading it is a star whose core is at the cap — the second level, if it exists, is *loaded* there, weakly (over-demand just above 1, not the 3× of a black-hole horizon), in matter that shines. That makes the neutron star the first place to test the rule, not the last. What the record has for it is §2.

## §2 Neutron stars (visible matter at the cap)
- **NS.1 Threshold mass.** Ordinary (TOV-branch) stars end at central lapse ½: M_thr = 1.78 M☉ on SLy and APR4 alike. Heavier stars must be *something else* — the flat-core branch. [3634, 3636]
- **NS.2 The heavy pulsars exist.** J0740 (2.08 M☉), J0952 (~2.35 M☉) exist; so the flat-core branch must be **radially stable** (owed: the whole question, 3636 §5). A rule that makes the second level *soft* (no restoring force) fails here first.
- **NS.3 Radius.** A 2.08 M☉ flat-core star has R ≈ 11.2 km vs NICER's 12.4 ± 1 km — 1σ, live. [3636]
- **NS.4 Maximum mass.** The flat-core branch tops out near 2.8–3.0 M☉ at R ≈ 13 km; nothing heavier as a neutron star. [3636]
- **NS.5 The core is flat.** Pinned register ⇒ uniform lapse ⇒ no pressure gradient inside the core (a theorem of two ratified premises, 3636). The second level must be consistent with a core that carries the star's central count without a gradient.
- **NS.6 Saturated matter still shines.** J0740's *surface* is unsaturated and radiates thermally; the saturated core is buried. So the rule cannot make saturated matter intrinsically inert — only *thermally* dark (BH.6). The neutron star is the calibration point for OPEN-GR-SEA-SHELL-EMISSION-1.
- **NS.7 Tidal stiffness — the shared number.** The static response of matter at the cap is one quantity: it sets the flat-core star's Λ (future BNS with a heavy component) *and* the R-core's visible-shell k₂ (BH.4). Must be at least fluid-like (k₂ ≤ 0.02 at C = 3/8). [3684, 3685; CONV-043 Q3]

- **BH.2 The wave is relayed whole.** The ringdown is GR's at a = 0 and χ = 0.68 to 6×10⁻⁵ (3668 T3, 3675 T1); GR's admittance at 8M/3 is a *function* of ω, ℓ, m, χ, not a constant (3670) — no surface law meets it. ## §4 The Big Bang (every GP above the cap at once)
- **BB.1 The tilt.** n_s = 1 − 2/N* = 0.9649, N* ≈ 57 from N_CP ≈ 10⁸⁰, zero free parameters — PRED-C-96, anchored to Planck 2018. Whatever the second level does at Planck density must leave this leading-order result intact (it is "framework-conditional": FRW/VSL homogeneity + DP-sea). [predictions.md PRED-C-96; Patch 0771]
- **BB.2 Reheating.** If the overflow store is where the compaction energy sits, its release as the lattice relaxes below the cap *is* reheating: it must produce a radiation era, thermalize completely (the CMB is a blackbody to 10⁻⁵), and leave no relic second-level component today.
- **BB.3 The same cap.** The transition out of the second level happens at v = ⅔ — the same constant as NS.1 and BH.1. A rule with a different threshold in cosmology is two rules.
- **BB.4 Homogeneity.** Above the cap, every GP is in the same state: the second level must admit a *uniform* solution (no preferred centre), as the flat core (NS.5) already is.

## §5 On "duplicate the same framework inside the subquantum PSR"
The self-similar option — a second census with its own registers, its own shell-sum, its own cap — is the most economical story and the record is compatible with it. It brings two constraints of its own:
- **S.1 A second threshold.** A duplicated framework has a duplicated cap. That predicts a *second* saturation scale (a second lapse floor inside the first). Is it reached anywhere? Not in neutron stars (v barely above ⅔) and not at a black hole's wave horizon (v = 2, where v_eff under any register law stays below the second level's own ⅔-of-⅔ … the arithmetic of where a second floor would sit is the first thing to compute if this is the story). If it is reached only at Planck density, the Big Bang is where the self-similarity would show.
- **S.2 Termination.** A hierarchy that duplicates itself must end, or its last level leaks. Either the second level's cap is never reached in nature (S.1 says where to check), or its overflow has a terminal rule that is not another duplicate.
