## §2 The proposal — three things already in the story
1. **Discreteness of the Moment.** A CP performs one Perceive–Compute–Displace cycle per Moment. Perceive is a census of DI-bits; a census in one Moment on a finite lattice is a finite count. Call the maximum a CP can take in one Moment **K**. K is not a new constant: it is set by the lattice (the number of GP neighbours × the bits each can deliver per Moment) — the same discreteness that gives l_P and the Moment. The corpus's floor `PSR = l_P/2` is the same fact seen from the PSR side: a sphere of radius l_P/2 is the smallest that fits one cell; the PSR cannot resolve below it.
2. **Truncation with the direction kept.** When the census delivers D > K bits, the least-assumption discrete rule is that the CP takes K of them without preference (sampling). Then the recorded *absolute* count (the register, the clock rate) is capped at K, and the recorded *net* vector is the true net scaled by K/D. Nothing about the CP changes; it is the same rule, over-supplied. On the register this reads `v_eff = cap + (cap/v)(v − cap) = 2·cap − cap²/v`: the spring, with **χ(v) = cap/v** — no free constant.
3. **Conservation.** The D − K bits not taken are not destroyed; they remain in the DP sea at the surface and propagate back out — reflection. That is R-MIRROR-KEPT's |R| = 1 at full over-supply and a partial reflectivity where D barely exceeds K, and it is what carries linear and angular momentum: what the surface does not absorb it returns. The founder's "stiff putty or putty plus oscillation" is this: the part taken displaces CPs (transmission, momentum inward), the part returned is the echo.

GR and SR are untouched: below the cap the rule is the unmodified census, which is what derived them.

## §4 What it does not settle — the founder's question, sharpened
The corpus has two objects that were treated as one: the **scalar register** (the absolute count → the PSR → the clock rate → the lapse) and the **net vector** (SSV_net → the CP's displacement → the force). The budget rule caps the first and scales the second by the same K/D. Inside a saturated *star* (3634–3637) this matters: gravity's inward pull and the pressure's outward push are both nets from DI-bits, so both are scaled by K/D and **hydrostatic balance keeps GR's form** — the heavy neutron star is GR's star with a clock floor, and the flat-core sequence never arises. Inside the *R-core* (no pressure) the scaled net still points inward, so what holds the interior static is not yet in the rule: the corpus's answer has been "the register is pinned, so there is no gradient," which is the clamp's logic. Under the budget law the interior has a gradient (lapse ½ → 5/13); the CPs inside are pulled, and the static R-core needs the interior to be at rest for another reason — the c/2 kinematics the founder mentioned, or the lattice's own resistance to over-occupation at the centre (R-EXCL-RETIRED says co-occupation is one-Moment; a sustained over-supply at the centre is a different question).

**One question:** in the DI-bit mechanics, is the CP's displacement per Moment computed from *all* the bits that arrive, or only from those it can process in the Moment? If only the processed ones, the budget law is the theory's and §3 follows with no new postulate. If all of them, the clock floor and the force do not saturate together, and the spring is a separate rule.
