## §2 The same ruling reaches the R-core
Israel on the ratified surface (3631 §1): σ = 1/(8πR), **P = σ/4** — the surface must carry a tangential stress equal to a quarter of its own energy density, or it is in free fall. A dust shell there falls in. So the R-core's *staticity* has always rested on the register holding a load; 3390 found the even-sector trace wall unstable at 8M/3 (b₂ < 0) and offered compliance as the regulator (OPEN-GR-SURFACE-STABILITY-1, branch (a)). R-CAP-YIELDS-UNDER-LOAD is that branch chosen — and it means the R-core is static only if the yield law has a **restoring** character (a stiffness that lets the surface settle displaced under a steady load). A pure creep (displacement per Moment ∝ load, no restoring term — the literal one-Moment-delay compliance of 3376 taken statically) leaves nothing static under load: the R-core surface would creep inward and the object would not be the R-core.

Those two requirements bound the stiffness from both sides — a computable window, once the law's form is named.

## §4 The picture question (one, and it is the gate)
When a saturated register is loaded, does it **settle** at a displaced value that depends on the load (elastic: a stiffness, reversible — the surface bulges under a tide and springs back, the R-core surface sits a little inside 8M/3, a loaded core sits a little below lapse ½), or does it **keep moving** while the load persists (creep: no static R-core under its own surface stress; heavy stars' cores relax to GR on a timescale set by the compliance)? A third reading — it yields **to a second, absolute floor** — is the two-floor picture. The corpus's one-Moment-delay compliance (3376) is a rate law and reads as creep unless a restoring term is added; the Love-number work (3631–3633) assumed a settled, displaced level set — elastic. The founder's "the way we have constructed it" suggests the settled reading; it is not stated.
