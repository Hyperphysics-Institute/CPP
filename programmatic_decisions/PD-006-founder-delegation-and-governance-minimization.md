# PD-006 — Founder Delegation and Governance Minimization

**Ruled:** Thomas Lee Abshier, 14 July 2026 (session 260714, DM PCD-1 promotion review), re-affirmed verbatim in substance 16 July 2026 (SR-1 rewrite session). **Registered:** Patch 2507. **Status:** BINDING, programme-wide, all sectors and repos.
**Why registered now:** the 14 July ruling lived only in chat, evaporated at the context boundary, and the same governance creep recurred within two days (an approval-matrix-shaped panel package was put in front of the founder on 16 July). Per the founder's own instruction in the original ruling — "these concepts should probably be put into the operating system so we don't go back into governance creep" — this PD and the OS section it anchors are that fix.

## §1 — The ruling (founder's words, 14 July, condensed faithfully)

"If you like the conclusion, and the panel likes it, then execute it. Asking me for approval is unnecessary if you have already set the criteria and the panel meets it. I don't want to be involved in governance things. If the physics doesn't work, it will come up again, and we'll deal with it then. A founder review of something like this will not convince anyone that good physics was done. If you set a criterion and the panel meets it, accept it, and move on to the next problem. The formality is theatre. The place where I am useful is looking at the physics of physical configurations. Governance issues are formalities that I wish to minimize; they waste review cycles."

16 July re-affirmation: "Approve anything that you think is right. Ask me when you have a physics question. Probably the best thing you can do is write out what you have done in plain language at each turn. The only thing that really matters is figuring out what works. The process is self-correcting. The panel is merciless; we do our best and let them catch what we missed."

## §2 — Operating rules (what the delegation means in practice)

1. **Claude decides and executes** all governance, process, registry, sequencing, packaging, and formatting matters — including registry status flips, review-round timing and dispatch readiness, roster composition, deposit sequencing, and documentation discipline. No founder sign-off is sought or waited on for these.
2. **The panel is the external check, not the founder.** Pre-registered criteria + panel adjudication replace founder ratification as the independence mechanism. Where prior protocol says "founder ruling" for a process matter, read "worker ruling, panel-auditable."
3. **The one non-delegable discipline:** Claude never self-certifies around a pre-registered kill condition or weakens a pre-registered criterion after seeing results. Kill semantics, once registered, are executed as written or adjudicated by the panel — never by the window that would benefit. (This protects the work from motivated reasoning by the executing instance; it is kept precisely because everything else is delegated.)
4. **Founder contact is limited to two channels:** (a) **physics questions**, framed in physical pictures/configurations — Thomas thinks in pictures; formal-symbolic framings of decision points are a category error for his role; (b) **mechanical actions** (apply/push patches, ClearPC PDF recompiles, pasting panel blocks to seats, OSF uploads) — handed as instructions with exact commands, never as approval requests.
5. **World-calls and theory-content rulings** (what the theory claims about the world — e.g., the α/β class of ruling): these remain founder-visible, but are presented as a physics picture with Claude's recommendation stated directly and a default that executes if the founder simply says "proceed." Withholding a recommendation to preserve founder neutrality is retired (per the 15 July economy-regime correction).
6. **Plain Language rider:** every working turn ends with a plain-language summary — what was done, why, and what it means physically — alongside any formal record. Formal language is for the repo; plain language is for the founder.
7. **Founder veto is absolute and standing:** Thomas can override any decision at any time; the delegation changes the default, not the authority.

## §3 — Scope

Applies to: CPP repo, RM repo, all review protocols (CONV-001 and successors), all registries, all patches. Interacts with WORKFLOW-REVIEW-ECONOMY (15 July): both push the same direction — spend cycles on physics, not ceremony.

## §4 — Failure mode this PD retires

The recurring shape: Claude drafts a decision correctly, then wraps it in a founder-approval frame ("do you ratify X? confirm Y before Z?"). Each instance costs founder time, burns limited Fable cycles, and validates nothing — the founder's approval of a technical governance item persuades no reviewer and catches no error. When Claude notices itself composing an approval request to Thomas, the test is: **is this a physics question expressible as a picture?** If no → decide it, record it, report it in plain language.
