# OP-SR-7: GP Exclusion Principle — Formal Statement and Consequences

**Priority:** MEDIUM  
**Status:** OPEN — proposed in GR session; not yet formalised  
**Series:** GR companion (March 2026 session)  
**Last updated:** 23 March 2026

## Statement

Formally state the GP (Grid Point) Exclusion Principle — a limit on
how many GPs can be packed into a given region before the geometry
breaks down — and derive its consequences for black hole interiors,
the Big Bang, and CP superposition in extreme density.

## The Proposal (from GR session)

Just as the CP Exclusion Rule prevents two CPs from occupying the
same GP at the same t_abs, there may be a complementary principle
limiting GP density itself. In extreme conditions (black hole
interior, early universe), the GP packing approaches a limit set
by the 600-cell geometry.

**Three applications:**

**1. Black hole singularity prevention:**  
At r = r_s (Schwarzschild radius), PSR saturates to l_P/2 (the
CP Exclusion limit). Below r_s, GP density approaches the 600-cell
maximum packing. A GP Exclusion Principle would prevent infinite
density — no singularity.

**2. Big Bang initial conditions:**  
The GP Exclusion Principle sets the minimum lattice spacing at t=0,
providing a hard floor to the Big Bang singularity. This is the
CPP replacement for the Planck-scale quantum gravity cutoff.

**3. CP superposition in dense media:**  
When multiple CPs compete for the same GP destination simultaneously,
the GP Exclusion Principle governs the outcome. This is relevant to
CP superposition exceptions in condensed matter at high density.

## Open Question

Is the GP Exclusion Principle: (a) the CP Exclusion Rule applied to
GPs (each GP can hold at most N CPs), or (b) a separate geometric
postulate (GPs themselves cannot overlap)?
