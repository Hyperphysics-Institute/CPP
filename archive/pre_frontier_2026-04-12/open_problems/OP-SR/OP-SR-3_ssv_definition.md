# OP-SR-3: SSV Dimensional Definition — Scalar, Vector, or Tensor?

**Priority:** HIGH — blocks all rigorous SR/GR derivations  
**Status:** OPEN — inconsistent usage across SR paper versions  
**Series:** SR series (all papers); GR companion  
**Last updated:** 23 March 2026

## Statement

Provide a single, unambiguous mathematical definition of SSV (Space
Stiffness Vector) including: (a) its mathematical type (scalar, vector,
tensor), (b) its units, (c) its relationship to the DP Sea energy density.

## The Problem

The SR paper sessions identified dimensional inconsistency:

- SSV = k·m(γ−1): if SSV is dimensionless, k has units [mass]⁻¹
- "Energy density" interpretation: SSV has units J/m³
- "Vector field" usage: SSV has direction and magnitude
- General relativity requires a tensor generalisation: SSV → T^{μν}?

## The Three Options

**Option A — SSV is dimensionless:**  
k has units [mass]⁻¹. SSV represents a fractional compression of
the lattice. Clean but requires k to absorb all dimensional factors.

**Option B — SSV is an energy density (J/m³):**  
k has units m³/J. SSV directly represents the stored DP Sea energy
per unit volume. Natural connection to T^{μν} for GR.

**Option C — SSV is a vector field:**  
SSV has magnitude (energy density) and direction (pointing away from
mass/momentum source). Required for the full GR extension where
spacetime curvature is direction-dependent.

The sessions suggest Option C is Thomas's intent, but it needs to
be made explicit and the units fixed.

## Why This Is Critical

SSV appears in every CPP paper. An inconsistent definition propagates
errors across the entire programme. OP-SR-1, OP-SR-2, OP-SR-4,
OP-SS-7, and OP-QM-2 all depend on SSV being well-defined.

- Every SR, GR, and QM paper in the CPP programme
