# OP-SR-4: Full Einstein Field Equations from CPP Self-Consistency Condition

**Priority:** HIGH — described as "strong conjecture" in GR paper; proof deferred  
**Status:** OPEN — weak-field GR derived; full nonlinear GR not yet proved  
**Series:** GR companion paper (March 2026 session)  
**Last updated:** 23 March 2026

## Statement

Prove that the CPP self-consistency condition — the requirement that
each CP's SSV field is self-consistently generated by all other CPs
— is equivalent to the Einstein field equations:

$$G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = \frac{8\pi G}{c^4} T_{\mu\nu}$$

in the continuum limit.

## What the GR Paper Establishes (Rigorous)

The March 2026 GR session derived rigorously:
- Weak-field Schwarzschild metric: g_tt and g_rr from |SSV|_abs
- Factor-of-2 gravitational lensing (Eddington 1.75 arcsec result)
- Geodesic motion from 12-edge selection rule
- G = ħc/m_P² (Newton's constant from CPP, no free parameters)
- GW propagation at c from LSP perturbations

## What Remains — Four Sub-Problems

**1. Nonlinear Einstein equations:**  
The weak-field result uses linearised perturbation theory.
The full result requires showing the CPP self-consistency condition
is nonlinear in the same way as G_μν.

**2. Exact Schwarzschild solution:**  
Not the weak-field approximation, but the exact metric at all r,
including the Schwarzschild horizon r_s = 2GM/c². In CPP: solve
the self-consistency condition exactly for a point mass.

**3. Kerr metric (rotating bodies):**  
Frame dragging and ergospheres require the vorticity of SSV_net.
The Kerr metric parameter a = J/Mc (angular momentum) must emerge
from the CPP angular momentum of the rotating mass.

**4. Friedmann equations (cosmological):**  
The CPP equivalent of the cosmological metric for a homogeneous,
isotropic universe. Requires applying the LSP (Lattice State Packet)
at cosmological scales.

## The Conjecture Statement (from GR paper)

"The CPP self-consistency condition ≡ Einstein field equations
in the continuum limit."

The evidence is: both are second-order differential equations for
the metric; both have the same symmetries (diffeomorphism invariance);
both reduce to Newtonian gravity in the weak-field, slow-motion limit.
The full proof is deferred.
