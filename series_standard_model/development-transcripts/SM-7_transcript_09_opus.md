## Opus's Initial Response: The Triangle vs Tetrahedron Tension

Opus acknowledged the physical correction:

The original picture said "8 standing waves on a K₃ triangle = 8 colour states." That's algebraically correct but physically naive. The real cage is a tetrahedron — 4 triangular faces, 6 edges, 4 vertices. Each vertex connects to three faces. A displacement at any vertex propagates into all three faces simultaneously, not just one. The waves don't stay on one triangle.

The counting question was addressed: Thomas noted 6 edges × 2 states = 12, not 8. Opus clarified that SU(3) acts on the 3 vertices of one face, not on the 6 edges. The 8 generators describe the 8 ways to redistribute amplitude among 3 vertices. The edges are the channels, not the degrees of freedom being counted.

A key observation emerged: the inter-face coupling through shared edges IS the confinement mechanism. Energy on any one face constantly leaks into the others and comes back. The tetrahedral frame acts as a resonant cavity where colour energy bounces between all four faces and can never escape because every edge leads to another vertex inside the cage.

## Thomas's Resolution: Why Colour Lives on the K₃ Face

Thomas then provided the structural context that resolved the tension:

**Thomas:** The reason for the color charge, between quarks inside a nucleon, is on a triangular face, which is part of a tetrahedral structure.

He explained the baryon structure in CPP: A baryon consists of 3 quarks bonded to 3 vertices of a hybrid tetrahedron. The hybrid tetrahedron is composed of two hDPs (hybrid Dipole Pairs), each of which is a qCP paired with an eCP. Three quarks bond to three vertices, leaving the fourth apex open. That open vertex is the nuclear bonding site — where protons and neutrons connect to form nuclei.

The resolution: The K₃ face is not an abstraction. The quarks ARE the three vertices of one face of the tetrahedral cage. The SU(3) algebra lives on that face because that's where the quarks sit. The fourth vertex is structurally different — it's the nuclear bonding site, not a quark site.

## Thomas's Description of the Meson and Confinement

Thomas provided the complete physical story of quark confinement via the meson prototype:

**Thomas:** A meson is simply a single bond between two quarks of opposite charge, and a cohort of DP chains that form between the two quarks. This is the prototype gluon bond, which can stretch, fray, break, and hadronize.

Pulling apart a plus/minus qDP that was the last part of the last DP binding two distended quarks — the last DP splits, using the now unpaired qCPs as the nucleation point around which the new quark forms.

The distension past the point of additional qDPs adding to the resistance-to-distension force of the quark-antiquark bond is the left side of the quark dissociation curve. The right side, where the restoring force decreases with displacement, is the point beyond which DP fraying has begun. As qDP bonds fray and thermalise due to increased distance, the bond eventually ends with a single DP binding the quark-antiquark, and it is that DP which splits, and nucleates a new quark/antiquark pair. Thus, the concept of quark confinement appears.

## The Discovery: Axiom Reduction from 10 to 7

Thomas asked: "Does this solve anything useful in terms of the concepts we used in SM-7? Is there another mechanism that we axiomatized that we might be able to use this understanding?"

Opus identified the payoff: Axioms A8 (Edge Locality) and A9 (Face Saturation) — the two axioms governing the bond-count asymmetry in SM-7 — become derivable from the tetrahedral cavity physics rather than requiring independent postulation.

**Why A8 (EW couples to 2 bonds) follows from edge-mode structure:**
An edge mode is a DI-bit that hops one edge and returns — a length-2 walk. It only visits the two endpoints of that edge. On the K₃ quark face, each vertex has 2 internal edges. The edge-mode perturbation is structurally confined to those 2 bonds. This isn't a choice — it's what "edge mode" means.

**Why A9 (colour couples to 12 bonds) follows from the tetrahedral cavity:**
A face-mode displacement enters a vertex and radiates into every connected face. Thomas's description: "each CP apex is connected to three triangles, each of which are oscillating and transmitting and reflecting the wave." The displacement visits all z=12 nearest-neighbour bonds because the tetrahedral cavity has no walls. The colour self-energy samples the full neighbourhood. This isn't a choice — it's what "face mode in a tetrahedral cavity" means.

**The Walk-Dimension Gauge Principle (A6'):**
Four axioms (A6 Edge Abelianity, A7 Face-Bond Circulation, A8 Edge Locality, A9 Face Saturation) reduce to one principle:

> Walk dimensionality determines gauge coupling range. 1D walks (edges) are local — couple to 2 internal bonds. 2D walks (faces) radiate globally — couple to all z bonds.

Axiom count: 10 → 7. Axiom-to-prediction ratio: 1.1 → 0.78. The theory now predicts more things than it assumes.

## Significance

This session produced three results:

1. **Physical mechanism for confinement:** The tetrahedral cage is a closed resonant cavity. Colour energy entering any face is redistributed to all four faces through vertex splitting. It can never escape. This is confinement from cage geometry, not from a potential V(r) = kr.
