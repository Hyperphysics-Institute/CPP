\begin{abstract}
The four cage structures proposed by Conscious Point Physics (CPP)
for the quark generations --- tetrahedron, icosahedron, dodecahedron,
and icosidodecahedron --- are shown to embed exactly in the 600-cell
polytope as bonded distance shells.  Every edge of every cage is a
real lattice edge of the 600-cell.  The cage sequence is not a free
choice: it is the unique sequence of regular and semiregular polyhedra
that appear as bonded shells around any vertex of the 600-cell.
Shell~3 (12 vertices at distance $d \approx 1.176$) has \emph{zero}
lattice edges and cannot form a cage, creating a structural gap
between the bottom quark (Shell~2) and the top quark (Shell~4).

A power-law mass scaling $m \sim V^{2.38}$ (calibrated from
$m_s \to m_c$) predicts the bottom quark mass to 3\%, independently
confirming the SM-7 Koide prediction to 2\%.  The top quark is
12$\times$ heavier than this law predicts, and the Shell~3 gap
provides a qualitative explanation: the transition from surface-dominated
to volume-dominated mass generation.

The attractive-edge fraction (the ratio of opposite-charge to total
edges under balanced charge assignment) is shown to be exactly $2/3$
on the tetrahedron (forced for all assignments), $2/3$ on the
icosahedron (maximum), and achievable on the dodecahedron and
icosidodecahedron.  The universality of $2/3$ across the cage
hierarchy may connect to the Koide ratio $K = 2/3$ derived in SM-3.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ============================================================

In Conscious Point Physics (CPP), fermion masses arise from the
Zitterbewegung (ZBW) energy stored in geometric cage structures
on the 600-cell lattice.  The charged lepton mass spectrum
(SM-6~\citep{abshier2026sm6}) and the heavy quark mass spectrum
(SM-7~\citep{abshier2026sm7}) have been derived from the K$_3$
eigenvalue perturbation mechanism, which gives the Koide phase
$\theta$ and hence the mass ratios within a generation.

However, the Koide formula does not explain \emph{why} there are
exactly three generations of heavy quarks, or why the mass scale
jumps so dramatically between generations (from $m_s = 93$~MeV to
$m_t = 173$~GeV --- a factor of 1850).  A structural model for
quark generations, proposed by Thomas Abshier in collaboration
with Grok (xAI), posits that each quark generation is hosted by a
progressively larger polyhedral cage:

\begin{itemize}
\item Strange quark: tetrahedral cage (4 vertices, 6 edges)
\item Charm quark: icosahedral cage (12 vertices, 30 edges)
\item Bottom quark: dodecahedral cage (20 vertices, 30 edges)
\item Top quark: 30-vertex cage (identity to be determined)
\end{itemize}

This paper reports a computational discovery: all four cages embed
\emph{exactly} in the 600-cell as bonded distance shells, and the
sequence is uniquely determined by the lattice geometry.

\label{sec:shells}
% ============================================================

The 600-cell has 120 vertices.  From any vertex, the remaining 119
vertices are distributed across 8 distinct distances.  We computed
these shells by explicit construction of all 120 vertices and
pairwise distance calculation.

In addition to the shells, the 600-cell contains 600 tetrahedral
cells (K$_4$ complete subgraphs), each with 4 vertices and 6 edges.
Every vertex participates in 20 tetrahedra.

\begin{theorem}[Bonded shells of the 600-cell]
\label{thm:shells}
The 600-cell has exactly three distance shells with nonzero lattice
edges: Shell~1 (icosahedron, 12V, 30E, $z=5$), Shell~2
(dodecahedron, 20V, 30E, $z=3$), and Shell~4 (icosidodecahedron,
30V, 60E, $z=4$).  Combined with the tetrahedral cells (4V, 6E,
$z=3$), there are exactly four polyhedral cage types with lattice
edges in the 600-cell.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By explicit computation.  The 120 vertices of the 600-cell were
constructed from the standard coordinates (8 axis-aligned vertices,
16 half-integer vertices, 96 golden-ratio vertices from even
permutations of $(\phig/2, 1/2, 1/(2\phig), 0)$ with sign changes).
All $\binom{120}{2} = 7140$ pairwise distances were computed,
yielding 8 distinct values.  For each shell, all pairs of vertices
within the shell were checked for adjacency (distance $= 1/\phig$).
Shells 3, 5, 6, 7, and 8 have zero internal lattice edges.
Shells 1, 2, and 4 have the edge and coordination counts listed in
Table~\ref{tab:shells}. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Shell identification]
Shell~1 is the \emph{vertex figure} of the 600-cell --- the
polyhedron formed by the nearest neighbours of any vertex.
The icosahedron shares the $H_3$ symmetry of the 600-cell's $H_4$
symmetry group.  Shell~2 is the dodecahedron, the dual of the
icosahedron.  Shell~4 is the icosidodecahedron, the rectification
of the icosahedron.  All three belong to the icosahedral symmetry
family, consistent with the $H_4$ symmetry of the 600-cell.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Shell~3 gap]
Shell~3 contains 12 vertices at distance $d \approx 1.176$ with
\emph{zero} lattice edges connecting them.  This creates a structural
gap in the bonded-shell sequence between Shell~2 (bottom quark) and
Shell~4 (top quark).  The physical significance of this gap is
discussed in Section~\ref{sec:top}.
\end{remark}

\section{Mass Scaling}
\label{sec:mass}
% ============================================================

If quark mass scales as a power law in the vertex count,
$m \sim V^\alpha$, then calibrating $\alpha$ from the strange--charm
pair gives:
\begin{equation}
\alpha = \frac{\log(m_c/m_s)}{\log(V_c/V_s)}

\end{equation}

\section{Charge Structure and the 2/3 Ratio}
\label{sec:charge}
% ============================================================

In CPP, cage vertices are Conscious Points with definite charge
polarity ($+$ or $-$).  Edges between opposite-charge vertices are
attractive (ZBW-active, storing oscillatory energy); edges between
same-charge vertices are repulsive.  Under a balanced assignment
($V/2$ positive, $V/2$ negative), the \emph{attractive fraction} ---
the ratio of opposite-charge edges to total edges --- characterises
the cage's oscillatory capacity.

\begin{theorem}[Attractive fraction on the tetrahedron]
\label{thm:tetra23}
For the complete graph $K_4$ with any balanced charge assignment
(2 positive, 2 negative vertices), the attractive fraction is
exactly $2/3$.  All $\binom{4}{2} = 6$ assignments give the same
result.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
With 2 positive and 2 negative vertices, the number of opposite-charge
pairs is $2 \times 2 = 4$.  The total number of edges is
$\binom{4}{2} = 6$.  The attractive fraction is $4/6 = 2/3$. \qed
\end{proof}

For the larger cages, the attractive fraction depends on \emph{which}
vertices carry which charge.  We computed all balanced assignments
exhaustively (for $V \leq 20$) or by sampling ($V = 30$):

\begin{remark}[Universality of $2/3$]
The ratio $2/3$ is achievable on all four cages: forced on the
tetrahedron, the maximum on the icosahedron and icosidodecahedron,
and achievable (though not maximum) on the dodecahedron.  If a
physical selection principle drives cages toward $2/3$ (energy
minimisation, symmetry, or stability), then the attractive fraction
is universal across the cage hierarchy.

The Koide ratio $K = 2/3$ (SM-3~\citep{abshier2026sm3}) arises from
the K$_3$ eigenvalue structure: $K = \lambda_+/(\lambda_+ + |\lambda_-|)
= 2/3$.  Whether the cage attractive fraction and the Koide ratio
share a common geometric origin is an open question.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Bond type distribution]
At the $2/3$ assignment, the attractive edges decompose as
eDP~:~qDP~:~hDP~:~repulsive $= 1:1:2:2$ (averaged over all four-type
charge refinements).  This ratio is combinatorial: with equal numbers
of electric-type and quark-type CPs, the cross-type pairs (hDP) are
exactly twice as common as either same-type pair.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[No K$_4$ in larger cages]
The icosahedron, dodecahedron, and icosidodecahedron contain
\emph{zero} K$_4$ subgraphs.  This means they cannot be decomposed
into hybrid tetrahedra (hTetras).  Larger cages must be assembled
from individual CPs, not from pre-formed hTetras.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The dodecahedron anomaly]
The dodecahedron is the only cage whose maximum attractive fraction
($4/5 = 0.800$) exceeds $2/3$.  With $z = 3$ and a nearly bipartite
graph structure, it allows extreme charge separations.  If the bottom
quark's cage operates at $4/5$ rather than $2/3$, it would have a
higher fraction of ZBW-active edges than the other cages.  Whether
this affects the bottom quark mass or mixing properties is an open
question.
\end{remark}

\section{The Top Quark and the Shell~3 Gap}
\label{sec:top}
% ============================================================

The top quark mass ($m_t = 172.7$~GeV) is $12\times$ heavier than
the $V^{2.38}$ law predicts from the icosidodecahedral vertex count.
The power-law exponent needed to match the top mass from the strange
mass is $\alpha \approx 3.6$, compared to $\alpha \approx 2.4$ for
charm and bottom.

The Shell~3 gap provides a structural explanation.  The bonded-shell
sequence is:

\begin{center}
Cell $\to$ Shell 1 $\to$ Shell 2 $\to$
\fbox{Shell 3 (gap)} $\to$ Shell 4
\end{center}

For the strange, charm, and bottom quarks, each successive cage is
the next bonded shell --- there are no gaps.  For the top quark,
the cage at Shell~4 is separated from Shell~2 by an unbonded shell.
This creates a large protected internal volume (from $d = 1.0$ to
$d = 1.414$, including the gap at $d = 1.176$) where DP chains can
grow without thermalising.

The mass scaling transition can be understood as follows:
\begin{itemize}
\item For small cages (Shells 1--2): internal chains are short,
  and mass is dominated by the cage surface --- the vertices
  radiating chains outward into the DP Sea.  Scaling: $m \sim d^2$
  (surface-like).
\item For the top quark (Shell~4, beyond the gap): the large
  protected interior allows long chains from the central CP to the
  cage surface.  Mass is dominated by the 4D hypervolume of
  organised DP chains filling the interior.  Scaling: $m \sim d^4$
  (volume-like).
\end{itemize}

The 12 unbonded Shell~3 vertices may act as intermediate anchoring
points for chains spanning the gap, further enhancing the internal
chain density.  A quantitative model of this enhancement is left
for future work.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Complementarity of cage model and Koide formula}

The cage model and the Koide formula (SM-6, SM-7) address
\emph{different} aspects of the mass spectrum:

\begin{itemize}
\item The \textbf{Koide formula} gives mass \emph{ratios} within a
  generation.  It derives from the K$_3$ eigenvalue perturbation on
  whatever cage hosts the quarks.  It predicts $m_b$ and $m_t$ from
  $m_c$ to 1--2\%.
\item The \textbf{cage model} gives the mass \emph{scale} of each
  generation.  It derives from the vertex count and shell distance
  of the cage.  It predicts $m_b$ from $m_s$ and $m_c$ to 3\%.
\end{itemize}

The 2\% agreement between the cage prediction and the Koide
prediction for $m_b$ --- from completely independent physics ---
is a non-trivial consistency check.  If both are correct, the
complete mass theory would be: cage geometry sets the scale, K$_3$
eigenvalues set the ratios.

\subsection{Why these cages and no others}

The cage sequence is determined by the 600-cell geometry:

\begin{enumerate}
\item The tetrahedron is a \emph{cell} of the 600-cell (600 copies).
\item The icosahedron is the \emph{vertex figure} (Shell~1, $z = 5$).
\item The dodecahedron is Shell~2 ($z = 3$), the dual of the icosahedron.
\item The icosidodecahedron is Shell~4 ($z = 4$), the rectification of the icosahedron.
\end{enumerate}

All four belong to the icosahedral symmetry family ($H_3 \subset H_4$).
No other regular or semiregular polyhedra appear as bonded shells.
The number of quark generations is fixed at four (not three --- CPP
predicts four caged quark types corresponding to the four bonded
structures, though only three are observed as free quarks due to
the top quark's rapid decay).

\subsection{Open questions}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The exponent $\alpha = 2.38$:} Is this derivable from
  the 600-cell geometry?  It is the ratio $\log(m_c/m_s)/\log(V_c/V_s)$,
  and both $m_c/m_s$ and $V_c/V_s = 3$ are geometric quantities.
  The value is close to $12/5 = 2.4$ (the inverse of the coupling
  ratio $E/F = 5/3$ from SM-7) and to $2 + \sin^2\theta_W = 2.23$.
\item \textbf{The Shell~3 gap mechanism:} How exactly does the gap
  enhance the top quark mass?  Is it through increased internal
  chain density, Shell~3 anchoring, or a qualitative change in the
  mass generation mechanism?
\item \textbf{The $2/3$ connection:} Is the universality of the
  $2/3$ attractive fraction related to the Koide ratio $K = 2/3$?
  Both emerge from the 600-cell geometry --- one from K$_3$
  eigenvalues, the other from cage charge structure.
\item \textbf{The dodecahedron anomaly:} Does the bottom quark's
  cage operate at $2/3$ or at its maximum of $4/5$?  If $4/5$, does
  this affect the $b$ quark's properties beyond mass?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
% ============================================================

The quark cage hierarchy of Conscious Point Physics is not a model
choice --- it is a geometric consequence of the 600-cell lattice.
The four bonded polyhedral structures (tetrahedron, icosahedron,
dodecahedron, icosidodecahedron) are the \emph{unique} cage types
that the lattice supports, and they appear at successive distance
shells with a structural gap at Shell~3.

The $V^{2.38}$ mass scaling law, calibrated from two quark masses,
predicts the third to 3\% and agrees with the independent SM-7
Koide prediction to 2\%.  The top quark anomaly ($12\times$ heavier
than the law predicts) is structurally correlated with the Shell~3
gap --- the only break in the bonded-shell sequence.

The attractive-edge fraction $2/3$ is achievable on all four cages,
creating a potential link between the cage charge structure and the
Koide ratio $K = 2/3$.  Whether this link is coincidental or
structural is the central open question.
