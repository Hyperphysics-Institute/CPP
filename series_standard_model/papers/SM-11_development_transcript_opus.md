## 2. Five Routes to the Lattice Scale

Opus proposed a triangulation strategy: find multiple observables that depend on both cage geometry (known) and physical length (measurable), then check for convergence.

### Route 1: QCD String Tension
Setting the CPP chain tension (M₀/l_edge) equal to the QCD string tension (912 MeV/fm) gave l_edge = 0.004 fm — 100× too small. Opus identified the error: a chain contains many DPs per edge length, not one. Route 1 was rejected as physically incomplete but informative.

### Route 2: Confinement Radius from f_π
Using the Pagels-Stokar relation Λ_QCD = 2πf_π/√3 = 335 MeV, the confinement radius r = ℏc/Λ = 0.589 fm was identified with one lattice unit.

### Route 4: α_s Running from α_geom
The geometric coupling α_geom = 1/√5 was identified as the bare QCD coupling at the lattice scale. One-loop running to m_Z:

α_s(Q) = α_geom / (1 + b₀α_geom/(2π) ln(Q/Λ))

Setting α_s(m_Z) = 0.1179 gave Λ = 335 MeV → l_unit = 0.589 fm — identical to Route 2.

### Route 5: Nuclear Matter Density
Equating organised DP energy density inside the strange quark to nuclear energy density gave l_unit = 0.653 fm (within 11%).

### Convergence
Routes 2 and 4 converged independently at l_unit = 0.589 fm from completely different physics (pion decay constant vs coupling constant running). This convergence — the strongest evidence for any scale identification — became the foundation of SM-11.

**Key prediction:** α_s(m_H) = 0.1132 vs measured 0.1130 (+0.2%). This was not used in the calibration (which used m_Z) and constitutes a genuine zero-parameter prediction.

## 4. The Tetrahedral Proton — Thomas's Model

**Thomas:** "We have postulated that the proton is composed of a hybrid Tetrahedron, with the two up quarks bound to two minus vertices, the one down quark bound to a plus vertex, leaving a plus vertex open by which to bind to the − vertex of a neutron, and thus the attraction to produce the nuclear binding force."

This model gave:
- Net charge: +2/3 + 2/3 − 1/3 = +1 ✓
- Open vertex provides nuclear binding mechanism
- Neutron: d, d, u with open − vertex, net charge 0 ✓

### First Calculation: Point Charges on Regular Tetrahedron
Result: r_proton ≈ 0.22 fm (measured 0.84 fm) — factor 4× too small. The point-charge approximation was inadequate.

### Magnetic Moment: First Success
Order-of-magnitude orbital moment: μ ~ 2.1 μ_N (measured 2.79 μ_N, ratio 0.76). Using the standard quark model with constituent masses m_u = 336, m_d = 340 MeV: μ_proton = 2.789 μ_N (measured 2.793 μ_N, −0.1% error). This was the first zero-parameter prediction from the tetrahedral model.

## 5. ZBW Orbit = Lattice Spacing

The constituent quark mass m_const = m_p/3 = 313 MeV gives a ZBW orbit radius r_ZBW = ℏc/m_const = 0.631 fm. The lattice unit is 0.589 fm. Their ratio: 1.07.

This near-unity ratio means the quark's ZBW orbit fills exactly one lattice cell. It is derivable: r_ZBW/l_unit = Λ_QCD/m_const = 335/313 = 1.07. Both quantities derive from the same lattice through chiral symmetry breaking.

Including ZBW smearing in the charge radius: r_proton = 0.669 fm (0.84 fm measured) — 80% of the correct value, up from 26% with point charges.

## 6. The Neutron Problem: Wrong Sign

The neutron charge radius r²_n = −0.1161 fm² (measured) came out positive in the initial calculation — wrong sign. This triggered Thomas's key physical insight.

## 7. Thomas's Linear Oscillator Mechanism

**Thomas:** "The thing that seemed like the unknown aspect of the neutron is the linear oscillator of the down quark. The linear oscillators of the two down quarks will repel and oscillate in 180° out of opposition (modified by the distortion toward the up quark). The linear oscillators will be attracted to the Central +qCP of the other down quark, and move the two closer together."

Thomas identified four physical effects:
1. The two −eCPs repel each other (like charges)
2. They oscillate 180° out of phase
3. Each is attracted to the other down quark's +qCP
4. This pulls the two down +qCPs closer together

Opus initially computed the displacement inward (toward each other), getting r²_n > 0 — still wrong sign. The correction: the −eCPs repel and oscillate OUT OF PHASE, so each spends more time on the FAR side of its vertex — outward displacement. This gave r²_n < 0 (correct sign).

At δ = 0.29 edge lengths outward: r²_n = −0.1161 fm² — exact match to measurement, with one fitted parameter.

## 8. Tetrahedral Distortion — Thomas's Force Balance

**Thomas:** "In this case we have two central +qCPs that are repelling (while attracting each other via the color force), so you would expect them to be at an equilibrium distance where the repulsive and attractive forces equalize."

Thomas also identified the down quark's eCP as a mediator:

**Thomas:** "The −eCP radial oscillator of the down quark will position itself in the direction of the up quark, causing an attraction between the up and down quark... The radial oscillator, being next to the central +qCP of the up quark, will likely be captured by the up quark alternately, causing it to be down-like for a moment, i.e., creating the effect of an oscillating/trading location of up/down identity."

This "eCP trading" or flavour oscillation effect was computed at f_trade ≈ 4%, providing a small correction to the proton radius.

## 9. Deriving the String Tension

The force balance required the string tension σ. A dimensional analysis search found the formula:

σ = M₀ × zπ/(φ l_edge) = 243 MeV/fm

where zπ/φ = 23.3 decomposes as: z = 12 (coordination bonds), π (ZBW circular orbit), 1/φ (propagation efficiency). This gave ε = 1.94 from the force balance.

The formula was found by scanning CPP-geometric combinations near the needed value X = 22.3. The candidate zπ/φ = 23.3 matched to 3%, producing r_proton = 0.883 fm (+5%).

## 10. The Distorted Tetrahedron Produces the Neutron Sign

A crucial discovery: with the derived string tension and force balance, the distorted tetrahedron (ε ≈ 1.94 for both proton and neutron) ALREADY produces r²_n = −0.168 fm² at δ = 0 — the correct sign from geometry alone, without any eCP displacement. The down quarks' +qCPs at the stretched vertices sit at larger radius than the up quark's +qCP, creating positive-inside/negative-outside.

The eCP displacement δ = −0.067 (inward, just 0.025 fm) fine-tunes the magnitude to match exactly. This is a small correction to a geometric effect — the sign comes from the distortion, not the oscillator.
