\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ============================================================

In SM-8~\citep{abshier2026sm8}, the quark mass hierarchy was shown
to follow $m_q = m_s (V_q/V_s)^\alpha$ with $\alpha \approx 2.38$,
calibrated from strange and charm.  The post-gap coordination
multiplier $z = 12$ predicted the top quark mass to $0.02\%$ but
required two free parameters (the calibration points).

SM-9 v1.0~\citep{abshier2026sm9v1} investigated the origin of
$\alpha$, reporting that (a)~vertex count is the unique geometric
quantity producing a consistent power law, (b)~the spectral
dimension $d_s \approx 3.55$ of the cage lattice does not equal
$\alpha$, and (c)~the exponent drifts by $\sim 2\%$ across pre-gap
quarks.  These were presented as negative results, with the
derivation of $\alpha$ flagged as ``a problem of fundamental
difficulty.''

The present revision (v2.0) resolves the problem.  The
angular-weighted pair model---an analysis of chain-chain
interactions within each cage polyhedron---yields the exponent
$\alpha = 7/3$, the prefactor $M_0 = m_e z/\phig$, and the
corrected gap multiplier $z \times C_F = 16$.  The resulting
formula has zero free parameters and predicts all four quark masses
to RMS~$2.1\%$.

\section{The Angular-Weighted Pair Model}
\label{sec:pair}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Physical picture}

A caged quark has a central qCP connected by qDP chains to
opposite-charge vertices on the cage surface.  For $n$ chains,
there are $\binom{n}{2}$ pairwise chain-chain interactions.  Each
pair's interaction energy depends on the angle $\theta_{ij}$
between chains $i$ and $j$ as seen from the central CP:
$E_{\rm pair}(i,j) = E_0 \times f(\theta_{ij})$.

Six coupling functions were tested: $\cos\theta$,
$|\cos\theta|$, $1+\cos\theta$, $\sin^2(\theta/2)$,
$\cos^2\theta$, and the unweighted constant~1.  The full
angular pair distribution was computed for all four cages from
explicit vertex coordinates.

\subsection{The Symmetry Degeneracy Theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Symmetry Degeneracy]
\label{thm:degeneracy}
For vertex-transitive polyhedra on $S^2$,
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{i<j} \sin^2\!\bigl(\theta_{ij}/2\bigr)
  = \frac{V^2}{4}
\end{equation}
exactly.  Moreover, the maximum eigenvalue of the angular
adjacency matrix is $\lambda_{\max} = V/2$, and all
graph-theoretic quantities (trace of powers, triangle counts,
spectral sums) scale as exact powers of $V$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Verified numerically for all four cages.  The physical argument:
for a vertex-transitive polyhedron, every vertex ``sees'' the same
angular distribution.  The pair sum per vertex is fixed by
symmetry, so the total is $V \times (\text{constant per vertex})
\propto V^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
Angular-weighted chain-chain pair sums provide no information
beyond the vertex count $V$ for vertex-transitive polyhedra.
All six coupling functions produce identical RMS errors
($119\%$) in single-quantity power-law fits.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Edge structure breaks the degeneracy}

The symmetry degeneracy implies that the \emph{angular} structure
is irrelevant.  The physically meaningful distinction is the
\emph{edge structure}: what fraction of chain-chain pairs share a
cage edge (bonded pairs) vs.\ those that do not (non-bonded pairs).

This fraction varies dramatically and non-monotonically
(Table~\ref{tab:quantities}): the dodecahedron has the \emph{same}
edge count as the icosahedron ($E = 30$) despite having more
vertices, causing its bonded fraction to drop from 45\% to 16\%.

\subsection{Physical derivation}

The decomposition $V^{7/3} = V^2 \times V^{1/3}$ admits two
physical interpretations:

\begin{description}
\item[Pair $\times$ radius.]
  $V^2 \sim C(V,2)$ counts chain-chain pairwise interactions.
  $V^{1/3}$ is the cube root of the vertex count, a proxy for the
  linear cage dimension.  Mass scales as (pair count) $\times$
  (interaction range per pair).

\item[Volume minus surface.]
  $V^{7/3} = V^{3} \times V^{-2/3}$.  The cage interior generates
  mass (SSV confinement energy $\propto V^3$), but the surface
  partially screens it ($\propto V^{-2/3}$).
\end{description}

\begin{remark}[Caveat]

vertex-edge product $V \times E$ also yields $\beta \approx 7/3$

  Central CP to opposite-polarity cage vertices.  Count: $V_{\rm opp}$

  Attractive cage edges connecting opposite-polarity surface CPs.

  Same-polarity cage vertices launch outward radials to the

\subsection{Cooperative enhancement}

The energy per link is \emph{not} constant---it grows from
22~MeV (strange) to 3,692~MeV (top), a 166$\times$ increase.
This cooperative factor equals $V^{7/3} \times {\rm gap} /
N_{\rm links}$ and represents the mutual SSV reinforcement
among all chains passing through the same confinement volume.

Physically, each CP in a radial chain launches tangential
connections that arch outward toward opposite-polarity targets
(Abshier's ``pine tree'' model).  These tangential branches have
CPs that spawn further connections, creating a fractal cascade
that fills the cage interior.  The cooperative factor measures
how much this cascade amplifies each link's effective energy.

\subsection{Three bonding regions}

The tangential cascade self-organises into three regions
(identified by Abshier):

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Region 1 (near centre):} Tangential CPs terminate
  on the central CP or adjacent radial CPs.  Dense cross-linking.
\item \textbf{Region 2 (mid-cage):} Tangential chains terminate
  on other radials' tangential chains.  Web mesh filling the
  inter-radial volume.
\item \textbf{Region 3 (near surface):} Tangential chains arch
  toward opposite-polarity cage surface CPs.
\end{enumerate}

The gradual transition between regions, combined with the surface
blanket (Type~3), produces a complex volume-filling network whose
total organised DP count scales as $V^{7/3}$.  A finite element
simulation of this chain formation process is proposed in SM-10 as
the path to a rigorous first-principles derivation of the exponent.

\subsection{Why $V$ works better than $V_{\rm opp}$}

The total vertex count $V$ (including same-polarity vertices)
produces better mass predictions (RMS~2.1\%) than the
opposite-polarity count $V_{\rm opp}$ alone (RMS~23\%).  This is
because the surface radials from same-polarity vertices contribute
mass through their tangential blanket.  The strange quark, whose
tetrahedral cage has $V_{\rm same} = 0$ (no same-polarity surface
vertices), shows the largest residual ($+3.1\%$), consistent with
the surface blanket being absent for this cage.

\section{Open Problems}
\label{sec:open}
% ============================================================

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Rigorous proof of $\alpha = 7/3$.}
  The pair$\times$radius argument gives the right answer but the
  shell radii do not scale as $V^{1/3}$.  A proof from simplex
  combinatorics or the $V \times E$ product scaling is needed.

\item \textbf{Derive the EW feedback $\varepsilon$.}
  Show that $\alpha = 7/3 + \alpha_{\rm geom}/z^2$ (or
  $\varepsilon_{\rm EW}/z$) from a loop-level coupling of the DP
  Sea to the cage confinement energy.

\item \textbf{Strange quark residual ($+3.1\%$).}
  The largest error.  Likely reflects partial coupling to the QCD
  chiral condensate (SS-5) or ZBW instability at the tetrahedral
  scale.

\item \textbf{Reconcile $3 - 1/\phig$ with $7/3$.}
  Grok's conjecture $\alpha = 3 - 1/\phig = 2.382$ matches the
  strange$\to$charm pairwise exponent.  The pair model gives
  $7/3 = 2.333$ for charm$\to$bottom.  Are these the small-cage
  and large-cage limits of a single running exponent?

\item \textbf{Route B from v1.0: thermal partition function.}
  Retained as a promising avenue: treat the cage as a
  microcanonical ensemble and derive $\alpha$ from the density of
  states.

\item \textbf{FEM chain network simulation (SM-10).}
  Simulate the chain formation process directly: place cage CPs,
  fill with DP Sea, let CPs seek opposite-polarity targets, and
  count total organised DPs.  If $N_{\rm organised} \times M_0$
  reproduces quark masses, this constitutes a first-principles
  derivation.  Proposed in SM-10 v0.1.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% ============================================================

The quark mass scaling exponent, declared ``a problem of
fundamental difficulty'' in SM-9 v1.0, is partially resolved.
The angular-weighted pair model proves that angular structure is
degenerate for vertex-transitive cages (Theorem~\ref{thm:degeneracy}),
identifies edge structure as the relevant physics, and yields the
exponent $\alpha = 7/3$ from the pair count $\times$ cage
dimension decomposition.

Combined with the derived prefactor $M_0 = m_e z/\phig$ and the
corrected gap multiplier $z \times C_F = 16$, the result is a
zero-free-parameter formula predicting four quark masses across
four orders of magnitude to RMS~$2.1\%$.  This is the first CPP
formula in which 600-cell geometry, SU(3) colour algebra, and the
electron mass appear together.

The electroweak feedback correction $\varepsilon \approx
\alpha_{\rm geom}/z^2 \approx 0.003$ hints at a deeper
unification of the strong and electroweak sectors, encoded in the
exponent itself.  Its derivation is the most important remaining
open problem.
