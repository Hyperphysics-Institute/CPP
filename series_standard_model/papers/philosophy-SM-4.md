## II. What a Consistency Check Achieves Philosophically

SM-4's consistency check — that K = 2/3 holds to 11 ppm — is carefully
distinguished from a prediction. The distinction matters philosophically as
well as scientifically. A prediction says: given this theory, this quantity
must have this value. A consistency check says: given that this theory is
true, the data should satisfy this constraint — and it does.

The philosophical status of a consistency check is lower than a prediction
but higher than mere fitting. In fitting, the model parameters are adjusted
to match the data — any model with enough parameters can fit any data. In a
consistency check, a specific theoretical constraint (K = 2/3, with ρ derived
rather than fitted) is tested against data. The constraint could fail; its
not failing is evidence in favour of the theory.

The 11 ppm consistency of the lepton masses with K = 2/3 is meaningful
because the K3 derivation of K = 2/3 uses no lepton mass data. The derivation
comes from graph theory (K3 eigenvalues), statistical mechanics (Boltzmann
equipartition at the Planck temperature), and CPP axioms (the ZBW resonator
Hamiltonian from C3 symmetry). The lepton masses play no role in the
derivation. When those masses then satisfy the derived constraint to 11 ppm,
that agreement is evidence — not conclusive evidence, but genuine evidence —
that CPP's K3 identification is correct.
