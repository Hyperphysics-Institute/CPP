\begin{abstract}
SM-8 established a zero-free-parameter quark mass formula
$M_q = m_e(z/\phig)V^{7/3}$ (RMS~$2.1\%$).  SM-9 showed that
the exponent~$7/3$ arises from pair counting times linear cage
dimension.  This paper develops a finite element chain network
model to explain \emph{why} this scaling holds.  A CPU
proof-of-concept reveals two physical regimes:
(1)~\textbf{intra-cage cascade} for strange, charm, and bottom,
where cross-linking chains with radially-varying cascade
rate~$f(r)$ reproduce all three masses; and
(2)~\textbf{Shell~3 relay} for the top quark, where DPs
dissociate to occupy the edgeless Shell~3 positions, forming a
synthetic icosahedral relay cage whose 12~vertices radiate to
Shell~4.  The relay produces $z \times C_F = 16$ from geometry
alone.  Success is measured against PDG mass ratios
directly---not $V^{7/3}$-derived targets---avoiding circular
validation.  The current model is a \emph{calibrated geometric
model} (4~fitted parameters for 4~data points); it becomes a
first-principles derivation when the GPU simulation reproduces
the cascade rates from DP-level dynamics alone.
\end{abstract}

\section{Definitions}
\label{sec:definitions}
% ============================================================

\begin{definition}[Organised Dipole Pair]
\label{def:orgDP}
A Dipole Pair (DP) is \emph{organised} if both of its constituent
CPs are bonded to opposite-polarity CPs belonging to the chain
network.  Formally, a DP at position~$\mathbf{x}$ is organised if:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Its positive CP is bonded to a negative CP of an adjacent
  organised DP or cage/central CP, \emph{and}
\item Its negative CP is bonded to a positive CP of an adjacent
  organised DP or cage/central CP.
\end{enumerate}
A DP with only one bonded end is a \emph{chain terminus}.  An
unbonded DP remains part of the disordered Sea.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Chain]
A \emph{chain} is a maximal connected sequence of organised DPs
where each DP's positive end is bonded to the next DP's negative
end: $\cdots {}^+\!\text{DP}_i{}^- \!\cdot\! {}^+\!\text{DP}_{i+1}{}^- \cdots$
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Cascade Rate]
The \emph{cascade rate} $f(r)$ at radius~$r$ is the probability
that a newly created cross-link DP's free end finds another chain
to bond with within one lattice spacing.
\end{definition}

\begin{axiom}[A10 --- First-Principles Chain-Network Mass]
\label{ax:A10}
Quark mass equals the total SSV field energy of the organised
chain network.  For a cage with $\Norg$ organised DPs:
\begin{equation}
M_q = M_0 \times \Norg, \qquad M_0 = m_e \frac{z}{\phig}
  = 3.790 \text{ MeV}
\end{equation}
where $M_0$ is the energy per organised DP link, $m_e$ is the
electron mass, $z = 12$ is the 600-cell coordination number,
and $\phig = (1+\sqrt{5})/2$.
\end{axiom}

\section{Physical Model: Two Regimes}
\label{sec:model}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Regime 1: Intra-cage cascade (s, c, b)}

For pre-gap quarks, mass arises from a chain cross-linking
cascade.  Three chain types fill the cage interior:

\textbf{Type~1 (Radial):} Central CP to each of $V_{\rm opp}$
opposite-polarity cage vertices.  Each chain has $d/l_{\rm edge}$
DP links where $d$ is the shell distance and
$l_{\rm edge} = 1/\phig$.

\textbf{Type~2 (Tangential):} Along the $E_{\rm attr}$ attractive
(opposite-polarity) cage edges, each $\sim l_{\rm edge}$ long.

\textbf{Type~3 (Surface Radial):} From $V_{\rm same}$
same-polarity vertices outward to the thermalization distance.

At radius~$r$ from center, each pair of radial chains generates
\emph{cross-link DPs}.  The cross-link count scales as
$C(V_{\rm opp}, 2) \times r\theta_{\rm nn}/l_{\rm edge}$, where
$\theta_{\rm nn}$ is the mean nearest-neighbour angle between
radials.  Each cross-link DP's free CPs seek new partners,
generating secondary cross-links in a \emph{cascade}.

\subsubsection{Derivation of the cascade rate profile}

The cascade rate $f(r)$ measures the probability of successful
bonding at radius~$r$.  This probability is proportional to the
local chain density $\rho_{\rm chain}(r)$:

\begin{equation}
f(r) \propto \rho_{\rm chain}(r) \times \sigma_{\rm bond}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_{\rm bond} \sim l_{\rm edge}^2$ is the bonding
cross-section.  The chain density has two components:

\textbf{Radial component:} $V_{\rm opp}$ lines through a sphere
of area $4\pi r^2$ gives
$\rho_{\rm rad}(r) = V_{\rm opp}/(4\pi r^2)$, which decays as
$1/r^2$.  In $f(r)$, this produces an exponential-like decay
from center.

\textbf{Surface component:} Near $r = d$, the $E_{\rm attr}$
tangential chains add a localised density enhancement.

The minimal parameterisation consistent with these two components
is:
\begin{equation}
f(r) = f_0 \, e^{-r/(\lambda d)}
  + f_{\rm surf} \, e^{-(r-d)^2/(2\sigma^2)}
\label{eq:cascade}
\end{equation}
where $f_0$ is the central cascade rate (the key fitted parameter),
$\lambda \approx 0.3$ controls radial decay, $f_{\rm surf} \approx
0.5$ is the surface enhancement, and $\sigma \approx l_{\rm edge}$
is the tangential spread.

\textbf{Note:} The GPU simulation (Phase~3) will test whether this
functional form emerges from DP dynamics or requires modification.

\subsubsection{Scaling-limit heuristic}

The total organised DP count scales as:
\begin{equation}
\Norg \;\sim\; \underbrace{C(V_{\rm opp}, 2)}_{\sim V^2
  \text{ (pairs)}} \;\times\;
  \underbrace{\int_0^d r \, \frac{f(r)}{1 - f(r)} \, dr}_{%
  \sim d \sim V^{1/3}} \;\sim\; V^{7/3}
\label{eq:scaling}
\end{equation}
The pair count gives $V^2$; the radial integral over the cascade
amplification gives the remaining $V^{1/3}$ (one power of linear
cage dimension).  This connects the SM-9 pair-counting argument
to the FEM chain dynamics.

\subsection{Regime 2: Shell 3 relay (top quark)}
\label{sec:relay}

The top quark's cage (Shell~4, $V = 30$) is separated from the
central CP by Shell~3 ($V = 12$, $E = 0$).  The standard cascade
alone produces $\sim 2{,}100$~MeV---far short of 172,760~MeV.

\subsubsection{Relay formation energetics}

Shell~3's 12 vertex positions are lattice-determined---they exist
in the 600-cell geometry whether DPs occupy them or not.  The
central CP's field, propagating outward through the bonded shells,
creates a potential landscape at Shell~3 distance.  Sea DPs at
Shell~3 positions experience a 12-fold symmetric potential minimum
from the ambient lattice's icosahedral coordination structure.

The energetic argument: a DP at a Shell~3 vertex position gains
energy $\sim M_0$ by bonding to the radial chain from the central
CP.  It loses entropy $\sim k_B T_{\rm Sea}$ by leaving the
disordered Sea.  If $M_0 \gg k_B T_{\rm Sea}$ (which holds for
the heavy quarks), occupation is energetically favourable.

The synthetic Shell~3 cage is \emph{metastable}: it is maintained
by the organised chain network but has no lattice edges to anchor
it.  This predicts the top quark decays before hadronising
($\tau \sim 5 \times 10^{-25}$~s)---an observed empirical fact.

\textbf{Open question:} Does the relay form spontaneously in the
GPU simulation, or must Shell~3 positions be seeded?  This is the
most important test of the mechanism.

\subsubsection{Two-level confinement}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Level 1:} DPs occupy the 12 Shell~3 vertices,
  forming a synthetic icosahedral cage with DP-chain edges.
\item \textbf{Level 2:} Each Shell~3 vertex acts as a secondary
  central CP, radiating to $\sim 5$ Shell~4 vertices.  This
  creates $12 \times 5 = 60$ secondary chains criss-crossing the
  Shell~3--4 gap.
\item \textbf{Criss-cross web:} The 60 secondary chains bond
  tangentially, cascading within the gap at rate $f_{\rm relay}$.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{remark}[Why $z \times C_F = 16$]
$V_{\rm Shell\,3} = 12 = z$ (both from icosahedral symmetry---a
geometric identity of the 600-cell).  Each synthetic relay bond
carries $C_F = 4/3$ (independently derived in SS-2 from cage
hopping algebra).  Product: $12 \times 4/3 = 16$.
\end{remark}

\section{Simulation Design}
\label{sec:simulation}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Chain formation algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Dipole Chain Formation}
\label{alg:chain}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Cage CPs $\{C_1, \ldots, C_V\}$ at lattice positions;
  central CP $C_0$ at origin; Sea DPs $\{D_1, \ldots, D_N\}$
  at random positions within $r_{\rm therm}$
\Ensure Set of organised DPs $\mathcal{O}$
\State $\mathcal{O} \gets \{C_0, C_1, \ldots, C_V\}$
  \Comment{Seeds}
\State Build k-d tree $\mathcal{T}$ on all CP positions
\Repeat
  \State $\text{new\_bonds} \gets 0$
  \ForAll{$p \in \mathcal{O}$ with free positive end}
    \State $t \gets$ nearest unbound negative end in
      $\mathcal{T}$ within $r_{\rm bond}$
    \If{$t$ exists}
      \State Bond $p^+ \to t^-$; add $t$'s DP to $\mathcal{O}$;
        $\text{new\_bonds} \mathrel{+}= 1$
    \EndIf
  \EndFor
  \ForAll{$p \in \mathcal{O}$ with free negative end}
    \State $t \gets$ nearest unbound positive end in
      $\mathcal{T}$ within $r_{\rm bond}$
    \If{$t$ exists}
      \State Bond $p^- \to t^+$; add $t$'s DP to $\mathcal{O}$;
        $\text{new\_bonds} \mathrel{+}= 1$
    \EndIf
  \EndFor
\Until{$\text{new\_bonds} = 0$}
\State \Return $|\mathcal{O}| - (V + 1)$
  \Comment{Organised Sea DPs}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

For the top quark, add after line~1: if any organised chain
reaches Shell~3 distance, mark Shell~3 vertex positions as
available relay sites and add them to $\mathcal{O}$.

\section{Conclusion}
% ============================================================

The FEM chain network model explains why $V^{7/3}$ scaling holds:
pair counting gives $V^2$, radial cascade integration gives
$V^{1/3}$, and the product is $V^{7/3}$.  Two physical regimes
operate: intra-cage cascade (s, c, b) and Shell~3 relay (top).
The relay mechanism derives $z \times C_F = 16$ from the
600-cell's geometric identity $V_{\rm Shell\,3} = z = 12$.

The model is currently a calibrated geometric framework.  It
becomes a first-principles derivation when the GPU simulation
reproduces the cascade rates from DP-level dynamics alone.
Successful reproduction would constitute the first derivation
of quark mass ratios from geometry in any theoretical framework.
