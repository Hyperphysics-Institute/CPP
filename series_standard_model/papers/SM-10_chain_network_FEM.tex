\begin{abstract}
SM-8 established a zero-free-parameter quark mass formula
$M_q = m_e(z/\phig)V^{7/3}$ (RMS~$2.1\%$).  SM-9 showed that
the exponent~$7/3$ arises from pair counting and that the mass
is distributed across a chain network with three bonding regions.
This paper proposes and partially executes a finite element
simulation to compute quark masses directly from organised DP
counting.  A CPU proof-of-concept (Phases~1--2) reveals two
distinct physical regimes: (1)~\textbf{intra-cage cascade} for
strange, charm, and bottom quarks, where chain cross-linking with
a radially-varying cascade rate~$f(r)$ reproduces all three
masses exactly; and (2)~\textbf{Shell~3 relay} for the top quark,
where DPs dissociate to occupy the edgeless Shell~3 positions,
forming a synthetic icosahedral relay cage whose 12~vertices act
as secondary central CPs radiating to Shell~4.  The relay
mechanism produces the $z \times C_F = 16$ gap multiplier from
geometry: $V_{\rm Shell\,3} = 12 = z$ (icosahedral symmetry),
each relay bond carrying $C_F = 4/3$ (SU(3) Casimir).  Success
is measured against PDG mass ratios directly---not
$V^{7/3}$-derived targets---avoiding circular validation.
Successful reproduction of the PDG ratios by a GPU simulation
would constitute the first derivation of quark masses from
geometry alone.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation}
% ============================================================

The CPP quark mass programme has progressed through four stages:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{SM-8 v2.1:} Calibrated formula
  $m \sim V^{2.38} \times z$ (2~parameters, 0.02\% top quark).
\item \textbf{SM-8 v4.1:} Zero-parameter formula
  $M = m_e(z/\phig)V^{7/3} \times [z C_F]$ (0~parameters,
  2.1\% RMS).
\item \textbf{SM-9 v2.2:} Partial derivation of $7/3$ from pair
  counting; chain-type energy budget; three bonding regions.
\item \textbf{SM-10 (this paper):} FEM simulation to derive the
  masses from first-principles chain dynamics.
\end{enumerate}

The remaining gap: the exponent~$7/3$ is motivated but not
rigorously derived, and the gap multiplier $z \times C_F = 16$
had an algebraic derivation but lacked a geometric mechanism.
This paper addresses both.

\section{Physical Model: Two Regimes}
\label{sec:model}
% ============================================================

The CPU proof-of-concept (Phases~1--2) revealed that quark mass
arises from two distinct physical regimes.

\subsection{Regime 1: Intra-cage cascade (s, c, b)}

For pre-gap quarks, mass comes from a chain cross-linking cascade
within the cage.  \textbf{Radial chains} (Type~1) connect the
central CP to each of $V_{\rm opp}$ opposite-polarity cage
vertices.  At each radius~$r$, pairs of radial chains generate
\textbf{cross-link DPs} whose count scales as $C(V_{\rm opp}, 2)
\times r \theta_{\rm nn} / l_{\rm edge}$.  Each cross-link DP's
free CPs seek new partners, creating a \textbf{cascade} amplified
by $f(r)/(1-f(r))$.  \textbf{Tangential chains} (Type~2) on the
$E_{\rm attr}$ attractive cage edges and \textbf{surface radials}
(Type~3) from $V_{\rm same}$ same-polarity vertices complete the
network.

The cascade rate profile:
\begin{equation}
f(r) = f_0 \, e^{-r/(\lambda d)}
  + f_{\rm surf} \, e^{-(r-d)^2/(2\sigma^2)}
\label{eq:cascade}
\end{equation}
where $f_0$ is the central cascade rate, $\lambda \approx 0.3$,
and $f_{\rm surf} \approx 0.5$.  Three bonding regions emerge
naturally: dense cross-linking near center (Region~1), web-mesh
at mid-cage (Region~2), and surface convergence (Region~3).

\subsection{Regime 2: Shell 3 relay (top quark)}
\label{sec:relay}

The top quark's cage (Shell~4, $V = 30$) is separated from the
central CP by the edgeless Shell~3 ($V = 12$, $E = 0$).  The
standard cascade alone produces $\sim 2{,}100$~MeV---far short of
172,760~MeV.

Abshier proposed a two-level confinement mechanism: DPs
dissociate from the Sea to occupy the 12 Shell~3 vertex positions,
forming a \emph{synthetic icosahedral cage} with DP-chain edges.
Each Shell~3 vertex acts as a \emph{secondary central CP},
radiating to $\sim 5$ Shell~4 vertices.  The resulting
$12 \times 5 = 60$ secondary radial chains criss-cross the
Shell~3--4 gap, bonding tangentially with each other and
cascading within the gap region.

\begin{remark}[Why $z \times C_F = 16$]
Shell~3 has $V_{\rm Shell\,3} = 12 = z$ (both determined by
icosahedral symmetry---a geometric identity).  Each synthetic
relay bond carries $C_F = 4/3$ (independently derived in SS-2).
Product: $z \times C_F = 16$.  This provides a geometric
derivation of the algebraic gap multiplier.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Top quark non-hadronisation]
The synthetic Shell~3 cage has no lattice edges.  It is inherently
unstable, predicting the top quark decays before hadronising
($\tau \sim 5 \times 10^{-25}$~s).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Mass calculation}

$M_q = M_0 \times \Norg$, where $M_0 = m_e z/\phig = 3.790$~MeV
and $\Norg$ includes cascade DPs (all quarks) plus relay DPs
(top only).

Single-bond-per-CP gives trivial ratios.  Dipole chain formation
gives $\sim V$ scaling (too flat).  Universal cascade law
$f = A\rho^B$ gives 160\% RMS (too few parameters for the
dynamic range).

\subsection{Physical decomposition}

For pre-gap quarks, 51--88\% of mass comes from Region~1
($r < 0.2d$).  For the top quark, 99\% comes from the Shell~3
relay (1\% from standard cascade).

\subsection{Percolation interpretation}

The mass hierarchy maps to distance from the percolation
threshold: strange $f_0 = 0.74$ (sub-critical), bottom
$f_0 = 1.00$ (critical), top relay $f_0 = 1.00$ (near-critical
in the gap web).

\section{Simulation Design}
\label{sec:simulation}
% ============================================================

\textbf{Setup:} Central CP at origin; cage CPs at lattice
positions; DP Sea at density $\rho_{\rm Sea}$; Shell~3 relay
sites for top.

\textbf{Algorithm:} Each DP is a dipole (two ends bonding
independently).  Organised CPs seed each round; free ends bond to
nearest opposite-polarity unbound end within $r_{\rm bond}$.
Cascade repeats until no new bonds form.  For top: relay stations
at Shell~3 positions activate when chains reach them, generating
secondary radials and criss-cross web.

\textbf{Validation:} Tetrahedron first (strange), then
icosahedron (charm), dodecahedron (bottom), icosidodecahedron
(top).  Convergence test across $\rho_{\rm Sea} \in
\{10^2, 10^3, 10^4\}$.  Average over 50+ random Sea
configurations.

\section{Conclusion}
% ============================================================

The FEM chain network simulation has progressed from proposal
(v0.1) to calibrated two-regime model (v1.0).  The CPU
proof-of-concept established two regimes (cascade and relay),
calibration targets for the GPU ($f_0$ per quark), the Shell~3
relay mechanism ($z \times C_F = 16$ from geometry), and the
percolation interpretation of the mass hierarchy.

The GPU simulation (Phase~3) will test whether DP-level dynamics
reproduce the calibration targets without fitting.  If successful,
this would be the first derivation of quark mass ratios from
geometry in any theoretical framework.
