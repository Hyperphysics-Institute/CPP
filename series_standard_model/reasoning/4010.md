# Reasoning — Patch 4010 (I tried to build the lattice, and found something better than a lattice)

**STATUS: verbatim, at patch.**

## Building it

4009 named the blocker and its generator. So this patch built it: the icosian cut-and-project, with the
120 unit icosians as generators, coordinates held **exactly** in ℤ[φ] as integer pairs rather than floats,
so that the arithmetic is not the thing under suspicion.

## The structural fact I should have anticipated and did not

The icosian ring is a ℤ[φ]-module. It is closed under multiplication by φ. And **φ⁻ⁿ lies in ℤ[φ] for every
n** — the Fibonacci identity, φ⁻ⁿ = ±(F_{n+1} − F_n φ), which I verified exactly out to n = 12 rather than
recalling. Since φ⁻ⁿ → 0, **the ring contains points arbitrarily close together. It is dense in ℝ⁴.**

That is not a detail. It means the lateral lattice **cannot** be "all the icosians" — a selection rule is
mandatory, not a modelling convenience. And the selection rule is precisely what makes one point's
surroundings differ from another's.

## What the measurement said

Three windows, three radii. Counting points at distance ≤ 1 from each interior point — the would-be
600-cell shell — the answer comes out **30, 45, 46**, and at a wider window **141, 165, 173**.

**Never 120.** At any radius, at any window tested.

The one exception is the origin, which has exactly 120 — and that is an artifact of the window being
centred there. It is where the construction starts, not a property of the set. A less careful version of
this patch could have reported "the origin has its 600-cell, construction works" and shipped a lattice.

## Why I am not calling this a failed construction

Because it may not be a construction problem at all, and the shape of it is worth stating precisely.

Aperiodicity is **forced** — 4009 verified Coxeter's obstruction from the dihedral angle. And a
quasicrystal evades the crystallographic restriction **by having finitely many different local
environments**. That is the mechanism by which icosahedral symmetry is possible at all in an extended
structure; it is not an incidental blemish that a better window would remove.

So *"every GP is the centre of its own 600-cell"* has two readings and they are not close:

- **Strictly** — one identical environment at every point. That is the crystal condition. Coxeter excludes
  it for this polytope.
- **Weakly** — every GP has *a* 600-cell of GPs about it, while the rest of its surroundings differ. That
  may be satisfiable, and it is what a construction should target.

## Why I did not just take the weak reading

It would have unblocked everything. The scaling study needs a lattice; the weak reading plausibly gives
one; the founder would very likely have meant something compatible with it.

But the strict reading is how the sentence is written, and it sits at axiom level next to A2. **Quietly
weakening an axiom-level statement so that a computation becomes possible is the one move a delegated
worker must never make** — PD-006 hands me governance and mechanics, and PD-007 was written precisely to
stop me choosing the branch that rescues a ledger row. The weak reading is the convenient branch. So it
goes up.

## Bounded, so it is not over-read later

A **ball** window was tested and failed. The correct Elser–Sloane window is the projection of the **E₈
Voronoi cell**, and I did not build it. **It is not shown that no construction works** — only that the
corpus pointer (*the graph is constructible from the icosian registration*) is a promissory note rather
than a recipe, and that the obvious first attempt does not discharge it.

No lattice shipped. No panel. No verdict moved. `[PCD-EXT]`.
