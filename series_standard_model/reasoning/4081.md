## The test

K3 needs the W bracelet's decisions to be exact ties. A real weak event has an incoming particle, and an
asymmetric perturbation breaks exact equality. **But with finite registers, a perturbation below one LSB
quantises away and does not break the tie at all.** So the question is quantitative: ties survive iff the relative
perturbation ε at the decision point is below 2⁻ᵇ.

Measured: ties hold at 1.0000 up to ε ≈ 10⁻⁵ and die by ε ≈ 10⁻¹, with the knee tracking 2⁻ᵇ as predicted
(b = 8 survives 10⁻³; b = 16 is already at 0.0375 there). Controls behave: ε = 0 gives 1.0000 at every depth
(4080 reproduced), ε = 1 kills ties at every depth.

## The tension this exposes — and it is the honest shape of K3

**EM safety wants deep registers** (leak ~2⁻ᵇ, 4080). **Bracelet ties want the perturbation below one LSB**, which
deep registers make *harder*. K3 lives only in the window **ε_bracelet < 2⁻ᵇ < Δ_EM**. I had not seen this tension
before running it; 4080's "deeper is simply better" was too easy.

## What SF-2 says, and it is the best news this arc has had

SF-2 §5, verbatim: the W bracelet *"activates when an external charge is captured at its **D₆-symmetric
centroid**"*; the activated state *"disintegrates statistically per local SSV-gradient probabilities."*

**A charge at the symmetry centre is a fixed point of D₆ — it does not break the symmetry.** So the perturbation
that would have been order unity, and would have destroyed the ties outright, **is absent in SF-2's own
construction, by symmetry rather than by assumption.** And what then selects the decay channel is precisely a
degenerate SSV comparison — the exact place a chiral tie-break would act. The corpus's existing catalysis picture
and K3's requirement fit without either being bent to the other.

**I want to be careful about how much that is.** It removes the leading obstruction. It does not prove ε is small
enough, because of the residual below.

## The residual — and it is a physics question, not a computation

The captured charge **moves**. A moving charge at the centroid is not D₆-symmetric: its direction of arrival picks
out an axis. So ε_bracelet is set by the charge's **directional asymmetry**, not by its full amplitude. Whether
that residual falls below one LSB depends on what the bracelet's SSV comparison actually sees — the charge's
position (symmetric, fixed point) or its motion (asymmetric). **That is a question about the physical picture and
it is the founder's**, per PD-008: it is not a governance or sequencing matter, and no computation I can run
settles what the substrate registers.

## Convenient branch — marked

**Mixed.** The convenient half is SF-2's centroid: it is a genuine fit and I found it by looking, not by
construction. The inconvenient half is the tension in §2o, which I discovered by running the test and which makes
4080's conclusion ("deeper registers are simply better") wrong — deeper registers help EM and hurt the bracelet.
I report both. **What would kill K3:** the founder answering that the bracelet's SSV comparison sees the incoming
charge's motion at full strength, which puts ε at order unity and leaves no window.
