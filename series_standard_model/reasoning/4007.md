## What the ruling settles, and what it does not

> *The way the axioms read is that the GPs speak to the CPs, and the CPs move as instructed by the GPs.*

That is **agency**: the CP does not sample the field and does not compute. It executes. A1′'s RISC division
of labor, stated from the CP's end.

It is not, by itself, the range answer, and I want to be careful not to take it as one — that would be
reading a founder's sentence as settling more than it says, which is its own failure mode. What it does is
point at where the range answer lives: if the GP hands down the instruction, the question becomes what the
GP computed it from.

**A3′ answers that, and specifically:** *each GP broadcasts **to its PSR shell** the Lattice State Packet*,
and per the AP-4 ↔ A3′ harmonisation (3610) *the receiver computes the moments of the census it receives.*
So a GP's state is assembled from packets **arriving from its first shell** — at rest PSR = l_P, one edge.

**The coupling is field-range at exactly one shell.** Not zero-range: the sources are other GPs. Not
mean-field: the reach is one hop. So **4006's zero-range closure is not the branch the substrate is on**,
and the d = 1 correlation 4006 measured is real physics rather than an artifact of a model we do not use.

That is the branch I would not have picked for convenience, and it is the one that leaves work open.

## The larger finding, which the ruling shook loose

Chasing "what does the GP receive from" sent me to A2, and A2 says the substrate is a **tessellated**
600-cell. `programme_orientation.md` §177 is explicit: *every Grid Point is the centre of its own 600-cell;
shell 7 of vertex A is shell 1 of neighbouring vertex B.*

**A single 600-cell is the first shell around one host vertex. It is not the substrate.** And 0694, 0813,
1100, 4005 and 4006 — the entire χ_η route, mine included — build exactly that single 120-vertex polytope.

For a **local** observable this is fine: η_v depends only on N[v], and 0813's η, the on-site variance and
the d = 1 term are all computed on the right neighbourhood. **For a correlation length it is the wrong
object, not merely a small one.** ξ is a property of the tessellation. Measuring it on one cell is not a
low-resolution measurement of ξ; it is a measurement of something else.

So 4006's caveat gets upgraded rather than repeated. I wrote *"cannot resolve ξ beyond ~2."* The correct
statement is **ξ is not defined on that object at all**, and the scaling study must be built on a
tessellated patch, not on a bigger single polytope. Respecified in `todolist.md`.

Worth noting where this came from: not from auditing my own work, which I had already done twice in this
lane, but from following the founder's sentence to the axiom it pointed at. The audit I ran at 4003 did not
find it and neither did the one at 4004.
