# Reasoning — Patch 4016 (my wording raised the alarm; the alarm was wrong and the real issue is elsewhere)

**STATUS: verbatim, at patch.**

## The question, and where it came from

The founder asked whether the neutrino result needs revising against the lattice. He asked because **I put
two sentences next to each other at 4015** — *the cut-and-project patch gives z ∈ {12,13,14,18,19,26}* and
*SF-4 requires z = 12* — which reads as a flagship paper being contradicted by my own patch.

It is not, and I should not have written it that way. The cut-and-project class was **already established
not to be the substrate** — 4013 closed the strict reading for it, and 4015's z-spread was a *second reason
that class is wrong*, not a first reason SF-4 is. A construction known to be wrong producing a wrong z says
nothing about SF-4.

## What SF-4 actually says

I went to `sf-4_neutrinos.tex` rather than to the DM-lane citation that started this chain, having spent
three patches this session on exactly that mistake:

> *z = 12 as 600-cell coordination number — **THEOREM** (inherited from SS-1, SM-1)*

glossed twice as *each vertex has 12 nearest neighbors arranged icosahedrally*. **That theorem is true.**
All 120 vertices of one 600-cell have exactly 12 nearest neighbours; verified here and in three earlier
scripts.

## The real step, which is not what he was worried about

The theorem is about **one** 600-cell. **SF-4 uses z as a per-GP property of the substrate** — *every Grid
Point emits its fixed DI-bit complement toward **one of its z = 12***. And the founder ruled at 4009 that
space is **innumerable** 600-cells.

**Whether every GP of the extended substrate has exactly 12 nearest neighbours has never been shown. It is
inherited from a single-polytope theorem.** There is no construction to check it against — which is the
4010/4013 blocker, arriving from a direction that has nothing to do with chirality.

That is a genuinely different finding from the one he was asking about, and a smaller one than "SF-4 is
wrong". It is an unchecked inheritance, not an error.

## Why I did not leave it at that

Because I wanted to know what rides on it, and the answer surprised me. **SF-4 depends on z twice** —
linearly through `M₀ = m_e·z/φ`, and to the tenth power through `σ_ν = z^(−2·d_eff)`. So

    Σm_ν  ∝  z^(−9)

Reproducing from the paper's own inputs gives **64.9 meV at z = 12**, matching its published value, so the
dependence is read correctly and not reconstructed wrongly. Then:

| z | Σm_ν | ratio |
|---|---|---|
| 11 | 142 meV | 2.19× |
| **12** | **64.9 meV** | **1.00** |
| 13 | 31.6 meV | 0.49× |
| 14 | 16.2 meV | 0.25× |

**z is an integer.** There is no small perturbation available — the nearest alternative value is a
factor-two move on a cosmologically constrained prediction.

I want to be precise about what that is and is not. It is **not** evidence that z ≠ 12, and not a reason to
doubt SF-4. It is the reason the inheritance step deserves an actual check rather than an inheritance: a
step nobody has verified sits underneath a number with no room to absorb being wrong.

## What changes

The founder's outstanding lateral-construction question was a chirality-lane blocker. **It now carries
SF-4's absolute neutrino mass scale as well.** That is worth him knowing when he decides how much the
question is worth.

## What I did not do

Did not revise SF-4 — nothing found warrants it. Did not claim the substrate's z is anything but 12 — the
only construction tried is the known-wrong one. Did not decide the outstanding question.

No panel. No verdict moved.
