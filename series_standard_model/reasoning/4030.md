# Reasoning — Patch 4030 (I tested the rescue I would have wanted to work)

**STATUS: verbatim, at patch.**

## The rescue

4015 measured z ∈ {12, 13, 14, 18, 19, 26} on the extended patch, using a **distance threshold** — count
everything within ε of the edge length. 4020 then set the lateral target as *flat 4D, uniform z = 12,
near-icosahedral with the frustration carried as strain*.

The obvious thought, and one I had been circling for several patches: **the z-spread is an artifact of the
threshold.** In real quasicrystals and metallic glasses coordination is **rank-based** — the twelve
*nearest* neighbours — not *everything within ε*. Under a rank definition z = 12 holds by construction, and
the class might have met the target all along. Eleven patches of failed constructions would have turned out
to be a definitional mistake.

It is the answer I wanted. So I tested it rather than adopting it.

## It fails, twice, and the second is decisive

**The coordination shell is over-populated.** At the nearest-neighbour distance the patch holds **13, 18,
19 or 26 points** where the 600-cell holds **exactly twelve**. So "the twelve nearest" is a **choice among
equals**, not a determination — and that is the same degeneracy failure 4023 found in η's top-four
selection, turning up in a completely different object. Rank does not define what threshold failed to
define; it just hides the ambiguity behind an `argsort`.

**And the chosen twelve do not lie in a 3-flat.** s₄/s₁ = **0.52** against the 600-cell's **4 × 10⁻¹⁶**.
This is the one that settles it. The 600-cell's twelve span a 3-flat *exactly* — that is what makes the
vertex figure an icosahedron at all. The patch's twelve do not, by a wide margin. They are not a strained
icosahedron; they are a different object, and no choice among the equidistant points repairs it.

## What it does to the target

4020's "uniform z = 12" was too weak, and I wrote it. The real requirement is that the nearest-neighbour
shell contain **exactly twelve points** — not twelve on average, not twelve selected from a larger
equidistant set — **and that those twelve span a 3-flat**. Both hold in the 600-cell by construction.

A target that a failing candidate can satisfy is not a target, and "uniform z = 12" was satisfiable by
`argsort`.

## The method note I most want kept

**This test only means anything because the offsets are centred before the SVD.**

My first version did not centre them, and returned **0.56 for the 600-cell itself** — which would have read
as *the patch is as good as the reference*, and passed a failing class on the strength of the reference
looking equally bad.

Knowing to centre comes from **Patch 4018**, which found that the neighbour icosahedron is **not centred on
its vertex** — its offsets sum to 2.2918 rather than zero. An earlier result in this same session was
needed to make today's measurement mean anything.

That is the concrete form of the argument PD-008 rests on, and it is worth having an instance rather than
the principle: a fresh window would have had 4018 in the corpus but no reason to reach for it here, and the
uncentred version returns a clean-looking number that nothing else would contradict.

No panel. No verdict moved.
