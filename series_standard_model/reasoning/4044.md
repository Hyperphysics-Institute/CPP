# Reasoning — Patch 4044 (a real prediction that predicts nothing measurable)

**STATUS: verbatim, at patch.**

## The mechanism

The icosahedron and the dodecahedron are **centrally symmetric**, so the parity-odd part of the hopping
symbol — Im[Σ_v e^{ik·v}] = Σ_v sin(k·v) — vanishes **identically** for both shells CPP currently has.

**The chiral 60-point shell is not centrally symmetric.** That is the entire parity-odd channel, and it
exists only because the shell is chiral.

## It is real

Against two achiral controls flat at ~1e-16 at every k tested, the chiral shell's |Im| rises to
**7.86 × 10⁻⁶ at k = 6**.

## And its order was predicted before it was measured

Icosahedral invariant degrees are **2, 6, 10, 15**. The degree-15 invariant is the **pseudo**-invariant:
even under the rotation group I, **odd** under reflection. So the first parity-odd term a chiral
icosahedral shell can contribute is **O(k¹⁵)**.

Measured log-log slopes: 14.53, 14.84, 14.77, 14.63, 14.38, 14.06 — trending monotonically toward 15 as k
falls, the shortfall at large k being higher-order sine terms.

I want the ordering on record: the degree came from the invariant theory first, and the slope confirmed it.
Had I measured first I would have had a number near 14.5 and a temptation to call it fifteen.

## The near-miss

At k ~ 1 the term sits **below double precision**. An earlier run at k = 0.4–0.9 returned ~3 × 10⁻¹⁵
**scaling as k¹** — float noise, not signal.

Reading that as a measurement would have been exactly the error this session has spent all day
cataloguing, and it is the second time today an estimator has returned a number that meant "I cannot see"
rather than "there is nothing there."

## What it means

**A chiral substrate does make the vacuum optically active.** The prediction is definite in form.

**But it enters at (k·a)¹⁵.** At any accessible k that is suppressed beyond conceivable measurement — far
worse even than SR's l = 6 floor at ~l_P/10³⁰.

**Risk retired, and it was a live one.** When 4043 proposed this test, a chiral vacuum could have been
ruled out on the spot by the absence of observed vacuum optical activity. It is not excluded. The chiral
substrate survives the observational test it invited.

## And the hope fails

4043 hoped the chirality strength would set the size of an **observable**, and so become calibratable
against one measurement and predictive thereafter.

**The observable is unobservable. It calibrates nothing.**

4042's free magnitude remains free; chirality is still a structural parameter rather than a prediction; and
the route 4043 proposed to promote it is closed. A different observable would be needed, and **I do not
have one to name.**

That is the honest headline, and it is smaller than the one available — "chiral substrate predicts vacuum
optical activity" is true and would read much better without the exponent.

No panel. No verdict moved. FI-C-9 = V3 stands.
