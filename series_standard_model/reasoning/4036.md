## The cages are the taxonomy

SM-2's mass breakdown distinguishes particles **by cage** — tetra, icosa, dodeca. Capotauro puts the W⁰
ring on the Petrie hexagon of the **first-shell icosahedron**. These are not descriptive labels; they are
what assigns each particle its row.

So on an FCC substrate the cages would still have to be exact lattice configurations, or the taxonomy has
no exact referent.

## And they cannot be

A 5-fold rotation has trace **1 + 2cos 72° = φ**, irrational. Trace is basis-independent, so no 5-fold
rotation lies in GL(3,ℤ): the icosahedral group has no faithful integral representation, and **no lattice
carries a regular icosahedron as an exact vertex configuration.**

That is the crystallographic restriction, and it is the same argument shape as 4018's H₄ result one
dimension down — the third time today an argument from this arc has transferred to a new object, which is
the sort of thing that only shows up if you keep the context.

**Measured rather than asserted:** the best regular icosahedron placeable on FCC sites, over 3000 random
orientations × 40 scales, has RMS vertex error **0.327** against a nearest-neighbour distance of 1.414 —
**23%**. A sampling bound, not a proven optimum; the true best could be lower and I have said so.

## And it exceeds the ruling I already have

4020, verbatim: *the 600-cell space is allowed to be **slightly distorted**, just like materials with
icosahedral packing.*

Real icosahedral matter distorts by a **few percent**. This is 23% — an order of magnitude more. **(C) is
not covered by the ruling I was leaning on it with.** It would need a new and much larger concession.
