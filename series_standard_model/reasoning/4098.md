## The derivation in brief

Current THEO-QM-10 (QM-5 Theorem 2): site operator ĉ_i, ĉ_i² = 0 from SSV exclusion,
mode algebra {a_k, a†_{k'}} = δ_{kk'} from eigenmode orthonormality, ONE CP per mode.

Revised with helicity bit b ∈ {+1,−1}: site operator ĉ_{i,b}.

Two CPs with the SAME (GP, bit) = same quantum state → THEO-1 + SSV + indistinguishability
→ strict algebraic exclusion ĉ_{i,b}² = 0.

Two CPs with OPPOSITE bits at the same GP = different states (distinguishable by DP arc
orientation, per Patch 4097 founder ruling) → SSV Coulomb cost only (no indistinguishability
exclusion) → ĉ†_{i,+1}ĉ†_{i,−1} ≠ 0.

Mode operators a_{k,b} = Σ_i u_k(i)* ĉ_{i,b} satisfy {a_{k,b}, a†_{k',b'}} = δ_{kk'} δ_{bb'}
from eigenmode orthonormality (same proof, applied per spin sector; the eigenmodes u_k(i)
are agnostic to the spin label). Cross-spin anticommutators are zero (orthogonal Hilbert
subspaces).

Result: 120 spatial modes × 2 spin slots = 240 fermionic states per species.
Pauli doubling: one spatial mode can hold two CPs (one b=+1, one b=−1).
Spin-½: {a_{k,b}, a†_{k',b'}} = δ_{kk'} δ_{bb'} is the spin-½ fermion algebra.

## Why the founder's DP-arc ruling (Patch 4097) is load-bearing

The physical argument distinguishing same-bit from opposite-bit co-occupation relies on:
"velocity is not intrinsic to a CP; it is carried by DP arcs established during acceleration."
Two opposite-bit CPs at the same GP have opposite DP arc orientations — they are physically
distinguishable at the substrate level, not just by label. This is what licenses
ĉ†_{i,+1}ĉ†_{i,−1} ≠ 0: the two CPs are not in the same quantum state.

Without the founder's ruling, the argument "opposite-bit CPs are distinguishable" has no
physical grounding in CPP. The ruling makes it a consequence of the DP arc mechanism.
