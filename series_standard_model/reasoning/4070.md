# Reasoning — Patch 4070 (the founder's question answered: where does the right-hand rule come from? From our definition of B, and nowhere else.)

**STATUS: verbatim, at patch.** Lane EW (40xx), 16 Sep 2026. Founder: *"the obedience to the right-hand rule
in B = v × E indicates that there is handedness arising from somewhere, and it is not
arbitrary/even/symmetric/cancellable; the cross product only obeys the right-hand rule, and never the
left-hand rule. Where does that come from? That is my question."* Scope: a computation answering a founder
physics question ⇒ **not exempt.**

## The answer, stated first

**The cross product does not "only obey the right-hand rule." It obeys whichever rule we define it to obey,
and the physics is identical either way** — because B is *produced* by one cross product (Biot–Savart) and
*consumed* by another (Lorentz). Every observable contains **two**, and the handedness cancels exactly.

Rebuilt magnetostatics end-to-end under the **left**-hand convention (B_L ≡ −(v × E), with Lorentz flipped to
match) and compared physical observables:

| observable | right-hand vs left-hand |
|---|---|
| force between two moving charges (5,000 random configs) | **identical, 0.000e+00** |
| force between two current elements (5,000 configs) | **identical, 0.000e+00** |
| force on each element of a current loop (5,000 configs) | **identical, 0.000e+00** |

Not "agree to numerical precision" — bit-identical, because the two sign flips multiply to +1.

## The decisive check: the handedness is *removable*

The magnetic force can be written with **no cross product anywhere**: F^i = q F^i_j v^j, where F_ij is the
antisymmetric field tensor — in 4D, the **bivector SF-6 already uses**. A bivector is an oriented *plane
element*, not an arrow; there is no hand to choose. Computed both ways, the force agrees to 7×10⁻¹⁵.

**A real asymmetry cannot be removed by a change of notation.** The fact that the identical physics is
expressible with an object that has no handedness proves the handedness was never in the physics. This is the
core of the answer to the founder's question.

## Why the arrow picture creates the illusion

B is an **axial** vector — a 3D bookkeeping device for an antisymmetric 2-tensor. The oriented plane of DP
rotation is the real object; representing it by an arrow perpendicular to that plane requires choosing which
of the two perpendicular directions to use, and *that* choice is the right-hand rule. The arrow points along
an axis the physics never singles out. In CPP's native 4D language the question does not even arise: SF-6's
polarization bivector carries the content directly, and there is no perpendicular arrow to orient.

**Control (essential):** the same machinery *does* detect a real parity flip — E·B = +0.35 becomes −0.35 under
a true mirror (E polar flips, B axial does not). So the nulls above are results, not blindness: the test
distinguishes a convention swap (no change) from a physical mirror (sign flip).

## An error I caught in-patch, recorded

My first version of the loop test compared the **torque vector** across conventions and it appeared to flip
(discrepancy 3.97×10²). That is not a valid test: torque is *itself* axial (it is r × F), so it flips with the
convention exactly as B does. I was comparing two pieces of notation and would have read it as physics. The
corrected test uses the **force on each current element**, which is polar and measurable. This is the fifth
time in this arc that an instrument returned a confidently wrong answer and a category check, not the number,
was what caught it.

## So: is the founder correct in his own restatement?

He asked directly: *"perhaps you are saying that B = v × B being handed is literally irrelevant because plus
always repels plus... and that the handed relationship we see in EM current flow is simply a totally different
category, one that has nothing to do with chirality and parity... Am I correct?"*

**Yes — with one sharpening.** It is not quite that EM handedness is "a different category" of the same kind of
thing. It is that **there is no EM handedness at all** to be in any category. The rule describes how we chose
to name a plane by an arrow. What is physical is the DP polarization plane and the forces; those are
mirror-symmetric, as 4069 established by the pseudoscalar test. The founder's instinct that the two are
different in kind is right; the sharper statement is that one of them does not exist as a physical asymmetry.

## And the founder's strategic hope, assessed honestly

He hoped: *"if we could develop a strategy that produces the right-hand rule for DP Arc motion, we might be
able to extend it to explain the parity violation in W⁰ catalytic reactions."*

**That route is closed, and it is worth being explicit about why, because the closure is informative.** A
derivation of the right-hand rule would derive a *convention*, and a convention cannot be extended into a
parity violation — there is no P-odd content in it to carry over. Worse, if such a derivation *did* produce
genuine P-odd content in EM, it would **contradict experiment** (4069: v·B and E·B vanish identically for a
moving charge; no P-odd EM effect has ever been observed).

He also floated the converse: *"perhaps the right-hand rule of EM relationships falls out of the handedness
and parity of the W⁰ interactions."* **Also no, and for the same reason in reverse:** weak parity violation is
real and P-odd; the EM rule is a convention with no P-odd content. A physical asymmetry cannot be the source of
a notational one — there would be nothing for it to fix. If weak interactions somehow *did* fix the sign of the
EM convention, that would be an observable P-odd EM effect, which is excluded.

**The one true thing in the neighbourhood:** parity violation is real, maximal, and confined to the weak
sector. The arc's repeated return to the W bracelet is not an accident of search strategy — it is where the
phenomenon actually lives.

interaction involved. That would be a discovery in physics at large, not in CPP. Submitted to the next window
