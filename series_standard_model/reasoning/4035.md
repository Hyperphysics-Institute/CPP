# Reasoning — Patch 4035 (the counterexample I asked for before asserting)

**STATUS: verbatim, at patch.**

## Closing the caveat, cheaply

4034 tested only translation sets drawn from the motif's own vertex directions and flagged that a general
set might differ. The caveat closes without more testing: **for z = 12 exactly at a vertex, no other copy's
vertex may lie within e — its own motif already supplies twelve.** So copies cannot interpenetrate at the
edge scale whatever the translations are, and **the translation set drops out of the problem entirely.**
What remains is a point set with minimum distance e and exactly twelve neighbours at e.

## And then I nearly shipped a false step

The draft continued: *so it reproduces the 600-cell's local structure, which carries the 7.356° deficit, so
flat space forbids it.*

**False.** FCC has z = 12 **exactly**, in **flat** space, and it **tiles** — verified here: every interior
site at twelve, largest hole 0.9609 against a nearest-neighbour distance of 1.4142.

It is the most standard counterexample there is, and I would have written past it if I had not made a habit
today of asking for one before asserting. That habit has now caught four things.

## What the counterexample teaches

FCC shares **six** neighbours per edge — four tetrahedra plus four octahedra, closing 360° exactly. The
600-cell shares **five**, giving 352.644° and the 7.356° deficit. And FCC's coordination is
**cuboctahedral**: cos-angles −1, −0.5, 0, +0.5, against the icosahedron's −1, −0.4472, +0.4472.

**So the obstruction is "twelve *icosahedrally*", not "twelve".** SF-4's own words are *12 nearest neighbors
arranged icosahedrally*. **The adjective is doing all the work, and nothing in this arc had isolated it
until the counterexample forced it.**

## The dilemma has three horns

- **(A)** give up **tiling** — keep icosahedral z = 12, accept holes or curvature
- **(B)** give up **exactly-twelve** — accept the quasicrystal's 13/18/19/26
- **(C)** give up **icosahedral** — keep twelve *and* tiling. **That is FCC/HCP.**

4034 posed this as a two-way choice and it was wrong to. I had the two horns that hurt and missed the one
that does not.

## And (C) is already in use

Patch 2685's four z = 12 arenas are FCC ball, HCP ball, random-stacking Barlow ball and FCC-cubic. Patch
3133's sub-PSR cascade is an FCC lattice. Both were labelled **proxies**, and 4012 and 4020 found them and
filed them as precedent without my understanding why they worked.

**They work because they sit on the survivable horn.** The DM lane picked it before the fork had been
drawn — which is worth saying plainly, since I spent 4012 and 4020 treating their choice as a convenience
rather than as the constraint being satisfied.

## What (C) costs, and who decides

The adjective. SF-4, SM-1 and SS-1 all say **icosahedral**, and the 600-cell's vertex figure is what the
geometry programme is built on.

**But whether the mass ladder needs the arrangement or only the count is a question about SM's own
derivations** — and 4016 established that only the **count** enters Σm_ν ∝ z⁻⁹. If the arrangement is
load-bearing somewhere, (C) is expensive; if only the count is, (C) costs a word.

That is a reading of existing derivations, not a physics judgement, and it is raised with the FCC
counterexample attached so it can be settled rather than re-argued.

No panel. No verdict moved. SR-1 and SF-4 both unrevised.
