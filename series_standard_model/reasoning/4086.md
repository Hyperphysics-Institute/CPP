## The test, and the failure

The idea: the weak interaction is special because it **captures** a CP (SF-2 §5) and so reads that CP's own
register, whereas EM acts through DP-sea polarisation (SF-6) and reads only displacement magnitudes. If true,
linearity in b would follow from capture, and F2 and F3 would become consequences instead of clauses.

**It fails on the strong sector.** SF-2's own framework confines quarks and constructs the Z as an icosahedral
cage and the H as a dodecahedral cage — I verified the underlying shell structure independently here (12 vertices
at the first shell, 20 at the second, no shell between them, matching Theorems 4.2–4.4). **Confinement is
capture.** So the criterion separates EM from the other two, but not weak from strong — which is precisely the
separation F3 needs.

**The refinement does not rescue it.** What is genuinely unique to the weak interaction is that it *transmutes*
the captured particle, where confinement merely binds. But "only transmuting processes read the bit" names the
weak interaction instead of explaining it. It is the same stipulation in different words, and I would rather say
so than dress it up.

## The comparison that puts the remaining clauses in proportion

This is the part I think matters most for the founder's decision, and it cuts both ways:

- **F2/F3 — no worse than the Standard Model.** That only the weak interaction violates parity is *not derived*
  in the SM either. It is the assignment of SU(2) to left-handed doublets: a choice of representation. CPP
  stipulating the same thing is not a defect relative to the SM; it is the same stipulation in a different
  vocabulary.
- **F5 — worse than CPP's own standard.** The SM does not predict the Jarlskog invariant either; it is a free
  parameter. But CPP's whole claim is zero free parameters. **So leaving J unexplained is a gap by CPP's own
  standard**, not by the SM's — and that is the one place the axiom currently falls short of the programme's
  advertised bar.
