## On the ruling

He is right and the argument is better than the one I was using.

I handed H1's follow-on up because the answer would be convenient to me. That reasoning has a hole in it:
**handing over does not remove the bias, it removes the context** — and every retraction this session
(4004, 4008, 4012, 4016's correction, 4020's) came from having **too little** context. Not one came from

The right remedy for bias is the one the corpus already uses for wins: **finish, state it, submit it for
critique.** A completed argument reviewed with fresh eyes is a stronger check than an incomplete one handed
over with an apology — and it does not cost a whole window's re-bootup to get there.

Enacted as PD-008. What it does not license is stated in the ruling file: it is not permission to decide a

## The answer

**Step 1 — which reflection does a transfer matrix need?** A TM along a direction *d* is built from
reflection in a hyperplane **perpendicular** to *d*, and that reflection **flips** *d*. So the TM exists
**transverse to n̂**, where 4022 measured RP to hold, and **not along n̂**, where it fails.

That is the right shape for a NESS and it is worth pausing on: along the drive there is no equilibrium
reflection structure, transverse to it there is. The RP failure is not a defect of the measure; it is the
drive.

**Step 2 — which reflection implements VW-1's ℤ₂?** A parity, generated by the n̂-**fixing**
Θ = diag(1,1,1,−1). Verified rather than assumed: η(ΘX) = −η(X) **exactly**, 6/6 seeds at three amplitudes.

**Step 3 — operational RP.** The Schwarz bound, tested directly: **0/4000** violations n̂-fixing at both δ;
**2039/4000** and **2379/4000** n̂-flipping. The Gram-matrix result of 4022 reproduced by the inequality
that actually does the work.

**Step 4 — the verdict.** VW-1's chain survives restricted RP **where it is needed**, because the ℤ₂ is
generated by an n̂-fixing reflection and RP holds for exactly those. **The reflections where RP fails
generate the T-face, not the P-face, and VW-1 makes no claim about T.** The failure is outside its scope
rather than inside it.

## The convenient branch, marked

That last sentence is the convenient one. "Outside the scope" is exactly what a worker hoping the theorem
survives would want to be true, and I want the next window to attack it there first. Two specific places
to push: (a) whether the OS construction needs RP for a *generating set* of reflections rather than for the
one that implements the symmetry; (b) whether the T-face and P-face are as cleanly separable on a NESS as
0973's P-even/T-odd split suggests.

## The step I did not close

VW-1's chain needs the **infinite-volume** limit. The TM transverse to n̂ controls transverse clustering;
**along n̂ there is no TM, so clustering along the drive is uncontrolled by this argument.**

On the finite substrate this does not bite: ⟨η⟩ = 0 exactly, no limit is needed, VW-1 holds outright. On
the extended lattice the founder ruled in at 4009, it is open. Named rather than absorbed.

## The defect found in passing, and the check that caught me

0813's η is *sign det of the four highest-n̂-projection neighbours*. On the unperturbed lattice the 4th and
5th projections **tie at every vertex**, so "top 4" is arbitrary and swapping two tied rows flips the
determinant. **η is ill-defined there.**

My draft of that check said the degeneracy was total — all twelve projections equal at every vertex —
generalised from vertex 0, which is a pole and is not typical. I had written the assertion at the strength
of the claim, and it **failed**, which is the only reason the patch says 2/120 for the total case and
120/120 for the boundary tie. That is the same over-generalising error this session has cost five
retractions to, caught this time by a check I wrote against myself.

**No result is invalidated.** 0813 and 4005 both evaluate η on perturbed configurations where the tie is
broken. The gap is in the definition's *statement*, which never says a perturbation is required.

No panel. No verdict moved.
