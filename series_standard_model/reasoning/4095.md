## 1. Attack on §2q — the "Assumed" column is short by one row, and the row can be derived

§2q: a response linear in b = sign(ω·v) "gives emitted particles a net helicity … 0.249, 0.498, 1.000 at
couplings 0.25, 0.5, 1.0 — saturating at maximal V−A." So net helicity = c, a constant, and "maximal V−A" is
read as c = 1.

**D-1 search first.** `grep -i "v/c|at rest|Lorentz|boost|massless"` on the maturation document: the only hit is
4082's v/c. The arc never asked what happens at β < 1. But the corpus has: `chirality_continuum.tex` (CONT-2,
shipped) prints P_L^helicity(v) = ½(1+v), P_R = ½(1−v), imported there from textbook V−A kinematics. That is a net
polarisation of −v, not −1. The longitudinal polarisation of beta electrons has been measured as −v/c since 1957.

So constant c fails at every β < 1 (script H1, column 3: −1.000 at β = 0.1 against an observed −0.100). The
coupling that reproduces data is **c = β of the emitted particle** — and "linear in b with a universal sign"
does not say that. §2q's Assumed column therefore needs a third row: *the functional form of the coefficient*.

**Can the third row be derived instead of assumed?** The write rule b = sign(ω·v) uses the *mean* velocity v.
The substrate has no access to a mean velocity. A PCD cycle knows one thing about motion: the displacement
made this Moment. On a lattice with one l_P budget per Moment, a CP drifting at β makes a net longitudinal
hop in a fraction β of Moments — that is what "speed β" *means* on the lattice (SR-1: "bulk velocity consumes
part of the displacement budget"). In those Moments sign(ω·Δx) is defined and equals the helicity h; in the
other 1−β it is zero and the bit is blind. A response linear in the bit, at **full** strength, then weights

  P(h) = β·(1−h)/2 + (1−β)·½ = (1 − hβ)/2,

which is the left-chirality projection and is CONT-2's equation. Script H1, column 4: worst deviation 0.0029
from −β across β = 0.1 … 1.0. Robustness: the result needs only that the b-readable fraction of Moments is β;
it does not care whether the unreadable Moments are transverse hops, backward-forward cancelling pairs, or
ZBW dwell. Both microscopic pictures give the same number.

What this buys. (a) **Maximality is no longer a free strength.** F4 read "maximality not derived (4083)": with
the per-Moment rule the response is all-or-nothing in each readable Moment, and the observed sub-maximal
polarisation of slow leptons is *kinematic dilution*, not a weaker coupling. There is no c to tune. (b) The
helicity-vs-chirality distinction of the Standard Model — which the arc never mentions — appears on its own:
"chirality" is the per-Moment bit, "helicity" is its time average, and mass (β < 1) is what separates them.
(c) CONT-2's imported textbook formula acquires a substrate origin.

What it does not buy. **The sign.** Both hands remain available, as at every step since 4046. This is a fifth
leg *tightened*, not the missing leg supplied.

**Label:** the per-Moment write is a PCD-level choice made under PD-006/PD-007 — carried as **[PCD-EXT]**.
**Convenient branch — marked.** This finding favours χ₄: an audit that was asked to attack the candidate has

Nexus frame, it coincides in every frame and nothing is observable. If it deviates — in particular if the
