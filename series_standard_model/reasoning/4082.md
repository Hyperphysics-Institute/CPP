## Does it make sense? Yes — and it is testable, which is why it settles the matter

His answer: the captured charge is held for a few Moments, its momentum is absorbed by the bracelet, and on
dissolution the momentum is dispersed randomly rather than reconstituted on the charge. **As physics this is
sound**, and it does the work he intended: the charge's direction is not sitting at the decision point as a free
vector.

**But absorbed momentum does not disappear.** Conservation puts it into the bracelet's **bulk motion**. And CPP
has an **absolute frame** — DI-bits propagate at c in the Nexus frame — so bulk motion is visible at substrate
level: a moving ring sees direction-dependent arrival timing at order v/c. The residual asymmetry is therefore

  **ε ≈ p_captured / M_W**

which is the founder's own suppression mechanism (the mass ratio), quantified rather than assumed.

## What the numbers do

ε runs from 1.2×10⁻⁵ (nuclear beta decay) to 0.50 (top decay / on-shell W). Ties survive only while ε < 2⁻ᵇ, so
each process caps the register depth: beta decay allows b < 16.3, muon decay b < 10.6, top decay **b < 1.0**.
Since V−A is observed maximal in *all* of them, the binding ceiling is **b < 1 bit**.

EM safety pulls the other way and needs b > 36.5 (atomic parity violation), b > 19.9 (a 10⁻⁶ bound), or **b > 10.0
even on an absurdly generous 10⁻³ bound**.

**The window is empty by 9 bits at best and 35.5 bits realistically.**

## The refutation is structural, which matters more than the arithmetic

V−A is maximal across **five orders of magnitude** in captured momentum. Any mechanism resting on exact degeneracy
must weaken as the perturbation grows — so **K3 predicts parity violation should fade at high momentum. It does
not.** No choice of register depth rescues that, because the prediction is about the *trend*, not the level.

**K3 is refuted as a universal mechanism**, and with it the whole tie-break family: ties are fragile by
construction and V−A is robust by observation.
