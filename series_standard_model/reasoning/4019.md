# Reasoning — Patch 4019 (five arguments, one cause — and a question I had been asking wrongly)

**STATUS: verbatim, at patch.**

## Why I stopped closing classes

4009, 4013, 4017 and 4018 closed five construction classes by five arguments with nothing in common — a
dihedral angle, a window boundary, a Gram signature, a central asymmetry, an irrational trace. The obvious
next move was a sixth class.

Five unrelated arguments reaching the same answer usually means **one cause wearing five costumes**, and
finding it is worth more than a sixth closure. So I looked for it instead.

## The cause

Counted from the graph rather than quoted: f-vector (120, 720, 1200, 600), and **5 tetrahedra around every
edge** (6C/E). The regular tetrahedron's dihedral angle is **arccos(1/3) = 70.5288°**. Five of them:

    5 × 70.5288° = 352.644°   against the 360.000° a flat structure requires
    deficit = 7.356° per edge      360/θ = 5.1043, not an integer

**A positive angular deficit is positive curvature.** The 600-cell is intrinsically spherical. It lives on
S³ because it cannot do anything else, not because someone chose to put it there.

Every one of the five closures is that fact in local dialect. A flat periodic tiling fails because the
angles do not close; a cut-and-project window fails at its boundary; a Bravais lattice fails because the
neighbour set leans inward; H₄ fails to be crystallographic because φ is irrational — and φ is in the
tetrahedral packing in the first place. **One frustration, five vocabularies.**

It is also the 4D form of the classic icosahedral frustration, and **the same number**: five regular
tetrahedra leave 7.356° around an edge in 3D too. That is why icosahedral short-range order is everywhere
in real matter and no icosahedral crystal exists. This is a known piece of condensed-matter physics arriving
at the substrate question from outside.

## The part that is a correction to me

I have been putting the same question to the founder since 4010: strict reading or weak reading — does
**every** GP have a 600-cell around it, or only some, with environments differing?

**That was the wrong axis.** The deficit does not care how many GPs have a 600-cell. It cares whether the
600-cell is **regular**. Those are independent questions and I had them fused. The real choice is:

- **(A) Flat space, distorted cage.** Keep z = 12 and near-icosahedral local order; give up the icosahedron
  being exactly regular; the 7.356° absorbs as strain. This is what real icosahedral matter does, and real
  icosahedral quasicrystals are the existence proof that it is constructible. **Open, and the survivor.**
- **(B) Exact cage, curved space.** Keep the regular 600-cell and pay the curvature. **Priced at 4017 and
  closed**: the only regular honeycomb of 600-cells is hyperbolic and costs z = ∞.

Six patches of referral were putting a narrower question than the material warranted. The founder has been
answering a question about **distribution** when the live one is about **rigidity**.

## What I am not claiming

The deficit forbids **exact** regularity in flat space. It says nothing whatever against a **distorted**
cage — and a distorted cage may be the right physics rather than a concession, since it is what nature does
with icosahedral order.

This patch **explains** five closures; it does not add a sixth, and the two classes 4018 left open are
still open. Nothing here decides anything: whether the substrate's cage is exactly regular is a
physical-picture question, and it is asked rather than answered.

No panel. No verdict moved. SF-4 unrevised.
