## ω exists — the write rule survives

SPIN-1 derives spin from an unpaired −eCP **moving** through the DP Sea and capturing a DP. So a seeding CP has a
spin axis ω (the captured DP's orbital axis) *and* a velocity v (the capture requires the motion). **Helicity
b = sign(ω·v) is well defined**, and 4076's B2 repair of the founder's spin bit stands.

## The orientation is unspecified — and that is necessary, not a gap

I checked SPIN-1 for any rule fixing the orbital plane relative to v: there is none (its only orientation
discussion is the DP's internal phase lock; qDP planes are deferred). **That silence is required.** If ω were
always parallel to v, every particle would have the same helicity and there would be no spin-up/down. If always
perpendicular, helicity would vanish identically and there would be no weak coupling. **Only a free orientation
works — and a free orientation is exactly what makes ω the spin state and b the particle's physical helicity.**

Measured: ⟨b⟩ = −0.0014 over an unpolarised ensemble — the substrate carries no net handedness, which is what
CAPACITY-1's V3 says — while each particle has a definite b. A response linear in b gives the decayed population a
net helicity of 1.000 at coupling 1 from an *unpolarised* source; an even response gives zero at every coupling.
That linear case is the observed fact that beta electrons are longitudinally polarised from unpolarised nuclei.

My first J2 compared population counts between b > 0 and b < 0 groups, which measures the imbalance of the

conclusion was read. Eighth and ninth instances in this arc of a check that had to be repaired before it could
be believed.
