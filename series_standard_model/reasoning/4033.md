# Reasoning — Patch 4033 (twenty-five patches, and the object was never specified)

**STATUS: verbatim, at patch.**

## Trying to bound the error

4032 left one item: nobody has bounded the error between SR-1's stated approximation and SM's exact use of
the same structure. PD-008 says bound it.

The first step was to build the thing SR-1 describes. And that is where it stopped, because **I had never
built what SR-1 actually names.** Its construction is *"modular repetition of 600-cell motifs with
overlapping Voronoi cells."* That is **not cut-and-project** — which is what 4010, 4013, 4015, 4026 and
4030 all tested. Cut-and-project projects a higher-dimensional lattice through a window; modular repetition
unions translated copies of a motif. Different objects.

Seventh instance today of building on my own reading instead of the corpus's, and the one with the most
work stacked on top of it.

## So I built it, and the reading broke

Most literal reading available, labelled as mine: translate the 120-vertex motif by t·u for u in the motif,
union the copies, overlap when t < 2.

**No t gives anything like uniform z = 12 — best 2.7% of sites. And at several t the nearest-neighbour
distance is 0.0000: coincident points.**

A point set with coincident points is not a lattice, and SR-1 plainly does not intend one. **So the reading
is wrong — and that is the finding, not that the construction fails.** I want that distinction kept, because
the tempting write-up is "SR-1's construction doesn't work", and what I actually have is "my reading of one
sentence doesn't work."

## The patch

*Modular repetition of 600-cell motifs with overlapping Voronoi cells* names a **family**, not a recipe. It
fixes **neither the translation set, nor the overlap rule, nor what happens where motifs collide.**

**So the error term cannot be bounded until the construction is specified.** You cannot measure the
deviation of an unbuilt object from an exact requirement.

## What this says about the last twenty-five patches

Every lattice in this arc — the icosian cut-and-project at 4010, 4013, 4015 and 4030, the anisotropic patch
at 4026, and this one — **was my guess at what the corpus means.**

Five construction classes closed. A founder ruling obtained. A target sharpened twice, then tightened again
at 4030. Real results, and I do not think they were wasted: the closures are true of the classes they name,
and 4017's z = ∞ pricing kills a branch regardless of which construction is intended.

But underneath all of it: **the lateral lattice was never unbuilt because it is hard. It was unbuilt
because it was never specified.** Twenty-five patches of testing candidates, when the object had no
definition to test against.

That is worth more than any single closure, and it is not a comfortable thing to write.

## What is needed, and it is small

**(i)** SR-1's construction written out to build level — translation set, overlap rule, collision handling;
or **(ii)** a founder ruling that some named standard construction *is* the intended one.

Either way it becomes an afternoon's work. **The blocker is one sentence in a shipped paper, and it is a
specification gap, not a physics gap** — which is a far better place to be than where this arc has been.

## Not claimed

Not that the approximation is bad. **Unbuilt is not wrong**, and I have no measurement either way. My
literal reading is filed as **refuted** — evidence about the sentence's ambiguity, nothing more.

No panel. No verdict moved. SR-1 and SF-4 both unrevised.
