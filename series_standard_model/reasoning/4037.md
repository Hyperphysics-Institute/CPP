# Reasoning — Patch 4037 (what is most likely wrong)

**STATUS: verbatim, at patch.**

## His argument, and where I agree

*The masses all seem to work properly, and they are most likely correct because their predictions are
accurate and correspond to reality.*

I agree with the conclusion. What does not go through is the inference from it — *therefore the wrong thing
is elsewhere* — because it assumes the mass successes are evidence about the geometry. They mostly are not,
and that is checkable rather than arguable.

## The strongest prediction uses none of the geometry in dispute

*icosahedr* appears **0 times in SF-1**. Two in SF-3, twenty-two in SF-4, twelve in SM-2.

SF-1 is the cleanest thing the programme has: **Koide K = 2/3, θ = 132.731°, m_μ and m_τ**, from *"one
calibration (m_e) and zero shape parameters."* Its geometry is a **three-vertex colour triangle**, with the
three charged leptons as *"stationary occupation patterns of a single three-vertex colour cage."*

**A triangle embeds in any lattice.** 4036's crystallographic obstruction is about regular *icosahedra* on
lattice sites; it does not reach a triangle.

## And the table that does use the cages computes nothing

4002 is the hinge, and it is worth restating rather than citing: SM-2's Mass Contribution Breakdown is a
**fixed-fraction partition of the calibrated PDG total** — divide the W row by 80380 and it is 1/2, 1/6, 0,
1/20, 0, 17/60, summing to one. All twelve rows do this. Nothing about a particle's constituents enters the
arithmetic of its row.

**So the cage column labels rows. It does not generate numbers.** The numbers came from experiment; the
cages were written beside them. **A table constructed that way cannot be evidence for the taxonomy written
into it.**

## So the two things are disjoint

SF-4 does use z — but 4016 showed only the **count** enters (M₀ ∝ z, σ_ν ∝ z⁻¹⁰), not the arrangement.

Which means: the only place the icosahedral *arrangement* is load-bearing is a table that computes nothing.
That is precisely why *the masses work* is compatible with the substrate geometry being wrong, unspecified,
or merely local. The two are not in contact.

## What I think is most likely wrong

**The global lattice construction — and most likely wrong because nothing in the mass sector ever needed
it.**

Everything doing load-bearing work in SM is **local**: a three-vertex triangle, a count of twelve, a
first-shell distance ratio giving χ = φ⁻³. None requires a global 600-cell tiling of flat ℝ⁴.

And that also explains 4033: **an unused commitment never gets pinned down.** The construction was not left
vague through oversight; it was left vague because nothing downstream ever pulled on it hard enough to
force a definition.

**SR-1 does pull on it** — its W2 world-call rests on the substrate's symmetry class. So the conflict this
arc has spent thirty patches on is between SR's **global** commitment and SM's **local** ones, and the
local ones are satisfiable in many global structures.

## Why this is the opposite of despair

If the global construction is unused by the mass sector, it is **free to be chosen to satisfy SR alone**,
and 4036's three-horned dilemma becomes SR's problem rather than the mass programme's. The thing that looked
like a foundational contradiction may be an over-commitment in one sector that nothing else depends on.

## And it is a hypothesis, stated as one

It is consistent with everything measured from 4002 to 4036, and it **predicts something checkable: no SM
result changes if the global tiling is replaced, provided the local structures survive.**

**That is not tested.** Testing it is the next piece of work, and it would settle this rather than leave it
as a good story — which is the distinction this session has spent all day learning to keep.

No panel. No verdict moved. No paper revised.
