## Why the note is worth its line

The founder's reason was the right one: *"it lets the next context window know that it is a starting place
for examination… at least we don't have to reproduce all the work you just did."*

Sixty-one patches produced one sentence that a successor needs and would not otherwise find: the cage is
not required to be regular, and the strain it carries has a **number**.

## The three guards

A note that said only *"slight irregularity is permitted"* would be worse than no note, because every later
mismatch could be filed under it. So the note carries:

- **the 7.356° itself** — derived, not chosen; forced arithmetic given icosahedral local structure and flat
  space, so it licenses a *specific* strain rather than irregularity in general
- **that z = 12 is inside A2** — so a reader knows which of the three horns would actually amend the axiom
  and which would not
- **the open question** — A2's parenthetical is one 600-cell's f-vector while its verb is *tessellated*, and
  no construction meeting that word has been exhibited

The third is the one I most want a successor to see, because it is the thing that cost 4009–4034 and it is
still unresolved.

## A formatting fact worth recording

The first draft put the note **inside** the axiom table. A paragraph in a markdown table splits it into two
tables, and A3′ through A11 would have rendered as a separate block. Moved below the table and the table
verified unbroken.

Small, but the registry is the most-read file in the corpus.
