## Why 4051–4054 failed, in one sentence

Steinberg's theorem: in a finite reflection group the stabiliser of a point is generated by the reflections that
fix it. So an H4⁺-orbit of a point lying on **any** mirror hyperplane is the full H4-orbit — achiral, a congruent
copy of an achiral object. Every 600-cell vertex, edge midpoint, face centre and cell centre lies on a mirror.
4051's "chiral shell" (a left-icosian orbit, i.e. the vertices themselves), 4053's retraction, and 4054's
conjugation lift all seeded from special points. They could not have produced a chiral orbit. **The only chiral
H4⁺-orbits are those of points on no mirror, and those have 7200 points.** 4053 wrote the correct target ("an
orbit of the rotation half that is not an orbit of H4 itself") and marked it "not attempted." This is that attempt.

## The construction

U = (600-cell, radius 1) ∪ (H4⁺·p, radius 1.7) for generic p. Direct 4D analogue of 4041's 3D cluster
(icosahedron + generic I-orbit). The decoration sits at radius 1.7 so that no decoration point comes within 1/φ
of a core vertex; the core keeps its exact 12-neighbour first shell.

## What the script establishes (`code/4063_chiral_4d_cluster_z12.py`, float64, all quantities O(1))

- **C1** H4 built as 4×4 matrices from its 60 root reflections: 14,400 elements, 7,200 proper, 7,200 improper.
- **C2** The generic orbit has 7,200 distinct points and **no improper element of H4 preserves it** — checked
  against all 7,200. Since any isometry of U preserves the radius-1 shell (the 600-cell) it lies in H4, so the
  symmetry group of U is **exactly H4⁺** and U is chiral. Exhaustive, not sampled.
- **C3** Controls: orbits of a vertex (120 pts) and an edge midpoint (720 pts) are preserved by all 7,200
  improper elements — the search finds mirrors when they exist.
- **C4** Every one of the 120 core vertices has first shell = exactly 12 points at 1/φ, affine span a 3-flat.
  Nearest decoration point at 0.8205.
- **C5** A shell-complete pseudoscalar χ = +43.04 on U's decoration, −43.04 on its mirror image (sum 0 to
  10⁻¹⁵), and 3.8×10⁻¹⁴ on the mirror-point control. A "3 nearest neighbours" functional is **not** well
  defined on a degenerate shell and gave a spurious −12 on the control before I placed the cutoff in a
  distance gap — the 4040 tie-break trap, met and avoided in the same patch.

**Result.** 4054's "gap is dimensional" is closed at cluster scale: a chiral 4D point set with icosahedral
(H4⁺) symmetry, z = 12 on every core vertex, exists, is proved chiral by exhaustive group count, and carries a
mirror-odd invariant resolved 10¹⁵ above float. The minimum decoration is 7,200 points per shell — a theorem,
not a design choice.

## What this does not claim

- **Not a lattice.** U is a finite cluster, exactly as 4041 was in 3D. The global flat-R⁴ tiling question
  (4019's 7.356° deficit; 4030/4034's "no translation both tiles and keeps z = 12") is untouched and is, per
  4037, the least-tested foundation of CPP. Chirality and tiling are separate obstructions; this patch removes
  only the first.
- **Not V−A.** 4048's ~2% cap on structural helicity bias was measured on the 3D cluster; a 4D helicity
  measurement on U is a bounded next computation, and 4054's second requirement ("a graph dense enough to
  resolve it") is now met — 7,200 decoration points give ~10⁵ short paths, not ~120. Registered as the next
  EW item, not done here.
- **No verdict moves.** FI-C-9 = V3 (CAPACITY-1) says the substrate does not *spontaneously* become chiral.
  U is *explicitly* chiral by construction: the generic point p is a choice, and a chiral U requires that choice.
  That is exactly the corpus's V3 picture — a handedness supplied as a primitive — now with a concrete 4D
  object that could carry it. Whether the substrate *is* U-like is a physics question (founder), not this patch's.

## Convenient branch — marked

**This is the convenient branch:** it lands a positive result on a lane that had three consecutive
non-landings, and it lands cheaply. I checked the two ways it could be hollow: (i) the orbit could secretly be
an H4-orbit — ruled out by the exhaustive count and by Steinberg (generic p has trivial stabiliser); (ii) the
symmetry group of U could be larger than H4 — ruled out because it must preserve the 600-cell shell. What would
overturn it: a demonstration that the 7,200-point decoration cannot be realised by the substrate's constituents
(CPs/DPs per GP) — a density argument, not a symmetry one. Submitted to the next window for critique.
