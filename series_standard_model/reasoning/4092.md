# Reasoning — Patch 4092 (the capture supplies only one plane; the second comes from motion, and that selects helicity)

**STATUS: verbatim, at patch.** Lane EW (40xx), 17 Sep 2026. Founder: *"please proceed."* The bounded question
from 4091: which of the 60 orthogonal plane pairs does a captured DP occupy? Scope: computation + a structural
result ⇒ **not exempt.**

## The capture cannot select a pair

SPIN-1's inner CP (r_in) and outer CP (2 r_in) orbit the **same** central −eCP about a **common axis** — coplanar
(4090). **A single-DP capture gives one plane, not two.** So the capture geometry cannot pick an orthogonal pair
by itself, and 4091's 60 pairs are not selected by it.

## And the obvious fix runs backwards, which is what makes this informative

The natural repair is a **second captured DP** supplying the second plane. **SPIN-1 assigns single-DP capture to
fermions and double-DP capture to bosons.** So if handedness required two captured DPs, **only bosons could be
handed and fermions could not** — exactly backwards, since helicity and V−A act on fermions. **The second plane
cannot come from a second DP.**

## It comes from motion — and that is 4089's leg, now forced rather than chosen

For a moving fermion: **plane 1** is the captured-DP orbit (the spin plane); **plane 2** is the
(fourth-axis, direction-of-travel) rotation produced by its motion through the sea. This is not a new postulate;
4091 showed the fourth-axis leg is dimensionally unavoidable, and U2 here shows the only alternative source is
excluded by SPIN-1's own fermion/boson assignment.

## What the geometry then selects

The pseudoscalar comes out **exactly 2·cos(angle between spin axis and direction of travel)**: +2.000000 at 0°,
+1.732051 at 30°, +1.000000 at 60°, 0.000000 at 90°, −2.000000 at 180°.

**It is blind to transverse spin and tracks only the component along the motion. That is helicity — selected by
the geometry rather than postulated.** The arc has been *assuming* helicity was the right variable since 4076;
this is the first patch in which the geometry picks it out on its own.

## An error caught in-patch

My first version built the spin plane from an arbitrary orthonormal basis. Its **orientation jumps
discontinuously** with the angle, so 0° and 180° both came out +2.000000 — a spurious equality that would have
destroyed the sign structure. The spin bivector must be oriented by the spin axis itself (its 3-space dual).
Fixed before any conclusion was read; tenth instance in this arc of a check needing repair before it could be
believed.

## Convenient branch — marked

**Convenient, and checked accordingly.** "The geometry selects helicity" is the most satisfying sentence the arc
has produced, and it vindicates a choice made five patches earlier. Against that: the result is a projection
identity, not a dynamical derivation — it says *if* the two planes are the spin orbit and the motion leg, the
invariant is the helicity component, which is close to a restatement of 4074's B∧B = 2e·ω in physical clothing.
And **both hands remain available** (0° and 180° give ±2.000000): the geometry selects the **quantity**, never
the **sign** — unchanged since 4046.
