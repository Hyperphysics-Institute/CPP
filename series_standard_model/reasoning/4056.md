## The one place left

4055 closed the product hierarchy and named the non-product class — repeated indices, functions of sums —
as **where a counterexample to RP would have to live if one exists.** I looked there. It exists.

## The algebra is exact and p-independent

n_i² = (n_i)₂ + n_i, so for i ≠ j under multinomial(K, p):

    E[n_i² n_j²] = K⁽⁴⁾p_i²p_j² + K⁽³⁾(p_i²p_j + p_i p_j²) + K⁽²⁾p_i p_j

For F = Σ w_a n_a² on the + side, ⟨Θ(F)F⟩ = K⁽⁴⁾s² + 2K⁽³⁾st + K⁽²⁾t², with s = Σw_a p_a² and
t = Σw_a p_a. **PSD iff K⁽⁴⁾K⁽²⁾ − (K⁽³⁾)² ≥ 0.**

That quantity is **−K²(K−1)²(K−2)** — negative for every K ≥ 3, **and independent of p.**

**The indefiniteness sits in the multinomial itself**, not in the measure. No choice of tilt could have
avoided it, which is why this was never going to be found by varying δ on the single-site class.

## And it bites in the sector that was supposed to be safe

Gram minimum eigenvalue, n̂-**fixing**: δ = 0 → PSD; **δ = 0.10 → −5.44 × 10⁻³; δ = 0.35 → −4.74 × 10⁻².**

δ = 0 is PSD for a specific and instructive reason: there the measure is **uniform**, so p² is **parallel**
to p, s and t are proportional, and the indefinite 2×2 form is **never probed**. The tilt is what makes p²
and p independent and opens the negative direction.

**Explicit witness**, so this is not an artifact of symmetrising a Gram: a single constructed F gives
**⟨Θ(F)F⟩ = −2.02 × 10².** RP demands non-negativity for every F on one side; one F refutes it.
