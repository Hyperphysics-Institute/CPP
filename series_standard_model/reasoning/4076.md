## What I think — the honest assessment

**The spin bit, as defined, does not give chirality.** A ±1 value attached to an address is a scalar; parity
relocates the address and leaves the value unchanged. "The direction of the spin, up or down on the fourth axis"
is parity-**even** — the same class as polarity, which 4071 already showed cannot rescue a deficient set. I want
this stated first because it is the founder's stated purpose for the idea.

**But the repair is one line, and it keeps the entire proposal.** If the bit is *written* with the seeding CP's
**helicity** — b = sign(ω·v), its 3D spin axis projected on its direction of motion — then b is parity-odd
(20,000/20,000 draws flip). The register, the DI-bit transport, the majority rule at each GP, the seeding by an
unpaired CP: all unchanged. Only the rule that sets the bit changes, from "which way it spins" to "which way it
spins *relative to where it is going*." Helicity needs no new variable — 3D spin comes from SPIN-1's captured-DP
orbit, and velocity the CP already has.

**And this answers the founder's own question — "what response does the bit dictate?" — structurally.** A rate
R(b) yields parity violation in observables **iff R depends on b linearly.** An even response (R ∝ b²) gives
exactly zero asymmetry even when b is P-odd. So the response must be odd in the bit. That is not a free choice: it
is forced if the bit is to mean anything for parity.

**And it lands exactly on 4075's requirement, in the founder's own variable.** 4075 concluded: EM must read the
fourth-axis component through even functions, the handed process must read it linearly. B3 is that statement with
"fourth-axis component" replaced by "spin bit." **The spin bit is a concrete implementation of χ₄'s carrier —
with propagation machinery the corpus already has.** That is a real advance: χ₄ was an abstract alignment rule;
the spin bit gives it a register, a messenger, and a write rule.

**The proposal's other result may be its bigger one, and it is not about chirality.** A ±1 register gives exactly
two slots per 3D location — the founder's "room for another orbital DP with the opposite spin bit in the same 3D
space." That is Pauli doubling. **It also revises a registered theorem's basis:** THEO-QM-10 derives Pauli
exclusion and spin-statistics from *one* CP per GP (via THEO-1). A spin bit gives two. Not a contradiction of
THEO-1 — that concerns co-occupation of the same point — but THEO-QM-10's derivation would have to be rewritten
in terms of (3D address, spin bit) pairs. Registered, not attempted here.
