## K2 — the founder's preference, tested on its own claim

**His claim is that helical packing fits without stretching. It is correct, and I verified it independently.**
Boerdijk–Coxeter stacking uses **perfectly regular** tetrahedra — edge-length spread 2.5×10⁻¹³ across 400 cells.
Against 4019's deficit (recomputed here from scratch: 5 × 70.5288° = 352.6439°, deficit **7.3561°**), the helix is
a genuine alternative to the 4020 ruling that permitted distorted cages. His instinct that this is preferable to
variable-position GPs is well founded: **it preserves exact cell geometry, which the distortion ruling sacrificed.**

**What it costs, which he should know before preferring it.** The twist per tetrahedron is arccos(−2/3) = 131.81°,
an irrational multiple of 2π, so the helix **never closes and never repeats** — no exact closure out to 10,000
tetrahedra. The corpus would be trading a *periodic* lattice for an *aperiodic* one. Every construction that assumes
a repeating GP address grid along that direction would need re-examination. That is not fatal — it may even be what
4030/4034 were pointing at when they found no translation both tiles and preserves z = 12 — but it is a large
change, and it is the opposite of a simple one.

**And two things it does not deliver.** A single helix gives **z = 6**, not 12: it is a 1D column, not a
space-filling lattice; bundling helices to fill 3-space at z = 12 is an open construction. And on chirality, both
hands are equally valid stackings (torsion ±283.55) — **the helix supplies a hand, not a choice of hand**, exactly
like the four routes before it.

**So my evaluation is: K2 is a strong lattice proposal and a weak chirality proposal.** It belongs to the
4019/4020/4034 thread, where it might genuinely resolve something, more than to this one.

## K3 — still the cheapest, and I still rank it first for chirality

No new variable; it specifies a rule the corpus left open; and degeneracy is where a binary rule gets the
all-or-nothing leverage V−A's maximality needs. **One dependency I should have named at 4077:** my simulation used
a reference direction, and in CPP that must be locally available and **3-dimensional** — an SSV gradient, say. It
cannot be n̂, which is the fourth axis and not a 3D vector. The bounded next test is whether the PCD cycle has
genuine degeneracies and what resolves them today.

## K4 — withdrawn, and the reason is one I should have seen at 4077

P-odd = (C-odd) × (T-odd) is a sound identity, and CPP has the T-arrow. But the C-odd element I offered was **the
matter excess** — and Sakharov's conditions say baryogenesis *requires* C and CP violation as an **input**. So
using the matter excess to produce parity violation assumes what it is supposed to explain. **K4 is circular as
stated.** It would need a C-odd element independent of baryogenesis, and none is on file. I offered it at 4077 as
"most valuable if it holds"; that was one patch of enthusiasm ahead of the check.
