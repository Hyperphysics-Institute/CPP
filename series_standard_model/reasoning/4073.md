## Before accepting "an axiom is unavoidable," I checked the one escape the answers left

SPIN-1 builds spin from a captured DP **orbiting** the CP. So composites rotate even though CPs do not. If
composites could be handed, no axiom would be needed.

**S1: they cannot — conditionally.** B∧B is a 4-form. Any sum of rotations confined to a 3-dimensional subspace
has B∧B ≡ 0, however many planes: verified exactly with up to five superposed rotations. So if CPP's physical
rotations all live in the 3-space perpendicular to n̂, no composite is ever handed. **The condition is real and I
am not hiding it:** it is F1a, where physical 3-space sits relative to n̂, which is still undefined.

**S2: what handedness actually needs.** B∧B = 2 e·ω exactly: the alignment of a 3D spin axis ω with the partner of
a rotation *into* the fourth axis. It is **helicity-shaped.** That is worth noting because helicity is precisely
what the weak force distinguishes — but I am recording it as a structural resemblance, not a derivation.

**S3: the substrate already holds both hands.** The 600-cell's vertices are the unit quaternions of the binary
icosahedral group. Left and right quaternion multiplication each preserve the 600-cell, carry opposite duality
(±0.789568), and are swapped by conjugation. **H4⁺ is already split into a left factor and a right factor.** The
axioms simply do not prefer one.

## Why S3 changes the decision

It opens a third location for the axiom that adds **no new variable** (L3): a rule that one of the two existing
factors governs a process — for example, the W⁰ catalytic step acting by left-isoclinic transformation only.
That is structurally how the Standard Model does it: V−A is a property of the *coupling*, not of the particles.
The founder's answers make new variables the heavy kind of addition; L3 avoids one.

**Its danger is specific and I am naming it:** a rule attached to the W⁰ step may be single-use, which is exactly
the founder's original worry. F8 — reaching K3 Δp_LR and baryogenesis — is the test that decides whether L3
survives. If it cannot reach them, L2 (a rotation into the fourth axis, the founder's "spin pole") is the fallback,
and its helicity-shaped pseudoscalar (S2) is the reason to prefer it over L1.
