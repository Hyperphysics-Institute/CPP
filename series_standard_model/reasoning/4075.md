# Reasoning — Patch 4075 (axiom maturation, step 4: χ₄ passes the electromagnetism filter — conditionally, and the condition is a sharp division of labour)

**STATUS: verbatim, at patch.** Lane EW (40xx), 16 Sep 2026. The founder applied 4074 (his message said "4070", a
typo — 4074 confirmed on origin). This patch runs F2, the filter I flagged as most dangerous. Scope: computation +

## Record check first

**SF-6 never specifies whether the DP displacement that constitutes E and B is three-dimensional or includes the
fourth axis.** No "3D", no "ℝ³", one mention of 4D (in the open-problem statement). So F2 was never going to be a
lookup. It is a choice the theory has not made, and χ₄ forces it.

## The result

χ₄ rotates a displacement component toward the fourth axis by s·θ. The 3-space remainder goes as cos(sθ) — even in
the hand s. The fourth-axis part goes as sin(sθ) — odd. **The handedness lives entirely on the fourth axis.**

- **Reading A** (EM reads 3-space only): both hands give **identical** fields, E·B = 1.3×10⁻¹⁴. EM parity-even.
- **Reading B** (4D proximity feeds the fourth-axis part back): the hands give different fields, E·B up to 95.7,
  and it **flips exactly with the hand** (correlation −1.0000). EM parity-odd — excluded by experiment.

**So χ₄ survives F2 if and only if EM reads the fourth axis only through even functions.** The founder's own
framing — "processed according to proximity" — is Reading B if proximity is 4D and unrestricted, which is why I
called this the dangerous filter. It does not kill χ₄; it constrains how χ₄ and SF-6 fit together.

## Why the condition is more than a patch-up

It gives a **division of labour**: electromagnetism reads physical 3-space; the handed process reads the fourth
axis, linearly. That is worth stating carefully, because it would, if it holds, explain the most striking fact in
the arc — *why only the weak interaction violates parity*. It would be because only the weak step reads the
address axis. **I am recording that as a requirement χ₄ imposes, not a result.** Nothing here shows the W⁰
catalytic step actually reads the fourth axis linearly; that is F8's work.

## An error caught in-patch — the sixth in this arc

My first run of Reading B returned E·B ≈ 10⁻¹⁴ even though the two hands' electric fields differed by 5.7. I had
built B as v × E from the *modified* field, and v × E is perpendicular to E by construction — so E·B was forced to
zero regardless of the physics. **The control was blind, and its failure is what exposed it.** Corrected to build B
from the source's own motion (SF-6: the moving charge swings the DP poles), which is independent of the test
displacement. After the fix, the control returns a large, sign-flipping E·B, so Reading A's zero is a genuine result.

This is the same shape as 4070's torque error and 4049–4054's blind zeros: a quantity constrained by construction
read as a measurement. The discipline that catches it is unchanged — a control that must fire when the effect is
present, run before any null is believed.

## A P-even side effect, recorded so it is not later mistaken for a parity problem

Under Reading A the 3D field is multiplied by cos θ for **both** hands. A fixed θ is absorbed into charge
normalisation; a θ that varies between interactions could be observable. It is parity-even and does not bear on F2.

## Convenient branch — marked

**Convenient.** χ₄ passing F2 keeps the founder's candidate alive, and the division-of-labour reading is attractive
enough to overstate. Two things I did against that: I report the pass as **conditional** and name the founder's own
"proximity" framing as the reading that fails; and I state the weak-sector explanation strictly as a requirement.
**What would overturn it:** showing SF-6's derivations *need* 4D proximity in the displacement (e.g. that some
shipped EM result uses the fourth-axis component linearly) — then Reading A is unavailable and χ₄ fails F2.
