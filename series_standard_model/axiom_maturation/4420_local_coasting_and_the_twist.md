## 4. The twist (script)

**Each CP's own response is orientation-blind; the cohort's total is not.** His argument covers each CP's response to its own
motion through the sea, which depends on its velocity, not on how the plates are tilted. But the cohort as a whole follows the
interplate field, and in a moving capacitor that field depends on orientation (plates across the motion against along it). For a
tilted capacitor the momentum the cohort carries (the field momentum, in classical terms) is not parallel to the motion, and that
transverse momentum is the angular-momentum side of the Trouton–Noble problem. (Inertia itself is not orientation-free either:
the response to a push along the motion differs from one across it, γ³m against γm; that does not matter for a coasting
capacitor.)

**The forces between moving charges.** The capacitor is held together by forces: the attraction between the plates' opposite
charges, the cohort's pull on the plates, and the binding that keeps the plates rigid. In CPP the force between CPs moving
together follows the census channel, which is exactly the Liénard–Wiechert form (4411–4412). For two opposite charges moving
together, tilted at θ to the motion, those forces are equal and opposite but **not along the line joining them**. The off-line
tilt comes from the velocity-dependent (magnetic-like) part of the force: the component across the motion is reduced by
(1 − v²). It does not come from retardation, since for uniform motion the field points from the source's present position. The
script finds a couple of (v²/2) sin 2θ at low speed (in units of the static force times the separation): zero along and across the
motion, largest near 45° at low speed, growing as v². That is the classical Trouton–Noble couple.

**Why nothing turns, classically: two equivalent statements.**
- **(a) Lorentz's corresponding states:** a structure in equilibrium at rest is in equilibrium when moving, provided every force
  holding it transforms alike; each constituent then feels zero net force, and the binding forces' couple offsets the electric
  one.
- **(b) Laue (1911):** in the lab frame there is a nonzero electromagnetic couple, but the moving, stressed body carries momentum
  across the direction of motion, so its angular momentum changes at exactly the rate of the couple (torque = dL/dt), and no
  rotation results. In the rest frame there is no couple at all.

(4419 §5's "a closed static system feels no net torque" was loose in the same way and is corrected here.)

**In CPP:** every force between CPs, including the binding of the plates and the cohort's pull, must follow the same census rule,
so that a moving capacitor is in equilibrium exactly as a resting one is. That is 4410's requirement ("every force, not only the
electric one, must transform the same way"), which 4412 met with the census channel for the direct CP–CP force. His
orientation-blind response of each CP is necessary but not sufficient.
