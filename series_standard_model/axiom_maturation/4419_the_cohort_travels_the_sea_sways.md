## 4. How the summation treats the cohort (script part 1)

last step), suppose the cohort enters the normalization at the landing GP but not the differential. The regenerated f is then

problem. (Under 4414's withdrawn iterated rule the same factor would compound into a decay; the script shows that only for
contrast.)

## 5. The front and rear signals must net exactly v (script part 2)

He uses "force" for displacement per Moment (4418), and 4414 has no pushing. So his "push against the forward advance" and
"greater push forward" map onto the **front signal** and the **rear signal** of his rear-minus-front summation (4414), not onto
separate forces. Their net must be exactly v, at every speed, for every species, free or bound. That is 4412's marginality
condition, restated, not a new one. 4413's timing ratio gives exactly v/c for a front-back pair of this kind with reflectors at
rest.

Any residual would act as a drag (or a self-acceleration) through the absolute frame. For scale, assuming a drag linear in
velocity: a common deceleration of the whole solar system would be invisible to the planetary ephemerides, but a linear drag
also acts on orbital motion. The ephemerides bound anomalous accelerations at roughly 10⁻¹³ to 10⁻¹⁵ m/s², depending on the
planet. At about 10⁻¹⁴ m/s² on 30 km/s of orbital motion, that caps the drag near 3 × 10⁻¹⁹ per second, about 10⁻⁶² per
Moment, as an order of magnitude. Secular changes of orbits, lunar laser ranging and binary-pulsar period decay constrain it
further. A drag nonlinear in absolute speed would act through the solar system's ~370 km/s and be anisotropic. In practice the
net must be exactly v by construction.

**A moving capacitor must not twist.** If the sea's push on a moving cohort depended on how the plates are tilted to the motion,
a charged capacitor moving through the absolute frame would turn. Trouton and Noble (1903) looked for this second-order effect
and found none, as did later repetitions (Tomaschek 1925–26; Chase 1926–27; Hayden 1994). In relativity the absence follows
from Laue's analysis (1911): the angular momentum of the field is balanced by the momentum flux of the plates'
non-electromagnetic stresses, so a closed static system feels no net torque. In CPP, the sea's action on a tilted cohort must
leave no net torque at every orientation.
