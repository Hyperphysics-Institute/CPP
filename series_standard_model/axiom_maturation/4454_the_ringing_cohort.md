## 2. "Assumption, not certain"

**He is right that it is an inference, not a certainty.** The shape is read from many runs, one click per run (4452). It is the best reading of three
sets of measurements (the histogram's sharp start, the line widths, two-photon interference, 4453), on the assumption that the photons are alike.

**But "statistical versus causal" is not quite the choice.** Each run is causal: an excitation, then one photon. Only the timing is random. And experiments

electron–photon coincidence experiments (for example Eminyan, MacAdam, Slevin and Kleinpoppen, 1973), the scattered electron marks the moment of
excitation and the photon is timed from it. Their histograms are the same exponential. These are causal timing measurements, one click per run.

**Its internal wavelength must shrink as its energy grows: unlike a bell.** Strike a bell harder and it gets louder, not higher. A photon's colour is set
by its energy alone: twice the energy means twice the frequency and half the wavelength, from radio waves to gamma rays. Energy × wavelength is the same

Compton result.) So in a ringing cohort with twice the energy, **the internal wavelength must halve.** The length of the whole packet is set separately, by
how long the emission lasts: about half a metre for hydrogen's lines, about 8 m for rubidium's. And a single photon cannot ring louder at the same
colour: brightness is the number of photons. The cohort picture must reproduce that. For matter the rule is wavelength = Planck's constant ÷ momentum,
which is why faster electrons in a microscope resolve finer detail (about 0.12 nm at 100 eV, 0.0025 nm at 200 keV).

**Under motion, its frequency and energy must shift together: unlike a clock.** An internal resonance behaves like a clock: seen from a moving frame, it
runs slow. A photon does not. Seen by an observer moving towards the source, it is bluer and more energetic, its frequency and energy rising by the same
factor, so energy ÷ frequency stays h in every frame. A photon also has no frame in which it is at rest for a standing wave to ring in. For matter, the
"internal ring" idea is de Broglie's 1924 internal clock at mc²/h, the ZBW-scale 1.24 × 10²⁰ Hz; he obtained wavelength = h/momentum from it only by
showing that, seen from a moving frame, the clock appears as a travelling phase wave. So **what rings at 10¹⁵ Hz must behave as a travelling phase pattern,
whose frequency and energy shift together, not as a clock carried along with the cohort.** (Not claimed as new: this is de Broglie's argument.)

## 5. Smaller points

- **Slit separation and energy: partly right.** For a single photon, interference works at any separation; a higher-energy photon just gives finer
  fringes (spacing = wavelength × screen distance ÷ separation). But in practice, a real source's light is coherent only across a width of about

  micrometre separations. So for a given source and detector, a higher-energy photon does need slits closer together: a coherence and resolution
  requirement, not a condition on a single photon.
