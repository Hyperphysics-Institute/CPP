# The Count Follows the PSR's Volume, so the Open Question Is Only the Shape of the SSV_abs → PSR Curve: Does Each Equal Step of Stress Shrink the PSR by the Same Percentage, All the Way to the Floor?

**Patch:** 4368. **Lane:** foundations, with GR. **Session:** 242.
**Founder:** `founders_voice/4368_count_follows_psr_volume_absolute_gp_density.md`.
**No verify script:** the content is one identity, n = ρ_GP·(4π/3)·PSR³, and a restatement; nothing new is computed.

## 1. The founder's picture, and what it settles

GPs sit at a fixed absolute density ρ_GP on the rigid lattice (GR-1j L203–206). The PSR sphere shrinks as SSV_abs rises,
and it holds fewer GPs only because its volume is smaller:

    n = ρ_GP · (4π/3) · PSR³,   so   n/n_∞ = q³   exactly   (q = PSR/PSR_∞)

So the count is **not** a second rule beside the PSR law. It is the PSR law cubed, and in this geometric sense it is
"obvious". This settles the direction of 4365's framing: **the PSR comes first and the count follows**, not the other
way round. The 4364 source rule reads naturally in the same terms: a CP's stamp scales with **the volume of its own
Planck sphere**, which its GP already has once it has computed its PSR.

## 2. What remains open: one curve

4365–4367 asked whether "each unit of stress removes the same fraction of the GPs". Under §1 that is exactly the same
statement as **each unit of stress shrinking the PSR by the same percentage**, one third of the GP fraction, since
n ∝ PSR³. So everything reduces to the shape of the SSV_abs → PSR curve, the conversion the corpus has never specified:

| curve | what it says | status |
|---|---|---|
| same percentage per unit of stress (PSR = PSR_∞·e^{−ε}) | the 4365–4367 rule | second order ½ = Mercury; background scales out; third order −1/6 (no horizon, shadow +4.6% over GR) |
| the ratified law R-PSR-LAW-LOG (3390) | 1 − ε + ε²/2 + O(ε³) | **already the same-percentage curve through second order** (it matches e^{−ε} there); third order left open |
| GR-1c's Padé, (1 − ε/2)/(1 + ε/2) | exact isotropic Schwarzschild | agrees through second order; third order −¼ (horizon; GR's shadow) |
| inverse proportion (count ∝ 1/SSV_abs) | 4365 (A) | fails Mercury (β = 5/2) and the clocks |

The founder has, in effect, already ratified the same-percentage curve for weak fields: his 3390 ½ **is** that curve's
second order. The remaining choice concerns only strong fields: whether the percentage stays the same all the way down
to the black-hole floor (exponential, −1/6), or bends there (Padé, −¼, GR's black hole).

## 3. Question to the founder (physical picture)

As stress rises in equal steps, the PSR shrinks. For ordinary gravity, your ½ already means it shrinks by the same
percentage at each step. Does it keep shrinking by that same percentage at every step, all the way down to a black hole?
If yes, black holes have no horizon and cast a shadow about 5% larger than Einstein's, which telescopes may soon check.
Or does the shrinking change pace near a black hole, so that black holes come out exactly as Einstein's?
