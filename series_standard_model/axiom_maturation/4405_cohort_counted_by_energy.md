## 2. Equal fall, and what "the same increase" must mean (script part 1)

For slow CPs, adding the same increase in speed to each makes the whole body speed up by that amount, whatever it is
made of. But in CPP most of a body's mass is CPs moving close to the one-PSR-per-Moment cap (the ZBW swing, 4324–4326;
R-CP-NO-REST-MASS: at most one PSR per Moment). A CP at the cap cannot take the same extra speed along its motion, and
adding speed to a near-cap CP changes its momentum by a large amount that depends on its direction. The script shows it:
two toy materials differing only in their share of near-cap CPs gain 73 and 78 times the intended increase when the same
velocity is added and capped, a composition-dependent fall that MICROSCOPE excludes.

**One form works:** each CP gains momentum in proportion to its energy (E/c² times the increase). Then the body's
centre of energy gains exactly the intended increase, for every material (script: 1.0000 for both). So **equal fall holds
if "the same increase" means each CP's momentum gain goes as its energy.** That answers 4404 §6 (the direct increase) and
sharpens 4403 owed item 2, which stays open until the GP computation is shown to deliver it. 4403's internal-clock
picture would deliver it automatically.

## 4. Action and reaction (script part 3)

The Earth pulls the ball through the ball's passive mass; the ball pulls the Earth through its active mass, the SSV_abs it
sources. Momentum is conserved only if the ratio of active to passive mass is the same for every material; lunar laser

## 6. Answer to his "What do you think?"

Both answers work, with one condition the theory's own speed limit imposes. A body at rest falls from the first Moment
because the gradient acts on each CP directly, and heavy and light bodies fall alike because every CP gets the same
gain. But most of a body's mass is CPs swinging close to the one-Planck-sphere-per-Moment limit, and those cannot simply
take on extra speed. The rule that works, and makes every material fall exactly alike, is that each CP gains momentum in
proportion to its energy.

The same weighting makes his cohort rule right. His cohort already includes the polarized DP cloud and the DP-arc energy
of the moving CPs, and GR-1a already defines a particle's mass as its cloud's energy. Counting by energy is what gives a
bound nucleus and a warm ball the inertia they have. And the pull a body exerts on the Earth has to come from that same
energy-weighted cohort.
