## 2. His first picture works (script parts 1–2)

**The steady swing, adding up to the energy, is right.** A CP swinging millions of times at one steady rate while it emits is what the measurements
need: a packet of millions of cycles (sharp lines and long fringe reach, 4448), at one rate (the colour), lasting the decay time. And his summation is

measured transition strength, radiates with a lifetime of 1.6 ns. Each swing gives up about 1 part in 4 million of what is left (about 2.6 millionths of
an electron-volt at first, less later). About 4 million swings in one lifetime release 63%, and the sum over the whole decay is the 10.2 eV of the drop.

**What 4446–4448 ruled out** was only that the count sets the colour, or that the energy is a number of quanta, one per swing. The colour is the swing's
rate. So his three quantities fit together:
- **the rate** is the colour, f;
- **the energy** released is the sum over the swings, ΔE = hf;
- **the count** is f times the decay time, about 4 million per lifetime.

**The single burst is shorter than 4448's one-cycle photon, so its failures are worse.** It still carries the frequency of the whole drop energy, as
he says. But a half-wave pulse with all its energy radiated at once would spread its colour over at least a third to a half of itself, and lose its
fringes beyond about half a wavelength. Atomic photons show neither, so the burst is not needed, and it is ruled out.

## 3. What sets the rate (script part 3)

What is left is what makes the steady swing run at ΔE/h. The orbits' own revolution rates do not set it: 4446 showed that neither orbit's rate is the
light's. For one-level jumps (n + 1 → n), the light's frequency always lies between the two orbits' rates (2 → 1: 8.2 × 10¹⁴ < 2.47 × 10¹⁵ < 6.6 × 10¹⁵
Hz). For large orbits all three converge (within 1.5% at 100 → 99). The proof is one line: the light's frequency, divided by the Rydberg frequency, is
(2n + 1)/(n²(n + 1)²), which lies between 2/(n + 1)³ and 2/n³. But this follows from the smooth energy levels, E_n ∝ −1/n²: the energy difference
across one step equals the slope of E_n at some orbit in between, which is that orbit's revolution rate. It is the correspondence principle, not a
clue to a mechanism. For larger jumps between large orbits the light tends to a multiple of the orbit's rate (a harmonic) and can exceed both orbits' rates: 6 → 3 at 1.13
times, 7 → 5 at 1.22 times, the rate of the orbit entered.

So the rate is ΔE/h, with no simpler classical source. In standard physics it is the difference of the two states' rhythms (4446, which he has set
aside). In CPP terms it is open, and it is the question of §5.

## 4. Targets for his draining reservoir (script part 4)

His picture has the energy stored in DP arc-separation throughout the orbital space, discharged as the CP swings. The measurements give it three
quantitative targets:

**What his "acceleration equal to the charged state" must give** (Claude's reading of his summary): a steady swing that drains at this rate, not a single
acceleration.
