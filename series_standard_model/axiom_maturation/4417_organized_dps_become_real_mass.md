## 2. It is field mass, with a carrier

This is the classical idea of electromagnetic mass: a charged or current-carrying system's field adds to its inertia
(J. J. Thomson 1881, with the coefficient corrected by Heaviside 1889; Abraham 1902–03; Lorentz 1904). His version gives it
a carrier, the realized DPs, and a place, the volume the field occupies.

On place, one caveat. In general relativity the field's energy density is local, but the source of gravity also includes
stresses: a static field has a tension of u along its lines and a pressure of u across them, so between a capacitor's plates
it sources about 2u/c², and the plates' own stresses cancel the surplus. Only the total over the closed system is E/c²
(Tolman, Laue). Inertia has the same feature: a moving capacitor's field momentum depends on its orientation (the
Trouton–Noble setting). So "the extra mass sits between the plates" is true of the energy, and true of the mass only for the
closed whole.

## 4. What relativity requires of "as per the amount of separation" (script parts 1–2)

**The specification.** Relativity fixes the extra mass at the stored energy divided by c², and a capacitor's energy goes as
the square of its voltage (CV²/2). Double the voltage, and the DPs between the plates separate twice as far, while the extra
mass must grow fourfold. So realness must track the stored energy, which goes as the square of the separation. His wording
("as per the amount of separation") sets no power law; this is what it must come to. A linear law would double the mass,
which the direct test of E = mc² (Rainville et al. 2005, about 4 × 10⁻⁷) and the nuclear masses, which include the Coulomb
energy, would see.

**The symmetry allows it.** By 4416's symmetry, any measure of a DP's realness that does not care which way it is pulled is
even in the separation, so it starts at the square unless it has a kink at zero separation. The symmetry allows the square
law; it does not force it. For real DPs, with their random internal separation (4416), the square law would hold for the
ensemble, not for each DP.

**A spring picture: an identity, given one commitment.** If each DP's separation is held by a linear restoring "spring", the
energy stored in the separated DPs is ½ E·P per unit volume (part 2; this is an identity, the springs' polarizability being
defined by them). In a linear dielectric the field energy is ½ ε_bare E² + ½ E·P, so the spring energy is the **whole** field
energy only if ε_bare = 0, that is, if the vacuum's ε₀ is entirely the DP sea's polarizability (D = P). That is a strong
commitment about the vacuum, not a mild assumption: Gauss's law then makes the sea screen every charge completely, with E set
by the springs' balance. If it holds, the energy in the realized DPs is the field energy, and realized mass equal to that
energy over c² is then E = mc² applied to them. For B, the founder's 2471 correction (energy goes as the speed squared "via
