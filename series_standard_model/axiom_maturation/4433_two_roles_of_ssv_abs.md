## 2. The relay, stated correctly

Claude's "passed-along register values" was loose. He states that each GP emits the integrated **sum** of the DI-bits it receives from its
PSR shell, each Moment, and the registry (4354–4355) agrees that an origin's influence reaches beyond its PSR only by that re-radiation. But the
registry reads a far CP at one GP as the **mean** over its whole PSR shell, and that mean-value property is why GR-1j's statics make the relay
lossless and Laplace (1/r statics, gradient 1/r²). The two agree only up to the number of GPs on the shell: the mean is his sum divided by the
shell count. That count changes with the PSR inside a well, and without the division a raw sum would grow at every hop. Whether his sum
carries that normalization is open.

## 3. Gravity through the gradient, not the amplitude (script part 1)

His answer separates two roles that 4432 ran together. The near cloud's SSV_abs at the CP's GP can be enormous, but it is centred on the
CP, so it contributes nothing to the difference across the PSR's limbs. A distant body adds a tiny SSV_abs, but also a tiny gradient, and the
gradient survives the superposition. In the script's illustration the cloud adds 10⁶ at the CP and zero to the limb difference; the body
adds 0.1 at the CP and 2 × 10⁻⁵ to the limb difference; together, the limb difference is the body's alone. **So the near cloud does not
drown gravity out:** gravity acts through the limb difference (4403: the change of drift per Moment is the census difference across one
PSR), and the cloud, being symmetric about the CP, has none. (Units: 4403's census is u = ΣGM/(rc²). In those units the cloud's share,
G(γ − 1)m/(rc²), is negligible anyway; the drowning concern of 4432 arises only if SSV_abs is the raw realness-weighted count, which is the
case illustrated here.) (A moving CP's cloud is lopsided front to back; that lopsidedness is its own
net, which sets its step. Gravity's gradient adds to it.)

**One condition, for equal fall:** the limb difference may be normalized by the **background** baseline (K = 1 + 2U is fractional), but not by
the CP's own SSV_abs; otherwise a fast electron, with a larger own S, would be pushed less. The relevant tests are the free fall of internal
kinetic and binding energy (weak-equivalence-principle tests) and, at the fast limit, light bending and the Shapiro delay. GR's deflection of a
fast particle depends on its speed (4403: the limb, clock half gives half of light's bending), so the condition is that the clock-half push not
depend on the CP's own S, not that the bending be the same.

## 4. Realness weighting, and the baseline (script part 2)

**Realness weighting is his stated rule (unregistered).** A neutral DP, both CPs on one GP, contributes zero SSV_abs; contributions grow with
separation and polarization. That settles 4432's paired-DP question: the sea's unseparated DPs drop out. Its replacing 4416's per-contribution
reading (P) for paired DPs is pending registration; it is consistent with AP-4 (co-located partners give no net E).

**It must sum to mass-energy.** Read as a count of fully real CPs (quarks and electrons, 100% each), the weighting would not be proportional to
mass: the count per unit mass differs between hydrogen and iron by a large fraction, and composition tests (Eötvös, MICROSCOPE, about 10⁻¹⁵)
require gravity to follow mass-energy, binding energy included. So the realness weighting must sum to mass-energy, which ties to 4431's
(γ − 1)M. The script below assumes N ∝ M (nucleons as the unit).

**The vacuum baseline.** With paired DPs counting zero, the baseline that WC-DP-SEA-PV's K = 1 + 2U measures gravity against must come from
real charge. A candidate: the universe's real charges, summed with the 1/r statics of the AP-4 relay. Counting nucleons as the unit of real
charge, a body's fractional addition to that baseline at distance r is (N_body/r)/(N_universe/R_universe). For the Earth at its surface that is
2.5 × 10⁻⁹ against its measured potential GM/(rc²) = 7.0 × 10⁻¹⁰; for the Sun at 1 AU, 3.5 × 10⁻⁸ against 9.9 × 10⁻⁹. The ratio is 3.54 in both
cases, because it is the single number c²R_u/(GM_u). Any sum proportional to M/r gives the same ratio for every body, so the Earth–Sun agreement
is guaranteed, not evidence. **The only content is that the factor is of order one: Sciama's 1953 relation, GM_u/(R_u c²) ~ 1**, a known
order-of-magnitude coincidence (with M_u ~ 10⁸⁰ nucleons and R_u ~ 4.4 × 10²⁶ m as rough inputs). Caveats:
- the point-mass form N_u/R_u is an approximation; a uniform sphere seen from its centre gives (3/2)N_u/R_u, a factor of about 2.4;
- a sum to cosmic distances depends on the cosmology (horizon, expansion, and retardation: the relay runs at c, so it sums over the past light
  cone);
- if G is set by R_u/M_u, G varies over cosmic time unless that ratio stays fixed; lunar laser ranging limits |Ġ/G| to about 10⁻¹³ per year.

It fits his long-held picture of gravity as raised SSV_abs, as an order-one candidate for the baseline, not a derivation.

**Two roles of SSV_abs, then:**
- **The background** (the universe's real charge, plus bodies' additions): sets the local light speed and PSR (K; 4415's reversible μ-ε
  rescaling), and its gradient moves CPs (4403's limb rule).
- **The CP's own near share** (its realness-weighted cloud): sets its step f = |V|/S (4425) and its directional PSR shortening (4429–4430).

**The split is required, not optional.** With this baseline, in raw count units the background at the CP's GP is about N_u/R_u ≈ 2.3 × 10⁵³ per
metre, while a single fully real charge even at one Planck length gives only about 6 × 10³⁴: the background exceeds the near cloud by at least
about 10¹⁸. If S in f = |V|/S included it, f would be pinned near zero (4425's dilution). So the step must use the CP's own share only, and the
background must enter only through the local light speed (4415). AP-4's summation at a GP does not tell sources apart, so how the split arises is
the main open condition. This also reverses 4432's picture: with this baseline the far universe, not the near cloud, dominates the SSV_abs at the
GP. The synthesis is Claude's, drawn from 4415, 4425, 4430 and today's answers.

## 7. Answer to his "What do you think?"

its own source, so many of them near the electron can each add a contribution; that answers the DI-bit rule. On the relay, he's right and I

His realness rule settles the ordinary DPs: a DP whose two charges sit together counts for nothing. That leaves the question of what empty

within a factor of a few of its actual gravity. That is an old coincidence noticed by Sciama, and it fits his picture of gravity as raised SSV_abs,
though it doesn't prove it. But that background would be far larger than the electron's own cloud at its GP. So the electron's step must come
from its own share alone, with the background entering only through the local speed of light. How the sum at a GP separates the two is now the
main open question.
