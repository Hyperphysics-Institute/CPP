## 3. What one DI-bit carries: a length (rows 2)

"Move one GP" is a displacement of one GP spacing per Moment, a velocity step of 10⁻³⁰ to 10⁻³² c. The angular momentum it
changes is mass × velocity step × lever arm:

```
  s = l_P/1e30    : dv = 1.0e-30 c;  electron-scale CP at r_C  : angular momentum per DI-bit = 3.33e-31 hbar
  s = l_P/1e30    : dv = 1.0e-30 c;  1 kg at 1 m               : angular momentum per DI-bit = 2.84e+12 hbar
```

**It differs by about 10³⁵ between an electron and a one-kilogram wheel.** A fixed displacement per DI-bit is a quantum of
length (and velocity), not of action. It cannot by itself make every spin come in the same ħ/2 steps. A fixed action
per exchange would require what one DI-bit transfers to scale inversely with the mass and lever arm of whatever it acts
on, which "move one GP" does not do.

## 4. The coincidence, recorded and not claimed (rows 3)

```
(3) the coincidence (recorded, not claimed): (Planck momentum) x (smallest PSR) = m_P c x l_P/2 = 0.5000 hbar
    identically, because l_P and m_P are defined with hbar; the only content is the 1/2 of the PSR floor.
    and one Planck momentum is 2.39e+22 x m_e c: no single DI-bit can hand that to an electron.
```

The founder's phrase, "at the distance of the smallest PSR is the amount of action", lands exactly on ħ/2 if the momentum
is the Planck momentum. That is bookkeeping, since the Planck units contain ħ. The only real content is the ½ in the PSR
floor, which is itself underived. And a Planck momentum is 10²² times an electron's, so no single DI-bit could deliver it.
It is recorded so that it is not rediscovered and taken for a derivation.

## 5. What I think

The founder's rule usefully fixes what a DI-bit *does*: it nudges a CP by one GP. It also gives the covering condition a
number: 10⁶⁰–10⁶⁴ DI-bits per Moment. But ħ/2 must be the same for every spinning thing, which needs a quantity that is
the same whatever the mass and size. A displacement is not such a quantity. Two routes remain:

- **ħ enters with the units.** CPP sets the PSR and the Moment equal to the Planck length and time, and those contain ħ.
  If so, ħ is an input to CPP, like c, and the programme's task is only the ½. Its natural candidates are the PSR floor's
  l_P/2 and the pass-through ZBW's two crossings to return (4298 §6).
- **ħ is derived.** CPP would then need an independent unit of energy per DI-bit or per Moment, from which ħ = energy ×
  Moment would follow.
