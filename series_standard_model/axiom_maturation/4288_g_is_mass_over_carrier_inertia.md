## 1. The ruling

A CP has **no rest mass**; it is the element from which mass arises. A moving CP makes DP-arcs whose reaction **is its
inertia**, which is SF-6's DP-Sea-Polarization inertia, m_eff = m₀γ (SF-6 l. 227–233, with m₀ a tuned toy parameter). It
moves at its SSV-net V_i, so light speed is unlikely except perhaps at the smallest level. He asks what force makes the DP
orbit, and suggests a resonance. He proposes that the orbiting CPs carry multiples of ħ, with the magnet their difference.

## 2. The general formula: speed drops out (rows 1)

Each orbiting CP has charge q_i, angular momentum L_i and moving inertia m_i, so its moment is μ_i = q_i L_i/(2m_i). With
spin S = ΣL_i and the electron's relation μ = −g(e/2m_e)S:

  **g = −(2m_e/eS) Σ q_i L_i/(2m_i).** No speed appears; only angular momentum over inertia.

```
    L_o = 0.2929, L_i = 0.2071  ->  g = 0.1716  (4286: 0.1716)
  speed-c massless outer (4286/4287): inertia = motion energy/c^2 = m_e/2, pass-through inner (L_i = 0) -> g = 2.0000
```

This reproduces SPIN-1's failure and the 4286/4287 light-speed result as special cases. The light-speed case gave g = 2
only because a speed-c pole with half the rest energy as motion has inertia **m_e/2**. That inertia is what mattered.

**The founder's objection is accommodated.** g = 2 does not need light speed. It needs the circulating −eCP's
DP-arc inertia to be **half the electron's mass**. It can then run at any V_i: at SPIN-1's own outer radius it would
move at 0.085c. Light speed is only the special case R = ħ/mc.

**A consistency, not a derivation.** If the inner CP has the same inertia (m_e/2), the DP's two CPs together carry
exactly m_e. This echoes 4286's half-and-half energy split. It is not derived: SF-6's m₀ is tuned, and nothing on file
yet computes the inertia of a CP circulating in a bound DP.

## 4. The ħ-quanta difference (rows 3)

```
    combinations with L_o + L_i = 1/2:  none -- a sum or difference of whole hbar is never hbar/2
    L_o = +0.5, L_i = +0.0 (inner pass-through): g = 2 needs each CP's inertia m_c = 0.50 m_e  (two CPs: 1.00 m_e of moving inertia)
    L_o = +1.0, L_i = -0.5 (counter-rotating  ): g = 2 needs each CP's inertia m_c = 1.50 m_e  (two CPs: 3.00 m_e of moving inertia)
    L_o = +1.5, L_i = -1.0 (counter-rotating  ): g = 2 needs each CP's inertia m_c = 2.50 m_e  (two CPs: 5.00 m_e of moving inertia)
```

- **Whole units of ħ cannot make the electron's spin.** No sum or difference of whole ħ equals ħ/2. The half is the
  electron's defining oddity, and a whole-ħ picture has to produce it some other way.
- **The magnet counts angular momentum divided by inertia**, not angular momentum alone. So "the difference of the
  action" equals the magnet only when both CPs have the same inertia.
- **"One unit and two"** (ħ and ħ/2, counter-rotating, fields adding) can give g = 2, but only with each CP at 1.5 m_e,
  which puts 3 m_e of moving inertia inside an electron of mass m_e. Nothing on file removes the extra 2 m_e.
- **The only combination with both CPs lighter than the electron** is the outer carrying all ħ/2 and the inner carrying
  none. That is the pass-through lily-pad, the founder's own 4287 picture.

## 5. What I think (answering the founder)

His ruling makes the question sharper, not harder. The electron's magnet, on his outer-minus-lily-pad picture, comes
down to one number: **the DP-arc inertia of the circulating −eCP, which must be half the electron's mass.** The speed and
radius are then set by the resonance he describes. That resonance ("what force makes the DP orbit") is the open dynamics:
SPIN-2's standing-wave sub-harmonic is the corpus's candidate for it.

His instinct that the magnet is "action carried by the orbiting CPs" survives in a precise form. The magnet is the
action ħ/2 carried by the outer CP, divided by that CP's inertia. It does not survive as a difference of whole-ħ amounts.
