## 1. His answer

The atom's core is bound CPs; moving, it displaces CPs of the DP Sea each Moment, and the resulting DP-arcs carry its KE and momentum. The core CPs and the
arc CPs "form entangled bonds," which happens "when any two CPs collide/transfer Moment/forcefully displace, which is the case with the atomic core and DP
Sea motion." So "the mass and KE/DP-arcs of a moving particle, form an entangled entity," accounted for by the Nexus tensor; "every time a collision happens
to the atomic core, all CPs associated with the mass-KE quantum is affected," and "[t]he effect is instantaneous because the update of the Nexus tensor causes
all the entangled CPs in the quantum to update their displacement in relationship to any part of the system suffering a collision." He holds that this is
the case "in all interferometry, quantum erasers, polarized and measured photons and particle experiments," and that entanglement exists to conserve
momentum and energy.

## 3. Two conditions the data fix

### 3.1 The update must depend on where each part is

"Respond together" is necessary but not enough. If every part of the quantum received the same change, the two paths would keep their relative phase and
the fringes would not move or blur at any separation (script rows U1). What the experiment shows is a change that depends on the gap d (rows U2, the 4491
closed form): a momentum kick q changes each part's phase in proportion to its position along q, so parts a distance d apart differ by q·d. In CPP terms
(Claude's statement): if phase is the orientation of the GP's V_i (FI-QMRG-1), the Nexus's update must turn V_i by different amounts at different places,
by an amount that grows across the quantum in the direction of the kick — "in relationship to any part of the system suffering a collision," read at its
strongest, with "relationship" meaning distance along the kick. A single known kick only shifts the fringes, by q·d; the blur comes from averaging over the
emitted photon's direction, that is, from the record the photon carries (4491 K3). The kick from absorbing the laser photon is the same for every atom and
only shifts the fringes.

The same rule covers his erasers and polarization experiments, with one change of property (Claude's reading). In the Walborn eraser (4491 §4) the plate

photon, in a partner. This restates the standard complementarity condition; 4491 (f), how a record with no kick changes where the guiding arcs send the atom,
stays open in his terms.

### 3.2 The core's bonds with the Sea must leave no path record

His bond rule says the core bonds with the Sea CPs it displaces, every Moment, all along its flight. If those displaced Sea CPs kept a mark of which path
the core took after it passed — a trail — the atom would carry a which-path record from the start, and it could not interfere at all. Atoms do interfere,
and so do molecules of about 2,000 atoms (4489 §5). So every Moment's bond with the Sea must leave essentially no record of the path once the core has moved
on: the displaced Sea CPs either travel with the quantum as its arcs (so they come back together with it at recombination) or return to their prior state.

How faint "essentially no" must be (rows S1): over a 1 ms flight, taking one Moment as t_P (the corpus value: the founder, 20 July 2026, in
`founders_voice/kernel_psr_reradiation_2026-07-20.md`; the 0733 correction, l_P as one displacement step per Absolute Moment; QM-4's τ_corr = t_P), there
are about 1.9 × 10⁴⁰ Moments. Keeping half the fringe contrast then needs each Moment's trail to
distinguish the two paths by less than about 10⁻²⁰. The trail need not be zero, but it must be that faint. (Claude's statement; the model takes the
Moments as the independent record events. If the core displaces many Sea DPs per Moment, the bound per DP is stricter still; correlated re-displacement of
the same DPs would loosen it.)

This condition is the reason his picture can hold together: the arcs are the part of the Sea the quantum carries with it, and only what the quantum leaves
behind can decohere it. **It is already in the corpus, and it is a consistency condition between his own rulings (D-10).** 4481 §2's grating condition says a
kick ties two systems only when it could be read; the Sea must take the core's per-Moment pushes the way a grating does, with no usable record. 4491 §1
reads "collision" as an interaction that leaves a record, with Copilot's 4485 exceptions (interactions whose alternatives act alike disperse nothing). Under
his R-COLLISION-DISPERSES ("The only thing that diminishes the entanglement is collision"), his own rule that the core collides with the Sea every Moment
would disperse the atom's coherence unless those collisions fall in the no-record class. So §3.2 is required by his two rulings together, not only by the
data.

## 7. Reply to the founder

That answers it: the core and the arcs in both paths are one quantum, so a bump on the core changes the whole quantum at once through the Nexus, and nothing
has to travel from one path to the other. It's your 4478 rule, one group per quantum, carried over to an atom.

The fringes add two conditions.

- **The change must depend on where each part is.** If every part got the same change, the two paths would stay in step and nothing would blur. A kick
  turns each part's phase by an amount that grows across the quantum in the direction of the kick, so the two paths end up out of step by an amount set by
  the gap between them. That's what makes the blur depend on the gap. The same rule covers the erasers: there the plate twists the polarization differently
  in each path instead.
- **The atom must leave no trail in the Sea.** If the Sea DPs the core pushes aside kept a mark of which path it took, the atom would be marked from the
  start and could never interfere. Over a millisecond's flight that mark would have to be fainter than about one part in 10²⁰ per Moment. Your own two
  rulings need this too: if only collision disperses entanglement, and the core collides with the Sea every Moment, those collisions must be the kind that
  leave no record. This also shows that QM-4's Sea decoherence rate, read literally, would wipe out an atom's fringes in 10⁻⁴² of a second. The repo applies
  it to measurement and large objects by example but never says when it acts, so QM-4 needs that rule.

My question for you is in §4.
