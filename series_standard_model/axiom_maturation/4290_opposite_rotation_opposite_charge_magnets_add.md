**Patch:** 4290. **Lane:** EW → QM/SPIN. **Session:** 240.

**Corrects:** Claude's reading in 4289 §7, which said that on the founder's picture the inner carries no spin.

## 1. The founder's picture, and how I misread it

- **His picture:** the outer −eCP circle carries **ħ**, the inner +eCP lily-pad **ħ/2** in the opposite direction, and
  the spin is their difference, ħ/2. ħ/2 is the fundamental unit of spin; ħ is two units. The dipole's opposite charges
  make DP-arcs in opposite directions, and only the sum is detected.
- **How I got "inner carries none".** 4287 recorded that the inner's field "points in the opposite direction". I took
  both CPs to circulate the same way, because that is when opposite charges make opposing magnets (§2). With ħ/2 as the
  unit and both circulating the same way, the spin is ħ/2 only if the inner carries nothing. His clarification says the
  inner circulates the **other** way. That changes the sign of its magnet.

## 2. The sign rule (rows 1)

```
  -e going +:  mu_z = -0.50
  +e going -:  mu_z = -0.50
  +e going +:  mu_z = +0.50
  -> a -e going one way and a +e going the OTHER way make magnets pointing the SAME way (they add).
     Opposite charges make opposing magnets only when they circulate the SAME way.
```

A positive charge circling clockwise is the same current as a negative charge circling counter-clockwise. SF-6's DP-arc
field reduces to this far from the electron (SF-6 l. 319, Maxwell limit). So the picture's two statements, **opposite
spin directions** and **opposite DP-arc (field) directions**, cannot both hold for a +eCP and a −eCP. One has to give.

## 3. If the spins are opposite, as stated (ħ and −ħ/2): the magnets add (rows 2)

```
  m_out = 1.00, m_in =  1.00 m_e:  mu_z = -0.7500  ->  g = 3.0000
  m_out = 0.50, m_in =  0.50 m_e:  mu_z = -1.5000  ->  g = 6.0000
  m_out = 1.50, m_in =  1.50 m_e:  mu_z = -0.5000  ->  g = 2.0000
  m_out = 2.00, m_in =  1.00 m_e:  mu_z = -0.5000  ->  g = 2.0000
  g = 2 needs  1/m_out + 1/(2 m_in) = 1/m_e  (so m_out >= m_e always; equal only if the inner is infinitely heavy).
  least total moving inertia: m_out = 1.7071, m_in = 1.2071, total = 2.9142 m_e = (1+1/sqrt2)^2 = 2.9142
```

- **The spin arithmetic works.** With ħ/2 as the unit, ħ − ħ/2 = ħ/2 cleanly. This removes 4288's objection, which
  assumed whole ħ as the unit.
- **The magnet comes out too strong unless the CPs are heavy.** If the two CPs carry inertia comparable to the electron's
  mass or less, the magnet is 1.5 to 3 times too large (g = 3 to 6). Matching g = 2 needs the outer CP's inertia to be
  at least the electron's whole mass, and the pair's moving inertia to be **at least 2.91 m_e**.
- **What that would mean.** The electron's mass m_e would be what remains after a binding of about −1.9 m_e c² cancels
  most of its parts' motion-inertia. This is not ruled out: tightly bound systems do this, and the Coulomb binding of the
  DP to the core is negative. But nothing on file computes such a binding, and it would make the electron's mass a
  near-cancellation. That is a strong claim, to be derived, not assumed.

## 4. If the magnets oppose, as also stated: the spins add (rows 3)

```
  S = 1.5 hbar -- not the electron's spin.
  With hbar/2 as the unit and both circulating the same way, S = 1/2 only for (1/2, 0): the reading 4287-4289 used.
```

With the same amounts circulating the same way, the magnets oppose but the spins add to 3ħ/2. Keeping ħ/2 then forces the
inner to carry nothing, which was the reading of 4287–4289 (g = m_e/m_out, so g = 2 at m_out = m_e/2).

## 5. What I think (answering the founder)

His resolution of the arithmetic is right: with ħ/2 as the unit, subtraction gives ħ/2. What it exposes is a choice
between two pictures that cannot be merged:

| | inner circulates **opposite** to outer (ħ, −ħ/2) | inner circulates the **same** way (ħ/2, 0) |
|---|---|---|
| spin | ħ − ħ/2 = ħ/2 ✓ | ħ/2 + 0 = ħ/2 ✓ |
| magnets | **add** | **oppose** (inner's is zero here) |
| g = 2 needs | CP inertias ≥ 2.91 m_e in total; mass is a near-cancellation | outer inertia = m_e/2 |
| inner lily-pad | carries ħ/2 | passes straight through, carries nothing |

Either can give the measured magnet. The first gives the inner lily-pad a full role and uses his unit counting, at the
price of a large, underived binding energy. The second is lighter and simpler, but the inner does no spin work.
