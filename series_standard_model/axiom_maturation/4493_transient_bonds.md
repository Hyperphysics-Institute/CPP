## 3. What it settles, at its most favourable (D-15)

- **No wake for steady motion.** That meets 4492 (b)'s no-trail condition in his terms, under §4's condition. 4492 (f) answered.
- **The standard comparison, with its conditions.** The cleanest standard analogue is ideal (potential) flow past a body: fluid displaced ahead, closing
  behind, no net force and no wake (d'Alembert). A charge moving uniformly likewise carries its field with it and does not radiate. Both hold only below a
  threshold: a body in a medium faster than the medium's waves sheds a wake (Cherenkov), and a superfluid stays wakeless only below Landau's critical
  velocity. And the standard cloud is fore–aft balanced and supplies no drive, which is why CONJ-FP-1's Condition B says such a relay gives no step
  (reopened at 4411 (e)). In CPP the front–rear difference must also produce the step, exactly v (4419 item 4). (Claude's statement.)
- **Why the paths can rejoin.** What the quantum carries at any moment is its present, local bonds. If the comoving cloud depends only on the present
  position and velocity, then at the point where the two paths meet, the bonds sit at the meeting point whichever path the core took, and they tell nothing
  about the path. Paths with different kick histories rejoin with the same cloud only if their final momenta match, up to the recoil 4492 already treats.
  A bond released so that it leaves nothing behind carries no path information forward. (Claude's reading, with that premise.)
- **"Pushes on the atomic core,"** read as the rear contribution to the front–rear differential (4414's rear-minus-front, carried in payloads since 4415),
  not a mechanical push; 4419 §5 and 4420 §2 read his "pushes" the same way. (Claude's reading.)

## 4. The condition: the released DP must tell nothing about the path

**Trail and drag overlap but are not one test.** A trail that carries energy is drag, and drag slows the atom. But a trail can also carry no energy: a DP
left turned (an orientation or phase record) costs nothing, and in ideal flow past a body, with no wake and no drag, every fluid parcel is still left
permanently shifted (Darwin drift, 1953). Such an energy-free trail decoheres without slowing anything. Conversely, drag into disturbances much longer than
the path separation would slow the atom without telling the paths apart. (Claude's statement, after the critic.)

**Which one is the live risk.** The planetary ephemerides already cap any drag near 10⁻⁶² per Moment (4419 §5, order of magnitude), far below 4492's
coherence bound of about 10⁻²⁰ per Moment. So an energy-carrying trail is already excluded far beyond what coherence needs. What is left is the energy-free
record.

**The condition, stated.** The requirement is that the released DP's final state carries no path information: it must be the same whichever path the core
took, or its record of the bond must be cleared in the Nexus. A return to its exact prior state is enough but not required: a DP shifted by the same amount
on either path, or indistinguishable from its neighbours, tells nothing. What would spoil the fringes is a DP left turned or shifted in a way that depends on
which path the core took, or a release that hands the tie on to neighbouring DPs (a dispersal under R-COLLISION-DISPERSES, 4491), which spreads it along the
path. The 10⁻²⁰ bound (4492 S1) is on the path distinguishability left per Moment, not on the size of the residue. (Claude's statement.)

**A note on bends.** The gratings change the atom's direction. At a change of motion the release must still leave nothing path-dependent. (Noted, not
pursued.)

## 9. Reply to the founder

That works, and it matches what you said in October: "charge in front, discharge in back." The cloud travels with the core, and its members are picked up
ahead, bonded while the core goes by, and let go behind. Nothing is left in the wake. It's the standard picture for anything moving steadily through a
medium, below the speed at which it would start shedding waves.

One refinement. "No trail" isn't quite the same as "no slowing." A trail that carries energy would slow the atom, but planetary orbits already rule that out
almost entirely. A trail can also cost no energy: a DP left turned or shifted in a way that depends on which path the core took. That kind would spoil the
stripes without slowing anything, so it's the real risk. The condition is that each DP, once released, says nothing about which path the core took. It
doesn't have to be exactly as it was before, but it must not hand the bond on to its neighbours.

One new puzzle: the DPs along the path the core doesn't take are never touched by it, so how do the arcs there belong to the quantum?
