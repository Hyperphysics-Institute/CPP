## 7. Answer to his "What do you think?"

I think his picture fits the division where the turning is light's and the push fades. "Bends like light" alone doesn't decide it, since both
divisions give light's bending at the limit. But he says more: near light speed almost all of the electron's cloud is carrying its motion, as
separated DP pairs, so its structure is nearly light's. If its make-up becomes light's, its parts should too: it turns as light turns, and it loses the
push that light doesn't feel. Standard physics has close parallels: a fast particle's bending tends to light's, and the gravity of a mass sped up
toward light speed becomes that of light.

What his picture fixes is the end point. At light speed the push is gone and only the turning is left. It doesn't yet fix how fast the push fades on
the way. If the push acts only on the resting share, it fades in proportion to that share. That overshoots relativity by about a tenth sideways at
half light speed. Relativity needs the square of that share. One candidate supplies the extra factor: the resting part is a moving clock, so it might
take the push only once per beat of its slowed rhythm. But that candidate only works if a fast electron answers a push equally in every direction,
which isn't how relativity's inertia behaves. So I've asked him how the push actually reaches the electron, with several possibilities.

There is also a puzzle in his limit. The electron's rest mass stays the same at every speed. If near light speed only the central CP isn't carrying
the motion, then either that one CP holds the whole rest mass, or the cloud's DPs hold rest mass and carry the motion at the same time.
