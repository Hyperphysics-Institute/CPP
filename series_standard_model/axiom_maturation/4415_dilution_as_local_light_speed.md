## 4. Two conditions, and one disagreement

**(i) μ-ε must follow only a field's gravitating energy, and here I disagree with part of his statement.** A static
electric or magnetic field does not add DI-bit counts (AP-4 fixes them); it raises payload magnitudes, and with them SSV_abs,
linearly in the field. If μ-ε tracked SSV_abs, light would slow measurably inside a capacitor or a magnet. It does not: in a
1 T field quantum electrodynamics predicts index shifts of about 9 × 10⁻²⁴ and 5 × 10⁻²⁴ (birefringence 4 × 10⁻²⁴), and the
PVLAS experiment's final result (Ejlli et al. 2020) is consistent with zero at a sensitivity a few times above even that
(script part 4). So ordinary fields can raise the μ-ε that sets light speed only by their gravitating share: a 1 T field's
energy weighs about 4 × 10⁻¹² kg per cubic metre, a potential of order 10⁻³⁹ over a metre. **His "μ-ε is higher in a space
with more fields" holds for gravitational fields and, in practice, not for electric and magnetic ones.** (Light is slowed in
matter, n > 1, but that is the bound charges' polarization, which must be kept distinct from the sea's μ-ε.)

This is a real problem for CPP, because gravity's K is itself tied to raised SSV_abs: **why does gravity's SSV_abs raise K
and a static field's not?** One registered hook bears on it: AP-5's D4 clarification (3706) says a stationary field's static
overflow count "is re-supplied each Moment and is energy-free". Whether that is what separates the two is owed.

**(ii) The gradient must supply GR's change of speed.** The rendered rule keeps a body's local speed fixed. In GR a body
crossing a static well speeds up inside it by the energy-conservation amount (part 3: at outside speed 0.1 and depth
U = 10⁻³, local speed 0.1094 inside; at U = 10⁻⁶, a rise of 10⁻⁵). WC-DP-SEA-PV omits PV's mass rule, which is where PV gets
matter's fall, so in this convention that change must come from 4403's limb-difference acceleration (which already gives
Newton) and be undone on the way out; together with the μ-ε rescaling it must give GR's motion, including light deflection
(the factor 2 PV supplies), perihelion advance (which needs K's 2U² term, on record) and the Shapiro delay. In this
convention the split is: μ-ε gives the reversible slowing with light; the gradient gives the fall.

## 7. Answer to his "What do you think?"

On the main point he is right, and it removes the obstacle I raised. The slowing I found came from treating each step as
rebuilt from the last one, so any watering-down compounded and never came back. In his picture the sea's DP-arcs and
separation renew the front-back difference each Moment; the surroundings only change how far a step carries, through the
local speed of light. So where μ-ε is higher the particle slows exactly as light slows, and when it leaves it is back to its
old speed with nothing lost. That is the rule already on record for light near a mass, now extended to matter. (That no
instrument riding along can tell is my addition; it depends on rulers shrinking there too, which is still a working
assumption.) It does leave a question: the arcs are organized by the particle's motion and the motion by the arcs, and what
keeps that loop from fading still has to be shown.

He is also right that nothing "comes back": every GP sends the same number. That means my 4414 count calculation, which gave
exactly the right speed, no longer applies. The front-back difference must be in what the DI-bits carry, and whether that
gives exactly the particle's speed is open again.

Where I disagree: more fields of every kind cannot raise μ-ε in the way that slows light. Light is not measurably slowed
inside a charged capacitor or a strong magnet; experiments find no change down to a few parts in 10²². Electric and magnetic fields
can raise it only through the tiny weight of their energy. So it is gravity's fields that slow light and matter together,
and the theory owes an account of why a mass raises μ-ε and a charged plate does not. Last, because the slowing with light
leaves a body's local speed unchanged, the speeding-up of a body falling through a well must come from the gravity rule of
4403, in exactly general relativity's amount.
