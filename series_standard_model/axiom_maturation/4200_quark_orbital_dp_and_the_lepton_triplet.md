## 1. Terminology — "GT gap" meant two things

At 4199 I wrote "the Gamow–Teller gap" meaning **the gap in our scheme**: 4199's picture gives only
anti-parallel lepton spins (Fermi), and Gamow–Teller decays need parallel ones. Nuclear physics uses
"GT gap / GT quenching" for an unrelated feature of nuclear excitation spectra (the pasted Copilot
text is fair on that). **Nothing in that nuclear-structure problem is ours to solve here.** The
nucleon-spin summary pasted with it is correct and is the fact used in §2.

## 2. What the measured decay requires

Neutron and proton are both spin ½. In a **Fermi** decay the nucleon's spin projection is unchanged
and the lepton pair leaves in a **singlet** (spins opposite — 4199's picture). In a **Gamow–Teller**
decay the nucleon's projection flips by one unit and the lepton pair leaves in a **triplet** (spins
parallel). So a GT decay needs **exactly one unit of angular momentum handed from the nucleon to the
pair** — not a portion. Rows verbatim (D-11):

```
g_A = 1.2754;  GT:F = 3 g_A^2 : 1 = 4.88 : 1
fraction of free-neutron decays with PARALLEL lepton spins (GT) = 0.830
fraction with anti-parallel lepton spins (Fermi)                = 0.170
```

## 3. The postulate against that

- **Source: right.** The founder puts the unit in the **quark's own orbital ZBW DP**, not in the +qCP
  core I asked about. That is the 0.631 fm circulation of the glossary's quark entry (4178), and it
  is the quark's spin. A spin-flip of the nucleon *is* a change in that orbit's sense.
- **Destination: right.** The unit goes to the Sea DP that becomes the antineutrino, pulled *"from
  the volume next to the … DP orbit."* With it, the antineutrino's spin is turned parallel to the
  electron's: the triplet. Without it, 4199's singlet. **The two channels are the two cases of one
  event: whether the adjacent quark orbital hands over its unit.**
- **Switch: fails as stated.** He proposes the orbital must first be *activated* by kinetic energy
  from random collisions. But a **free neutron at rest in vacuum decays by GT 83% of the time** (§2).
  Nothing collides with it. So the ground-state quark orbital must already be able to hand over the
  unit; activation cannot be the switch. (Activation may still matter in nuclei — that is the real
  GT-quenching problem — but the free neutron rules it out as the mechanism.)
- **"A portion": no.** Angular momentum is transferred in whole units or not at all; a partial
  transfer would leave the lepton pair in no state that exists.

## 4. What the branching then asks of CPP

The measured ratio is GT : F = 3 g_A² : 1. The **3** is the three orientations of a spin-1 pair —
geometry the picture supplies for free once the transfer exists. **g_A = 1.275** is the one number
that would have to come out of CPP: the relative ease of pulling the Sea DP *with* the quark's unit
against *without* it. **Not attempted.** If it came out near 1 the picture would be in trouble; near
1.3 it would be a result.
