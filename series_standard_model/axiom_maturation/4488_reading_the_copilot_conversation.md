## 3. Tranche 3, first part: "all interactions entangle," and the back-reaction

- **His thesis, in its most favourable reading (D-15).** Every interaction leaves the partners correlated, and the correlation is entanglement wherever the
  outcome had coherent alternatives; where it seems absent it is spread out of reach, not gone. That reading is right for the cases 4481 discussed (distributed
  entanglement, decoherence). Copilot's exceptions are of a different kind and are true zeros, not hidden ties: an exchange with only one possible outcome
  (the exchanged quantity already definite), alternatives that act alike on the partner, and maps that take product states to product states (a swap passes
  entanglement on without adding any). He accepted the qualification in the conversation (4485, "I accept your qualification, and limitations").
- **His bonded-entity claim, at its strongest, is standard physics.** He holds that "there is an entanglement-type of connection within every
  nuclear/chemically bonded entity," even in a warm environment. Bound states are entangled inside: the two electrons of a covalent bond form a spin singlet,
  and an atom's electron and nucleus are entangled in their relative and centre-of-mass motion. That inner entanglement survives warm surroundings because the
  internal levels are gapped, so ordinary collisions cannot read or disturb it. In practice no experiment reaches it as a Bell test; in principle it is there.
  (Claude's statement; this is his claim, made exact.) Which bound groups carry one group entity is 4479 (a) and 4478 (h), unchanged.
- **The back-reaction (his second question in tranche 3).** In its most favourable reading — the mirror's recoil is correlated with the photon's direction,
  and a later measurement of the photon picks which recoil applies — he is right, and so is Copilot that no further kick is delivered and the mirror's own
  statistics do not change (THEO-QM-5). His reason it cannot be detected (the recoil is small) and Copilot's (the recoil states cannot be told apart) measure one
  thing when "small" means small against the mirror's own spread of momentum σ_p: the overlap of the two recoil states is about exp(−q²/8σ_p²) for a momentum
  difference q, near 1 when q ≪ σ_p. A second route to the same result is the surroundings recording the recoil (decoherence). (Claude's reading.) This is
  4481's readable-kick condition again.

## 5. "The entanglement matrix will produce a displacement" (D-15)

**Most favourable reading:** the entanglement element decides **which** of the displacements already allowed to a CP occurs, in step with its partners — it
selects; it does not push. That matches his September 2025 website wording ("Quantum Group Entities (QGEs) orchestrate saltatory Displacement Increments ...
under conservation constraints"; archive, Part 4), and Copilot's summarized answer in tranche 4 describes CPP the same way. In that reading Copilot's caution
("do not make entanglement a new force"; "it shapes the probabilities of locally allowed, conservation-compatible GP transitions") is **agreement**, under two
conditions.

**The conditions.** (1) The selection adds no momentum or energy of its own. By R-CP-ONLY-SOURCE (AP-4's snapshot re-imprint) the CP is the only source of
every signal's size and direction, so a push that no CP sources has no place; AP-4d gives energy no payload slot; and a push carrying its own momentum would
count momentum twice. (2) The selection weights at one side, summed over the other side's outcomes, must not depend on the other side's setting (THEO-QM-5).
Choosing among allowed moves signals just as a push would if B's weights shift with A's setting. His comparison with the strong and electric forces holds in
one respect (it changes where CPs go) and must fail in the other (those forces carry momentum through the relay; this must not). (Claude's statement of the
conditions.)
