## 3. Three measured constraints (script)

**1. Every photon of one colour carries the same energy, within resolution** (script part 1). Single-photon calorimeters measure each photon's energy one at
a time, independently of its colour, which is selected optically. Transition-edge sensors (Cabrera and others, 1998) do this for optical photons with a
resolution of about 0.15 eV, a spread of a few percent per photon. X-ray microcalorimeters reach about 2 eV at 6 keV, a few parts in 10⁴. Such sensors have
also timed and weighed photons from the Crab pulsar (Romani and others, 1999). Photons of one colour give one energy, h × frequency.

**So energy ÷ frequency is h for every photon: a fixed action per cycle of the colour, and that is measured.** His "not fixed; more displacement, more action"
fits experiment under one reading of "cycle":
- **Per Moment:** the action in one Moment is about energy × the Moment's duration, which does grow with the energy. On this reading his statement is right.
- **Per cycle of the colour:** the action is h, the same for every photon. If a larger displacement added action per colour cycle while the in-and-out rate
  stayed the same, photons of one colour would come in a spread of energies (illustratively, 1.5 times the displacement would mean 1.5 times the
  energy). That is not seen.

So his mechanism must lock the displacement to the rate: a bigger displacement must come with a faster in-and-out rate, in exact proportion.

**2. Every colour travels at one speed, and wavelength × frequency = c** (script part 2). Fermi's gamma-ray burst GRB 090510 (Abdo and others, 2009): after
about 7.3 billion years of travel, a 31 GeV photon arrived 0.829 s after the trigger. Assuming the high- and low-energy photons left together, their speeds
differ by at most a few parts in 10¹⁸, at gamma-ray energies. At lower energies the evidence is that wavelength and frequency, measured separately, agree on
c (for example Evenson and others, 1972, with a 3.39 μm methane-stabilized laser, to a few parts in 10⁹). Two readings of his "propagation rate":
- **How often the displacement swings in and out** (the frequency): it rises with the energy, and the wavelength follows as c ÷ rate. This fits.
- **How fast the displacement moves along the photon**, an internal speed that changes with energy: if the pattern is rigid and fixed to a photon moving at c,
  as his radio-wave comparison says, the wavelength in the lab is c ÷ frequency whatever the internal speed, so the internal speed would not set the
  colour. And if wavelength = internal speed ÷ frequency, with the speed depending on energy, then wavelength × frequency would differ from c for different
  colours: the dispersion the measurements exclude.

§5 asks which he means.

**3. Photons come with no lower energy limit** (script part 3). If action grows with displacement and there is a smallest displacement with a fixed action,
photons would have a smallest energy. But photons are seen with no lower cutoff: FM radio at 100 MHz carries about 4 × 10⁻⁷ eV per photon, AM radio about
4 × 10⁻⁹ eV, and extremely-low-frequency waves around 10⁻¹⁴ eV. There is also a scale problem. If the smallest step carried h in one Moment, and a Moment is
near the Planck time (the Moment's size is not fixed here), that would be an energy of about 10²⁹ eV. So the smallest increment, whatever it is, cannot set
a floor on photon energy.
