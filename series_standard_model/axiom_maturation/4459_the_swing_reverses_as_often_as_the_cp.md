## 3. The swing reverses as often as the CP (script part 2)

This restates, from his dipole picture, what 4445–4449 found (4445 §2: a charge swinging at the light's own frequency gives hydrogen's 1.6 ns packet; 4446:
the Compton rate and the orbits' revolutions both fail; 4448, 4449: the rate does; also 4453, 4455; and 4458 case (a) excluded the carrier seen directly).

**A dipole radiates at the rate it oscillates.** Each time the CP reverses its direction, the DP displacement it radiates reverses. So the swing along the
photon reverses as often as the radiating CP did, the colour is that reversal rate (one full cycle for each out-and-back), and the crest spacing is c divided
by it.

That puts a number on the emitting CP:
- **Hydrogen's Lyman-α** (121.5 nm, 2.467 × 10¹⁵ Hz) needs the radiating motion to go out and back about 2.5 × 10¹⁵ times a second, reversing about
  4.9 × 10¹⁵ times.
- **The free electron's zitterbewegung** (Schrödinger's, for the Dirac electron, 2mc²/h ≈ 2.47 × 10²⁰ Hz; the corpus also carries other ZBW rates, an
  open conflict), if it radiated, would give that rate: wavelength h/(2mc) ≈ 1.2 pm, half the Compton wavelength, photon energy 2mc² ≈ 1.02 MeV, more than
  the electron's own rest energy. Hydrogen's lines are not seen there.

**So the part of the CP's motion that radiates during the emission must run at ΔE/h. Any faster ZBW motion underneath must not radiate**, as his 4445
picture of a mass's non-radiating ZBW already requires.

**His second answer** names the energy difference between the orbitals as what drives the oscillation. He does not give its rate. A rate of ΔE/h is the
Bohr condition (4446), not something he stated here. (A classical comparison: an orbiting charge radiates at its orbital rate, and for a one-level drop ΔE/h
lies between the two orbits' rates (4449); that is the correspondence principle, not evidence for the mechanism.)

**On the word "ZBW".** His first answer calls the radiating oscillation a ZBW oscillation. In 4446 he said "the ZBW oscillation is not the photon; the packet
is", and the photon is "an envelope holding a total energy sum of many ZBW cycles". So either his ZBW in the atom is the slower swing at ΔE/h, or the fast
ZBW rides underneath without radiating (4458 case c) and what reverses at the colour's rate is the slower swing. The existing candidates for the two rates
coexisting are 4454's ringing cohort (a collective mode) and 4455's relayed chain. Claude cannot tell which he means from the text; §6 asks.

## 7. Answer to his "What do you think?"

That's the piece that was missing. A swing that goes forward and back with the CP, and that cancels when two parts meet out of step, is what light needs.
It explains the dark bands: two swings half a cycle apart add to zero, and, if energy goes as the square of the swing, the energy that isn't there goes to
the bright bands instead, so nothing is lost. And if his forward and back is across the beam, it gives polarization.

It also fits what we found about the source. A dipole radiates at the rate it swings, so the photon's swing reverses every time the radiating CP reverses.
For hydrogen's ultraviolet line, that means a back-and-forth about two and a half million billion times a second. The free electron's ZBW is about a hundred
thousand times faster, and if that radiated, the light would be gamma rays of about a million electron-volts. So whatever radiates has to run at the
colour's rate, ΔE/h, and any faster motion underneath must stay silent. His second answer says the energy difference drives it, though not at what rate.

So I've asked two things: which way his forward and reverse points, across the photon or along it; and whether the back-and-forth he calls a ZBW in the
atom is the free electron's ZBW or a slower swing.
