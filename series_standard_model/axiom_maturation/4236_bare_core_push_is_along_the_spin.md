## 2. What the signs say

The ejection rule throws an electron *against* its own spin, always (4201; ONESIGN item 1, the primitive). The
neutral-current push on a passing electron is along +Q_W σ (4222). Isospin part: +1 at a bare core — **along** the
spin; −2 at a linear oscillator — **against** it, doubled.

So an elastic capture-and-re-eject of the passing electron by the ejection rule, the route (a) of 4213, has the
right sign and a plausible double weight at the linear oscillator, and **the wrong sign at a bare core.** Whatever
happens to a passing electron at a transient W⁰ next to a bare +qCP — an up quark's core with no resident
−eCP — pushes it *along* its spin, which the handed throw never does. Route (a) as posed cannot be the bare-core
event. The occupancy reading of 4235 (−2 = reversed event + cancelled core event) survives in form — it needs a
bare-core event of one sign and a linear-oscillator event of the other — but the bare-core event is not the handed
throw and is not described anywhere in the corpus.

## 3. What this is, and the question it leaves (PD-006(a))

This is the Standard Model's T₃ structure read literally: the electron's axial coupling is fixed, and the sign of
the push flips with the constituent's weak isospin. In CPP the electron's handedness is its own A and is fixed
(THEO-CHIR-1); what flips must be a property of the *constituent the W⁰ forms next to*. The corpus gives the W⁰
centroid one act — capture a linear −eCP and re-eject it against its spin (Prop. capture, 4201). It gives no act
for a passing electron at a centroid beside a bare core.

**The picture question:** a transient W⁰ forms beside an up quark — a +qCP core with its orbital eDP and no linear
−eCP. A free electron passes. What does the centroid do with the electron's −eCP: capture and re-eject it by the
handed rule (then the push is against its spin, and the measured sign at a bare core is wrong), or something
else — deflect it without capture, or capture and hand it back the way it came? The measured sign says the
bare-core act sends the electron *along* its spin, one unit; the linear-oscillator act sends it *against* its
spin, two units. Route (a) needs the bare-core act named.
