## 8. Answer to his "What do you think?"

His picture would settle the question if it holds in CPP's own terms, and it reconciles two things that looked opposed. Because the
electron's partly real cloud sits a Planck length away, an inverse-square fall-off makes it outweigh everything else by tens of orders of
magnitude: a nearby atom by about 10⁴⁶, even the whole Earth by 10²⁹. So everything arriving at the electron's GP would be, in practice, its
own share.

Three things stand in the way.
- **The DI-bit rules.** CPP's rules allow only one DI-bit per GP from each source, so the near cloud's advantage has to come from what its
  DI-bits carry, not from how many there are.
- **The relay.** CPP's relay brings far sources in with the gentler 1/r reach of a potential, and under that reach the Earth's, and the whole
  universe's, charges would outweigh the cloud unless matter counts only by its tiny gravitational share.
- **Gravity.** If the near cloud drowns out everything, it drowns out gravity too, so gravity would have to act on the moving electron some
  other way.

On the ordinary DPs, the registered rules may already agree with him. If a DP's two charges sit on one GP, what goes out from that point
cancels, and only pulling them apart makes it count. I've asked him that directly.
