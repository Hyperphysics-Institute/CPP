## 3. "Is there a maximum amount of effect?" — monogamy

Two readings, both with answers. **(1) The effect on one CP per Moment** is already capped in the corpus: a CP moves at most a bounded step per Moment, and
AP-5 caps what a GP takes in (D > K saturation). **(2) The amount of entanglement** — his "percentage total effect" — is capped by monogamy: a unit has a fixed
budget, and what it shares with one partner it cannot also share with another. For three two-level units, the tangle (0 for none, 1 for complete) obeys

## 4. What the ledger must hold

His first accounting method — each CP keeps a table of its "percentage of entanglement" with every other CP — fails on two counts, both in the table above:

- **A pairwise table misses ties that belong to a group.** In the GHZ state every pair shows zero entanglement, yet A is completely tied to B and C together
  (row T1). (A pairwise table of classical correlations would not be all zeros, but it still could not tell GHZ from a plain mixture.) Three-photon GHZ
  correlations have been measured (Pan et al., Nature 403, 515, 2000), so nature uses such ties. This bears on CONV-031 §3 adoption 4(b), "multipartite
  entanglement within the CPP ontology."
- **The full joint state is far larger than a table.** For N two-level units it takes 2^N numbers; a pairwise table takes about N²/2 (row S1). At N = 300 the
  joint state already has more entries than there are particles in the visible universe.

His second method, the Nexus computing the vector, is the right shape (§1), but the vector cannot be N entries; held exactly, it is 2^N. He asked the
practical question himself. The physics offers two compressions that need no new rule:

- **Locality.** States reached by local dynamics from weakly entangled starts, and the ground states of gapped local systems, obey "area laws": their
  entanglement grows with a region's boundary, not its volume, and they store compactly as tensor networks with a bounded bond size (Copilot's χ). Generic
  states after long evolution do not; they are close to maximally entangled. There the second compression is what keeps the ledger small.
- **Decoherence.** Once two alternatives have left records spread irreversibly through the surroundings, beyond any practical recovery, the cross terms
  between them affect no future result for all practical purposes, and dropping them is safe. The rule must not prune records that can still be undone: a
  quantum eraser recombines branches that left a record (4481; 4488 (e)).

## 5. His truncation "below one GP displacement"

His criterion is the size of a tie's effect on a CP: drop it when its effect would be below one GP displacement at the finest scale. His example is
large-scale collisions, whose ties are spread thin. Three readings:

- **(T-a) Drop only what can no longer change any outcome.** The decoherence pruning of §4, with the eraser limit. It predicts nothing new. It is what the
  corpus already holds: the QM development notes say CPP "derives its collapse mechanism (bit-sea decoherence) from first principles already in the
  framework, not as a new postulate" (`QM_series_development.md`), and QM-4 states "No collapse postulate is required," with global unitarity preserved by the
  Nexus (thm:unitary). Momentum: dropping branches conserves it on average, as in standard measurement; it is conserved in each run only when the branching
  respects total momentum. This answers his worry about large-scale collisions in the same terms standard quantum mechanics does. (Claude's statement.)
- **(T-c) Drop weak ties while they could still interfere — his criterion read at its strongest.** Cut any tie whose effect per CP falls below one GP
  step. In tensor-network terms this drops small Schmidt weights, the standard truncation, but done in the dynamics it is not unitary. Under monogamy the
  weak ties are mostly the thin environmental ones, while a large object keeps its strong internal ties, so this is close to the opposite of the familiar
  collapse models. Its testable signature is the loss of small-amplitude branches that are still coherent: low-amplitude interference and weak-value
  measurements would show it. (Claude's reading.)
- **(T-b) Drop by the size of the object.** The GRW/CSL family of collapse models: superpositions of heavy enough objects lose their interference. A
  secondary reading of his words. It is bounded by experiment: molecules beyond 25,000 atomic mass units, about 2,000 atoms, have shown interference fringes at
  more than 90% of the expected visibility (Fein et al., Nature Physics 15, 1242, 2019), and entangled macroscopic mechanical oscillators of about 10¹² atoms
  have been shown (Ockeloen-Korppi et al., Nature 556, 478, 2018). Collapse models are constrained as a rate–length pair, not a sharp threshold, and they
  conserve mean momentum while heating slightly, which is bounded too.

(T-b) and (T-c) would both contradict QM-4's thm:unitary, so either needs his ruling, not Claude's choice. The registered homes for a threshold are OPEN-QM-5
("Entanglement Decoherence Threshold at ~10¹⁵ eV ... from Nexus lattice path limits") and OPEN-P-SD-4 (decoherence threshold for Nexus visibility).

## 6. The contingent force and the memory corollary

- **"An additional contingent force (conservative over the system of paired photons)."** At its strongest: what is done to A changes what B will do, and
  the pair's momentum balances. In the crystal the two photons' momenta add up to the pump photon's, with opposite sideways momenta, so finding A's fixes
  B's. Agreed, under 4488 §5's two conditions: no momentum of its own (the balance was fixed at the source, and the measurement picks which), and B's chances,
  averaged over A's outcomes, unchanged by what is done at A. "Contingent" is the right word: B's response depends on A's outcome, not on A's setting alone.
  (Claude's reading.)
- **Memory by tracing the light cone.** At its strongest: if the dynamics is reversible, as QM-4's thm:unitary and QM-1 v2.0's unitarity Proposition (sketch
  grade) have it, no past is ever erased; it stays written in the present joint state of everything the event touched. That holds in principle. Two notes:
  the record is spread across the whole light cone, so only something with a global view, the Nexus, could read it back; and any real cut, (T-b) or (T-c),
  erases what it drops. Under (T-a), memory rests on the records within the realized branch, not on reversibility of the whole. One way to keep full
  retrieval alongside a real cut is two ledgers: a working state the CPs move by, cut, and a complete archive the Nexus keeps. (Claude's suggestion; his
  choice.) Nothing here is claimed about biological memory.
- **"How a CP remembers anything."** Under A1′'s RISC division of labour, GPs compute, hold and refresh state; CPs carry attributes; DI-bits carry and
  reset. No registered object keeps a history. On his Nexus route, the CPs need no table at all; whatever is remembered is held by the Nexus.

## 9. Reply to the founder

You've answered §6. The shared connection is the Nexus. It collects every CP's report each Moment, works out one vector for the whole connected system, and
hands it back as each CP's input, with the GP's fifth element as the local port. The repo already has the mathematical form of this (SD-4's global Nexus
field, which adds up every GP's change); yours is the physical picture of it. It passes the Bell test from 4488, because B's result comes from a shared
calculation, not from anything B carried.

On your open question, yes, there's a maximum. Each two-level unit has an entanglement budget of one, and what it shares with one partner it can't also
share with another. So everything is entangled with everything, but thinly. A Bell pair is strongly tied only because neither photon has spent its budget
elsewhere yet.

Two cautions on the ledger. A table of pairwise percentages can't be it: three particles can be fully tied as a group while every pair shows zero. And the
exact joint state doubles in size with each added unit, so the Nexus needs a way to compress it.

On your cutoff, your criterion is the size of the effect, so it drops weak ties, not heavy objects. Dropping only what can never be recovered is what QM-4
already says and changes no prediction. Dropping weak ties while they could still interfere would be a real change, and it would contradict QM-4's
unitarity, so it's your call.
