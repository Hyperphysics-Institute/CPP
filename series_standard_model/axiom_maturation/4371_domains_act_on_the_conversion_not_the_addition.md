## 1. The idea

As PSR spheres go from partly filled, to touching or fully populated, to fully overlapping, the founder proposes that
each domain has its own law of addition and re-radiation, not the inverse-square law of the partly-filled regime. His
case study takes "touching" and "fully populated" to happen at the same spacing.

## 2. In empty space the addition cannot change (script part 1)

The relay updates a GP from an average over its neighbourhood. When its sphere is partly filled, the average is over a
thin band at the PSR. When the sphere is fully populated, the average is over the whole ball, with no empty core left
to be informed by re-radiation. That is the founder's "no longer any empty space inside the PSR".

But outside a source, the average of 1/r over **any** of these regions about a point equals 1/r at that point:
- exactly, for a shell (zero difference at three test points);
- exactly, for the whole ball;
- by Monte Carlo, for a band: full ball 0.33322, band 0.33318, thin shell 0.33317, against 1/3.

This is Newton's shell theorem, and GR-1j's statics rest on it (T-2). So **in empty space a body's stress keeps falling
off as 1/r whatever the fill**: the change of domain alters how a GP is informed, not what it adds up to. Inside matter,
where CPs' PSRs touch and overlap, the domains are real departures from simple addition (AP-5's saturation protocol).
They act on the interior and on the body's total, not on the field outside.

## 3. Where the domains can act: the conversion (script parts 2–3)

A GP's own sphere fills with its own DI-bits as its PSR shrinks: fill f = N/(GPs in its sphere) = 1/(8q³)
(R-DIBIT-COUNT-AT-FLOOR). This is the founder's progression from partly filled to fully populated, happening in empty
space at every GP.

| point | q | fill f | speed-up Einstein needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| ordinary space | 1.000 | 0.125 | 1 |
| photon sphere, steady curve | 0.607 | 0.560 | — |
| photon sphere, Einstein | 0.577 | 0.650 | **1.077** |
| cap, steady curve (ε = ln 2) | 0.500 | 1.000 | — |
| cap, Einstein (q = ½) | 0.500 | 1.000 | **1.125** |

The speed-up is S = (d ln q/dε)_Einstein / (d ln q/dε)_steady = 1/(1 − ε²/4).

- **The sphere is more than half full right where the pitch is decided.** So a conversion that shrinks the PSR faster
  per unit of stress *as its sphere fills* would act at the photon sphere, and would be invisible in ordinary space,
  where the fill is fixed at ⅛.
- **The amount needed is modest:** the PSR shrinks about 8% faster per unit of stress at the photon sphere and 12.5%
  faster at the cap.
- **Consistency:** Einstein's curve reaches the floor q = ½ at ε = ⅔, which is AP-5's ratified cap v = ⅔. The corpus's
  cap and its black-hole floor already agree on Einstein's curve.

## 4. The price

Any speed-up S ≠ 1 brings back a residual of order ε₀² in a local measurement made in a background (4365 §4, Padé:
+ε₀²/4). For G it is unobservable. For α it matters only if α's reading carries the lapse's sensitivity
(TODO-4364-EPSABS). Below the cap, the speed-up has to come from the conversion, so the 4365 uniqueness argument
("steady percentage is the only invariant form") is given up in strong fields. It is kept in ordinary space, where the
fill does not change.

## 5. Question to the founder (physical picture)

A grid point's Planck sphere fills with its own DI-bits as it shrinks: about one-eighth full in ordinary space, more
than half full near a black hole, completely full at the black-hole point. As the sphere fills up, does it shrink faster
for each added unit of stress? If it shrinks about 8% faster when it is around 60% full, and about 12% faster when
completely full, black holes ring exactly at Einstein's pitch, and nothing changes in ordinary space.
