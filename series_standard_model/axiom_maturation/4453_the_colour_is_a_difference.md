## 2. His reading of the histogram (script parts 1, 2 and 4)

**On its own, the histogram allows a delay before emission.** 4452 said the histogram alone cannot fix each photon's shape. Taken together, three
measurements do:

- **The histogram's sharp start rules out a long dwell before a full packet** (script part 4). If an exponential dwell were followed by a packet a
  lifetime long, the clicks would start near zero and rise to a peak before falling. The measured single-atom histogram rises sharply at the moment of
  excitation, within the excitation pulse and the detector's timing, and then falls as a pure exponential (Darquié and others, 2005; 4452). So any
  dwell must be much shorter than the lifetime.
- **The line's width rules out a short burst after a delay** (script part 1). A delay does not change a packet's spread of colours; it only shifts its
  start, so the width is set by the packet's own length. Measured excitation line widths, with Doppler broadening removed, agree with 1/(2π ×
  lifetime): sodium's D2 line about 9.8 MHz, rubidium's about 6.1 MHz. (Reference tables compute these widths from the lifetimes. Relating an
  absorption width to the emitted packet relies on reciprocity: what an atom absorbs and what it emits share one line shape.) A burst of 1000 cycles
  would spread over hundreds of thousands of MHz.
- **Two-photon interference rules out short bursts at random delays.** Photons from two separate atoms interfere well (Beugnon and others, 2006,
  consistent; near-perfect in later experiments, 4452). Short bursts arriving at random delays would rarely overlap in time, so they would rarely
  interfere.

**Expected coherence length.** A photon lasting a lifetime should keep its fringes, when split and recombined, over path differences of order light speed
× lifetime: about 4.9 m for sodium's line and 0.48 m for Lyman-α. These figures are expected, not measured here; quantum-dot single photons show the
corresponding fall of visibility against delay (Santori and others, 2002). A 1000-cycle burst would lose its fringes beyond about a millimetre.

**So the emission begins at once and lasts about a lifetime, fading.** The click comes somewhere along the way. When a photon is detected is itself what
quantum mechanics leaves random. His "commits" may point at that measurement question, which is noted, not adopted. That is why the line's width and the
histogram's decay time are the same number.
