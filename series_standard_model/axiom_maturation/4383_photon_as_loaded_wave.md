## 1. The founder's two-part photon (his words, summarised)

The photon is a correlation wave: the whole volumetric disturbance of the DP sea produced by an accelerated charge. It
has two parts:

1. **The GP_PSR (the DI-bit shell),** which carries the LSP and passes its influence on at the speed of light.
2. **The CP (half a DP) inside the PSR.** The CP is pushed by the LSP the shell carries and moves an amount set by
   SSV_net (V_i). **The CP does not move at the speed of light.**

The PSR shrinks as SSV_abs grows near a mass. In a thick shell, SSV_net moves like and opposite charges according to the
sign of the shell the DP is in. He opens with a question: given that a DI-bit reaches anywhere from 0 to 100% of the PSR
computed at GP_origin, **where is the PSR, and what propagates the photon's PSR?** (§5.)

*(Claude's gloss: the PSR shrinkage is the first-order effect of GR-1i L551–556.)*

## 2. What this would remove: the speed-limit obstruction (4382 §4.1)

4382's lead obstruction was that light already moves at the top speed, so no landing could be "fast". In the founder's
picture, what moves at the top speed is the **shell's influence**. The sign-dependent response sits on the **CP**, which
moves slowly and can be helped or held either way.

**For that response to change light's speed at all, the CPs must load the shell wave**: they must feed back on its
propagation, as the charges of a dielectric load a light wave. **This is Claude's reading, not in the founder's text**:
he says the shell carries the influence and the CP is pushed, not that the CP slows the shell. If the CPs do load the
wave, this is a concrete form of 4382 §4.1's escape (i), "light is already held in ordinary space". The vacuum speed of
light would then be the loaded speed.

**That raises a tension about which speed is c.** If vacuum light is loaded, the bare shell step (one PSR per Moment) is
faster than the observed c, and only the signal front travels at one PSR per Moment. The founder says the shell
"transfers its influence at the speed of light". Which one is c is a question for him (§6 Q3).

## 3. If the CPs load the wave: what a half-and-half change does (script parts 1, 1b, 2)

**Leading model: the pairing is inside every DP** (part 1b). A DP sits in one shell, and its + and − CPs respond
oppositely (founder). Its loading is the sum of its two halves' compliances.

| what changes by equal ± amounts on the two halves of a DP | DP loading | light |
|---|---|---|
| **stiffness**: the push needed to move a CP a set distance | 1/(1 − δ²) | **slower**: v = √(1 − δ²) if the CPs supply all the vacuum loading |
| **compliance**: the distance a set push moves a CP | unchanged, to all orders | unchanged |

Compliance is 1/stiffness, so both rows describe the same hold-back. What differs is **which quantity changes by equal
± amounts**: equal ± in stiffness gives a net increase in loading; equal ± in compliance gives none.

**Domain-scale alternative** (part 2). If the ± sites sat in separate domains instead of inside each DP, a 3D random
network gives speed ≈ 1 − δ²/3 (stiffness) or ≈ 1 + δ²/6 (compliance). That matches the standard second-order formula
ε_eff = ⟨ε⟩ − ⟨δε²⟩/(3⟨ε⟩) (Landau & Lifshitz, *Electrodynamics of Continuous Media*, §9). In 1D, the long-wave speed is
the harmonic mean of stiffness for any sign arrangement (part 1). In a 1D line ε plays the inertia role, which is why the
1D compliance and inertia cases agree. The 3D arrangement on the frustrated 600-cell (3827) is open.

**The coefficient** (½ per DP, ⅓ for 3D domains) differs from 4382's 1D form 1 − κ²ϱ². κ absorbs it.

**When averaging applies.** The quasi-static averaging needs two things:
- the wavelength ≫ GP spacing, which always holds;
- the wave's frequency ≪ the CPs' response rate.

"The CP moves slowly" may break the second. The loading would then depend on frequency, possibly with the opposite sign
above a resonance. **Observational constraints:** gravitational light bending and Shapiro delay are achromatic (radio
and optical VLBI agree), and gamma-ray-burst limits on vacuum dispersion are tight. Any loading change must be
frequency-independent from radio to gamma rays.

## 4. A first-order hazard: the response in ordinary space (script part 3)

Suppose like and opposite shells already move CPs differently in ordinary space (a modulation δ₀), and the well adds δ₁.
Then the second-order term c(δ₀ + δ₁)² contains a **first-order** piece 2cδ₀δ₁.
- If it enters light and clocks universally, it renormalises G and the calibration absorbs it.
- If it enters light propagation differently from the Newtonian channel, it shifts γ. Cassini allows
  |γ − 1| < 2.3 × 10⁻⁵.

At minimum it is a first-order term to account for. **The clean case is δ₀ = 0:** the sign-dependent response appears
only with the shell excess. This is 4380's β₀ condition again.

## 6. Questions to the founder (physical pictures)

1. **The two halves of one DP.** In a thick shell, the DP's like-charge CP is helped and its opposite-charge CP is held.
   Are the help and the hold **equal and opposite**? And picture the hold as a spring tying the CP back: does the
   opposite-charge shell **add a spring** as strong as the one the like-charge shell **loosens**? Equal springs added
   and loosened slow light. Equal changes in how far each push carries the CP do not.
2. **Ordinary space.** Far from any mass, with ordinary shells, does a CP already move differently in a like-charge
   shell than in an opposite-charge one? Or does the difference appear only when the shell is thicker than normal?
3. **Which is the speed of light?** Is it the shell's own step of one Planck sphere per Moment? Or the slower speed of
   the whole disturbance, the shell together with the CPs it pushes?
