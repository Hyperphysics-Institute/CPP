## 3. The radial standing wave (script parts 1–4)

**A wave that bounces across its own width while it travels is a waveguide mode.** Its frequency has a part from the bounce across and a part from the travel
along, combining as ω² = (c k_along)² + ω_across². Its speed along is c × √(1 − (ω_across/ω)²):
- if none of the frequency comes from the bounce, it travels at c;
- if a tenth does, 0.995 c; if half, 0.87 c;
- **if all of the colour came from the bounce, it would not travel at all.**

His radial standing wave, the message running from the centre to the circumference and back, is a bounce across the photon. If it set the colour, its
share would be all of it, and the photon would sit still. That is the decisive point; it needs no further measurement.

**The relation is measured in free space.** Giovannini and others (2015) sent single photons with a sideways structure (Bessel and focused Gaussian beams) over
about a metre. They arrived several micrometres late, as the waveguide relation predicts for their structure. So light with a sideways structure does travel
slightly slower, in just this way.

**A profile many wavelengths wide costs almost nothing** (script part 4). A beam's profile, brightest in the middle and fading outward, gives a small sideways
share, about wavelength ÷ (π × width). For a profile 100 wavelengths wide that slows the beam to 0.999995 c; for 5 wavelengths, 0.998 c. A Gaussian
profile also spreads slowly as the beam travels. **His constant profile of amplitude against radius fits a beam many wavelengths wide.** But a radial
standing wave at the colour's own rate would need the photon's radius to be about half a wavelength (script part 3: 1.5 m for FM radio, 0.3 μm for visible
light, 6 × 10⁻¹³ m for a 1 MeV gamma ray), which is exactly the case where the sideways share is all of it.

**A fixed sideways rhythm built into every photon** would act as a mass. The bound on a photon mass, below 10⁻¹⁸ eV (Particle Data Group), caps such a fixed
rhythm below about 2 × 10⁻⁴ Hz. (That bound does not cap structure that varies from beam to beam, as Giovannini's photons show. And a fixed share of each
photon's frequency would slow every colour alike, which a comparison between colours such as GRB 090510 could not detect.)

**So the colour's oscillation must run along the photon,** handed on by the relay at c (4455's chain), with its rate set at the source by the CP's swing
(4445, 4449). The photon's in-and-out rate is then the swing's rate, carried forward.

  fixed here), a 1 Hz photon has about 5 × 10⁻⁴⁴ h of action per Moment. So the action per Moment of a single-GP step must be far below h. What that step's
  energy is in CPP terms is owed.
