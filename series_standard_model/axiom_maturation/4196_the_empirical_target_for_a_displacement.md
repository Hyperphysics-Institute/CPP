## 2. Back-engineering from the data, as mandated

The best-measured parity-violating effect on ordinary matter is the electron–nucleus neutral-current
interaction (caesium, 0.35%; polarised electron scattering). Its non-relativistic form is

> H_PV = G_F Q_W / (4√2 m_e c) · [ σ·p ρ(r) + ρ(r) σ·p ]

and the velocity that Hamiltonian gives is

> **v = p/m + [ G_F Q_W ρ(r) / (2√2 m_e c) ] σ**

**Read as a rule of motion, that second term is a displacement along the particle's OWN spin, present
only where the particle overlaps weak-charged matter, in proportion to that matter's local density.**
Rows verbatim (D-11):

```
G_F                         = 1.4359e-62 J m^3
weak-charge density (heavy) = -9.119e+43 m^-3   (-0.0912 per fm^3)
G_F * |rho_W|               = 8.17 eV
extra velocity along spin   = 1695 m/s   = 5.65e-06 c      inside nuclear matter
same, in free space         = 0 m/s                       (rho_W = 0)
hop-gate weight it implies  = kappa/sigma = 1.15e-05     (4194: drift/c = 0.49 kappa/sigma)
```

**This is 4193's gate, q (e·A), with the one thing 4194 showed it needed: a weight that is not
constant.** The weight is the local density of the *other* party's weak charge. In free space it is
zero — no creeping particle, no self-pushing magnet, 4194's bound met exactly. Inside a nucleus it is
~10⁻⁵. **The founder's demand is met by measured physics: the axial vector does displacement work, 1.7
km/s of it.**

## 3. Where this differs from the founder's picture — stated plainly

He expects the **environment's** axial vector to bend the trajectory. **The data say otherwise at
leading order:** the axial vector in the measured effect is the moving particle's **own**; what the
environment supplies is a **scalar** — how much weak charge sits at that point, essentially a count of
neutrons. An environment-spin-dependent piece exists (the nuclear anapole moment, seen in caesium) but
is an order of magnitude smaller and partly electromagnetic in origin. 4192 reached the same place
from the other side: the environment's share of a GP's axial register is 10⁻³⁷ or less.

**So the content question becomes sharper, not vaguer:** the register needs the CP's own A (A1′, on
file) **and a scalar that measures contact with weak-charged matter.** What in CPP is that scalar?
**Not located.** It is not Φ or V_i as they stand — those are nonzero in every atom, and the gate must
vanish there to one part in ~10¹⁰ (F2).

## 4. What the sub-Moment transit would be for

If the gate acts at each of n GPs crossed in a Moment, the displacement is n small biased choices
added up — a way to get a **graded** weight out of binary choices: the gate fires at those GPs of the
transit where the contact scalar is present, and not at the others. That gives the transit a job, as
the founder says, and it makes the drift proportional to the density crossed. **A reading, not a
derivation.**
