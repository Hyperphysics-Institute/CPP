## 1. The picture

A photon is a DP being stretched and rotated. Its CPs land on GPs whose origin charge is plus or minus, statistically
half and half. Where the DI-bit shell is thick (deep in a well), a CP landing on an **opposite**-charge GP is slowed, and
on a **like**-charge GP it is sped. So half the landings are slow and half fast.

## 2. What it could fix in 4381's list (conditionally)

1. **The time-reversal objection is moot (4381 §2.2).** 4379's fast and slow legs were outward and inward. A static
   field cannot tell those apart without a flow. The founder's picture uses no direction asymmetry at all: its two kinds
   of step differ by charge sign. **Caveat:** a site that attracts the photon's + CP repels its − CP, so for the DP as a
   whole every landing is mixed. How a bound pair responds (§4) is therefore central.
2. **It could reach light (4381 §3), under three unshown conditions.** The mechanism acts on any CP moving through the
   sea, so in principle it could slow light and matter clocks by the same X. Local light-speed invariance needs that
   (script part 4): a clock-only slowing would make the locally measured light speed 1/X. Universality needs:
   - the same δ for a CP moving at one PSR per Moment (photon) as for ZBW cloud CPs at other speeds;
   - a matter clock whose tick really is per-landing transit (4379's clock was a cloud round trip, not this);
   - the same response curvature c (§3) for both.

   None is shown. The **first-order** slowing is already shared by light and clocks through the PSR (GR-1i L551–556);
   X would be a second-order addition on top.
3. **First order cancels if b = 0 and s = 0 (§4); a net second-order term survives only if c > 0 (§3).** Sign balance
   by itself cancels first order in every case, and gives the needed second-order term in none.

## 3. Net slowing depends on how the averaging works (script part 1)

If the time per landing is τ = 1 + aδ + cδ², equal shares average to 1 + cδ². **Only the curvature c survives.** In
effect this is a choice between a harmonic mean (equal **distance** per landing, the speed is what changes) and an
arithmetic mean (equal **time** per landing, a fixed wait is added or removed).

| how the thick shell acts | mean | net effect |
|---|---|---|
| A. scales the CP's **rate of crossing** a fixed distance (×(1 ∓ δ)) | harmonic | speed 1 − δ², **exactly 4379's form** X = 1 − κ²ϱ² with δ = κϱ |
| B. adds or removes a fixed **wait** ±δ per landing | arithmetic | none |
| C. lengthens or shortens the **step** within a Moment, one landing per Moment | arithmetic | none |
| D. (illustrative only; massive-particle kinematics) crossing ±x bumps, v ∝ √(1 ∓ x) | harmonic | speed 1 − ⅜x² to leading order |

In case A, "the time lost exceeds the time gained" follows automatically from symmetric ±δ, which is the
hiking-over-hills effect. So the real physical question is A versus B: **does the shell stretch the crossing in
proportion, or add a fixed wait?** The result does not depend on which charge sign is the slow one; A and D are symmetric.

## 4. Conditions and obstructions

1. **The discreteness obstruction (most serious).** Light advances one PSR per Moment (GR-1i L554–556). That is
   already the local maximum, and a Moment is the smallest time step. A "fast" landing that crosses the next PSR in less
   than a Moment is not allowed. If light can only be **held back**, never sped up, there is no ± pair. A one-sided
   hold that is linear in the shell excess is a **first-order** slowing of light alone, which Cassini forbids (through γ).
   The only escapes:
   - (i) **Light is already held on some landings in ordinary space**, and "fast" means fewer holds. Nothing on file
     says so, and a held baseline brings back 4380's β₀ problem.
   - (ii) **The hold is itself second order**, one-sided but square-law. Script part 5 gives one candidate, which is
     **Claude's construction, not on file**. A rotating DP couples at first order only through its permanent dipole,
     which averages to zero over the rotation. The induced (stretch) part goes as E², always with the same sign. But if
     ordinary space already has a site field E₀, the change 2E₀δE is first order in the shell excess again. So this
     escape too needs no holding in ordinary space.
2. **Sign balance, if uncorrelated.** A bias b in the share of attracting landings adds a first-order term bδ, with
   |b| ≲ 1.6 × 10⁻⁵ at κ = 1. Random landing signs give b ~ 1/√(number of landings). But strict ± alternation is
   impossible on the 600-cell (3827: at least ⅓ of bonds frustrated). On a structured lattice, a straight ray could see a
   **direction-dependent** sign bias, which would be anisotropy, a Lorentz-violation risk. Owed: a check for
   directional bias along lattice rays.
3. **Shell symmetry and the sea's polarisation.** If the shell excess differs around + and − origin GPs by a fraction
   s, a first-order term sδ appears, with the same bound. Founder 4359 says DPs orient toward unpaired CPs, so the sea
   near a mass may be radially polarised, which gives s ≠ 0, not s = 0 "by symmetry".
   - **For the photon,** the neutral DP may cancel this at first order (b₊ = −b₋), if its response is the average.
   - **A single-charge clock** (an electron, or the unpaired CP of a ZBW clock) has no such cancellation. The result
     would be a first-order, charge-sign-dependent clock shift (electron versus positron), an equivalence-principle and
     CPT hazard far larger than any second-order effect.
   - **Near a charged body,** s ≠ 0 in any case. That needs checking against charge-dependence tests of gravity.
4. **Bound-pair response.** In a mixed landing the DP's response must be the average of its two ends. A rule where "the
   slower end governs" biases toward slow at first order.

## 5. What it does not settle

- **κ is not derived.** δ = κϱ needs the speed change per unit shell excess and the curvature c; neither is on file.
  WC-GR-EXTERIOR stays a calibration.
- **R₀₀ = 0 is still imported** (4381 §2.1).
- **Gravity waves.** The eikonal ringdown follows the light ring, so light's universality is what the GW250114
  comparison needs. Whether a gravity wave itself feels X depends on what a gravity wave is in CPP, which is owed with
  the tensor-wave operator.
- **Mercury (4381 §6 Q1) is unchanged.** With a universal X, Mercury still fixes the clock's total second-order
  coefficient at ½, so the PSR-driven part must move (to ¾ at κ = 1).
- **4381 §2.3 (the cloud's thermal boundary moving at first order)** is not removed. Per-landing transit would free the
  clock from a fixed reflection radius, but the boundary question is separate and first order.

## 6. Questions to the founder (physical pictures)

1. **The speed limit.** Light already moves at the top speed, one Planck sphere per Moment. When a photon's charge lands
   on a like-charge point, can it really go faster than that? If not, what does "pushed on" mean? Is light held back a
   little even in empty space, so that "faster" means held less?
2. **Stretch or wait.** When the thick shell holds the charge back, does it make the crossing take proportionally
   longer (like walking slower over the same ground), or does it add a fixed pause?
3. **Polarisation near a mass.** You said the sea's DPs turn toward an unpaired charge. Near a neutral mass, does that
   leave plus-origin and minus-origin points with **equal** shells? If not, an electron clock and a positron clock would
   tick at different rates at first order, and experiments would have seen it.
