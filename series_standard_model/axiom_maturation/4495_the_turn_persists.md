## 1. His answer

A metre behind the atom, the DP "is no longer under the influence of the charges in the atomic core,  it is turned the way the atom left it, and it is not
tied by entanglement to the motion of the atomic core, so the DP will be randomly jostled by the other forces passing through it from the space surrounding
it." It "will be left in the orientation of the push-pull of repulsion-attraction to the  atomic core left it in as the entanglement and forces acting upon
it fade to zero and leave it to be oriented as per the local forces of the space act upon it locally." He does not address the second part of 4494 §4, the
DPs along the arm where only the arcs went.

## 2. What it settles

- **The fork closes on the persistent side.** The turn stays as the atom left it. Reading (P) of 4494 §3.3 (each DP always pointing at the core's present
  position) and row T5 (the trail undone) are both excluded. TODO-4494-e closed.
- **Jostling moves the record; under reversible dynamics it does not erase it.** Being "randomly jostled by the other forces" is the re-randomization case
  of 4494 §3.3: later dynamics that are the same whichever arm the core took leave the overlap unchanged, and jostling by the rest of the Sea hands the record
  on. Being "oriented as per the local forces of the space," if those forces settle each DP into one state regardless of its history, would carry the
  history into the forces that did the settling. (Claude's statement; standard under reversible, unitary dynamics.) In the corpus reversibility is a
  Copilot proposal (4485, 4486), not an established CPP rule. If CPP's rule for a DP is many-to-one — truly dissipative — a local reset could erase the
  record; that is route (C), §3. His own earlier wording leans the other way: the website has DP orientation "randomizes due to entropy effects ... driving
  the system toward equilibrium with no internal forces" (CPP v1.1 part 1, line 1119; cited at 4494 §3.3). "Entropy" fits coarse-grained randomization,
  which does not erase the record. (Claude's reading.)
- **So, by 4494's table, a persistent turn in the core's arm alone removes the fringes on average (row T1).** Coherence across arms up to 16 cm apart is
  shown by determinate-phase interferometry (Asenbaum et al. 2017; 4494 §3.2). The fringes survive only by one of three routes.

## 3. The routes

**(A) Both arms trailed alike.** If, whichever arm the core takes, the DPs along both arms end up turned the same way, the trail says nothing about the path
(row T4). In his picture that needs the KE/DP-arcs in the arm without the core to turn their DPs as the core does in its own arm. He has not said this; it is
the unanswered second part of 4494 §4. Its cost (4494 §3.3): the core's electrical effect would then act in the empty arm too. On route (A), CPP agrees
with quantum mechanics.

**(B) An untied record does not count — compatible with his words; Claude's extension.** He says the turned DP "is not tied by entanglement to the motion
of the atomic core." The plainest reading of that is mechanical: the DP no longer follows the core (which is what excludes (P)), and it is untied as his 4493
transient bond releases ("disentangling when the DP-arcs discharge"). Whether an untied record counts for the fringes he has not said. Route (B) is the
argument 4494 §3.3's last bullet already made ("the trail outside the ledger"; there marked Claude's statement, not his position), now offered as a candidate
reading of his words, not a new argument. On (B), in his ontology the fringes come from the quantum's members kept by the Nexus (4489–4492), what removes
them is the dispersal of its ties by collision (R-COLLISION-DISPERSES), and a released DP holds no tie to disperse; the guiding arcs from both arms still
superpose at the screen (Claude's reading of his arcs picture), and the fringes form. That is not quantum mechanics: there, any record correlated with the
path removes the fringes in proportion to how well it tells the paths apart (Englert, V² + D² ≤ 1). So (B) would be a definite point where CPP and quantum
mechanics part.

**Experiments close to (B)'s case.** In Buks, Schuster, Heiblum, Mahalu and Umansky (Nature 391, 871, 1998), a quantum point contact beside one arm of an
electron interferometer senses the path through electrostatic coupling to the passing charge, without scattering the interfering electron, and the
visibility falls as the detector is made more sensitive. That bears directly on (B) taken as "external records that take no momentum from the particle do not
count," and is likely to contradict it. Sonnentag and Hasselbach (Phys. Rev. Lett. 98, 200402, 2007) found electrons decohering as they pass over a resistive
plate through the image charges they induce, with a small Ohmic energy loss. Whether either fully meets (B)'s conditions is owed (§5 c). (Claude's
statement.)

**(C) A reset that truly erases.** If a DP's settling under "the local forces of the space" is many-to-one, the record could be genuinely erased, and the
fringes would return if the reset finished before detection. That is not unitary, so it too departs from quantum mechanics (and from QM-4's thm:unitary).
(Claude's statement.)

**The question that decides between them.** Bumped gas does spoil the fringes: interference of C70 molecules fades as the surrounding gas is made denser
(Hornberger et al. 2003, filed at 4479 §3 and 4480). A bumped gas atom flies off with some of the molecule's momentum. A turned Sea DP carries no net
momentum once the core has passed — a turn in place; his push-pull nets to zero — and any net drag is in any case capped near 10⁻⁶² per Moment (4419 §5,
ephemeris bound, linear drag assumed). One principled rule in his terms (Claude's reading): a tie is **released** unless momentum or energy has been
exchanged **readably** (not lost in the receiver's own momentum spread, 4481 §2), his own reason for entanglement being "to conserve momentum and energy"
(4492). By his 4492 bond rule ("collide/transfer Moment/forcefully displace") the DP was tied while being turned, so this is a rule for release, not for
formation. Its test: records internal to the particle remove fringes without readable momentum — the Walborn polarization tag is energy-free and internal
(4445); in Dürr, Nonn and Rempe (1998) the record is internal with a negligible momentum kick, though the microwave pulse does change the atom's hyperfine
energy. So "records that take no energy, or no momentum, never count" is contradicted by those experiments; "external records of that kind do not count" is
not tested by them, but Buks et al. bears on it.

**Convenient branches (PD-008).** Route (A) is convenient to Claude because it keeps CPP in agreement with quantum mechanics; routes (B) and (C) are
convenient in another sense, because they give a crisp "CPP departs here" story. None is chosen; §4 asks.

## 6. Reply to the founder

That settles the first half: the turned DPs stay turned, and jostling spreads that record into the Sea without erasing it, as long as nature never truly
forgets. So the turned DPs along the arm the atom took mark its path.

The stripes can then survive in three ways. The arcs in the other arm could turn their DPs the same way, so both arms look alike; CPP then agrees with
quantum mechanics. Or a DP that's no longer tied simply doesn't count. Or the local forces truly wipe the DP clean. The last two would be real departures
from quantum mechanics, and a 1998 experiment probably already rules against the second: a detector sitting beside one path of an electron, sensing it only
through its electric field, spoiled the stripes.

One rule in your terms: a tie is released unless momentum or energy changed hands in a way that could be read. A bumped gas atom carries off momentum, so it
counts; a turned DP keeps none, so it's released. My question for you is in §4.
