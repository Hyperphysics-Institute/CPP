## 2. What it answers, and Claude's assessment

- **4480 §5, first question (push and tie).** Answered in part: the tie by his momentum-conservation answer; the push not addressed, read as yes: emission conserves momentum, and that conservation is what ties
  the molecule to the light (his "entanglement is normally associated with momentum conservation"). (Claude's reading.) This agrees with the standard account, with one condition: an exchange of momentum ties two systems when the kick could in principle be read from one of
  them, i.e. when it is not lost in that system's own spread of momentum (a grating or a mirror takes the kick without keeping a usable record, which is why
  gratings do not wash out fringes). With that condition, entanglement is the rule, not the exception; special sources (his "beta-boron
  crystals", i.e. beta-barium borate, BBO, in spontaneous parametric down-conversion) are special only in making a clean, isolated pair that a Bell test can
  reach. In ordinary matter the correlations are spread over so many partners that no test can gather them, which is what decoherence is (4479 §3, 4480 §3).
  Agreed; standard physics, a fact to match.
- **4480 §5, second question (where the slit record is).** His answer: a fifth element, a tensor entry in each GP. Two points:
  - **What the eraser requires.** In the eraser the first photon's own pattern on the screen never changes: it shows no fringes whether or not the partner is
    read. Fringes (and their complementary antifringes) appear only when its hits are sorted by the partner's result (Walborn et al. 2002, who report coincidence
    counts only; the unsorted pattern is the sum of the fringes and the out-of-phase antifringes, smooth, as no-signalling requires). There the slit is marked
    in the first photon's own polarization (quarter-wave plates), correlated with the partner's. So the record need not be "removed" from the first photon when the partner is read; it needs only to be kept jointly by the two (the marking itself rides partly on the first photon), so that sorting
    by one shows the pattern in the other. This corrects the shorthand of Claude's 4480 question ("brings them back"). (Claude's reading.)
  - **A fifth element: where it could sit.** The registry's packet is LSP′ = {origin address, t; Φ, V_i, Q_ij}, ranks 0, 1 and 2, "rank 2 is the last rung"
    (A3′, AP-4, harmonisation note; R-RANK-2-REAFFIRMED, founder, 4 September 2026), provisionally amended at Patch 4120 with a fourth channel A_i (the axial
    vector, T₁_g; falsifiers TEST-A3G-1/2/3 live). The completeness proved is over the irreducible moments of the census; it says nothing about labels that are
    not moments. Which count gives "fifth" is not said (Φ, V_i, A_i, Q_ij are four channels; with the origin address, five). Three places are possible
    (Claude's list): (i) in the existing channels: in the eraser the slit is marked in polarization, which in CPP is presumably carried by the orientation of the
    DP vectors (V_i), so that record needs no new entry; (ii) in the origin-address field: a group tag read off the addresses a GP receives, not a new slot;
    (iii) a new per-GP entry, which would extend what a GP holds and go by the proposal road (AP-6, citing the AP-4 grouping anchor, 4478 j), a natural home
    for 4478's per-quantum membership. Under every option, a record stored locally in each GP cannot by itself give a pair's Bell correlations; the registered

- **4480 §5, third question (the partner).** His answer: the absorbed photon's entanglement passes to the atom and diffuses. That is reading (i) of 4480 §4 (the
  partner is now correlated with the atom, and through it with the atom's surroundings), and it agrees with the standard account when the absorber is an ordinary, unisolated atom (the entanglement passes to it and spreads into its
  surroundings, so the correlation becomes unreachable, not destroyed). Absorption as such does not end the tie. An isolated absorber keeps it: quantum
  memories absorb one photon of a pair and later re-emit it still entangled (Clausen et al., Nature 469, 508, 2011; Saglamyurek et al., Nature 469, 512, 2011),
  and atom–photon and atom–atom Bell tests are done (Blinov et al., Nature 428, 153, 2004; Hofmann et al., Science 337, 72, 2012). What diffuses the tie is the
  absorber's contact with its surroundings, not the absorption. (Claude's qualification.) Recorded as his choice; the collapse reading (ii) is kept as the rival.
