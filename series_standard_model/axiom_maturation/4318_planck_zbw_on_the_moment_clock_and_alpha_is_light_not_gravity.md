## 3. Q2 answered: the difference is changing content, and that explains 4317

4317 §5 showed that any rule applying to every GP alike cancels in the Planck-to-charge ratio, so 11.7 needed something
the oscillation does that a static charge does not. **His answer is that thing, and it is consistent with AP-4 (4311):
the count is fixed and the content changes.** A static charge's broadcast is constant; a Planck ZBW's broadcast changes
every Moment. So 4312–4313's "organised emission of 11.7 GPs" is not 11.7 *emitters*. It is a measure of how much the
broadcast content changes, in units of one charge's static content. That is why no count of emitters gave it (4317).
The object to compute is therefore **the per-Moment change in the LSP produced by an oscillating CP**, not a footprint.

**One refinement to the walk.** In measured physics a photon delivers its whole quantum ħω or nothing. α does not set
*how much* is transferred; it sets *how likely* the transfer is (amplitude √α per emission or absorption). So the
coupling question in CPP terms is: **when a one-quantum wavefront passes a CP, why does it transfer its quantum with
amplitude √α?** One concrete face of this on c04's own electron: the Thomson cross-section is (32/3)α² = 5.7×10⁻⁴ of
the c04 cloud's area πr_th², and r_e = 2α r_th (r_e is the radius at which the Coulomb energy e²/4πε₀r equals mc²; r_th is
c04's cloud radius).
