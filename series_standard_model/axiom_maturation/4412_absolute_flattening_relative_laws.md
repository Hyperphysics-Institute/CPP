## 2. Does it sound right? Yes: it is the Lorentz–Poincaré position

An absolute frame exists, and in it moving things are physically flattened and their clocks physically slowed; because
every ruler and clock is affected the same way, no laboratory can detect its own motion through that frame, and the laws
read the same in all of them. That is SR-1's stance in substance (an absolute Moment and a preferred foliation at the
ontological level, matching special relativity in everything observable; 4408 §2), and it fits this arc's findings: the
moving CP's light sphere compressed in front and elongated behind (4409), moving clocks slowed and rulers contracted along
the motion only (4408, 4410), and the census of a moving CP flattened by exactly 1/γ (4411).

Two qualifications. Lorentz derived the contraction from electrodynamics, while SR-1 takes the kinematics as input; this
arc is trying to supply the Lorentz-style derivation (4410, 4411), so the position is the target, not yet the result. And
"each CP is a laboratory frame" is best read as each CP's rest frame defining a possible laboratory (a single CP carries no
rulers or clocks; the laboratory is any body riding along with it).

## 3. What it settles: forces between CPs

His principle answers the first half of 4411 §7: **two CPs moving together, one ahead of the other, push each other
equally**, as at rest. That rules out a raw vector sum of DI-bit arrivals as the rule for the direct force between CPs
(lopsided at first order, 4411 §4) and selects the census channel, which gives the Liénard–Wiechert structure (4411 §3),
conditional on the census weight 1/R per arrival and on the multi-hop relay check (4411 (a)). Trouton–Noble and
Trouton–Rankine confirm the equal-force conclusion empirically, whatever the mechanism.

One gap: part of the force between two CPs passes through the sea (B is acted on by DPs that A polarized). So "settled"
holds for the direct channel; the sea-mediated part must also come out free of first-order asymmetry (owed).

## 4. What it requires of the coasting mechanism

In CPP a CP does not carry its own velocity (the 7 July ruling; R-CP-NO-REST-MASS); each Moment it is displaced by its
SSV_net, d = (|SSV_net|/SSV_abs)·PSR. So coasting needs a **non-zero** forward SSV_net, and CONJ-FP-1 (the founder's
2 August 2026 ruling) supplies it: the DI-bits a moving CP gets back from the DPs of the sea return sooner from ahead than
from behind, giving one net drive whose sign follows the sea's response (Condition A: a repulsive response gives a forward
drive). Under Liénard–Wiechert propagation that drive would be zero and "nothing could coast" (Condition B).

**Reading, marked as Claude's:** his message does not address the second half of 4411 §7; that the CP–sea interaction may
be lopsided because the sea is at rest rather than a co-moving partner is Claude's inference.

On that reading, his principle sharpens the requirement. The drive must be **self-consistent at every speed**: at velocity
v it must reproduce exactly a displacement of v per Moment (CONJ-FP-1's marginality link, C·PSR = SSV_abs), with no
leftover dependence on speed a laboratory could detect, and the family of coasting states must be neutrally stable (its
stability link). Its linearity in speed was checked only up to a tenth of light speed; at higher speeds it must take the
relativistic (γ-dependent) form, which nothing has shown.

**The observational stake:** the drive must be the same for every species and every binding state (free and bound, and
whatever local SSV_abs each CP sees) to very high precision. The Earth moves at about 10⁻³ of light speed relative to the
cosmic background, so any mismatch enters at about 10⁻⁶ of the coupling at second order, while preferred-frame bounds
(Hughes–Drever and other clock-comparison tests, modern Michelson–Morley and Kennedy–Thorndike tests, the PPN α₁ and α₂
limits) are many orders tighter (TODO-4364-PREFFRAME).

**The receiver-rule gap:** a rule of the form "DPs respond to the vector sum of arrivals, CPs to the census" cannot depend
on species or composition, because DPs are made of CPs: a moving DP-like bound state in a laboratory (positronium) would
then drive itself, which his principle forbids. Such a rule can depend only on being at rest in the absolute frame, which
makes it an explicit preferred-frame rule that must leave no observable trace.

## 6. Answer to his "What do you think? Does this sound right?"

Yes. It is the picture Lorentz and Poincaré held, and the one SR-1 takes: there is a real absolute frame, the lattice of
GPs, and in it moving things really are flattened and their clocks really slowed. Because every ruler and clock in a moving
laboratory is changed the same way, nobody inside can tell how fast they are moving, and the laws come out the same in
every lab. The work of this arc is to show the flattening comes out of the DI-bit relay, as Lorentz showed it came out of
electrodynamics.

His answer settles that two particles moving together push each other equally, so the direct force between particles must
follow the census, which gives exactly the right flattening. For his coasting mechanism, where the particle carries no
momentum of its own and the sea's uneven return is what moves it forward each Moment, it sets a demanding condition: that
forward push must always be exactly the size that keeps the particle at its current speed, at every speed, and work
identically for every kind of particle, free or bound, to very high precision. And sea particles are themselves made of
ordinary particles, so a sea-like pair moving through a laboratory must not be able to push itself along.
