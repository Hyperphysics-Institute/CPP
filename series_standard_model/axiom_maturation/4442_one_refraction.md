## 2. A double count to avoid (script part 1)

Light's wavefront, stepping at c/K, turns by the full index K (4389): 2v²g sideways at speed v, 2g for light. On Path 1 (4441) the electron also
feels the push on its whole energy, g sideways. **If the mixed cloud turned by light's full wavefront and also felt that push, it would bend too

## 3. The electron's own index, Claude's synthesis (script parts 2–3)

A wave of rest mass m and conserved energy E, in a sea where clocks run as K^(−1/2) and rulers as K^(1/2) (WC-DP-SEA-PV), moves as if through an index

  n² = K² − K (m/E)²,

the standard matter-wave (Jacobi) index. For light, m = 0 and n = K, light's index (4389). **Its refraction gives the path's shape, and so the
sideways acceleration, at every speed:** (1 + v²) g (script part 2): 1.25 g at half light speed, 1.81 g at 0.9c, 2 g for light. **The motion along
the path, and its timing, follow from conserving E in the same metric**, and that is where the clock half (the push) enters. A body falling from rest
falls along the gradient, so its fall is along motion. The sideways formula's limit g at rest is only the limit of that formula.

**What the index assumes.** It is the WC-DP-SEA-PV metric (K = 1 + 2U, γ_ppn = 1, a convention, 4392) together with special relativity's mass shell
E² = p² + m². So reading (ii) amounts to "the particle follows the geodesic of the assumed metric". 4424's inertia is not removed by it; it is absorbed
into the imported dispersion relation.

**The splits are regroupings** (script part 3, derived from n²). Read literally, the index splits as 2g − g(m/E)²: light's full K term, minus a
rest-mass term. The two bookkeepings on file regroup that same total:

- **4440/4441's:** the push 1 plus the space half v².

Neither is a term-by-term reading of the index. That is what makes the choice between them a bookkeeping at first order.

**The split question.** It loses force as an observational question: every split of this index predicts the same motion. It remains a mechanism
question: what in CPP produces this n², and its rest-mass term in particular.

**The neutron interferometer: the slow-speed limit, measured.** In 1975 Colella, Overhauser and Werner sent slow neutrons (about 2.7 km/s,
wavelength about 1.4 Å) through a silicon-crystal interferometer tilted in the Earth's field. Gravity shifted their interference as the slow-speed
limit of the index predicts: ordinary Schrödinger refraction in the potential mgz. It agreed within the experiment's roughly 10% error. Later
refinements (Staudenmann et al. 1980; Littrell, Allman and Werner 1997) reached sub-percent precision and found a persistent discrepancy of about 1%,
attributed to distortion of the interferometer and to dynamical diffraction. Atom interferometry (Peters, Chung and Chu 1999) measures g with matter
waves to about 3 parts in 10⁹. These test the clock term and the equality of inertial and gravitational mass at slow speed. They do not test the ruler
term, the (1 + v²) structure, or K.

**The mixing threshold.** No measurement excludes his threshold. The part of the motion that his mixing governs is the speed-dependent turning, and at
the neutron's speed it is about 10⁻¹⁰ of g, far below anything the interferometer could see. A slow neutron can be a wave and still have its fall
carried by the clock term. And the test is on neutrons, not electrons. A threshold is disfavoured only by GR-consistency: a sharp threshold puts a
kink in the bending against speed, and a gradual mixing gives a smooth departure from 1 + v². Either is non-GR, and moderate speeds are unmeasured
(4441).
