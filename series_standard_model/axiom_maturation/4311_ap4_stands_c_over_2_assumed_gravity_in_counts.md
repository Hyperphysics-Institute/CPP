organised.

## 2. The c/2: registered, exterior-GR-exact, and assumed at the floor

## 3. Where the 10¹⁶ comes from, and how it goes away

Two assumptions, neither derived, collide:
- **the floor PSR ≥ l_P/2** (§2), and
- **the identification of "the smallest PSR" with the crowding radius √(N/4π)** (4304, 4308: the surface-count reading).

The crowding radius is a fact of the sharing rule (about 8.5 × 10¹³ GPs). Nothing says the PSR shrinks to it. Dropping
that identification removes the contradiction with no theory-wide change, and it is the weaker of the two. The floor
remains a GR-lane physics question (a no-horizon surface at 4/3 r_S, with the exterior identical to GR outside it, so
nearly unobservable).

**Is the c/2 the source?** It is half the collision. But even if the floor went and the PSR fell to zero at a horizon,
the surface count would not become a cross-check: any N would then find a radius at which it just fills a shell. **The
resolution is to drop the identification, not to change the floor.** The triangulation must come from elsewhere.
