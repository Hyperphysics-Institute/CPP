## 2. What the model says, and how crudely

The model fixes three things: the electron's spin is the departed d quark's sense; the antineutrino's
spin is minus the refill sense; leptons are ejected along q × own spin. Read on **one axis**, with no
averaging over the triplet orientations, the electron's asymmetry relative to the neutron spin is
−(2P − 1) where P is the chance a d quark's spin is parallel to the neutron's.

- **Sign of A: right.** The electron goes preferentially *against* the neutron spin.
- **Sign of B: right.** In the GT channel the antineutrino's spin is +s_q and it is ejected along
  its spin, so it goes *with* the neutron spin; measured B = +0.987.
- **Magnitude of A: wrong by 3.5–5.5×** on one axis. Measured −0.118; the model's crudest reading
  gives −0.42 (measured quark spin fractions) to −0.67 (SU(6)).

## 3. What that demands — not what it proves

In the Standard Model A = −0.118 is a **near-cancellation** between Fermi–GT interference and the
pure GT term; a one-axis reading cannot see that. So the honest statement is: **the model must
reproduce that cancellation through the full three-orientation averaging of the triplet channel,
weighted by the refill-sense bias f (4203), or it fails.** That averaging is a definite calculation
with no free parameter once f is known — and f is the number still blocked on the persistence
question (4203, still owed). **So the two open items are one item.**
