## 1. 4193 — the hop-score table, by selection instead of by sign products

4193 classified six candidate hop-score terms by multiplying the C, P, T signs of their factors. A critic
should not re-multiply the same signs. The script instead **runs the rule**: twelve icosahedral neighbour
directions (the 600-cell's vertex figure, closed under inversion), a random register (V, A, q), the hop
chosen by argmax, and then the same argmax on the transformed register. A symmetry is respected iff the
chosen hop transforms covariantly, over 400 random states per cell. Rows verbatim (D-11):

```
term in the hop score                   C     P     T     CP    CPT   (fraction of 400 random states where selection is covariant)
e.V            (existing rule)          even(1.00)  even(1.00)  even(1.00)  even(1.00)  even(1.00)
e.(V x A)      (Lorentz-like)           even(1.00)  even(1.00)  ODD (0.00)  even(1.00)  ODD (0.00)
e.A                                     even(1.00)  ODD (0.00)  even(1.00)  ODD (0.00)  ODD (0.00)
q e.A          (founder gate, 4193)     ODD (0.00)  ODD (0.00)  even(1.00)  even(1.00)  even(1.00)
(A.V) e.A      (4155 bA)                even(1.00)  even(1.00)  even(1.00)  even(1.00)  even(1.00)
(A.V) e.V      (b as a weight)          even(1.00)  ODD (0.00)  even(1.00)  ODD (0.00)  ODD (0.00)
```

Identical to 4193 §3, cell for cell. **The assignment hop = T-odd** enters exactly once — as the rule
`e → −e` under T — and it is right for the reason 4193 gave: the time-reversed trajectory retraces the
same edge in the opposite sense. One condition 4193 did not state: under T the decision moves from the
departure GP to the arrival GP, so T-invariance of the *rule* requires the register the rule reads to
travel with the CP. It does — the resident CP dominates its GP's register by 10³⁷–10⁵⁰ (4184/4192) — so
the classification holds. **4193 §3 CONFIRMED.**

## 2. 4216 — the neutral-current sign, and a false start

4216's test rests on the non-relativistic form `H = G_F Q_W/(2√2 mc) · ½{σ·p, ρ}` taken as standard. The
critic instruction was to re-derive the γ₅ → σ·p/mc sign rather than take it. Three independent legs:

**(a) The Dirac reduction, numerically.** Explicit positive-energy spinors at |p|/m ≈ 0.05, γ₅ in the
Bjorken–Drell convention (P_L = (1−γ₅)/2): ⟨γ₅⟩ / (⟨σ·p⟩/m) = +0.999 in three random trials. The sign of
the reduction is **+**, to O(p²/m²).

**(b) The Hamiltonian from the couplings, not from a textbook form.** Z exchange with
J̃ = Σ ψ̄γ^μ(g_V − g_A γ₅)ψ, g_A = T₃, integrated out: L_eff = −(G_F/√2) J̃·J̃, the sign anchored by the same
step for W exchange giving the Fermi Lagrangian −(G_F/√2)[ūγ(1−γ₅)d][ēγ(1−γ₅)ν]. The electron–quark
cross term is L^PV = **+**(G_F/√2) Σ_q C₁q (ēγ^μγ₅e)(q̄γ_μq), C₁q = 2g_A^e g_V^q — which is
Roberts (arXiv:1408.5463) eq. 3.9. The non-relativistic nucleus gives Σ C₁q n_q = −(Q_W/2)ρ, so
H_PNC = **+**(G_F Q_W/(2√2)) ρ γ₅_BD, and with (a): v_extra = +(G_F Q_W ρ/(2√2 m)) σ — **along Q_W σ**.

**(c) The literature, two conventions.** Dzuba–Flambaum write H = (G_F/√2)(−Q_W/2)γ₅ρ (arXiv:1210.3891
eq. 1) in the Landau–Lifshitz convention where γ₅ = −γ₅_BD; the review physics/0508016 eq. 52 writes
H = +(G_F/(2√2))Q_W γ₅ ρ. The two agree with each other and with (b) once the γ₅ conventions are matched.

Q_W(n) = −1.000 and Q_W(Cs, tree) = −74.0 (measured −72.6) come out of the same couplings, so the input
sign is measured, as 4216 said. **4216 §3 CONFIRMED: the electron drifts against its spin in neutron-rich
matter, the direction the W throws it.**

**The false start, kept on the record.** My first draft of the script wrote L_eff from memory with a
leading minus and concluded "OPPOSITE to 4216." That conclusion lasted one search. The correction came
from deriving the sign (leg b) and from two sources that disagree by a visible convention (leg c).
Had I stopped at the draft, this patch would have withdrawn a sound result — the D-1 shape again, one
level down: not "search the corpus for the mechanism" but "search the literature for the convention."
The script prints the corrected chain and names the error.

## 3. 4206 — the pure-GT "exact match": one structural, two coincidental

4206 §2 reports that the inheritance model gives a = −1/3, A = −2/3, B = +2/3 in the pure Gamow–Teller
limit — "the Standard Model's values, exactly, all three — using only the SU(6) d-quark polarisation 5/6."
The conventions it asked to have checked (X = 3⟨cos⟩; ⟨cos θ_eν⟩ = ⟨cos θ_e⟩⟨cos θ_ν⟩ about the common
axis) are right — Monte Carlo of the 4206 model at pol = 2/3 returns −0.332, −0.667, +0.662 against
−0.333, −0.667, +0.667. **The numbers are as 4206 computed them. The claim about them is not.** Rows
verbatim (D-11):

```
                                                           a       A       B
SM pure GT, J->J, J=0.5  (A = -1/(J+1), Clebsch)      -0.333  -0.667  +0.667
SM pure GT, J->J, J=1.0  (A = -1/(J+1), Clebsch)      -0.333  -0.500  +0.500
SM pure GT, J->J, J=1.5  (A = -1/(J+1), Clebsch)      -0.333  -0.400  +0.400
model, SU(6): Delta d_n = 4/3, pol = 2/3              -0.333  -0.667  +0.667
model, measured Delta d_n = Delta u_p = 0.84, pol=0.42  -0.333  -0.420  +0.420
model, fully polarised quark, pol = 1                 -0.333  -1.000  +1.000
SM at the SU(6) point lambda = -5/3 (full formula)    -0.190  -0.238  +0.952
```

- **a = −1/3 is a genuine structural match.** It is polarisation-independent on both sides: the pair
  leaves back-to-back with a one-third bias whatever the parent spin does.
- **A and B match because 2/3 = 2/3.** The Standard Model's pure-GT A for a ½ → ½ transition is
  −1/(J+1), a Clebsch–Gordan factor that knows nothing about quarks. The model's A is −pol, where pol is
  the per-quark d polarisation Δd_n/2, which SU(6) sets to 2/3. **The two numbers share no input.** Move
  the quark polarisation and the model's A moves; the SM's does not. The SM evaluated *at* the SU(6)
  point (λ = −5/3) gives A = −0.238, not −0.667 — so the model and the SM agree at two different
  parameter points, not at one.
- 4206 §2 flagged itself as "the result I like" under PD-008 and asked the critic to check conventions.
  The conventions were fine. The attack was needed one layer up.

**Verdict: 4206 §2 is downgraded from "test passed, nothing fitted" to "one structural match (a), one
coincidence (A, B)."** SF-2 v1.06 carries the stronger wording in its §5.7.1 postdiction paragraph; the
sentence is replaced at Patch 4223, where the reason the coincidence dissolves is shown (the correct model
does not use the quark polarisation at all).
