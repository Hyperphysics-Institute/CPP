## 2. The same-flip condition, met in his terms

The flip in each arm is set by the momentum that arm carries and happens "in the same way." That meets TODO-4496-a provided **each arm's share is set by the
split, not by where the core is**: for an even split, each arm carries half the atom's momentum whichever arm the core took (momentum is a vector, so the
two halves point along their arms; "half" is shorthand), so each arm's flips are the same in both cases and tell nothing about the path. If the share
followed the core, the flips would follow it too, and the record would return. His "the arc-cloud is the totality of the KE/Momentum/action" (4496) supports

## 3. The empty-arm bump

**His answer is route (ii) of 4496 §3.2, with a twist.** A bump on gas in the empty arm "will affect the atom ... going through the other slit": the effect
reaches the core in the other arm. He bets on both channels; a relay-speed version is excluded for a single quantum by Guerreiro et al. 2012 (4477 §3), so
the channel read here is the Nexus. (Claude's reading.)

**What quantum mechanics requires** (Claude's statement). If a gas atom in arm B is bumped in a way that could be read — its recoil fully tells that the atom
passed there — then an atom detected right afterwards is found in arm B, every time; for a partial record, more often than not (4481 §2's readability
condition). So in his picture a readable bump in the empty arm must bring the core into that arm: 4478's per-quantum gathering ("gathered at once") applied
to the core. "Affects the atom in the other slit" read as "moves the core to this slit" meets this. Read as "nudges the core while it stays in the other arm,"
it would give a record in arm B with the atom found in arm A on immediate detection, which quantum mechanics forbids.

**The guided-particle comparison.** In guided-particle theories a which-path record can form in an arm the particle did not take: the "surrealistic
trajectories" of Englert, Scully, Süssmann and Walther (1992), with Hardy (1992) on empty waves, and surreal average trajectories observed in
weak-measurement reconstructions by Mahler et al. (2016). In those cases the record sits in a degree of freedom other than position — a cavity photon in
ESSW, a partner photon's polarization in Mahler et al. — and it does destroy the fringes. When the record is a position that separates at once, as a
recoiling gas atom's does, the guided particle and the record agree (Dürr, Fusseder, Goldstein and Zanghì, Z. Naturforsch. 48a, 1261, 1993; Tastevin and
Laloë, arXiv:1802.03783, 2018). So for a gas atom that recoils, CPP needs the first reading: the core goes to the arm where the bump was.

**Friction.** "The elemental interactions that produce energy loss to friction" are, in standard terms, the collisions of Hornberger et al. (4479 §3): each
readable one takes momentum and removes the fringes. Consistent with his account.

## 4. The blocked slit

He has already answered this: with one slit blocked, "the core and DP-arc cloud entity carry all the energy through only one slit." Read at its most
favourable, an atom that passes the open slit keeps all its energy, which is what quantum mechanics says and what experiment shows: if half the kinetic energy
were lost, the speed would fall by a factor √(1 − ½) ≈ 0.71, the de Broglie wavelength would grow by √2, and single-slit diffraction angles by about 41%;
observed open-slit patterns match the incoming λ = h/p (neutrons: Zeilinger, Gähler, Shull, Treimer and Mampe, Rev. Mod. Phys. 60, 1067, 1988; atoms:
Carnal and Mlynek, Phys. Rev. Lett. 66, 2689, 1991), and light passing a half-blocked aperture keeps its frequency, as every slit or grating spectrometer
shows. It is the same gathering by which a photon's energy lands whole (4477, 4478).

What his words leave open is the mechanism at the block: what the blocked arm's arcs do there — turned back to rejoin the atom, never sent into that arm, or
gathered as in §3. (Claude's statement.)

## 8. Reply to the founder

On the flips, that meets the condition, as long as each arm's share is set by the split and not by where the core is. With an even split each arm carries half
whichever way the core went, so the flips match and tell nothing. You also answered the tube question directly: "the same way."

On the empty-arm bump, there's one requirement. In quantum mechanics, if a gas atom in one arm is bumped in a way that could be read, the atom is then found
in that arm. So in your picture that bump has to bring the core into that arm, the same gathering as for a photon. It can't just nudge a core that stays in
the other arm. Guided-particle theories can have a record form in the "wrong" arm, but only when the record isn't a position; a recoiling gas atom is.

On the blocked slit, you'd already said it: all the energy goes through the open slit. That's what experiment shows. If half were lost, the atom would slow to
about 71% of its speed and its diffraction pattern would widen by about 41%, and it doesn't. What's left open is what the arcs in the blocked arm do at the
block.

On your two influences, the experiments you were looking for exist. Signals never beat light speed, and Bell-test correlations have been bounded at least
ten thousand times faster than light (Yin and colleagues, 2013), for any preferred frame in which Earth moves slower than a thousandth of light speed; CPP's
own frame may sit just outside that. That matches your split: the relay carries signals at c, and the Nexus carries correlations at once.
