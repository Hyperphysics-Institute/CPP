# Charge Cannot Hold a Spinning Electron at the Compton Scale; the Force That Can Is the One That Already Drives the ZBW

**Patch:** 4294. **Lane:** EW → QM/SPIN. **Session:** 240.
**Founder:** `founders_voice/4294_question_what_force_holds_the_rim.md`. He asks what force holds the rim of 4293's
dumbbell: a locked dipole seems to hold it only if very close, so does it still come down to charge?
**Verify:** `series_standard_model/code/4294_force_needed_to_hold_spin.py`.

## 1. The founder is right: 4293's "DP bond" named no force

4293 said the rim is held by "a sea-mediated DP bond". That was a placeholder. Computing the force actually required
shows that charge cannot supply it at the sizes that matter.

## 2. How large must the dumbbell be? (rows 1)

```
    rim speed = w (2R/3) = 3/(2R) c  ->  below c needs R > 1.5 r_C   (R = 1.5: speed 1.000 c)
```

To carry ħ/2 without the rim exceeding light speed, the dumbbell must be at least 1.5 Compton lengths long.

## 3. Charge against the force needed (rows 2)

```
  R =   1.5: needed 3.333e-01   full Coulomb 3.243e-03 (0.0097 of it)   hub polarisation 2.525e-04 (7.57e-04)   rim speed 1.000 c
  R =   3.0: needed 4.167e-02   full Coulomb 8.108e-04 (0.0195 of it)   hub polarisation 7.889e-06 (1.89e-04)   rim speed 0.500 c
  R =  10.0: needed 1.125e-03   full Coulomb 7.297e-05 (0.0649 of it)   hub polarisation 1.917e-08 (1.70e-05)   rim speed 0.150 c
  -> at the smallest size (R = 1.5 r_C, rim at c) a full unscreened charge supplies under 1% of the force.
     Its share grows as R (needed force falls as 1/R^3, Coulomb as 1/R^2): full charge = needed at
     R = 1.125/alpha = 154.2 r_C = 1.125 Bohr radii, rim speed 0.0097 c  (SPIN-1's regime, 4286 F).
     But the hub is neutral: what the rim actually feels is its polarisation, 1e-3 or less of what is needed.
```

- **At the Compton scale charge falls short by the factor α ≈ 1/137**, even as a full, unscreened charge. The needed
  force there is about m_e c²/r_C, while Coulomb gives k e²/r_C² = α times that (rows 3).
- **Charge alone could hold a spin-ħ/2 structure only at about a Bohr radius, moving slowly (0.01c).** That is exactly
  why SPIN-1's orbits landed at Bohr scale: it used charge, and ħ/2 then forced that size.
- **The neutral hub offers much less.** The rim feels only the hub's polarisation, which gives 10⁻³ of the needed
  force at best. That is the founder's point: the locked dipole's attraction is far too weak unless the ends are very
  close, and close ends cannot carry ħ/2 below light speed.

## 4. Another force: the ZBW's own (rows 4)

**The ZBW oscillation already needs such a force.** A CP swinging at the Compton frequency with an amplitude of a
Compton length needs a restoring force of about m_e c²/r_C. Charge supplies only α of it. So whatever pulls a ZBW CP back
toward its partner is, by the same arithmetic, **not charge**. It is roughly 137 times stronger at that range. The
corpus's route (H) gives its stiffness (μ ω_Z², ω_Z = m_e c²/ħ) but, in the searches made here, not its physical origin.

Applied between hub and rim, a bond of route (H)'s stiffness has exactly the needed strength. With ħ/2 it places the
parts as follows:

```
    R = 1.5000 r_C;  rim radius from the centre 1.0000 r_C, speed 1.0000 c;  hub radius 0.5000 r_C, speed 0.5000 c
    -> the rim runs on route (H)'s circle (hbar/mc, at c); the hub on c04's r_th (hbar/2mc, at c/2).
```

**The rim runs on route (H)'s circle at exactly light speed; the hub circles at c04's r_th at half light speed.** No
radius was tuned: this follows from the Compton frequency, ħ/2 and the one-third/two-thirds inertia split.

## 5. The cost: the energy is not negligible

```
    energy check: orbital energy mu w^2 R^2 = 0.5000 m_e c^2 -- NOT negligible (4292/4293 assumed it was).
```

With a ZBW-strength bond, **half the electron's rest energy is in the orbit** (motion plus bond). 4292 and 4293 assumed
binding negligible and set m_e = 3m. That accounting fails here. Motion-energy has inertia, and CPs have no rest mass
(founder 4288), so the three CPs' inertias, the bond's energy and g must be recomputed together, self-consistently and
relativistically (the rim is at c). **4293's g = 2 is therefore provisional.** It holds under its own accounting, which
this patch shows is not the physical one. Note that 4286's route (H) circle needed exactly this split: half the rest
energy in motion, half stored.

## 6. What I think

- **The founder's intuition is correct.** Charge cannot hold the electron's spinning structure at any size where the
  spin is carried below light speed, except at the Bohr scale with an unscreened charge, and a neutral hub cannot provide
  one.
- **Another force is needed, and CPP already relies on it.** The ZBW oscillation needs a force about 137 times stronger
  than charge at the Compton range. If the same force binds the rim, the numbers fall onto the corpus's own radii: route
  (H) for the rim, r_th for the hub.
- **Next:** redo the electron's g with self-consistent energy and inertia (F1′); identify the ZBW force (§7).
