## 2. Branch (i) under the default binding — rows verbatim

If the orbital is held electrostatically — the +2/3 core polarises the neutral eDP and the field gradient
pulls it in — then "the anchor already taken by the refill half" would have to mean the refill half *shields*
the core from the old orbital. A neutral eDP is a dipole; the core is a monopole:

```
eDP separation d = 1.60e-50 fm   inner-dipole / core-monopole field at the orbital = 7.62e-50
eDP separation d = 1.00e-03 fm   inner-dipole / core-monopole field at the orbital = 4.76e-03
eDP separation d = 1.00e-02 fm   inner-dipole / core-monopole field at the orbital = 4.76e-02
eDP separation d = 1.00e-01 fm   inner-dipole / core-monopole field at the orbital = 4.76e-01
eDP separation d = 2.10e-01 fm   inner-dipole / core-monopole field at the orbital = 1.00e+00
```

Screening needs the eDP's internal charge separation to be a third of the orbital radius — 0.21 fm — while
the corpus's DP separation is the grid scale (c01, 4168), fifty orders below. **A neutral inner dipole
cannot screen a monopole. Under an electrostatic binding, the pair creation leaves the core's pull on the
old orbital unchanged, and nothing releases it.** Branch (i) fails on that binding. Branch (ii) — the W⁰
acting on the orbital directly during its transit of the pocket — is untouched by this, but is also
uncomputable without the same missing rule.

## 3. The question, in a picture (PD-006(a))

The +qCP core has one eDP circling it at 0.63 fm — the quark's orbital. A second eDP arrives at the core.
**What holds the first one in orbit — the core's charge pulling on the dipole's near end, a gradient in the
Sea's SSV set up by the core, or a geometric slot in the cage — and does the arrival of the second one
displace the first?** If the binding is the core's charge: the second (neutral) eDP changes nothing and the
first stays; then the release must be the W⁰'s own act on the orbital as it sweeps through the pocket. If
the binding is a slot — one orbital per core, as an occupancy rule of the cage vertex — then the refill
half taking the slot *is* the release, and the "simultaneous convergence" is a rule, not a coincidence. If
it is an SSV gradient, the second eDP's own gradient could cancel the first's binding at the orbital
radius; that is computable once the gradient's form is stated. **Which of the three is the picture?** This is
the missing axiom-level input for the whole quark sector, not just for this release.
