## 2. The weak tie (4489 §7.2)

His answer: a tie "too distant to affect it when it was measured, cannot affect its entangled photon; that's all that happens"; the photon "still does
everything a photon does, photoelectric effect, interference, SPDC, etc., on its own," including self-interference one photon at a time; its own CPs "are
entangled with each other as a quantum (having passed that entanglement message on Moment by Moment from one GP to the next)"; and the tie falls below "the
threshold of irrelevance because it produces no change/no displacement when measured light-years away."

### 2.1 Readings, most favourable first

**(R1) The no-signalling statement.** "Cannot affect its entangled photon ... when measured light-years away" is, at its most favourable, the statement that
measuring a far partner never changes the near photon's own statistics — for any strength of tie and any distance (THEO-QM-5). On this reading he is right
without condition. What can remove a photon's own fringes is the existence of a strong tie, never the measurement of the partner (§2.2 (1)). Distance needs
no reinterpretation on R1.

**(R2) Kept and inert.** The faint tie is not deleted; it is kept and moves nothing, because its share is too small to move any CP. Nothing is erased, so
QM-4's thm:unitary is untouched — QM-4: "Apparent collapse is an artifact of tracing" over the bath. This is stricter than 4489's (T-a), which allowed
pruning what can no longer change any outcome: it is (T-a) without even its pruning. The photon's self-interference comes from its own CPs being one
quantum, its membership passed GP to GP: 4478's per-quantum membership with 4478's inheritance, carried by placement (ii) or (iii) of 4481 (awaiting his
ruling, 4478 §6). Agreed: a photon with only a faint tie to anything else interferes with itself at essentially full visibility (script row D3).

**R2 is the branch convenient to Claude**, because it keeps QM-4 as filed, and it goes beyond his 4489 words: there he proposed "to truncate the accounting
of entanglement beyond a certain magnitude," which is a cut, not "kept and inert." R2 is taken here only because his 4490 words say the tie "cannot affect"
rather than that it is removed. §4 asks, in a picture that tells the two apart.

**(R3) Dilution, not distance.** His example puts the weakness in distance. If "too far away" is meant as a cause of weakness, it is read as dilution: the
partner has since shared itself with so much else that the pair's share is negligible (monogamy, 4489 §3). (Claude's reading; second to R1.)

### 2.2 Conditions

**(1) A strong tie does remove the photon's own fringes.** By Englert's relation (1996, in the corpus at 4445), fringe visibility V and the distinguishability
D of a which-way record obey V² + D² ≤ 1. A photon whose path is fully recorded shows no fringes on its own (row D1). In the Walborn eraser filed at 4445
and 4481 §2, the path is marked in the photon's own polarization, which is entangled with the partner; the unsorted pattern is smooth, and fringes appear
only when its hits are sorted by the partner's polarizer result, in coincidence. Where the partner itself holds the which-slit record, the same holds (Kim,
Yu, Kulik, Shih and Scully, Phys. Rev. Lett. 84, 1, 2000). So "it still does everything a photon does ... on its own" holds for weak ties and fails for
strong ones; the strength of the tie decides.

**(2) Distance alone weakens nothing.** Entangled pairs sent from the Micius satellite to ground stations 1,203 km apart violated Bell's inequality by
2.37 ± 0.09 under strict locality conditions (Yin et al., Science 356, 1140, 2017). His own 4485 wording, a connection "regardless of address," says the
same. On R1 this costs him nothing; on R3 it fixes what "too far away" must mean.

**(3) Irrelevance must be judged on the sum, not tie by tie.** Records each too faint to matter compound. The script's table, verbatim (D-11):

(g is the overlap of the two record states a tie leaves; each weak tie alone shifts the visibility by one part in a million, which is what "faint" means

The checks are identities and arithmetic: an illustration, not a test.) A rule that drops each sub-threshold tie on its own predicts full fringes however
many there are; the full ledger predicts that a million leave 37% and ten million almost none. Observed: in an atom interferometer, scattered photons whose
wavelength is longer than the path separation each remove only a little coherence, and many of them together remove much more (Kokorowski, Cronin, Roberts
and Pritchard, Phys. Rev. Lett. 86, 2191, 2001). The corpus's own form is QM-4's thm:lindblad: dephasing at a rate set by the DP Sea coupling,
γ = (sea_strength)² E_P/ħ, from the bath as a whole. So a "threshold of irrelevance" that matches experiment applies to the summed effect of a quantum's
ties, never to each tie alone. Under R2 this is automatic: nothing is dropped, so the sum is always there. (Claude's statement.) Collisions with gas
molecules also decohere (Hornberger et al., 2003, in the corpus at 4479 §3 and 4480), but there a single collision is already a strong record, so it is
the case of condition (1), not of compounding.

**How "one GP step" maps to g is not derived.** His criterion is a CP displacement under one GP step; the script measures faintness by g. The map between
them is owed (§5 b).

### 2.3 Scale of the threshold

His threshold is "below the increment of 1 GP displacement at the finest granularity" (4489). On the corpus's working values one GP step is far below the

lattice spacing; 4310 carries two candidate spacings. So the threshold sits even further below anything an experiment touches, consistent with R2: it
describes what is irrelevant, and it cuts nothing an experiment could see. (Claude's statement.)
