## 2. What "2M" means

In the units the corpus uses for gravity (G = c = 1), a mass M is written as a length, GM/c². **2M is the radius
2GM/c², the Schwarzschild radius**: in Einstein's theory, the event horizon of a black hole of mass M, below which
nothing gets out. It is where the fall speed from far away reaches light speed. **8M/3** (four-thirds of 2M) is the
corpus's floor, where a clock held still runs at half rate and the DI-bit shell fills the Planck sphere
(R-DIBIT-COUNT-AT-FLOOR). Numbers (script part 1):

| object | M = GM/c² | 2M | 8M/3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sun | 1.48 km | 2.95 km | 3.94 km |
| Earth | 4.4 mm | 8.9 mm | 11.8 mm |
| 62-solar-mass merger remnant (GW150914) | 92 km | 183 km | 244 km |
| Sgr A* | 6.4 million km | 12.7 million km | 16.9 million km |

For the Sun and Earth these radii lie deep inside the body, where they mean nothing physically. They matter only when
the whole mass is inside them, as in a black hole.

## 4. The sending sphere at 2M (script parts 3–5)

His reason for the shape is clear and physical: the GP is crowded with DI-bits from the mass side, so its broadcast
reaches less far that way. Outside a black hole this matches every measurement made so far (4397), for every mass, as
he says. The question is what happens at 2M.

**Premises:** (i) a CP never steps beyond the inward reach of its own sending sphere (R-CP-NO-REST-MASS; light is the
sphere's edge, 4396); (ii) the Moment is the grid's time, with clocks set as in the river picture; (iii) the shift
follows the fall speed β = √(2M/r) all the way in. Then the inward reach, 1 − β, reaches **zero at 2M**, and an
infalling CP's steps shrink to nothing there. The ways through:

**(A) The sphere keeps shifting all the way: the matter hugs 2M.** The time to get within ε of 2M grows without limit
(18M of time to come within 0.01M, 73M within 10⁻⁸M). The matter piles up just outside 2M (a sketch: in a collapsing
star the inner layers freeze as the horizon passes them). Seen from outside it is a black hole: the space outside is
Einstein's and the ringing should be Einstein's, **provided the piled-up layer does not reflect**. On a discrete grid,
inward waves near 2M are squeezed until they reach the finest spacing after about 4M·ln(2M/ℓ) (some 460–650M, 0.14–0.20
s at 62 solar masses). Any reflection from there would come back as **echoes** about 8M·ln(M/ℓ) later (0.3–0.4 s); none
has been confirmed. This differs physically from Einstein's theory, in which an infaller reaches 2M in a finite time of
its own. **No tiny core forms on the grid.**

**(A′) The same, with a final lattice hop.** If the last step can be one lattice hop rather than an ever-shrinking
fraction, the CP crosses 2M after a finite number of Moments (of order 600M), and a core can form. This is A with a core.

**(B) The sphere stops shifting at the floor.** If the shift stays at its floor value √3/2 inside 8M/3 (AP-5's cap applied
to the shift), the inward reach stays 0.134 per Moment and the CP keeps falling (about 20M from 8M/3 to the centre: some
6 ms at 62 solar masses, about 10 minutes for Sgr A*; this is a grid-frame speed, not comparable directly to 3703's c/2
in its own frame). The sideways reach stays ½; that equals 3703's matter clock **by construction** (the sideways reach is
the clock rate, and the floor is where the clock rate is ½). B has serious costs:
- **On a flat grid with Einstein's equations, a capped shift is not empty space** (script part 4): it implies mass
  spread through the interior as 1/r² with m(r) = 3r/8, and 1 − 2m/r = ¼ everywhere inside, so there is no horizon at
  all. Either the interior is not Einstein's, or the mass is spread out rather than in a tiny core.
- **Light riding the capped sphere gets out** (outward reach 1.866), so the hole has a visible surface at redshift 2.
  For Sgr A* that is struck by the no-surface bound (Broderick and Narayan): matter falling onto such a surface would
  glow far above what is seen. 3623's non-thermal storage is the corpus's only answer.
- **The ringing** stays Einstein's only if gravitational waves ride a separate, uncapped geometry (3675's sea). That
  two-geometry assumption must be stated; a transparent or reflecting interior inside 3M otherwise rings differently
  (4400).

**(C) The listening reading** (4401) keeps his shape and lets everything drain, but he has now said the sphere is the
sending one. On "what we have said before": the corpus has read the PSR as the sending sphere (4007, 4397 §3), in

**(D) The CP's step is not set by its own sending sphere.** GR-FE-1 says the CP "displaces the distance associated with

**(E) The CP is carried by the falling DP sea** (4401 owed item 1), rather than stepping across the grid on its own.

## 7. Answer to his "What do you think?"

His sending sphere, crowded on the mass side, is a clear physical reason for the shape, and it works for the Sun, the
Earth and every measurement made so far. At a black hole it meets one obstacle: at 2M the sphere's inward reach falls to
zero, so a CP whose step is limited by its own sphere cannot cross. Then either the CPs hug 2M as the grid counts
Moments (it looks like a black hole from outside, may give echoes, and makes no core unless a final lattice hop is
allowed), or the sphere stops shifting at the floor (but then the hole has no horizon and a surface that should glow,
which Sgr A* argues against), or the CP's step is set by something other than its own sending sphere.

## 8. Question to the founder (physical picture)

Picture a CP falling into a black hole. As it nears 2M, the side of its Planck sphere facing the hole keeps getting
shorter. **What sets how far the CP steps inward each Moment?**
- **Its own Planck sphere's reach toward the hole?** Then near 2M its steps shrink toward nothing. Either it never quite
  reaches 2M and the matter gathers in a layer there, or its last step is a single grid hop and it gets through.
- **Or something else: the SSV_net its GP computes, the sphere of the GP it is stepping to, or the DP sea carrying it
  inward?** Then it can cross 2M and fall to the core.
- (The third possibility, that the sphere stops changing at the floor, lets the CP through but gives the black hole no
  horizon and a surface that should glow, which observations of the Milky Way's central black hole argue against.)
