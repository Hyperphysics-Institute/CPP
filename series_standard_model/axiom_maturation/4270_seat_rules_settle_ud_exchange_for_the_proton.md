# Seat Rules Settle the u–d Exchange for the Proton: L1, g_A = 1.310

**Patch:** 4270. **Lane:** EW → strong (TODO-4234-DELTA item (ii)). **Session:** 239.
**Founder:** `founders_voice/4270_ruling_seat_rules_up_two_of_four_down_four_of_four.md`.

## 1. The rule

The up quark bonds only to the minus vertices (−qCP, −eCP): 2 of 4. The down quark bonds to any vertex, 4 of 4, the
+eCP only when that vertex is open (free nucleon). A d at a minus vertex needs its linear −eCP to sit on the far side
of its core, and it binds there more weakly than an up does.

## 2. What I think

**(i) For the proton, the rule fixes L1, by counting seats.** The proton has two ups and two minus vertices, and the
ups can use nothing else. For the d to take a minus seat, an up would have to take a plus seat, which the rule
forbids. So the u–d-exchanged configuration (d on a minus seat, u on the d's seat) is not in the proton's state, and
the u–d exchange term is zero.

Only the u–u exchange acts, which is 4269 (L1): **g_A = 1.310 (+2.7%)**. L2 (1.271) needed every quark in every seat,
and the founder's rule excludes that for the proton. It is now closed as a branch, not merely left unadopted.

A d could slip into a minus seat that an up has momentarily vacated mid-swing, since the founder has said the
diagonal seat is sometimes empty. That is not an exchange: no up sits on a plus seat at that moment. It belongs to the
d's own one-body motion.

**(ii) The d's extra freedom acts on the d alone, and only on its one-fifth share.** In a free proton the +eCP vertex is
open, so the d can share its time between the two plus seats. That spreads the d's own state over two sites. It is a
one-particle effect with no Pauli term, it changes only R_d, and R_d carries one fifth of g_A. The +qCP-to-+eCP
distance is not on file, so it has not been computed (filed).

The effect is lost when the +eCP is bonded to a neighbour, as in a nucleus. That makes it a possible
free-versus-bound difference in the d's motion. Its sign and size are unknown; this is an idea to test, not a claim.

**(iii) The rule matters most for the neutron, which has not yet been computed in its own frame.** In udd, one up
holds one minus seat, and the two ds share the other minus seat and the plus seats. The ds are identical to each other,
so d–d exchange can act across all the seats they share. Until now μ_n has come from the proton's R values through
isospin. The neutron's own frame (seats, modes, d–d exchange) is the proper calculation, and the μ_n prediction
(−1.6% at m_q fitted to μ_p) is therefore provisional. Filed.
