## 1. Storing KE as DP-arcs is inertia, and inertia was already in the runs

The founder's picture is that the +eCP stores kinetic energy as DP-arcs while it accelerates and hands it back as SSV
gradient (potential energy) while it decelerates. That is conservative inertial motion: energy passing between motion
and position. It is the class of dynamics 4292 and 4296 simulated, and those runs broke up. So this picture alone does
not change the verdict. It would change it if the DP-arc store adds something beyond Newtonian inertia: a delay between
making and discharging arcs (a memory), a loss, or a rule that only certain amounts can be stored.

## 2. Where the +eCP is fastest

```

```

With the +eCP between two −eCP poles, the centre is a hump, not a valley. The +eCP is **slowest at the centre and fastest

they need against their mutual repulsion.

He calls the configuration low-probability: too intricate for charges to fall into by chance and then become the most
abundant particle. The runs agree. Four classical electrons have failed: the free three charges (4292), the dumbbell

electron is a **ground state**, something every electron settles into whatever its history. Classical orbits do not
behave like that; they keep their starting conditions. Atoms are the precedent: classically unstable, stable only because
of quantisation.
