# 4360's Second-Order Tension Survives an Independent Critic; It Turns on Whether the Relay's PSR Is the Clock's PSR

**Patch:** 4361. **Lane:** foundations, with GR and EW. **Session:** 241.

**Critic:** an independent fresh-context critic (this session) on the 4360 §4 items; its quotes checked here.

## 1. The founder's two points

- **"The band receives the same message whatever the PSR."** Correct, and it is what 4360 assumes: the landing band gets
  the same N DI-bits. The "stronger per grid point" of 4360 is, as he says, the **relayed field beyond the band per unit
  volume**: a PSR-sized region of the far field holds fewer GPs in a well, so the same relayed influence is shared among
  fewer. The ratio reading cancels exactly that per-volume factor, because V_i and SSV_abs are both per-GP quantities at
  the receiving GP. Wording corrected; the mechanism unchanged.
- **"The ½ is a calibration, possibly to revisit."** Right about its standing: β_SR1 = ½ was fixed by Mercury (Patch
  3389), not derived. But it is a calibration to a measurement. In the corpus's isotropic dictionary ½ is the value that
  gives β = 1 (√−g_tt = 1 − U + (β − ½)U²), which Mercury's perihelion pins to ~10⁻⁴. Revisiting it means keeping
  Mercury.

## 2. The critic's findings (verified quotes; derivations its own, checked)

- **k is a convention; k·SSV_abs,0 is not.** "Any rescaling k → αk absorbed into the ΔSSV normalisation leaves every
  observable unchanged" (GR-1 L541–545); kΔ|SSV| = GM/rc² exactly (GR-1 L284). SSV_abs = SSV_abs,0 + Δ exactly (GR-1j
  L193–194). **SSV_abs,0 is never given a value**, so 4360's first-order condition k·SSV_abs,0 = 1/3 is undetermined. To
  meet the clocks it must hold to ~1 part in 10⁸: either a structural identity or a fine-tuning.
- **Linear, not logarithmic.** The harmonic (Laplace) quantity is Δ itself (GR-1j L216–218; census linearity L196–200),
  and ε = kΔ = U = M/r̄ (GR-1 L284; 3634 L6). So rewriting ε as a logarithm is a relabelling, not an escape: exact ratio
  invariance needs PSR = (1 + 3U)^(−1/3) = 1 − U + 2U², which as the clock PSR would mean β = 5/2 (Mercury fails).
- **The κ ↔ U convention** is fixed (ε = U); 4360's numbers stand (annual swing 3.1×10⁻¹⁷ against 1.2×10⁻¹⁷).
- **Two lapses (3703)** coincide below the AP-5 cap, so they do not matter at Earth. If the dilution used the spatial
  ruler instead of the lapse, the residual would be 3.75U², a swing of ~2.6×10⁻¹⁷: still ~2× the bound.

## 3. Where it stands

The founder's mechanism keeps α fixed at first order (if k·SSV_abs,0 = 1/3), and at second order meets one of two
walls: **either α swings yearly at ~2–3× the clock bound, or, if made exact, Mercury fails.** The single fact that
decides it: **is the PSR that sets how many grid points share a relayed message (the relay's reach, GR-1j L209–211) the
same PSR that sets a clock's rate (3634, the one Mercury fixed)?** The corpus says yes. Only a relay reach distinct from
the clock PSR, with its own second-order law, would let both hold.

## 4. Question to the founder (physical picture)

Two things shrink in a gravity well: how far a DI-bit message reaches in one Moment (and so how many grid points share
it), and how fast a clock ticks. Are they the same shrinkage, one PSR doing both jobs, or could the message's reach shrink
by its own rule? If one PSR does both, the mechanism and Mercury's orbit disagree at the second level of precision, by
about two to three times what the best clocks can see.
