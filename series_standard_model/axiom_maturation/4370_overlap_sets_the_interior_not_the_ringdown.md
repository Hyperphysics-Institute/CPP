## 2. Why the overlap cannot move the ringdown or the shadow

In that empty space the census is u = C₂/r, fixed by the total enclosed census alone (GR-1j T-2, exact, L330–340). The
PSR there follows from u through the SSV_abs → PSR curve. So the exterior depends on **two things only**: the total
amount of stress the body puts out, and the shape of the curve.

The overlap the founder describes happens **inside the matter**, where CPs sit within one PSR of each other. Where two
CPs' PSR spheres overlap the stress is double. That is the ordinary addition of their contributions, which the census
already contains (GR-1j's census linearity; 4367). It can change the interior and the total (the body's mass), but not
the shape of the curve the exterior follows, and so not the pitch relative to the measured mass.

**Consequence for 4369's question.** If black holes are to ring at Einstein's pitch, the speed-up has to be in the
SSV_abs → PSR curve itself, as it applies to any grid point, in empty space as much as in matter. The founder's picture
of what happens near the N point is the right place to look, but the effect has to live in the conversion, not in the
packing (§5, question 2).

## 4. What the halt must still answer

Inside a packed body, the pull on an outer CP comes from **all** the mass inside it, not only its six neighbours. Its
neighbours' pulls cancel by symmetry, but the whole interior's does not, except at the centre (the inward pull inside a
uniform ball grows with distance from the centre). So the halt needs the outermost layer to hold against the combined
pull of everything beneath it. In the corpus's terms this is STOP against DRAIN at the core: under AP-5 D1 a saturated
register reads no gradient (STOP), while 3703's DRAIN keeps a residual K/D. His picture says the core stops. That is
consistent with DRAIN carrying matter to the core and stopping there, but the mechanism has to cover the whole-interior
pull (question 1).
