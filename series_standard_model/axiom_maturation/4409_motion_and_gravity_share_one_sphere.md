That is 4397's shifted sphere with the fall speed β replaced by v. **The sign:** the front reach 1 − v plays the part of
4397's "toward the Sun" reach 1 − β. So in his picture a CP held near a mass has, locally, the light geometry of a CP
moving **toward** the mass at the fall speed, which is space flowing **outward** past it: the outgoing (white-hole)

## 4. The swing: velocity versus speed (script part 3)

He is right that the backward leg's **velocity** is always below the body's, with forward faster, and that the legs
average to the body's velocity: exactly when averaged over time (the CP stays with the body), and as a simple average of
the two leg velocities only non-relativistically (0.289 against 0.3 in the script's example).

What sets a clock is **speed**, the size of the motion. **Compared with the forward leg**, the backward leg is always
slower, for every swing, so its clock always runs faster than the forward leg's. **Compared with the body**, the backward
leg is slower in speed only while the swing speed is below 2v/(1 + v²). For a swing near the cap (the ZBW, 4324–4326) the
backward leg rushes backward at nearly light speed: at body speed 0.3 and swing speed 0.999 it moves at 0.998, faster in
speed than the body. That is related to the non-linearity he mentions, and he is right that it does not change the
overall result: over a full cycle the moving clock slows by exactly γ (4408).

His view that a backward-moving CP cannot outrun the body's forward motion in speed conflicts with the corpus's near-cap
ZBW, on which 4408's non-circular γ result rests. That is put to him in §6.

## 7. Answer to his "What do you think?"

His picture of the moving CP is right: it sends out its sphere from where it is, then moves forward inside it, so the
sphere reaches less far ahead and further behind. That is, at each point, the same shifted sphere that gives gravity in
4397, with the falling speed replaced by the body's own speed; with his sign, standing near a mass looks like moving
toward it. His front-to-back-only compression is what makes light's round trip take the same time along and across the

clock, though, depends on speed regardless of direction, and if the swing is near light speed, as the corpus has the
ZBW, the backward leg is fast in speed too. As he says, the overall result is unchanged, but whether the swing is that
fast is worth his answer.
