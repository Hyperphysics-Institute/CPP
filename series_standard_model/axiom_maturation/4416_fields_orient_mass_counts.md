## 2. When it holds: a per-contribution count

**A symmetry, for any weight (script part 1).** Separate each DP along a field: + forward by half the separation, −
backward by half. The DP's two contributions to an unsigned sum are even in the separation, and to a signed sum odd. That is
exact, for any weight with distance (1/r, 1/r², or any per-contribution magnitude). So **an unsigned count of contributions
changes only at second order**, while signed and directional sums change at first order. In a model sea of 200,000 DPs in
a central charge's field, doubling the separation doubles the signed scalar census and the vector sum, and roughly
quadruples the count change, at every receiver. (The count change's size and sign depend on the CPs nearest the receiver
and on the sample, so only the scaling means anything.) In a uniform field, where the separation is a rigid opposite shift of
the two sublattices, the count changes only through granularity and the sea's edge (part 2); in an infinite smooth sea it
would not change at all. This assumes each DP's two members start together. Real DPs have an internal separation of random
orientation, so for them the first-order count term cancels only on average.

**The reading it needs.** AP-4 defines SSV_abs = Σ|polar| + k·Σ|strong|, and each payload's E is the vector sum at its
origin GP. Two readings follow:
- **(P) per contribution:** |polar| counts each CP's contribution before vector summation, the A3′/AP-4 reconciliation's
  rank-0 count Φ (registry, line ~329). His split then holds at first order, by the symmetry above.
- **(V) per payload:** |polar| is the magnitude of each arriving payload's vector sum. Then an applied field raises
  Σ|E_local + E_applied| at every GP: at first order where the applied field dominates, at second order where the local field
  is random and dominant. "Fields add no CPs, so no SSV_abs" does not follow.

## 5. A direct tension with his 4408 answer, and the gravity of field energy

**The tension.** Asked in 4408 whether the hot ball pulls harder, he answered that it does: "because there is more CP
enrollment associated with the mass when it is hot because of the DP-arcs and DP-separation of the DPS produced by the
velocity of CPs (whether paired or unpaired, in arcs or DP separation, polarized, orbital or linear, moving internal to the
mass because of thermal energy, or moving with respect to the absolute frame as velocity drift of the mass). Any CP in
absolute velocity/drifting as a result of being part of a mass cohort will generate increased SSV_abs and exposed SSV_net."
There, DP-arcs and DP separation add CP enrollment and SSV_abs. In 4416 a field's DP separation and arc speed add none. The
same mechanism is affirmed in one answer and denied in the next. His qualifier, "as a result of being part of a mass cohort",
may mark the real distinction (CPs of a mass cohort against sea DPs oriented by a field). 4408 also bears on B: it says a CP's
absolute speed changes its PSR, larger moving backward, so smaller moving forward; under WC-DP-SEA-PV the PSR is the SSV_abs
effect, so faster arc CPs would shift the PSR, against "arc speed adds no SSV_abs". (The script models E separation only; the
B case rests on "no CPs added".) This is put to him (§7).

**The gravity of field energy.** Relativity says a charged capacitor weighs more by its stored energy divided by c².
For an ordinary capacitor that is about 10⁻¹⁷ kg, far below what a balance can resolve. What experiment supports is the same for the electrostatic
energy inside nuclei, by inference from bodies of different composition:
- Kreuzer (1968), a Cavendish-type comparison of fluorine-rich and bromine-rich matter: the active-to-passive mass ratios of
  fluorine and bromine agree to about 5 × 10⁻⁵, so nuclear electrostatic energy is an active source at roughly the
  few-percent level.
- Lunar laser ranging (Bartlett and van Buren 1986, from the offset of the Moon's centre of mass from its centre of figure):
  aluminium and iron agree to 4 × 10⁻¹², about 10⁻⁸ to 10⁻⁹ for the electrostatic part; Singh et al. (2023) improved this to
  about 4 × 10⁻¹⁴.
- MICROSCOPE (titanium and platinum, 10⁻¹⁵) tests the passive side.

**Orders.** A field's energy goes as E² or B², and kinetic energy as v²: both are second order. So "no change at first order"
says nothing about whether field energy gravitates; the gravitating increment is second order, the same order as the count
shifts of part 1. It is G-sized: a 1 T field's energy weighs 4 × 10⁻¹² kg per cubic metre, a potential of about 3 × 10⁻³⁹ over
a metre (part 3). That is far too small to slow light measurably, so it is consistent with his split and with 4415's data,
but it must not be zero, and it cannot be the count redistribution of part 1, which vanishes in a uniform field while the
field's energy does not.

**A guess, labelled as Claude's:** arcs and separation add nothing to SSV_abs at the field's own strength and exactly their
mass equivalent at gravity's strength, the same conversion for every form of energy (rest, kinetic, thermal, field). Neither
of his texts states a scale distinction; this is offered only as one way the two could fit.

small to slow light) and comes in at second order, but relativity and the tests on nuclei say it is not zero.
