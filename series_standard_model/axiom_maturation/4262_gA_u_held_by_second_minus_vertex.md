## 3. What I think (answering the founder's question)

**(i) The picture answers the question, and it matches the number 4261 had to guess.** The u is held in the plane by
its ZBW with the second minus vertex. g_A comes out at 1.333, against 1.332 in 4261 (B), where the held width was set
by hand to the breath's own. The founder's mechanism now supplies that width, so it is no longer an assumption.

**(ii) The inequivalent seats do not change the numbers; they sharpen one question.** Under route (H) every ZBW mode
runs at the Compton frequency, so its ground-state width is set by that frequency, not by how strong its binding is.
The −qCP-versus-−eCP difference therefore leaves every width, and the rows above, unchanged. It would matter under a
classical-amplitude route (rejected at 4243 (C)), or if it distorted the triangle. Seat inequivalence also does not
disturb 4240's 5/3: Fermi antisymmetry concerns exchanging the two identical up quarks, not the two seats, and the
Slater determinant of two different seat states is the correct form.

The question is the diagonal pull on each up quark near the other up (charges ±1 for eCPs, ±2/3 for qCPs, assumed):
- **u_q toward the −eCP**, where u_e sits: −1 + 2/3 = −1/3, net attraction by charge.
- **u_e toward the −qCP**, where u_q sits: −2/3 + 2/3 = 0, so the charges cancel. u_e's diagonal pull is then carried
  by the strong force alone, which requires the −qCP to attract a +qCP core that is not its own.

If it does not, u_e has no diagonal mode and is unheld across its u–d line again. With the u's weighted equally,
g_A would then be 1.369 (+7.4%) before exchange, and the exchange term cannot be computed for an unheld state. This goes to the
founder (§4).

**(iii) "Moving toward each other at random times" is the Pauli term.** Independent modes already give the right
frequency of the moments when both ups swing toward the d. What those moments add is overlap between the two ups'
breaths. With colour = vertex (4261 (C)) the overlap enters as an exchange term, which removes low momentum. With the
u now held firmly along the u–u line, the overlap is 0.35 (it was 0.61 with the looser hold of 4261), and g_A goes
from 1.333 to **1.310 (+2.7%)**. On the founder's picture, this row is the zero-parameter result.

**(iv) What is left is the m_q tension, not missing motion.** μ_p falls with g_A (2.675, −4.2%), as in every route.
Holding μ_p in this state needs m_q = 299.6 MeV. The residual +2.7% in g_A and −4.2% in μ_p is the same tension
4243 (T) and 4261 found with SS-2's assigned m_q = m_p/3.

## 4. Founder question (a physical picture)

The up quark that sits at the −eCP vertex is pulled toward the diagonal −qCP vertex. But the other up quark sits
right there, and its +2/3 cancels the −qCP's −2/3. **So is that pull a strong-force pull — does the −qCP vertex grip
the second up quark's core by the strong force, as it grips its own?** If yes, both ups are held and g_A = 1.310
stands. If the pull is charge only, it vanishes there, and the second up is loose across its line to the down quark.
