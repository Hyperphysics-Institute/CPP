## Copilot's answers (summarized; a third party's text, not the founder's, not filed verbatim)

- **Recoil:** yes. A photon carries momentum h/λ. A mirror reflecting it straight back takes up 2p (a tiny velocity for a large mass, as in radiation
  pressure); an atom emitting a photon recoils equal and opposite, with a small recoil energy p²/2M that matters in precision spectroscopy and laser cooling.
- **Entanglement:** the photon and the recoiling atom can be entangled in momentum when the emission direction is not fixed (a joint state over directions).
- **"Can", not "are":** momentum conservation alone does not entangle. It needs several coherent alternatives for how the momentum is shared; with one
  definite outcome the state is separable. For a free atom emitting spontaneously, entanglement is the natural result.
- **An atom held in a crystal:** the recoil is shared by the larger body, and coupling to many uncontrolled parts makes the clean two-body tie inaccessible
  (decoherence). Distance alone does not destroy the entanglement.
- **Three cases:** Mach-Zehnder (one quantum, two paths), BBO (two photons), emission (photon plus recoiling matter, crossing the light-matter line).
- **Next question it raises:** whether reflection from a massive object entangles depends on the object's momentum uncertainty and on whether the recoil
  states can be told apart.
