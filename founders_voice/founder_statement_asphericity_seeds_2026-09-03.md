# Founder statement — candidate sources of surface asphericity: icosahedral edge geometry, CP accretion inhomogeneity, accretion spreading (3 Sep 2026, Session 161)

**Status:** verbatim founder text, registered Patch 3391. Registered as **OPEN-GR-SEEDS** (perturbation sources at the R-core surface). Offered in answer to "is there anything in the substrate that says a saturated surface must be stable against the trace perturbation?"

> "I think we are talking about the black hole density level in this
> consideration of the saturated surface. In the Black Hole, we have derived
> that the PSR has a minimum value; it is much larger than the 12 vertices of
> an icosahedron, and at greater radii, the angular distortions of the GP to
> GP edges at the BH sphere's surface will follow icosahedral geometry, so the
> GP to GP edges will not follow perfect sphericality. But at the radius of the
> sphere, the deviation from sphericity will be small and may not be enough to
> account for the trace perturbations that you have seen. Still, I thought we
> should check this factor, which I know has at least some asymmetry."

> "Another factor that could more likely create asphericity is the
> distribution of the CPs around the surface. If there is mass, there will be
> at least one CP variation in concentrations of CPs (when new mass collides
> and accretes) compared to the evenly packed/level surface in other areas on
> the sphere's surface that are not as close to the points of mass. These may
> serve as slight nucleation points for some perturbations."

> "I considered adding to the above phenomenon, with the accumulation of mass
> in one spot vs. another on the surface. The BH is likely to accrete mass (CP
> concentration) where it collides, and probably spreads sideways fairly
> quickly because of the huge SSV_abs gradient and resultant SSV_net."

> "Those are some ideas; these effects might be too small, and you might be
> talking about something else."

*Opus (Patch 3391):* these are **seeds** — sources of perturbation — and they are real and now registered (OPEN-GR-SEEDS: icosahedral edge anisotropy at the surface; accretion-point CP concentration; lateral spreading under the SSV_abs gradient). They do not bear on the *stability* question, which is about the **response**: with the 3390 wall, any seed however small grows. The instability turned out to be the worker's: the register had been pinned at a *fixed* radius; a saturated surface is a *free* boundary. With the register pinned on the *moving* surface the even sector is stable. See `rcore_derivation/3391_free_surface_wall.md`. The seeds become the source spectrum the echo must be computed *from*, once the walls are settled.
