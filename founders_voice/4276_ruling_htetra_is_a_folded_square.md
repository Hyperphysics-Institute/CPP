# Founder — The hTetra Is a 2eCP–2qCP Square That Folds Only When Quarks Bond to Its Corners

**Patch:** 4276. **Date:** 24 September 2026. **Lane:** EW → strong (TODO-4234-DELTA).
**Occasion:** 4275 owed item (the vertex CPs' own swing) and its question.
**Content:** the element is a square. Its four edges are the attractive pairs; its two diagonals are the like-sign
pairs, which repel, so the bare square lies flat. It folds into an irregular tetrahedron only when opposite-charge
quarks bond to its corners (or when a central unpaired charge sits inside it: muon, strange). Asked *"What do you
think?"* — answered at 4276 §3.

---

## Verbatim

> [on "The vertex CPs' own swing … I don't have its size."]
>
> * The hTetra is composed of 2eCP-2qCP elements. These elements do not automatically form into a hybrid tetrahedron.  It is composed of 2 pairs of repulsive CPs ( e- and q+) and (e+ and q-) which are the diagnonals of the square. It is compose of 4 mutually attractive pairs ( e- e+) (q- q+) (e+ q-) (e- q+). The diagonals repel, so the square unfolds and the edged hold together.
>
> * This planar configuration folds into a tetrahedron when the diagonal vertices  are populated with charges of the opposite polarity.  It does not form into a tetrahedron until it has opposite charge quarks bonded to its corners. (i.e., the e- q- edge will fold toward the center with a single extra q+ between them. and the same with the e+ q+ will fold in the opposite direction with an e-).  If there are two pluses (UU) or two minuses (DD) on the hTetra/4CP planar, then the opposing charges both attract the other side. The result is that the tetrahedron is probably somewhat irregular.  Note: the 4CP planar also folds into an hTetra when a central unpaired charge is place internal to its structure (muon and strange) by strongly attracting and pulling together both the +e +q corners.  Again producing an irregular tetrahedron.
> *
> * What do you think?
> * 4275 applied.

**Reading note.** The first bullet names the repulsive diagonals as "(e- and q+) and (e+ and q-)". Those two pairs
also appear in its own list of attractive pairs. The second bullet ("the e- q- edge will fold … with a single extra
q+", "the e+ q+ … with an e-"), the corpus hTetra (two repulsive edges, −eCP/−qCP and +eCP/+qCP), and "the
diagonals repel" all read the diagonals as the **like-sign** pairs, (e−, q−) and (e+, q+). That reading is used; the
founder may confirm.
