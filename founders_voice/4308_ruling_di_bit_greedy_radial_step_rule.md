## Verbatim

> You spoke of a cell concept that would work. How do you imagine those cells sharing the DI bits to produce the desired result, and does that seem like a natural use of the system's configuration, or is it just a way to get the result we want?
>
> * Consider this: all DI-bits from GP_origin enter the GP lattice on the first sub-moment, find their first GP_empty1, and stop; then all DI-bits move a second time to GP_empty2, then to GP_empty3... until each has traveled a distance of one PSR.
> * The operational question is how each DI-bit chooses the next GP_emptyx.
> * I postulate that the DI-bit increment rule is: keep moving radially (never reverse) at each sub-moment increment; at each GP, scan the ~6 of the 12 nearest neighbors and choose the one that is empty and advances the farthest in positive radius; if none is empty, continue moving. At each GP, each DI-bit chooses the next GP that is the most radially positive increment.
> * Each DI-bit continues to increment forward until the distance traveled matches the displacement corresponding to SSV_abs. SSV_abs must be translated into a number of GP edges that the DI-bit sums, whose total equals the PSR. The DI-bit stops after taking the total number of displacements corresponding to SSV_abs/GP_PSR.
> * The average number of displacement volleys each CP executes is the number of incremental steps. That number of steps equals the average number of empty GPs each DI-bit stops at before it reaches its PSR for an SSV_abs at GP_origin. GP_origin computed the PSR and imprinted it on the DI-bit.
