# Founder statement — "I had never considered that the GW was anything other than a pressure wave"; is the GW an expression of EM waves? How is the GW generated in GR, and what mechanism in CPP could do the same? (3 Sep 2026, Session 161)

**Status:** verbatim founder text, registered Patch 3604. Not a ruling — a reflection and three questions, answered in `rcore_derivation/3604_how_the_gw_is_made.md`.

> "To this point, I had never considered that the GW was anything other than a
> pressure wave. Your calculations show that it behaves other than as a
> pressure wave, having shear and stress components. As you noted, the EM wave
> has this character. The question is whether the CPP interpretation of the GW
> can duplicate the GR effect."

> "I think this requires confronting how the GW is created. It appears that the
> GR version of the GW is a speed-of-light propagation of what is an EM wave,
> but with gravitational undulations in a shear and stress orientation, like an
> EM wave. Since the EM wave models the GW, it suggests I still don't understand
> how the CPs, DI-bits, and GPs respond to the collision of two gravitational
> bodies. How is the GW generated in GR? What is the mechanism that could do the
> same thing in CPP? The amount of correlation/triangulation in the theory to
> this point makes it unlikely that we have missed something fundamentally
> different. It seems more likely that my understanding of the GW is
> incomplete. It seems likely that the GW is simply an expression of EM waves:
> extreme movements of mass generate a massive EM wave, which would create the
> stress and shear effects you say GWs exhibit."

*Opus (Patch 3604):* the GW is not an EM wave (six observational discriminators, §1 of the record); the founder's instinct that it is *like* one is the right structural instinct — both are transverse polarizations of the Sea, EM the spin-1 (dipolar) kind sourced by charge, GW the spin-2 (quadrupolar) kind sourced by mass-energy — and the CPP mechanism is already registered as a channel (A3′ `Q_ij`) but not derived. The order-of-magnitude bounds of 3604 say what that derivation must deliver.
