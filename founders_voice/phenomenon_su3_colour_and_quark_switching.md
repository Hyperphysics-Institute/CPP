## The claim — what colour and a gluon actually are

Colour is not a property painted on a quark. It is **which vertex of the baryon's bonding tetrahedron the quark is sitting on.** A gluon is not a little ball thrown across a gap between quarks. It is a **quark switching vertices — a hop.** And SU(3), the symmetry the physicists trust without a mechanism, is nothing more exotic than **the grammar of three labelled vertices and the ways a quark can move among them.** The eight gluons are the eight ways three vertices connect. The mathematics (SS-1b) proves that this grammar is exactly SU(3); what follows is the billiard-ball story of what physically does the moving.

## The mechanism, step by step

1. **The frame is a bonding tetrahedron, not a cage.** The core of a baryon is a hybrid tetrahedron — an hTetra — built from a type-A and a type-B hDP. It is the framework that *bonds* the quarks. Three of its four vertices are occupied by quarks; the fourth is an open eCP vertex. The up and down quarks are *cageless* — each has a +qCP core and a polarized DP cloud. The down quark has an additional radial ZBW minus eCP. The up and down quarks bond to one of the four vertices of this shared framework hTetra. The hTetra has a plus and minus qCP and a plus and minus eCP. The down quark, appears to have a minus 1/3 charge at a distance and is therefore attracted to both the +qCP and +eCP vertices on the hTetra framework. The down quark is attracted more strongly to the plus qCP vertex than the minus eCP vertex (as there is both a strong and polar force attraction). (A proton is uud, a neutron is udd; the open vertex V₄ sits empty when the baryon is isolated.) The plus up quark bonds to the minus qCP vertex and is in ZBW oscillation. The down qCP core and minus qCP vertex are in ZBW oscillation. The second down quark is bonded to the plus eCP vertex; the linear minus eCP ZBW oscillates between the down quark plus qCP core and the plus eCP vertex. The fourth vertex is an open minus eCP (which we will see in later steps is filled by the up quark as it moved during one of the ZBW cycles).

2. **Colour is which vertex.** A quark's colour is the bonding-tetrahedron vertex its qCP occupies. Red, green, blue are simply the three occupied vertices. Nothing is painted on the quark — the colour is its seat.

3. **Every quark breathes.** Each quark ZBW-oscillates against its vertex, in and out, between two states. **State 1 (away):** the quark sits off the vertex, and the dominant force is the attraction pulling it back toward its oppositely-charged seat. **State 2 (superimposed):** the quark sits right on the vertex, and now the surrounding Dipole Sea attracts it back out as the direction in which there is an SSV gradient. The bond is the limit cycle between the two — the breathing, in and out, forever.

4. **The quarks are coupled.** Here is the part that makes it compliant with SU(3). SU(3) requires actual switching of quarks between vertices to produce the degrees of freedom required to meet the criterion of being an SU(3) system. Without the vertex switching, the oscillation of the qCPs is just a set of independent springs: each quark's SSV gradient — the slope of force it actually feels — is *shaped by where the other quarks are sitting in their ZBW-State1/2 cycle.* The environment decides whether and where the quark at one vertex moves. The occupancy of the other vertices and the force acting between the quarks determines lateral movement to another one of vertices. The three are not breathing in isolation; they are breathing in each other's fields which produces radial and tangential forces, enabling the required SU(3) degrees of freedom.

5. **A rebound can land on a new vertex — that is the hop.** Because of that coupling, and the genuine complexity of a three-body oscillation, when a quark rebounds out of State 2 the prevailing SSV gradient can place its *next* inward move, going into State 1 on a *different* vertex than the one it just left. From that new vertex, the displaced quark re-enters State 2. That switch — a quark changing which vertex it occupies — is a hop, and a hop is a quark changing colour. The ZBW oscillation is the muscle that performs the move; the move itself is the gluon.

6. **A new seat means new forces.** In its new position the quark sits in a different local arrangement — a different set of attractions and repulsions, a different ZBW profile. It is, in effect, a new little system operating under a new set of forces. This is why the hop is a real physical event and not a relabelling.

7. **Three vertices have exactly eight ways to be rearranged.** Count the switching. Six of them are edge moves — the three vertex-pairs, each with a "to" and a "from" channel. Two more are diagonal balances that shift the weight among the three vertices without a clean swap. Six plus two. **Eight gluons** — and the eight is *forced* by the three, as 3² − 1.

8. **Why it is SU(3) and not eight separate dials.** Because the quarks are coupled (step 4), doing one hop and then a second is *not* the same as doing them in the other order — and the difference between the two orders is itself a third hop in the set. That failure-to-commute is the whole content of "non-abelian." Eight *uncoupled* breaths would be a flat torus, eight independent dials that ignore each other. Eight *coupled hops* among three vertices close into SU(3). The coupling is the difference.

## Where SU(3) comes from

The "3" is the three-ness of the occupied vertices. The "8" is forced from it. The non-abelian closure — that the difference between hop-A-then-B and hop-B-then-A is always a third hop — comes from the coupling, because the moves share a world: each quark's gradient is set by where the others sit. Three labelled vertices, fully connected, leave no other grammar than SU(3). And the colour algebra is **flavour-blind** — it is the same three vertices whether the occupant is up, down, or strange — which is exactly why the proton, the Δ⁺⁺ (three ups), and the Ω⁻ (three stranges) all carry the same eight gluons. The colour does not care what flavour is in the seat; it cares only which seat.

## The link to beta decay (same framework, one vertex over)

The bonding tetrahedron is also the stage on which a neutron becomes a proton. In beta decay a down quark loses its radial −eCP (which leaves as the electron) and its +qCP core migrates to the open minus eCP vertex — down becomes up, the previously-bound vertex becomes the new open one, udd becomes uud. Same framework, same vertices; the strong-sector switching and the weak-sector transformation are two motions on the one tetrahedron. (Its own phenomenon story.)
