# Founder question — does the saturated register follow from the PCD/DI-bit/CP/GP story, or is it a new postulate? (5 Sep 2026, Session 162)

**Status:** verbatim founder text, registered Patch 3640. Not a ruling; a request for a mechanism. Opus's proposal is in `rcore_derivation/3640_budget_law_proposal.md` (PROPOSAL, not enacted).

> "This model sounds like it works, so we should document the specifics
> of how the spring stiffness returns each CP to its PSR position. I
> think we are postulating that when the CP's reach the critical BH
> density, what you call "saturated register," where the SSV_abs =
> SSV_abs_sat that the PSR stays constant at the Saturation PSR for all
> CPs inside the BH. This would be a constant of nature on the order of
> alpha, G, hbar, etc. When waves, Gravitation and EM, hit the surface
> of the BH, they will contribute DI-bits from the surrounding DP Sea to
> the GPs and the CPs in the surface of the BH. If the DI-bits sum to an
> SSV_net, they will produce a CP displacement, which will cause all the
> other CPs to displace, which has to be the case to allow for Linear
> and Angular Momentum. So, this sounds more like stiff putty or a
> combination of putty and oscillation. I don't think I have actually
> described the PCD/DI-bit/CP/GP dynamics. It feels like a new
> postulate, like in some way the rules change and once it is in the
> black Hole the rules of interaction change to produce this minimum PSR
> and c/2 velocity. It sounds like all these methods are consistent with
> empirics and GR, but I don't think they follow from the
> PCD/DI-bit/CP/GP story we have used to derive GR. I may be missing
> something. Can you think of how all of these factors could be put
> together to produce the effects we are currently examining, without
> changing all the work we have done with GR and SR?"
