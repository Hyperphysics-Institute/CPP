## F-1 — is the surface's clock the far observer's clock when a wave hits?

> "The same Moment is happening at the surface clock as at a distant observer,
> but the communication between the two will be separated by Moments, so the
> distant observer will not know of the surface clock's experience of the
> Moment until that effect reaches the distant observer."

*Opus reading (enacted 3382):* simultaneity is universal and is set by the substrate, not by the wave — the absolute-Moment slice is a global "now," and a passing wave does not tilt it; what the far observer sees is the same slice, delayed by propagation.
