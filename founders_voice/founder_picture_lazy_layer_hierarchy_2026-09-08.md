If each layer is a copy of the PCD framework one PSR scale down, the depth at a point is set by the over-demand D/K (about 3 at a black-hole wave horizon; very deep at Planck density, as the founder says; barely 1 layer in a heavy neutron-star core), and the copy's own "displacement" must act on a new degree of freedom — the CP's position within its floor cell — which *is* the second level of motion and, if bounded, supplies the restoring force (NS.2) as an emergent property rather than a new constant. Two things then need defining, not one: the activation overload (is it the same fraction, ⅔, in every layer's own units?) and which degree of freedom the copy's displacement moves. First computation: where the second cap sits and how many layers are active at v = 2 and at Planck density; first observable: the flat-core branch's stability in the heaviest pulsars. Concern noted: a copied shell-sum is a second wave family inside saturated matter (transmission at c/2ⁿ), which must not appear in the exterior ringdown (it does not, if relay is whole) and might appear in neutron-star thermal/glitch behaviour — a place to look, not yet a prediction.
