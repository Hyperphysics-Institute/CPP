## His message (written text, filed verbatim; the line of Claude's summary he quoted is omitted)

> Thomas: Here is another idea: I think local clocks will run slower because the effect of the CP will be felt sooner
> by the GPs in the DI-bit shell than in a less stressed space (i.e., the thicker DI-bit shell's influence will be felt
> closer to the GP_origin, which means that it will be felt sooner; the thicker the band influencing those DPs, the
> sooner they will experience the effect of CP_origin. The normal/free space cycle is: a thin DI-bit shell at the PSR,
> which radiates a thin shell, which re-radiates, which re-radiates, and finally the effect of the GP_origin affects the
> GPs between GP_origin and the DI-bit shell;  in the thick DI-bit shell, the re-radiation is unnecessary, as the
> GP_origin CP has already been affected by the thick DI-bit shell.) So a thicker DI-bit shell increases the CP's
> influence on the GPs within the DI-bit shell, which will dominate the SSV_net (V_i) in the DI-bit shell. This will
> result in a greater attraction toward the GP_origin to opposite-charge CPs in its environment (like-charge CPs will be
> repelled farther, but the opposite charge will be the dominant effect due to the closer proximity). This will slow
> propagation by collision, resulting in the appearance of a slower clock rate.
>
> Note: There may be an effect on the PSR, but it will probably be insignificant.  The thicker DI-bit band will
> increase, on average, the SSV_abs experienced by GPs in the thicker DI-bit shell. Thus, on average, SSV_abs will
> increase from the GP_origin to the PSR, which will reduce the PSR for those GPs. Such a decrease in PSR is the
> ordinary reason why the local clock rate changes. That is,  signals take longer to travel because of the shorter
> SSV_net (V_i) inside a smaller PSR.
