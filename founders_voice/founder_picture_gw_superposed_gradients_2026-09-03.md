# Founder picture — the GW as the time-varying superposition of two orbiting bodies' SSV_abs gradients, radiating at twice the orbital frequency (3 Sep 2026, Session 161)

**Status:** verbatim founder text, registered Patch 3605 as **P-GW-SUPERPOSED-GRADIENTS** (a picture; assessed in `rcore_derivation/3605_source_mechanism_and_the_wave_zone.md`).

> "It sounds like GWs (gravitational energy waves - gravitational waves) are
> produced as a superimposed dynamic SSV_abs field produced by the mutual orbit
> of two massive objects, whether they are BHs or stellar, and the faster their
> rotation, the greater the rate of the GW energy radiation. So, the mutual
> orbit of two gravitational bodies creates the time-varying SSV_abs gradient
> that we detect with LIGO. If that is the case, then we need to figure out how
> the gravitational fields of the two bodies superimpose and vary with time;
> that is, restated in CPP terms, how the SSV_abs gradient between the two
> gravitational bodies superimposes to produce the GW."

> "The mutual orbit around the center of mass of two masses causes a
> moment-by-moment change in the space configuration of the Gravitational
> field (the gradient of the SSV_abs) because each is a source of the SSV_abs
> gradient produced by the accumulation of CPs. In the case of two mutually
> orbiting gravitational bodies, the SSV_abs gradients produced by each
> superimpose, and that superimposition produces a time-varying SSV_abs
> gradient that radiates energy. They are essentially converting their Kinetic
> Energy into the organization of Gravity Waves."

> "The mass configuration of a single planetary, stellar, or BH body produces
> a smooth inverse square gradient. This is a type of organization of the DP
> Sea, and could be considered, in conservation-of-energy terms, to be the
> gravitational potential, and hence the potential-to-kinetic conversion as a
> test mass falls in the gravitational field (typical mgh type of
> calculation). But that gravitational-energy structure of the SSV_abs
> gradient in space associated with the mass in space becomes a time-varying
> SSV_abs(x,y,z,t) function, which changes each Moment when two gravitational
> bodies superimpose their radiated SSV_abs gradient. The function becomes
> more complicated than the simple SSV_abs(x, y, z) we used to calculate a
> body's mgh potential energy."

> "The question is whether this picture of an inverse-square-decaying SSV_abs
> gradient coorbiting another SSV_abs gradient source produces the
> superposition and radiated effect that is predicted by GR. On the surface,
> it appears to match qualitatively because we have a time-varying transverse
> wave oscillating at twice the orbital rate. The rate makes sense because the
> sequence is equidistant from Earth: one in front, one in back; equidistant,
> other in front, other in back, sequentially every single orbital cycle. So,
> there will be a double frequency of the GW compared to the orbital frequency
> of one of the bodies."

*Opus (Patch 3605):* right about the frequency, right about the source mechanism (near the pair the superposed gradients ARE a rotating stretch-one-way/squeeze-the-other deformation of the Sea — Newtonian tides), and right that the energy comes from the orbit. What the picture cannot do by itself is carry that transverse deformation to a detector: a propagating *scalar* field's tidal pattern is purely longitudinal at 1/r (symbolically verified); the transverse part dies as 1/r³. GR's tensor wave carries it at 1/r. So the picture is the *source*; the propagation needs the Sea's spin-2 transverse channel.
