## Copilot's analysis (summarized; it is a third party's text, not the founder's, and is not filed verbatim)

- **First answer:** a finite wave train has a spread of frequencies of order 1 ÷ its duration (the exact factor depends on the envelope); an infinitely long
  sinusoid has one frequency. It suggests teaching the grating in the causal order "finite duration → spread of frequencies → the grating separates
  wavelengths → observed width", rather than "a short photon cannot engage enough grooves". It distinguishes an exact total energy E = hν from an exact Fourier
  frequency ν.
- **Second answer, on his postulate:** the many-cohort DP assembly gives a possible picture of one spatially extended photon interacting across many grooves
  and absorbed as one quantum. But "innumerable arcs" alone does not give a narrow line: many contributions side by side (transverse) are not many cycles along
  the travel (longitudinal). It names two models: (A) a long, phase-locked longitudinal train of cohorts spaced a wavelength apart; (B) a short but spatially
  distributed object with a derived collective eigenfrequency, which would need its dynamical variable, equation, eigenfrequency, lifetime, grating coupling and
  line shape specified. It warns against "many waves each carrying part of the energy", preferring "one extended coherent excitation". It notes that the
  observed width is the source width convolved with the instrument's. Its recommended statement ends with the sentence he quotes. Two of its points are fair to him: a short but sharp CPP photon "would not automatically make CPP wrong", only show that it cannot be
  treated as an ordinary few-cycle wave packet; and it called 4469's correction ("a short pulse must be spread in colour, but a long one isn't automatically
  sharp") a very important one.
