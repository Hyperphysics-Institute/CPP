## Verbatim (written)

> * This brings up the question we discussed at length in our DM series investigation. What happens to the KE when two CPs collide and superimpose?
> * I think we should look at the larger system and treat it as a conservative resonant system, at least in bulk.
> * The up quark moving toward the -qCP vertex will eventually superimpose because there is no force opposing its motion.
> * The real question, the one I want to investigate, is what happens to the KE/inertia carried as DP arcs when the up quark and qCP superimpose? The answer is that the DP arcs did not disappear when the two charges superimpose.  Something happened to them.
> * Since the vertex CP is stabilized by (connected to) a bond to its hTetra neighbors, the up quark's attraction to the -qCP vertex will pull the vertex toward the up quark. As a result, the vertex bonds stretch, slowing its movement toward the up quark.
> * When the up quark and the vertex collide and superimpose, the up quark will have more inward velocity (toward the tetrahedral structure) than the vertex has outward velocity.
> * This will result in the up quark passing through the vertex CP and being decelerated as it gets farther apart. In other words, ZBW is SHM. The spring is the SSV gradient/V_i that accelerates the charges together and produces increasing DP-arcs as the force gets stronger as it gets closer, and reaches a maximum at the moment of superposition.
> * The DP arcs act on the space of the superimposed plus and minus charges. The next Moment, the DP arcs act on the two superimposed charges, causing the up quark to pass through and past the vertex CP into the space internal to the hTetra cage, where the up quark is now experiencing the like charge of the other hTetra Vertices that the CP vertex is bound to in the hTetra.
> * The force the up quark experiences inside the hTetra cage will be repulsive (given that its CP vertex was opposite-charged, and the CP vertex is bound to opposite charges). This drives the CP back toward its vertex CP.  The up quark again goes back into superposition with the vertex CP; then, the next Moment, the up quark moves into the open DP Sea. The up quark is driven outward by its DP arcs plus whatever attractive CP is in that outward direction.
> * As the up quark goes outward away from the qCP vertex, the up quark is driven outward by the DP arcs, but its outward velocity is slowed by the opposite-charge pull of the CP vertex, which will eventually slow it to a stop, then pull the up quark toward the CP hTetra vertex, and the cycle will go again.
> * So, the bottom line is that if this picture is correct, I have been misinterpreting the ZBW cycle for almost a year, and I have built much of the CPP theory on the superimposed, go-to-zero SSV gradient-at-superimposition, then reverse-direction model. The problem with that model was that the DP-arcs that were built up during the acceleration phase were dissipated, and a reverse DP arc was built up by the reverse flow.  This is non-physical. The SHM oscillation of the two charges passing through each other is much more satisfying, but changing this paradigm will have a massive effect on the Corpus of the CPP theory if it is true.
