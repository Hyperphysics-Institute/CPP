## §2 — Why this defuses the worker's 2863 objection

**2863's damped-spring analysis assumed the arc is a freshly excited
oscillation that must ring down.** On that assumption the worker showed
τ ≥ T₀/2π with both weak and strong coupling lengthening it, and
concluded that τ_Sea was conditional on an unpinned damping ratio ζ.

**That analysis answers the wrong question under this reframing.** The
substrate is *never* quiescent — the ZBW carrier rings permanently and
is not an excitation to be decayed. The current does not create an
oscillation; it **offsets one that is already running**. What must
relax is the *bias*, and a bias on a continuously driven system does
not relax by ring-down. Ring-down mathematics does not apply to it.

**The worker records this as its own objection being retired, not the
founder's position being revised.** 2863 §3's ζ-conditionality result
stands as correct mathematics applied to a picture that has now been
superseded.

## §4 — The objection the worker owes this reframing

**A microscopic update interval is not a collective relaxation time.**
Molecules collide every picosecond; sound absorption in the same gas
runs orders of magnitude slower. The substrate's Perceive-Compute-
Displace cycle renews *every CP's state* each Moment — that is the
automaton's construction and it is not in dispute. It does not follow
that a **collective organization spanning a volume** renews in one
Moment.

The founder's own words locate the transaction in *"the leading edge
**volume** of the current"* and in organization *"superimposed upon the
DP arcs"* — plural, extended, collective. **Whether that organization
is fully renewed each Moment, or is held in the environment across many
Moments before restoration, is the quantity that now sets τ_Sea.** It
is unpinned, and it is the successor to ζ rather than its elimination.

**If renewed each Moment: k = 1, and clause 2 needs only v/c < 0.15 —
which the ambient thermal Sea should clear by two orders of magnitude.
If held across N Moments: k = N.**

## §5 — The prize: inertia is the lag, so the lag is invertible

**This is the strongest structural content in the reframing, and it
addresses the evidentiary objection the worker raised at 2863 §5.**

If extraction and restoration were **perfectly balanced each Moment,
there would be no inertial force at all.** A closed exchange with zero
residual delivers nothing to a decelerating body. **The inertial effect
requires an imbalance, and the imbalance is precisely the lag between
extraction and restoration.**

> **τ_Sea is not merely bounded by inertia. τ_Sea is what makes
> inertial mass nonzero.**

That inverts the problem. At 2863 the worker proposed using the *absence
of observed inertial lag* as an empirical upper bound, and flagged that
the panel might reject an empirical input as failing clause 2's
evidentiary burden. **Under this reframing the relation is not a bound
but a functional dependence: m_inertial = f(τ_Sea), and f is
invertible.** Supplying measured inertial mass as input then has the
same epistemic standing as the programme's single existing calibration
input, m_e — a derivation with one measured constant, not an empirical
substitute for a derivation.

**Stated as the worker's reading of the founder's mechanism, requiring
panel adjudication.** The worker notes that this is the third
consecutive turn in which a route toward closing the programme's last
gate has appeared, and that it has not verified that f exists in
closed form, has not derived it, and is not asserting that it does.
