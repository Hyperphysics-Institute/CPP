\begin{abstract}
\citet{RukanGullaSkaar2026} show, within standard quantum optics, that truncating a
single photon with an optical shutter does not yield a photon-or-vacuum mixture but a
superposition and mixture of photon numbers up to infinity, a state that is
nonetheless \emph{locally} equivalent to a single photon on one side of the shutter and
vacuum on the other. The expected photon number $\Nexp$ diverges only in the limit of an
instantaneously closing shutter; for realistic speeds even a thousand created photons is
extremely unlikely. We ask whether this effect is compatible with the postulates of
Conscious Point Physics (CPP), and we find that it is --- structurally, and in three
independent places. (i) A photon in CPP is a perturbation of the Dipole-Pair (DP) Sea, a
real propagating disturbance in a physical medium, so a \emph{cuttable} photon-wave is
native rather than paradoxical. (ii) Many-photon Fock content is ordinary bosonic
multi-occupation of the $600$-cell eigenmodes (QM-5 second quantization). (iii) The
local-simple / global-complex structure is the standard partial trace over DP-Sea
degrees of freedom under the Nexus global-consistency constraint (QM-4 measurement). The
creation mechanism the authors identify --- violation of time-translation invariance ---
maps onto a time-dependent reconfiguration of the DP-Sea boundary by the shutter, i.e.\ a
dynamical-Casimir-type process, reproducing their Noether reasoning rather than
conflicting with it. The non-trivial, framework-specific result is regularization: the
truncation divergence is \emph{ultraviolet} in origin, and CPP carries a hard cutoff at
$k_{\max}=\pi/\lP$ (the same cutoff that renders the electron self-energy finite in QM-5).
Therefore $\Nexp$ is finite, and the divergence class is not assumed: RGS derive it,
showing that the Heaviside truncation produces a Fourier tail $\propto1/\omega$ which, with
their $E(\omega)\propto\sqrt\omega$ weighting, makes the photon number diverge
\emph{logarithmically}; and they regularize it with a formal high-frequency cutoff that
``can be arbitrarily high.'' CPP's specific content is that the
$600$-cell mode spectrum (QM-5) has an \emph{intrinsic} band top $\wmax=\sqrt{12}\,c/\lP=\sqrt{12}/\tP
=2\sqrt3/\tP$, set by the largest adjacency eigenvalue $\lambda_{\max}=z=12$, above which no modes
exist --- so the truncation sum is finite by the finiteness of the lattice spectrum, not by an
imposed cutoff, and the ceiling carries the coordination $z=12$ as a fingerprint. Two regimes
follow. For
a realistic shutter (removal time $T\gg1/\wg$) the mechanical switching already gives
$\Nexp\ll1$ and the lattice cutoff is dormant ($\tP$ is $\sim\!28$ orders below the optical
period); CPP and the continuum agree. The lattice acts only on the idealized instantaneous
shutter, where the continuum has a genuine logarithmic divergence: CPP forbids $T<\tP$, caps
$\wcut\le\wmax$, and yields $\Nexp_{\max}=C\,\ln(\wmax/\wg)\approx 64.5\,C$ for an optical photon ---
order tens, finite, with $C$ an $O(1)$ coefficient set by the photon amplitude at the cut. The
contribution is therefore one of \emph{compatibility plus foundational regularization}, not a
new mechanism: CPP embeds the structures RGS assume (mode decomposition, partial trace,
dynamical Casimir) in a real substrate and fixes the otherwise-formal cutoff at a physical
scale. The honest status is
therefore: \emph{compatible at the structural level; the divergence class is derived
(logarithmic), and the framework-specific result is that CPP supplies the finite value the
continuum's instantaneous-shutter idealization lacks --- a foundational consistency result,
not an experimentally accessible prediction. Zero new axioms; no theorem claimed; the single
remaining residual is the $O(1)$ prefactor $C$ (narrowed OPEN-TP-1).}

\section{Introduction}

A photon is an elementary excitation of the electromagnetic field and cannot be cut into
two photons. Yet one can plainly remove part of an optical pulse with a shutter.
\citet{RukanGullaSkaar2026} (hereafter RGS) resolve the apparent tension within ordinary
quantum optics. Because exact single-photon states have infinite temporal tails, a state
that ``looks like a single photon to the left of the shutter and vacuum to the right''
cannot be a one-photon state; RGS show that the truncated state is instead a
superposition and mixture of photon numbers from zero up to infinity, while remaining
\emph{locally} indistinguishable from a single photon or from vacuum in the two disjoint
regions. The creation of these photons is, in their analysis, a consequence of the
violation of time-translational invariance introduced by the shutter; by Noether's
theorem one then expects an apparent failure of energy conservation. The expected photon
number diverges only as the shutter closes infinitely fast; for realistic shutter speeds
even a thousand created photons would be extremely improbable.

This paper asks a single question: \emph{is the truncated-photon effect compatible with
the postulates of Conscious Point Physics?} The answer developed below is yes ---
compatible structurally, and in three independent places --- and the one framework-specific
consequence is a regularization of the RGS divergence by the CPP ultraviolet cutoff. We
do not re-derive the RGS result; we accept it as a correct statement about the continuum
theory (noting it is not uncontested) and read it against the CPP substrate.

The logic has a useful self-checking feature. CPP claims (QM-5, \citealp{abshier2026qm5}) that
standard quantum field theory is recovered as the continuum limit of $600$-cell lattice
dynamics. Since the RGS result \emph{is} standard QFT, CPP must reproduce it in that
limit, including the divergence. Bare compatibility is therefore nearly automatic and is
not, by itself, the contribution. The contribution is the place where CPP departs from
the continuum: the lattice carries a hard ultraviolet cutoff, the truncation divergence is
ultraviolet (and, as \S\ref{sec:class} shows, logarithmic), and instantiating RGS's own
formal cutoff at the lattice scale removes the continuum's instantaneous-shutter divergence.

\section{The Truncated Photon in the Continuum}\label{sec:rgs}

Let a single-photon wavepacket be
\begin{equation}
|1_\xi\rangle = \int d\omega\,\xi(\omega)\,\hat a^\dagger(\omega)\,|0\rangle,
\qquad \int d\omega\,|\xi(\omega)|^2 = 1 ,
\end{equation}
with temporal envelope the Fourier transform of $\xi(\omega)$. The shutter is not a
number-conserving projector on the one-photon sector; it is a time-dependent boundary
condition imposed on the field. RGS show that the resulting state has support on every
photon-number sector,
\begin{equation}
|\psi_{\mathrm{cut}}\rangle = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} c_n\,|n\rangle_{\mathrm{(mode\ structure)}},
\qquad \Nexp=\sum_n n\,|c_n|^2 ,
\end{equation}
and that the two reduced states obtained by tracing over the field on either side of the
shutter are, respectively, a single photon and vacuum to the precision set by a narrow
transition region. The hallmark of the mechanism is that $\Nexp$ grows without bound only
as the switching becomes instantaneous: a sharper shutter injects higher-frequency
content, and an idealized step demands content at arbitrarily high frequency. This is the
structural signature of an \emph{ultraviolet} divergence, and it is the hinge of
everything below.

\paragraph{Why this is dynamical-Casimir physics.} A shutter that changes the field's
boundary condition in time is, formally, the same class of process as a moving mirror or
a cavity of time-varying length \citep{Moore1970,Dodonov2020,CironeRzazewski1999,Braunstein2005},
which converts vacuum and single-quantum input into multi-photon output by a Bogoliubov
transformation mixing $\hat a$ and $\hat a^\dagger$. The dynamical Casimir effect has been
observed in superconducting circuits \citep{Wilson2011}. RGS make the mechanism explicit in
their own terms: ``mirror removal converts vacuum energy to real photon energy in the
forward- and backward-propagating modes,'' and they note that the squeezed-vacuum
partial-trace structure is the same one that, in the Unruh effect, makes the restricted
vacuum appear populated \citep{Crispino2008}. The number of created quanta is
$\Nexp=\|T_2\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2=\sum_n\lambda_n^2$, the Hilbert--Schmidt norm of the
Bogoliubov mixing operator; instantaneous switching makes it diverge, while smooth
switching converges. Crucially for what follows, RGS regularize their field integrals with
an explicit high-frequency cutoff that they state ``can be arbitrarily high'' (their Eq.~B14)
--- a formal device left unidentified with any physical scale.

We show that each surprising feature of the truncated photon lands on a standing CPP
mechanism rather than requiring a new one. The claim is one of \emph{embedding}, not

an instantiation of existing CPP structure, not a novel constraint on the effect.

\subsection{A photon is a cuttable disturbance of a real medium}
In CPP a photon is not a fundamental object but a perturbation of the Dipole-Pair (DP)
Sea --- the lattice vacuum whose ground-state dipoles transmit all interactions. A
propagating photon is a propagating disturbance \emph{in a medium}, and truncating a
disturbance in a medium is unremarkable. Where the continuum theory finds it awkward that
an ``indivisible'' particle can have part of it removed, CPP finds the extended,
clippable photon-wave to be the natural description: the wave aspect of wave--particle
duality is carried by an actual substrate excitation, and the shutter acts on that
excitation directly.

\subsection{Many-photon content is ordinary bosonic occupation}
QM-5 derives second quantization from the $600$-cell eigenmode expansion: any DI-bit

field operator $\hat\phi(r_i)=\sum_k(\hat a_k u_k(i)+\hat a_k^\dagger u_k^*(i))$. Photon
modes are bosonic (no occupation exclusion), so a state spanning many photon numbers is
ordinary multi-occupation of Sea modes. The ``$0$ to $\infty$ mixture'' is a superposition
over occupation numbers of the relevant DP-Sea modes --- native, not exotic.

\subsection{Local-simple / global-complex is the measurement structure of QM-4}
QM-4 solves the CPP measurement problem by identifying apparent collapse with

\subsection{The creation mechanism: a driven DP-Sea boundary}
RGS attribute photon creation to broken time-translation invariance. CPP states the same

\section{Derivation of the Divergence Class}\label{sec:class}

The regularization argument of \S\ref{sec:reg} needs the divergence to be ultraviolet, and
its numerical ceiling needs the spectral class. Both are settled by the RGS kernel itself;
neither is assumed.

\begin{lemma}[Logarithmic divergence class]\label{lem:logclass}
In the RGS construction the forward/backward spectra that enter the created-photon number are
\begin{equation}
\xi_\pm(\omega)=E(\omega)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\,\theta(\pm x)\,\xi(x)\,e^{-i\omega x},
\qquad E(\omega)=K\sqrt{\omega},
\end{equation}
where $E(\omega)\propto\sqrt\omega$ is the standard electric-field mode normalization of the
quantized field (consistent with the RGS quantization), and the expected number created from
the initial vacuum is the mode sum
$\Nexp=\sum_\xi\big(\|\xi^-_-\|^2+\|\xi^-_+\|^2\big)$. For any photon wavefunction with
$\xi(x)\neq0$ at the cut point $x=0$, the truncation spectrum is logarithmically divergent:
$\Nexp\sim C\,\ln(\wcut/\wg)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The Heaviside truncation $\theta(\pm x)\xi(x)$ carries a step discontinuity at $x=0$, so its
Fourier transform decays as $1/\omega$ at large $\omega$. With the normalization
$E(\omega)\propto\sqrt\omega$ this gives
$\xi_\pm(\omega)\sim\sqrt\omega\cdot(1/\omega)=1/\sqrt\omega$, hence
$|\xi_\pm(\omega)|^2\sim1/\omega$ and
\begin{equation}
\Nexp\sim\int^{\wcut}\frac{d\omega}{\omega}=C\,\ln\frac{\wcut}{\wg},
\label{eq:logclass}
\end{equation}
a \emph{logarithmic} divergence.
\end{proof}

This is exactly the conclusion RGS state for the instantaneous shutter (``the Heaviside
function leads to a Fourier transform with asymptotic behavior $\propto1/\omega$ \ldots
together with $E(\omega)\propto\sqrt\omega$, the norms diverge logarithmically''). The class
is therefore derived from their kernel, not posited.

\paragraph{The coefficient $C$ carries no hidden enhancement.} The prefactor
$C=O(1)$ is set by the photon amplitude at the cut $|\xi(0)|^2$ and the mode-sum measure; it
varies with the detailed cut profile but is bounded and admits no parametric enhancement
beyond the logarithm. A pulse cut near a node ($\xi(0)\to0$) gives a smaller $C$; one cut at
its peak gives a larger but still $O(1)$ $C$. There is no regime in which $C$ becomes large
enough to lift the ceiling out of the order-tens band; the only way to enlarge $\Nexp$ is
through the logarithm, i.e.\ a sharper cut, which the lattice caps. Pinning $C$ for a given
profile is the residual OPEN-TP-1.

\paragraph{Two regimes.} A real shutter has a finite mechanical removal time $T$. RGS bound
the gradual case by
\begin{equation}
\Nexp\le\frac{\kappa_0}{4T}+\frac{\kappa_0^2}{16T^2},
\qquad |T(\omega)|^2=\frac{1}{1+(\omega\kappa_0)^2/4},
\label{eq:rgsbound}
\end{equation}
valid for $1/\wg\ll\kappa_0\ll T$, where $\kappa_0$ is the integrated mirror permittivity.
For an optical photon ($\wg/2\pi=10^{15}\,$Hz) at transmissivity $10^{-4}$ this gives
$\Nexp\lesssim1$ only once $T$ drops to $\sim\!10^{-14}\,$s. Two regimes result:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Regime A --- realistic shutter ($T\gg1/\wg$).} The mechanical smoothing of
\eqref{eq:rgsbound} already holds $\Nexp\ll1$. The lattice cutoff is dormant: $\tP$ lies
$\sim\!28$ orders of magnitude below the optical period $1/\wg$, so $\wcut\sim1/T\ll\wmax$
and the lattice never binds. CPP and the continuum coincide.
\item \textbf{Regime B --- idealized instantaneous shutter ($T\to0$).} Here the continuum
has its genuine logarithmic divergence \eqref{eq:logclass}. CPP forbids $T<\tP$ (one
Absolute Moment is the sharpest physical truncation), so $\wcut\le\wmax$ and the
divergence is capped.
\end{itemize}
The gradual-bound framework of \eqref{eq:rgsbound} cannot itself be pushed to the lattice
scale: its validity window $1/\wg\ll\kappa_0\ll T$ at $T=\tP$ would require $\kappa_0$ both
$\gg1/\wg\approx1.6\times10^{-16}\,$s and $\ll\tP\approx5.4\times10^{-44}\,$s, which is
impossible. The Planck scale is reached only in the sharp regime, where \eqref{eq:logclass}
governs.

\paragraph{The two regimes in one statement.} For realistic shutters ($T\gg1/\wg$) the RGS
gradual bound \eqref{eq:rgsbound} ensures $\Nexp\ll1$ and CPP reduces to the continuum; for
the idealized instantaneous limit, the continuum's logarithmic divergence is capped in CPP by
the intrinsic band top $\wmax$, yielding $\Nexp_{\max}=C\ln(\wmax/\wg)$. The framework-
specific content lives \emph{entirely} in the second regime: the continuum's instantaneous-
shutter infinity is a cutoff artifact, and CPP supplies the finite value the idealization
should have carried. We therefore explicitly disown the v0.1 phrasing that the lattice renders
$\Nexp$ ``finite for every physical shutter'': for every \emph{realistic} shutter the lattice
is irrelevant in any observable way; it caps only the unphysical instantaneous idealization.
The result is conceptually foundational, not experimentally consequential.

\paragraph{The cutoff is intrinsic: the 600-cell band top.} The framework-specific step is
stronger than instantiating a formal cutoff by hand: the $600$-cell mode spectrum has an
intrinsic maximum. QM-5 quantises the lattice field on the $600$-cell adjacency matrix, whose
spectrum is the six distinct eigenvalues $\lambda\in\{12,\,1{+}\varphi,\,\varphi{-}1,\,
1{-}\varphi,\,-\varphi,\,-(1{+}\varphi)\}$, with mode dispersion
$\omega_k=c\sqrt{|\lambda_k|}/\lP$ \citep{abshier2026qm5}. The largest eigenvalue is the coordination
number $\lambda_{\max}=z=12$, so the field has a \emph{hard band top}
\begin{equation}
\wmax=\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}}\,\frac{c}{\lP}=\frac{\sqrt{12}}{\tP}=\frac{2\sqrt3}{\tP}
=6.43\times10^{43}\,\mathrm{rad/s},
\label{eq:bandtop}
\end{equation}
above which \emph{there are no modes at all}. The truncation sum is therefore finite not
because a cutoff is imposed, but because the lattice spectrum is finite: the $1/\omega$ tail
of \S\ref{sec:class} simply terminates at $\wmax$. This answers the obvious critique
(``standard QFT with a Planck-scale cutoff'') at its root. First, the cutoff is not a
heuristic regulator taken to a chosen scale; it is the literal top of the $600$-cell band,
fixed by QM-5's dispersion before this problem was posed. Second, it is not the generic
Planck value $1/\tP$ but $\wmax=\sqrt{z}/\tP=2\sqrt3/\tP$ --- it carries the $600$-cell
coordination $z=12$ as a fingerprint, distinguishing it from any theory that merely places a
cutoff ``at the Planck scale.'' The framework-specific content is thus concrete: an intrinsic,
$z$-bearing band top, not a renamed continuum regulator.

\section{Lattice Regularization of the Divergence}\label{sec:reg}

The one place CPP departs from the continuum is the fate of the divergence.

\begin{proposition}[Lattice regularization of shutter-induced photon creation]
\label{prop:reg}
Assume (a) the truncated-photon expected number $\Nexp$ diverges only in the
instantaneous-shutter limit, with the logarithmic ultraviolet form \eqref{eq:logclass}
derived in \S\ref{sec:class} \citep{RukanGullaSkaar2026}; and (b) the CPP field, quantised on
the $600$-cell adjacency spectrum, has an \emph{intrinsic} maximum mode frequency --- the
band top $\wmax=\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}}\,c/\lP=\sqrt{12}/\tP$ of \eqref{eq:bandtop}
\citep{abshier2026qm5}, above which no modes exist --- and forbids any shutter from switching faster
than one Absolute Moment $\tP$. Then $\Nexp$ is finite, and bounded over the set of \emph{all}
physical shutters by $\Nexp_{\max}=C\,\ln(\wmax/\wg)$ --- with the bound saturated only in the
idealized instantaneous limit; for realistic shutters $\Nexp\ll1$ by \eqref{eq:rgsbound} and
the bound is far from tight.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:logclass} the created-photon spectrum on $[\wg,\wcut]$ integrates to
$\Nexp=C\ln(\wcut/\wg)$, which diverges only as $\wcut\to\infty$. By (b) every lattice mode
has $\omega\le\wmax$, so the spectrum has no support above $\wmax$ and the effective
$\wcut\le\wmax$. Hence $\Nexp\le C\ln(\wmax/\wg)<\infty$, uniformly over shutters.
\end{proof}

The structural conclusion (finiteness) needs only that the divergence is ultraviolet and
that the cutoff exists; the numerical ceiling additionally uses the derived logarithmic
class \eqref{eq:logclass}.

\paragraph{The numerical ceiling.} With $\Nexp(\wcut)=C\ln(\wcut/\wg)$ and the intrinsic band
top $\wcut\le\wmax$,
\begin{equation}
\Nexp_{\max}=C\,\ln\!\frac{\wmax}{\wg}.
\end{equation}
For an optical photon ($\wg/2\pi=10^{15}\,$Hz, $\wg=6.283\times10^{15}\,\mathrm{rad/s}$,
the RGS reference case) and the band top
$\wmax=\sqrt{12}/\tP=6.43\times10^{43}\,\mathrm{rad/s}$,
\begin{equation}
\ln\!\frac{\wmax}{\wg}=64.5,\qquad \Nexp_{\max}\approx 64.5\,C,
\end{equation}
an order-tens ceiling. The $z$-fingerprint sits inside the logarithm as
$\ln(\wmax/\wg)=\tfrac12\ln 12+\ln(1/\tP\wg)=1.24+63.25$: the $600$-cell raises the naive
Planck-cutoff ceiling ($63.3\,C$) by exactly $\ln\sqrt{12}$. The leading-log coefficient
$C=O(1)$ is now the \emph{only} model-dependent input; fixing it requires evaluating the
Hilbert--Schmidt mode sum $\|T_2\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2$ over the $600$-cell modes (with the
density of states near the band top, \S\ref{sec:class}) --- the residual OPEN-TP-1.

\paragraph{The coefficient is continuum, not lattice-enhanced (v1.1).} A direct analysis (script \texttt{1712}) settles the \emph{structure} of $C$, if not yet its absolute value. Writing $\Nexp=\int|\xi(\omega)|^2 g(\omega)\,d\omega$ with the RGS tail $|\xi|^2\sim A/\omega$ and $g(\omega)$ the photon density of states, the logarithm is \emph{range-dominated}: it accumulates over the many decades $[\wg,\wmax]$ where the lattice dispersion is effectively continuum ($g\approx g_0$), so the leading-log coefficient is the continuum value $C=A\,g_0$ --- the $600$-cell does \emph{not} enhance it. The lattice's only coefficient-level contribution is the band-top van Hove pile-up ($g\sim(\wmax-\omega)^{-1/2}$), which adds a finite, $O(1)$ correction $\Delta$ to the log \emph{argument}: $\Nexp=C[\ln(\wmax/\wg)+\Delta]$.
