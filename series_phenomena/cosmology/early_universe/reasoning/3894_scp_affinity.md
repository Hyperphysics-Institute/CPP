He asked for the dictation to be turned into a coherent story, so I did that first and separately, in the founders_voice file, keeping his terms and his emphases and quoting him where the wording carries the idea. The assessment went in its own document, because mixing the two would blur what he said with what I concluded about it.

The story, once assembled, is tighter than a transcript makes it look. An SCP is the whole stack on a site, and the point that everything hangs on is that it carries a composition — a proportion of the two charge species — which varies between sites and therefore makes each stack a distinct entity rather than a generic quantity. That sorts them into Q-dominant and E-dominant, and then the asymmetry: Q-dominant stacks attract each other strongly, attract E-dominant ones less, while E-dominant stacks are indifferent and attract both equally. One class discriminates and the other does not. His own summary is the cleanest statement of it — the Q-dominant SCP is the asymmetric attractor.

There is a second skew reinforcing the first, which I nearly missed because it arrives late in the dictation. The products differ in persistence. Opposite-charge q pairs enter oscillation and hold; e pairs form but are, in his phrase, not faithful, and pair-swap readily; and when q pairs swap, the strong interaction binds all three, giving the two-minus-flanking-a-plus arrangement. So the q channel accumulates structure while the e channel churns. Affinity skews what meets what, and persistence skews what survives meeting.

Then the assessment, and the first thing to say is that this answers the warning I wrote two sessions ago rather than waving at it. I had recorded that if the unstacking were Poisson the fluctuation would be white and the candidate would die like the seven before it. Differential affinity is preferential attachment — a site's propensity to accrete depends on what it has already accreted — and that is correlated by construction. It cannot be Poisson. Better, it is the right class of process: rich-get-richer dynamics generically produce power-law, scale-free statistics, and scale-free is exactly what the spectrum wants.

But I made myself write the new risk down in the same patch, while the mechanism is fresh and attractive, because the property buying scale-freedom is the same one that threatens Gaussianity. Preferential attachment generically gives heavy tails, and heavy tails are precisely what killed the chain-of-chains cascade at 0730 — scale-free and non-Gaussian by two to three orders in excess kurtosis. The candidate must not inherit that fate by a different road, and the time to say so is before anyone builds on it rather than after.

The escape, if there is one, is multiplicity, and it is quantitative rather than hopeful. At the pivot the Hubble volume holds about ten to the fourteen Planck spheres, each with something like ten to the seventy-four points, so a single observable mode averages over ten to the eighty-eight points and, dividing by any plausible stack number, at least ten to the sixty-four stacks. Central-limit averaging over that many contributors flattens heavy-tailed microphysics unless the correlations reach far enough to defeat it. I carried the stack number as a parameter again rather than picking one, which is now the third session running that the discipline has held on that particular quantity.

So the deciding quantity has changed, and I think that is the session's real content. It is no longer the strength of the affinity but its correlation length. Short-range and the central limit wins, the field Gaussianises, and the scale-freedom has to come from the engine-tracking rather than from the clustering — which the candidate can live with. Long-range and the non-Gaussianity survives the averaging and the 0730 wall applies directly. Either way it is a narrower and more computable question than the one it replaces, and the question moved from "is the unstacking random?", which he has now answered, to "over what range is it correlated?", which I can go and work.
