# Reasoning capture — Patch 3920: the degeneracy, and correcting my own optimism

*Session 219, 11 Sep 2026, EU lane. Finding: `eu1_derivation/degeneracy.md`. Verify 10/10.*

Last session I closed by saying the remaining exit had become a tractable cross-lane problem with a stated target: reconcile the engine relation with the cosmological-constant lane's derived density, which agrees to two orders and scales identically. That was one session of over-confidence and the first thing I did this time was test it rather than build on it.

The test is short. Put their density into the field equation and ask whether it determines the expansion rate. It does not. The horizon radius appears squared on both sides and cancels exactly, leaving a bare statement that a dimensionless coefficient should equal three. So the equation is degenerate: it fixes a number and sets no scale whatsoever.

Which means my proposal was not merely optimistic, it was the wrong shape. Their density cannot supply the boost coupling, because it carries no scale to supply. The engine does something their density cannot do — it sets the scale — and the two are therefore not rivals at all. They answer different questions. That also means the framing I gave two sessions earlier, of the engine as a departure from the field equation, was wrong in this direction as well as in magnitude. It is not a departure; it is an answer to a different question.

Having corrected myself I looked at what the degeneracy actually is, and it is the best thing in the session. Their density is automatically of the self-consistent form at every epoch. It does not have to be arranged to track the expansion rate — it tracks by construction, because the inverse-square horizon dependence falls out of the pairing and degradation mechanism rather than being fitted to anything. So the vacuum problem in this framework is not a hundred and twenty-three orders, nor eighty-three, nor the two I quoted last session. It is a single dimensionless number that ought to be three and comes out around three hundredths, with nothing free in it — the lattice coordination, the fine-structure constant, and a bounded cycle average, all fixed before comparison. A derived number that misses is falsifiable, which is exactly why their lane has a falsifier firing on it rather than a fitting exercise.

The consequence I had not appreciated until writing it out is that a density proportional to the squared expansion rate is not a cosmological constant at all. It tracks. So the equation of state is not minus one, and their lane has already computed the present value over the admissible range. That is testable now and has nothing to do with this lane. The degeneracy is not a defect in the result; it is the result's observational content.

So I re-posed the exit, and the new form is harder than the one I retracted but better shaped. Since the density fixes a number and the engine fixes a scale, the question is what breaks the degeneracy and sets the scale, and whether that same structure is what fixes the coefficient. If one mechanism does both, then the perturbation amplitude and the vacuum coefficient come out together. That names a single object to go and look for, rather than a reconciliation between two things that turn out not to be in conflict.

I kept the convention caveat from last session in its own section and did not let anything above weaken it. My factor of a hundred and their published one-and-a-half to ten cannot both be right, the difference is almost certainly where the eight-pi sits, and it is theirs to settle. Saying so twice in consecutive patches is not redundancy; it is the thing that stops a cross-lane number from hardening by repetition.
