# Reasoning capture — Patch 3845: computing p, and what the attempt found instead

*Session 182, 9 Sep 2026, EU lane. Verbatim at-patch capture. Finding: `eu1_derivation/orientation_field_and_p.md`. Verify 8/8.*

I set out to compute p and stopped almost immediately, because working out how the anisotropy reappears requires knowing what the order parameter actually is, and I realised nobody had checked whether it exists in the corpus at all. Three patches have now developed C-4's coupling, mass scale and anisotropy on a degree of freedom that had never been located. That is the sort of thing that should have been settled at 3839.

It exists. FI-C-RC-1 registers n̂ as a substrate-foundational primitive, grounding what the glossary calls the Picture B substrate-orientation field, registered at Capotauro v2.0. So C-4's field is ratified rather than invented, which is a genuine clearing and one I would rather have had three patches ago than now.

More consequentially, it is a *primitive direction* — continuous, not vertex-quantised. That settles a fork I had not named in 3843 and which the whole p-question turns on. Under a vertex-quantised reading, a mean director at an intermediate angle has to be built by mixing the twelve axes, which means local mixing, which means local SSV_net of order unity, which means the covering anisotropy applies at full strength and C-4 dies at 7×10⁴. Under the continuum reading, a uniform n̂ configuration is translationally homogeneous for *every* n̂, and a homogeneous state has SSV_net = 0 exactly. So the displacement anisotropy vanishes identically across all uniform configurations, the potential is flat, and the mass from that mechanism is zero at every order.

Which means there is no p. The parameterisation I wrote last patch was the right question asked of the wrong branch. I am glad I labelled it explicitly as a scaling ansatz rather than a derivation at the time, because that is what makes withdrawing it cheap; had I presented it as a result, this patch would have been a retraction rather than a refinement.

The mass is not zero, though, and the corpus says where it comes from. FI-C-RC-2 registers n̂-induced edge perturbations with a magnitude fixed by a perturbative distance ratio — the same object used to ground the chirality magnitude at a derived rather than posited grade. That is the real mechanism, and structurally it is the same story as before: the anisotropy is proportional to the departure from exact cancellation, but the departure is now sourced by n̂'s own coupling to the geometry instead of by the cosmological perturbation. Transferring the requirement gives g(δ) ≤ 2×10⁻¹⁰, and in the natural quadratic case δ ≤ 1.4×10⁻⁵.

I did not read δ's value, and I want to be explicit that this was a choice rather than an oversight. FI-C-RC-2 sits in the Capotauro flagship and the chirality sector, and the charter's fourth bar keeps this lane out of cross-lane objects. Reading it is a deliberate excursion and should be taken as one with the maintainer's assent, not folded quietly into an EU patch because it happened to be convenient. This is now the second cross-lane item load-bearing for an EU result, the other being the stale-blocker correction from 3833, and I said so in the finding rather than continuing to work around both.

One thing I made myself write down: C-4's debt (1) has now been restated four times — the mass bound, then compute λ, then compute p, now evaluate λ from FI-C-RC-2's distance ratio. Each restatement genuinely narrowed the question and moved it toward something the corpus already owns, which is the right direction of travel. But four restatements of a single debt is also evidence that it was under-specified when it was first written, and a reader three months from now should be able to see that from the record rather than infer it.
