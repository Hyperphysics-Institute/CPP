# Reasoning capture — Patch 3813: the founder's kinetic-bulk / packed-sphere picture, registered and assessed

*Session 168, 9 Sep 2026, EU lane. Verbatim at-patch capture. Founder text: `founders_voice/founder_picture_kinetic_bulk_packed_sphere_2026-09-09.md`. Assessment: `eu1_derivation/founder_kinetic_picture_assessment.md`. Verify: `scripts/3813_kinetic_picture_checks.py` (4/4).*

The founder answered the sharpened Q1 with a picture that has three parts, and my first job was to keep them apart, because they fare very differently and a single "what do you think" verdict would be false to at least one of them.

The first part — the KE of a CP's first move is conserved as polarization of the bulk's DP arcs — I recognized as the thing I had left on the table at 3808: the D1-clipped flux of the pairing motion, named from the CP's side. What is new is that he makes it the source of the bath. The corpus has always posited kT ~ E_Pl by timescale separation without a source; this gives it one, and it folds the reheating budget and the bath clause into one problem. I recorded that as consistent and as a unification, without a number, because there is still no demand-magnitude instrument and PD-007 has not moved.

The second part — that this KE drives the expansion — I had to separate into two readings. As the driver it fails for reasons already in the corpus: a push is short (3805), a push driver is power-law (the tilt), and kinetic energy decelerates. I did not soften that. But as the carrier of the fluctuation it is the first thing that meets 3812's requirement without being told to: H_eff is κ₀ times kT times ln n̄, so a waver in the bath's energy is a waver in H of the same fractional size, collective and slow. I wrote it down as candidate C-1 and stopped there, because the δN step is not automatic — under the pure count law the e-fold total does not depend on kT and ζ would vanish. Whether the end of the window depends on kT is a computation, and it is the first thing T-3b should do. I put my expectation on record (ζ ≠ 0 through the end condition, S not equal to 57) so the answer can be judged against a prior.

The third part — the packed sphere — is the one I had to be most careful with, because it is attractive (it solves homogeneity and pairing in one stroke) and it is fatal if literal (two per address is n̄ = 2, a quarter of an e-fold, and a four-nanometre ball). The paper's count and the picture cannot both be read literally. I did not choose; I asked the one question that decides it and set the default to the reading that keeps the paper's count, labelled as an extension. I also did not retire the twelve-vertex picture — that is a founder ruling, and both pictures are now on record.
