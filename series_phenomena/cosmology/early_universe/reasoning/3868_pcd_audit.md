# Reasoning capture — Patch 3868: the PCD audit

*Session 193, 9 Sep 2026, EU lane. Verbatim at-patch capture. Finding: `eu1_derivation/pcd_audit.md`. Verify 10/10.*

The founder asked me to reconsider the closures reached without the PCD model properly in hand. The 3860 episode had shown I was reasoning about the protocol without it, and his question is whether other verdicts were reached the same way. It is the right question and I went through the arc's closures one at a time.

One re-opening was real, and it was the biggest verdict in the arc. The no-go at 3835 argues that only the count enters the e-fold difference, that the count is a locally conserved density, that conserved densities obey integral constraints forcing a blue spectrum, and therefore that no light mode can source the perturbation. Every step of that assumes the count is a flat local one. The protocol does not deliver a flat local count. The sub-PSR cascade result says the perceived signal maximises inward and is inverse-square class, and an inverse-square kernel is long-range. Long-range weighting is exactly what evades integral constraints, because those constraints bind local densities.

I worked out what it would do. In three dimensions the transform of a 1/s² kernel is 4π Si(kR)/k, which for large kR goes as 1/k, so the weighted power spectrum is the density's divided by k squared. A conserved density's blue k² becomes k⁰ — scale-invariant, which is precisely what ten sessions of candidate hunting failed to produce, and it would have come out of the protocol rather than from any candidate. For about twenty minutes this looked like the amplitude problem resolving itself.

It fails on the cutoff, and I should have seen that sooner. The kernel is not inverse-square out to infinity; it stops at the perceptual radius, because that is what the reach means. With the cutoff in place the transform is 4π Si(kR)/k, and for kR much less than one the sine integral goes as kR, so the transform tends to 4πR — a constant, the top-hat limit. The long-range enhancement exists only for wavelengths shorter than the reach.

Then I checked how far the observable modes are from that boundary, because "in the top-hat limit" would be a weak conclusion if they sat near kR ~ 1. They do not. At a hundred megaparsecs, kR is about five times ten to the minus sixty. Sixty orders inside the limit. At observable wavelengths the cascade weighting is indistinguishable from a top-hat over the perceptual radius, and the no-go is untouched. The route closes on a number rather than an argument, which is the right way for a promising route to close, and I wrote in the finding that I wanted it to work, because the record should show which way the worker was leaning.

The remainder of the audit came out as confirmation, and two of the confirmations are worth more than the word suggests because the cascade could have overturned them. C-4's interaction range was fixed at the perceptual radius on the strength of the shell-deposit clause; if the inverse-square weighting were dominated by small separations, the coupling would act at the grid spacing instead and the mode's mass would be thirty-seven orders too large rather than four. It is not dominated by small separations: the weighting exactly cancels the r-squared volume element, so every radial shell contributes equally and the integral's mass sits at large s. The range was right. And the dichotomy at 3837 worried me, because a cascade-weighted count looked like a third branch neither B1 nor B2 had covered — but at long wavelengths the cascade count is identical to a top-hat count over the reach, which is Branch B2 exactly. There is no third branch where it would matter, which is why the fork was exhausted.

The rest have no PCD dependence at all. The bath-temperature and composition closures never used a flat-count premise; the cascade is species-blind because the receiver sums magnitudes and only the strong slot identifies species, and a rate coefficient stays a rate coefficient however the count is sampled. The frustration and edge objections concern the pattern rather than the kernel that samples it, and a top-hat-equivalent kernel introduces no scales into a pattern.

So the answer to the founder's question is no: nothing in the arc reverses. I think that is a better result than it sounds, because before this patch every negative carried an unexamined dependence on how the protocol samples the count, and now none does. The two closures that could have flipped are positively supported rather than merely unchallenged. What I was careful about was not letting the audit's outcome be foreordained — the one candidate for reversal was tested at full strength, with its favourable limit worked out first, and it survived on a margin I quantified rather than asserted.
