# Reasoning capture — Patch 3816: the occupancy re-grounded (FORK-EU-OCCUPANCY-1 → Branch P, small-ball reading)

*Session 169, 9 Sep 2026, EU lane. Verbatim at-patch capture. Founder text: `founders_voice/founder_ruling_ignition_ball_one_psr_2026-09-09.md`. Finding: `eu1_derivation/occupancy_regrounding.md`. Verify: `scripts/3816_occupancy_regrounding_checks.py` (6/6).*

Before answering the founder's three questions I went back to what the rules actually say about the PSR, because the fork had leaned on a phrase — "the large early PSR spanning the sphere" — and I wanted to know whether the corpus permitted it. It does not. The glossary makes l_P the rest-frame PSR and calls it the per-Moment reach *ceiling*; the PSR entry says it shrinks with SSV_abs; R-PSR-LAW-LOG is a contraction law fixed by Mercury. Nothing on file lets the reach exceed l_P. So Branch P as 3814 wrote it would have been new physics, and I had been about to propose it to the founder as the default.

The way out was already in the glossary two lines down: l_P is the scale of a coarse 600-cell motif, not the GP spacing; GPs sit at finer, sub-Planck spacing, with "~10³⁰ per l_P" recorded as unverified. If the pre-ignition ball is one Planck sphere across, then 10⁸³ addresses at sub-Planck spacing fit inside it and a CP with its *ordinary* reach perceives all of them. The ball is small; the reach is normal. Same arithmetic as Branch P (64.5 e-folds, saturation by breadth), grounded inside the rules instead of outside them. I put that to the founder as the small-ball reading and asked whether "large early PSR" had meant a reach literally larger than l_P. He said the phrase was inaccurate and that he meant the ordinary l_P reach over a Planck-sized ball. That is the ruling.

His first question — is this a new single-use axiom — I answered no, and I think the answer holds up under scrutiny. Three ingredients. The ball's radius is a clause of the initial condition he already carries; it was implicit and is now explicit. That everyone perceives everyone on Moment 1 is *derived*: AP-3/AP-4 have the GP compute SSV_abs from the previous Moment's arrivals, and before ignition there are none, so every register is empty, SSV_abs = 0, the PSR is at its ceiling, and the reach covers the ball. The first Moment is the only Moment in the universe's history with empty registers — that sentence is the whole derivation. The genuinely new thing is definitional: n̄ in eq. Nstar means CPs within one rest-frame Planck sphere, not CPs per GP. With that, n̄ ∝ a⁻³ is a fixed volume times a thinning density, inflation ends at one CP per Planck sphere, and N_* = ⅓ ln N_CP − ln(R_init/l_P). The paper's thirteen seed GPs were a stand-in for a Planck-sphere count of about one.

I made myself check the resolution requirement rather than assert it, and the verify script caught me: I had written the required spacing as 1.6×10⁻²⁸ l_P from a sloppy cube root; it is 3.5×10⁻²⁸. The conclusion survives — the linear reading of the unverified 10³⁰ estimate is sufficient by ~300, the volumetric reading is short by ~10⁵³ — but the number in the document is now the computed one. The requirement is a consistency condition on the hierarchy, not an input to any prediction, so 0736's rule that the resolution enters no formula still stands.

The second and third questions answered themselves once the first was right. After Moment 1 the ordinary rule applies immediately — Moment 2 has every GP holding ~10⁸⁴ arrivals, SSV_abs at its lifetime maximum, the PSR contracting hard — and as the crowd thins the PSR recovers toward l_P, the same law run backward. No hand-off to tune. Full contact lasts one Moment because the rules permit no more, and one Moment suffices because the engine reads what is held (S-HENGINE-HELD) and D4 conserves it. His black-hole question from 3814 gets a cleaner answer than the fork gave: Moment 1 looks like nothing else; Moment 2 looks like the inside of a black hole; depth and breadth one tick apart.

What I did not want to bury is the consequence of that contraction. A contracted reach perceives a smaller count. If the engine's n̄ is the per-Moment *perceived* count and the PSR sits at a floor well below l_P through the crowded era, the end condition arrives early — by 3 ln(PSR_min/l_P) e-folds, which is large. If the engine's n̄ is the D4-held count, §3's law stands as written. I don't know which, and R-PSR-LAW-LOG doesn't reach that load. So I registered it as OPEN-EU-PSR-EARLY-1 with the pivot's survival as its pass line, and said plainly that this is where the empirics test the picture. I did not compute S or anything downstream of it; the fork is closed, the re-grounding is written, and T-3b is next in its own step. The panel sees the package with T-3b's result, not before — a re-grounding is not a win.

The paper's own sentence about a large early PSR putting the patch in causal contact is now re-grounded by the empty-register Moment, and whether any later epoch has a larger PSR than its successors is part of the new open item, not something the paper may lean on; that goes in the V1.5 note.
