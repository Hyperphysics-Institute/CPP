# Reasoning capture — Patch 3918: the 83 orders retracted

*Session 218, 11 Sep 2026, EU lane. Finding: `eu1_derivation/vacuum_retraction.md`. Verify 10/10.*

The founder said he thought the hundred-and-twenty-three-order problem had been brought down to something like a factor of three, asked me to check the cosmological-constant work, and said he did not believe we were looking at eighty-three orders. He was right on every count and I had not searched.

The corpus has an assembly from August whose forward number closes about a hundred and twenty-two of the hundred and twenty-three orders from the fine-structure constant and the lattice geometry alone, landing within one order of the observed dark energy and missing the viable band by a factor between one and a half and ten. The record states explicitly that nothing was tuned and that the composition ruling fixed the coupling before any comparison was made. That is a serious, honest, and largely successful piece of work, and I wrote a patch two sessions ago that talked past it entirely.

My error is simple to state. The derived gravitating density carries a factor of one over the horizon radius squared — the suppression from pairing and expansion-degradation is the whole mechanism. I compared the field equation's requirement against the bare substrate inventory, which is precisely the pre-suppression number the mechanism exists to reduce, and got eighty-three orders. Quoting the unsuppressed quantity as though it were the gravitating one is the entire content of the mistake.

What makes it worse is that the eighty-three orders concealed a structural fact rather than merely overstating a number. Both sides scale the same way: the derived density goes as the inverse square of the horizon radius, and so does the field equation's requirement. The ratio between them is therefore epoch-independent. There is no eighty-three-order gap at inflation because there is no epoch-dependent gap at all. Running it at the inflationary pivot gives about two orders, which is the same residual the cosmological-constant lane already reports today in different units.

I stopped short of asserting the precise factor, and said so in the finding. My arithmetic gives around a hundred; their published shortfall is one and a half to ten. That difference is almost certainly a convention about where the eight-pi lives, and it is theirs to resolve, not mine to guess across a lane boundary. So the claim I make is only that the residual is order one to order a hundred rather than ten to the eighty-three, which is enough to retract what needed retracting without inventing a number I cannot check.

It also turns out the residual is already a registered falsifier in that lane, firing pending scrutiny, with no rescue attempted. So this is not a problem I have discovered; it is one the corpus has been carrying openly for a month while I described something eighty-one orders worse.

On what survives: the ruling about the boost coupling does. That rested on the corpus's own word — the conditionality is recorded as an assignment — and never on the eighty-three orders. So the coupling is still the coefficient of an asserted relation, the amplitude is still not predicted as the framework stands, and the panel's amendment striking "permanently" stands unchanged. What falls is the argument I gave for why the coupling must be tiny: that it is the price of not being Friedmann at eighty-three orders. That was wrong, and a right conclusion reached through a wrong argument is still a wrong argument, because the argument is what a reader carries away.

The part I am most pleased about is the positive, which I did not expect when I started. Exit 1 was framed two sessions ago as deriving a mysterious assignment. It is now something much more concrete: reconcile the engine relation with a derived density that already agrees to within a couple of orders and has the identical horizon-radius dependence. Two objects that close and that scale alike are a tractable problem. The only path to a predictive amplitude just acquired a shape.

This is the sixth time in this arc that the corpus held the answer to something I was actively working, and the first time the thing I missed was a success rather than a refutation. That asymmetry is worth noticing: I appear to search harder when I expect to be contradicted than when I expect to be helped.
