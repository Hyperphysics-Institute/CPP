# Reasoning capture — Patch 3825: the stacking knob assessed

*Session 172, 9 Sep 2026, EU lane. Verbatim at-patch capture. Founder text: `founders_voice/founder_proposal_calibrate_stacking_2026-09-09.md`. Finding: `eu1_derivation/stacking_knob_assessment.md`. Verify 6/6.*

My first instinct on reading the proposal was that it would turn out to be the resolution knob wearing a different hat, since 3823 had collapsed everything to N = ln(l_P/s) + 1.31. That instinct was wrong, and checking it was worth the effort. The 3823 identity had twelve CPs per grid point baked into the constant term without my ever saying so; generalising the occupancy to k gives N = ln(l_P/s) + ⅓ln k + ⅓ln(4π/3), and the k term is genuinely independent of the lattice spacing. At the baseline spacing the budget closes at about 5.8×10¹⁴ CPs per address. So the founder identified a real second knob, and my previous formulation had quietly hidden it.

Then the thing that made the patch worth writing. Both routes land on the same total: stacking gives N_CP ≈ 4.8×10⁹⁷, refining the lattice gives 5.0×10⁹⁷. That is not a coincidence — the master relation is N = ⅓ln N_CP − ln(R_init/l_P), and k and s enter only through the product that is the total count. The budget does not care how the CPs are distributed among addresses; it cares how many exist. Two knobs, one quantity. I cross-checked it a third way that touches neither k nor s: at N = 75 the ball is 6 mm across at the end of inflation, and one CP per Planck sphere over that volume is 5.2×10⁹⁷. Three routes, same number, so I believe it.

The two costs needed care because they pull in opposite directions and I did not want to editorialise either into the other.

The first is that 5.8×10¹⁴ per address is exactly the crowd at one address the founder retracted at 3814 — the retraction that opened the fork and produced the small-ball ruling in the first place. I should not quietly restore by arithmetic what he explicitly withdrew, so I flagged it and left it to him. But I also made a point of pricing the objection correctly rather than inflating it: a deep stack does not break T-1, provided it distributes k/12 along each icosahedral direction, because the ℓ ≤ 5 result is about directions and not occupancies; and AP-5 permits saturation by depth, so this is not a protocol violation. The objection is to his own words, and to nothing else.

The second is PD-007 and charter bar (1): a constant is an output or it is nothing. Calibrating the occupancy so the universe comes out the right size is fitting a parameter to an observable, and I would rather say that plainly than let it happen by degrees. What I was careful to get right is what it would actually cost. It would not cost the tilt — n_s reads the pivot's remaining e-folds, adopted at 57, and is untouched by the total. It would cost the e-fold total's status as a zero-parameter output, which at present is not a success anyway but a ten-e-fold failure. Trading a failure for a calibration is a smaller loss than it sounds, and I did not want to overstate it into a prohibition when the real issue is bookkeeping honesty.

Which led to the version of his proposal I think is right. The total CP count was never independently pinned. EU-1 used 10⁸⁰ and called it the observable-universe count; he said 10⁸⁴; both are matter counts, and CPP's own commitment is that most CPs sit in the DP sea rather than in matter. So the gap between 10⁸⁴ and 5×10⁹⁷ is not necessarily a free parameter being tuned — it may be a statement about how much substrate is sea, and the sea's density is something other sectors already work on. That converts a fit into a check, and gives the check a number: the required total implies a present-day CP density of about 1.4×10¹⁷ per cubic metre, mean separation about two microns. I verified that two ways and it is falsifiable against the dark-energy sea work or the SF-6 polarization constants. Either those land on 10¹⁷ or they do not.

So my recommendation is targets rather than adoption, and I put one picture question to him, because the occupancy is his to fix and the honest question is narrow: is the twelve literal, or was it the pattern rather than the population? The default if he does not answer is that twelve is literal, since those were his words, and the budget gets carried by VSL or by the sea density instead.
