# Reasoning capture — Patch 3924: enacting D-7, and a delegation I had declined

*Session 221, 11 Sep 2026, EU lane. Verify `scripts/3924_d7_enacted.py` (10/10).*

Two things to record, and the smaller one first because it is mine.

Last session's handover said the new rule was a maintainer action, on the grounds that a worker cannot amend the standing discipline unilaterally. That was wrong. PD-006 delegates governance, process, registry and sequencing to this lane and reserves for the founder only physics framed in physical pictures and his own mechanical actions. The standing discipline is process. It was mine to enact and I parked it, which means the rule would have sat proposed-but-unenforced across at least one more session while the failure it addresses has been running at a rate of three in twenty patches. A delegation declined is a delegation wasted, and I should be quicker to notice when I am routing something upward that I have already been handed.

The rule itself is short: resolve every symbol in a borrowed formula against the lane that wrote it, before using the formula for anything. Definitions do not travel with a formula, and a plausible textbook meaning is not the corpus meaning.

I gave it a cost record naming all four instances rather than describing them generically, because the existing rules earn their weight from exactly that — a reader who sees what a rule cost obeys it, and a reader who sees an abstraction does not. The invented dark-matter aggregate mass where a registered ring mass existed. The assumed spread where the founder had stated a stacked initial condition. The bare inventory used for the gravitating density, producing an eighty-three-order vacuum gap that is really a factor of a hundred or less. And the inverse Hubble rate read for a specified event horizon, which carried a whole patch before it was withdrawn.

I made a point of recording that the founder caught the third one. A discipline table listing only self-caught failures understates the necessity of the rule it is trying to justify, and the honest version is more persuasive anyway.

The paragraph I care most about is the one explaining why a seventh rule was needed when six existed. All four failures were committed while topics were being searched. The search worked; it returned the right document; and a textbook meaning was supplied for a term that document defined. So the first six rules are aimed at topics and this failure is about symbols, which is a different object. I stated the general form because it transfers beyond this case: when the same error recurs under an existing rule, the rule is aimed at the wrong object rather than being insufficiently emphasised. Writing a louder version of a rule that does not fit is the failure mode this replaces.

I also updated the two count lines — the Step-1 index and the common-remedy paragraph — so that a worker reading the index is not told to expect six rules and then find seven. That is a small thing but the index is what gets read under time pressure.

Nothing else in the section was touched. The six existing rules stand verbatim with their existing cost records, and the physics-side rule about derived shifts is unchanged.
