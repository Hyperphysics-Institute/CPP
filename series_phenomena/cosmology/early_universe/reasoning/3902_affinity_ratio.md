# Reasoning capture — Patch 3902: the affinity ratio, and the check that stopped me

*Session 210, 11 Sep 2026, EU lane. Finding: `eu1_derivation/affinity_ratio.md`. Verify 10/10.*

I followed the discipline I set myself last session and wrote the structure and the couplings down before forming any ratio, which is the only reason I trust what follows even slightly.

The structure came out better than I expected, and it is the part of this patch I would defend hardest. The strong-charge species carries both kinds of charge; the electric species carries only one; and the strong channel requires both partners to carry strong charge. That alone gives the asymmetry: the electric-dominant stack couples at the electromagnetic strength whatever it meets, so it is indifferent, while the strong-dominant stack reaches the strong coupling only with its own kind, so it discriminates. That is precisely what the founder described in his walk-and-talk, and it falls out of charge content without being asked to. It is also entirely independent of any observation, which none of the rest of this patch can claim.

Then the couplings, fixed before the ratio. The electromagnetic one is what it is. For the strong one I used standard one-loop running to the Planck scale with six flavours and a two-hundred-MeV scale, which gives about two hundredths — no framework input and no freedom to choose. The ratio is then two point seven, against a predicted two point seven six, comfortably inside the band I had set at 1.7 to 6.5.

At that point I had a result and could have written it up. Instead I asked where the Hubble rate comes from, and the answer stopped me. In this corpus it is an amplitude-normalised quantity: the paper carries it as a bound, and the standard relation between the scalar amplitude, the slow-roll parameter and the Hubble rate fixes it from the observation. So computing the curvature amplitude end-to-end from it would be circular, and I nearly did exactly that — I had the arithmetic half-written and it gave four and a half parts in a hundred thousand against an observed four point six, which would have read as the amplitude derived and would have been an artifact.

What survives the check is narrower. Using the independently computed patch fluctuation, the required correlation length is a hundred and thirty-nine against the derived hundred and thirty-seven, which is one and a half per cent. And the observation enters that comparison only at the sixth power, because the length scales as the amplitude to the two-thirds while the Hubble radius scales as the amplitude to the minus a half. A tenfold error in the observed amplitude would move the requirement by only half again. So it is not trivially circular, and it is not observation-free either. A consistency check. I wrote the verdict in those words and put the refusal of the stronger claim in its own section, because the stronger claim is the one a reader will want to take away.

The last thing is the one I think matters most, and I made it a numbered check so it cannot be skimmed. This is the third session running in which a computed number has landed near a required one — the inverse fine-structure constant, then the order-unity saturation argument, now the coupling ratio. Three in a row is either a framework that works or a worker pattern-matching systematically, and I cannot tell which from the inside. What I can do is establish whether they are independent, and they are not: the brake fed the prediction and this patch tests the prediction. One chain, one observational input entering weakly, two couplings. So a reader should weigh it as one result rather than three, and I said so in those words.

Two caveats I put in the standing section rather than burying. The relation between the unstacking fraction and the affinity is the re-stacking-dominated limit of a two-rate steady state, and in the opposite limit the ratio goes to one, which would fail. And identifying the framework's strong coupling with the Standard Model's, run to the Planck scale, is an assumption nobody has argued for.
