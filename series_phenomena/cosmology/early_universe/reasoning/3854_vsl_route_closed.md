# Reasoning capture — Patch 3854: the VSL route closed

*Session 186, 9 Sep 2026, EU lane. Verbatim at-patch capture. Finding: `eu1_derivation/vsl_route_closed.md`. Verify 8/8.*

I went in expecting to compute a horizon integral in a varying-signal-speed background and instead spent the session reading, because the first thing I did was open EU-1's §Background to see what its VSL clause actually asserts. It asserts something more specific than I had been carrying forward for six sessions: that the horizon and causal-contact problem is addressed by a large early Propagation-Speed-Ratio rather than by a large number of e-folds, so that inflation is repurposed as the spectrum generator rather than the horizon-solver.

That is a claim about causal contact. The 3823 shortfall is a claim about size — whether the ball, inflated and carried through a thermal history, reaches the observable radius today. In standard inflation the same sixty e-folds settle both, which is exactly why they are easy to conflate, and I conflated them at 3823 when I named VSL as the escape. A theory can put the whole sky in causal contact by fast early signalling and still fail to make the universe large enough. The VSL clause simply does not speak to the budget.

Then I checked whether it could help anyway, because "wrong problem" is a weaker result than "no effect", and the only way VSL could bear on a size budget is through an enlarged early ball. It cannot, and the reason is a small identity I had not noticed. The requirement and the delivery both carry the same logarithm of the initial ball radius, so it cancels in the difference: the shortfall is ln(R_obs/(l_P a_ratio)) minus a third of ln N_CP, and R_init is nowhere in it. I verified across eight orders of magnitude in R_init and got 10.51 every time. Enlarging the ball reduces the requirement and the delivery by precisely the same amount. That is a cleaner way to close the route than any argument about what VSL means, and it also disposes of a natural next suggestion before anyone makes it.

The third thing is the one I find uncomfortable. The premise the route needs — a large early PSR — has not been available since 3816, where the founder ruled that phrase an inaccurate description and I re-grounded the paper's sentence to the first Moment's empty-register reach over a Planck-sized ball, explicitly noting that an enlarged later PSR was now an open question rather than an assumption the paper could lean on. Then 3837 worked that question and found both branches give a PSR at or below l_P, never larger. So when 3823 named VSL as the most promising escape, the premise had already been withdrawn, and by 3837 both its branches were closed. And it was carried forward as owed in five subsequent handovers — 3823, 3835, 3837, 3841, 3851 — every one of them written by me.

I put that in the finding as a bookkeeping note rather than burying it, because it is the second time in three sessions that an item carried as "owed and promising" turned out to have been closed by the lane's own earlier work; the first was C-4, which the chirality sector had refuted before it was proposed. Both were found by reading rather than computing. The pattern is that the handovers propagate open items faithfully and never re-examine their premises, so an item can outlive the thing that made it plausible and keep being handed forward with its original description intact. That deserves a governance note more than it deserves another computation.

What is left is clean. The shortfall depends only on the total CP count and the post-inflation thermal history — not on the ball, not on the reach, not on the lattice resolution except through the count. So the ten and a half e-folds are now a constraint on N_CP alone, needing about 5×10⁹⁷ against the founder's 10⁸⁴, which is exactly the number 3825 reached from the other direction and which already carries an independent observational pin in the present-day CP density. One route, one number, one check.
