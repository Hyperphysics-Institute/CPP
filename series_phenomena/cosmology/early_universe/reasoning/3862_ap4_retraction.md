# Reasoning capture — Patch 3862: retracting 3860's half-two failure

*Session 190, 9 Sep 2026, EU lane. Verbatim at-patch capture. Finding: `eu1_derivation/ap4_retraction.md`. Verify 10/10.*

The founder said the timing of DI-bit transmission was already laid out in the axioms and founders_voice and asked me to check before going further. He was right, and what I found retracts the central claim of my previous patch.

R-OUTWARD-FANOUT, his own clarification from the middle of August, says that at every hop a grid point's received DI-bit count splits equally among all neighbours with a positive outward radial component, and that the ten percent shell thickness comes from variation in the total hop count along paths from origin to the perceptual shell. That sentence settles it: propagation from origin to shell is a multi-hop cascade, and since the shell is the per-Moment reach, every one of those hops happens inside a single Moment. There is no waiting for the next Moment for influence to move inward.

Worse for my previous patch, the cascade was not merely described but computed, in the same August patch. The pass-3 results file counts six to twenty-two hops from origin to shell, reproduces the founder's ten percent band, and states the sub-PSR profile outright: the steady-state signal maximises inward and is inverse-square class with some lattice structure. There is a verify script on file. The item I treated as an unexamined hole is recorded in the frontier as substantially resolved at the field-profile level.

So two things I wrote at 3860 are wrong and I have withdrawn both. I asserted that the relay requires prior-Moment content and therefore cannot operate at the first Moment; it is a within-Moment hop cascade and requires nothing of the kind. And I declared the sub-Moment escape closed by founder ruling, when the ruling says only that ZBW is not sub-Moment — it says nothing about propagation hops, which the founder's own fan-out clarification establishes and the cascade computation counts.

Having retracted, I checked whether the Moment-1 count is merely un-undermined or actually supported, and it is supported. AP-4 says every grid point emits the same fixed number of DI-bits every Moment, which is unconditional and does not depend on anything having been received; and SSV_abs is explicitly count-like, more bits received meaning greater SSV_abs. Since n̄ is a count, what first-Moment contact has to deliver is exactly what the unconditional emission and the hop cascade do deliver. The check that 3816 flagged and that I reported at 3860 as returning negative in fact returns positive. Branch P's re-grounding stands, the e-fold count is a derivation rather than a posit, and the four patches I had said inherit a failed premise inherit nothing.

One residual is real and much narrower than what I claimed. The E and S vectors in the payload are snapshots of registers integrated in the previous Moment, so at the first Moment their directional content is empty. Bits carry count without direction. That does not touch n̄, which is a count, but it does bear on the ignition's dynamics, and I recorded it as an observation rather than inflating it into another problem.

What 3860 keeps is its first half, and I want that said clearly rather than buried in a retraction. Exactly-once deposit is forced by emission-budget conservation, since pass-through tallying would inflate the deposit total by the ten-to-the-thirty-two grid spacings of a shell-length path, and the shell locus then hands over the inverse-square law from counting alone. That was worth deriving and stands untouched.

The lesson is the founder's and it is sharper than the one I wrote two sessions ago. He asked whether this was a new axiom when the every-GP-contact idea first came up, and then told me to check the record. The mechanism had been on file for a month with a verify script attached, and I nonetheless declared a protocol failure, wrote a consequence chain undermining the base of the arc, and annotated two documents in place — all without searching for the mechanism the clause names. This is the third instance of the same gap in this arc and the most serious, because the first two wasted a session each while this one produced a false negative against sound work. The discipline I wrote at 3854 was to re-read the patch that registered an item; that is not enough. The rule has to be to search for the mechanism, not merely the item's history.
