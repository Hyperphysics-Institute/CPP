# Reasoning capture — Patch 3888: C-5, the evaporative generator

*Session 203, 9 Sep 2026, EU lane. Founder text: `founders_voice/founder_proposal_c5_evaporative_generator_2026-09-09.md`. Finding: `eu1_derivation/c5_evaporative_generator.md`. Verify 10/10.*

This is the first proposal in the arc I have had to take seriously as a candidate rather than assess and close, and I want the reasoning for that on the record rather than the enthusiasm.

The seven closed candidates all failed on one of two things, and I tabulated them to check I was not imagining the pattern. Six were initial-slice properties — a feature of the starting configuration, stretched, carrying one characteristic scale and no generator. Three later proposals were post-inflation, which makes them active incoherent sources and excludes them on the acoustic peaks. Every single failure is one of those two. The founder's proposal is neither: it runs during inflation and it generates continuously, which is exactly the gap I named at 3831 and then failed to fill for twelve patches.

The part that made me stop and check twice is the hinge, because it is a corpus fact and not something the proposal has to assume. AP-4 says every grid point emits the same fixed number of messengers every Moment — per grid point, not per Conscious Point. I verified that wording. It follows immediately that Conscious Points superimposed on one grid point are perceptually one: a site hosting twelve emits what a site hosting one emits. So the founder's evaporation raises the perceived count without changing the conserved count, because what grows is the number of occupied sites. The source is a rate.

And a rate is not a conserved density, which is the entire premise of the no-go. That result runs: only the conserved count enters the e-fold difference, conserved densities obey integral constraints forcing a blue spectrum, therefore no light mode can source the perturbation. If the perceived count is an occupancy count, the first step fails and the rest never runs. I was careful in the finding to call this an escape from the no-go's scope rather than a refutation of it, because the no-go itself said what its escape would be — a field other than the count, modulating the end condition — and the reservoir is that, with the founder's superposition playing the field's part.

Two further things came out favourably without my arranging them. The tilt is inherited rather than fitted: continuous generation at a rate tracking the engine gives equal amplitude per mode at horizon exit, which is why standard inflation is scale-invariant, and with the engine going as the remaining e-folds that hands back exactly the observed index. C-5 does not have to be bent to match. And the dark-energy analogy is structurally sound rather than decorative — superimposed points during inflation doing what dipole entities do during dark energy, both as evaporating stock filling new volume. That makes it one process at two epochs, and it ties to his own cascade clarification from five sessions ago. Economy of mechanism is evidence, and I said so, because a picture doing two jobs with one move is worth more than two pictures.

What I then had to be disciplined about is that none of this makes it work. The whole thing turns on a single question: does the occupancy count Conscious Points, or occupied sites? I settled the referent at 3876 as points per Planck sphere, but that was settled against the dilution question, where superposition never came up. The per-grid-point emission clause says the perceived count is occupancy. The held-versus-perceived fork I worked at 3837 is this same distinction, examined under a model that did not consider superposition either. So the lane has framed this twice and settled it neither time against this case, and it is now load-bearing: occupancy and C-5 lives, points-regardless and C-5 dies with the seven.

I registered four debts in order, with the referent gating the rest, and I put the amplitude second with an explicit note that PD-007 bars calibrating it. That matters because C-5's near-relatives have twice looked good until someone supplied a free parameter, and I supplied one myself two sessions ago.

The scope sentence I wrote last and deliberately: C-5 is the first candidate whose structure is right, and its viability reduces to one already-framed question. That is a materially better position than any of the seven reached and it is not a solution, and I would rather the record said both than let the first clause travel alone.
