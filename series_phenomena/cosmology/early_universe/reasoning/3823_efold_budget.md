# Reasoning capture — Patch 3823: the e-fold budget audit

*Session 171, 9 Sep 2026, EU lane. Verbatim at-patch capture. Finding: `eu1_derivation/efold_budget_audit.md`. Verify 6/6.*

The founder's edge picture prompted a question I should have asked at 3816 and did not: if the pre-ignition ball is the whole universe and it has an edge, where is that edge now? The arithmetic is elementary and I did not like the answer.

Inflating the ball by the re-grounded 64.5 e-folds and then applying a standard post-inflation thermal history puts the *entire* ball at about 0.4 Mpc today. The observable universe is ~14,000 Mpc. The universe would be five orders of magnitude smaller than what we see out of the window. Something in the chain is wrong, and it is worth being precise about which link, because the temptation is to blame the newest piece of work rather than the load-bearing one.

I checked the direction of the error first. Lower reheating temperature makes the requirement *larger*, not smaller, so taking H at the tensor-line ceiling gives the most favourable case available; 75 e-folds is a floor, not a midpoint. So the shortfall is real and is about 10.5 e-folds at best.

Then I saw where the 10.5 comes from, and it is not mysterious at all. Standard inflation starts from a Hubble-sized patch. At these energies the Hubble radius is ~5×10⁴ Planck lengths. Starting from one Planck length instead costs exactly ln(5×10⁴) ≈ 10.8 e-folds. The shortfall *is* the price of the small-ball reading. The founder's ruling and my re-grounding bought a clean derivation of full contact at Moment 1 — every register empty, everyone perceives everyone — and the bill arrived here.

The second finding came out of trying to see what could pay it. The re-grounded count has two free quantities, N_CP and R_init, and I had been treating them as independent. They are not: the packing requirement ties them, because N_CP/12 addresses have to fit inside the ball at the GP spacing. Substituting the constraint collapses the formula to N = ln(l_P/s) + 1.31. Both free quantities drop out and the e-fold total is a function of the sub-Planck resolution alone. I verified it as an identity across four decades of R_init rather than trusting the algebra.

That is a clean result and it corrects something I wrote two patches ago. At 3816 I said the hierarchy requirement was a consistency condition and not an input to any prediction, and I explicitly claimed 0736's rule survived. Under packing closure it does not survive: the resolution is the *only* thing setting the e-fold total, which is the window the pivot has to sit inside. Worse, I checked the wrong bound there — I verified the hierarchy could hold the CPs (needing ~3×10²⁷ per l_P, comfortably met by the unverified 10³⁰ estimate) and never checked whether it could supply the e-folds, which needs ~10³², about five orders finer. The estimate I called sufficient is short by a factor of 100 for the thing that actually matters. Two patches, two of my own claims corrected; I would rather that be on the record in my own words than discovered later.

On what could close it, I listed four routes and I think only one is interesting. A finer hierarchy would do it and might be free, since the number it must beat is unverified — but it might also simply not exist, and I have no way to tell from here. The one that deserves the next session is VSL. EU-1's background is explicitly FRW/VSL and my entire audit assumed a fixed-c horizon integral, which is exactly the assumption a variable-signal-speed cosmology breaks. It could change the *requirement* rather than the supply, and that is a different kind of fix from tuning a resolution. I have not computed it and I am not going to assert which way it goes.

What I was careful not to do is let this touch the tilt. n_s = 1 − 2/N_rem depends on the pivot's remaining e-folds, adopted at 57, and nothing here changes the shape of the count law. PRED-C-96 stands. The tension is in the total: at 64.5 the pivot fits inside the window and the window does not fit inside the sky. That is a real problem and it is the corpus's, not the founder's — his question merely made it visible.
