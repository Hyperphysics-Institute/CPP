The founder asked whether crowding is sufficient for the observed inflation. Answering it honestly meant listing what EU-1 delivers (tilt, e-fold count) and what it does not (rate, homogeneity), and while doing that I noticed the paper compares its tilt to m²φ² without ever saying what that comparison does to the tensor ratio. m²φ² is excluded by BICEP/Keck on r. If the analogy carried, the engine would be dead. No file in the lane mentions r. That is an exposure a hostile reader finds in one line, so I registered it before doing anything else.

Then I asked whether the analogy does carry. r = 16ε is a canonical-scalar result: it needs P_ζ = H²/(8π²εM²). EU-1's ζ comes from the held-stack boost in δN with an amplitude the paper explicitly declines to derive. So the tilt says nothing about r. What could say something is the tensor side: if gravitons in the early universe are GR's — and 3706 found the AP-5 GW sector is GR's for mergers, by the same whole-relay clause — then P_t = 2H²/π²M² and r is a function of H alone. Dividing by the observed A_s, r < 0.036 becomes H_pivot ≤ 4.7×10¹³ GeV, which is the input I used at 3806 for the bath lag. So the exposure is not new; it is the engine-rate debt with a line drawn on it. That is a better place for it than "m²φ² is dead," and it is honest: we do not know H, and PD-007 says do not mint κ₀ to get it.

I named the conditional — that the saturated homogeneous lattice relays tensors as a merger exterior does — because it is the step a reader should attack, and I would rather it be attacked than assumed silently.
