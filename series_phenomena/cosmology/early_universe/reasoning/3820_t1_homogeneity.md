# Reasoning capture — Patch 3820: T-1 delivered (icosahedral isotropy exact through ℓ = 5)

*Session 170, 9 Sep 2026, EU lane. Verbatim at-patch capture. Finding: `eu1_derivation/t1_homogeneity_isotropy.md`. Verify: `scripts/3820_t1_icosahedral_isotropy.py` (6/6).*

Q1 went unanswered — the founder said to proceed — so under the charter's own default no computation runs on a guessed amplitude variable and T-1 is next in the queue. The handover had predicted T-1 would be "nearly free" under twelve-per-GP, and it was, but not for the reason I expected, and the difference is the substance of this patch.

I expected to compute a small residual anisotropy and report how small. The charter's own E-2 said as much: twelve directions, therefore a quadrupole-scale residual aligned to those axes, worth quantifying because a large-angle CMB anomaly aligned to twelve axes would be a fingerprint. That expectation was wrong, and I should have seen it from the group theory before reaching for a calculation. The icosahedral group is not "a set of twelve special directions" in the way that intuition assumes; it is the most isotropic finite subgroup of SO(3), and its lowest non-trivial invariant harmonic sits at ℓ = 6. So every multipole from the dipole through ℓ = 5 vanishes identically, not approximately.

I verified it rather than citing it, in double precision and then to forty digits, because the claim is an exact-zero claim and exact-zero claims are where numerical noise masquerades as physics. The first run had the quadrupole coming out at 2.5×10⁻¹⁰, which is not zero, and I nearly wrote it up as "zero to numerical precision." It was not physics — it was a rounding step I had inserted myself to deduplicate the vertex list. Removing it dropped the quadrupole to 3×10⁻¹⁷ and the forty-digit run put ℓ = 1 through 5 at ~10⁻⁸⁰. The dipole zero (which the corpus has been asserting since 3805 and 3814 as "the momenta cancel") is now verified as an identity, and the quadrupole zero — no net shear at any GP — is new and was not previously claimed.

Two things follow that I want on the record with their honest weight.

The first is a closed door. The CMB's large-angle anomalies are at ℓ = 2 and ℓ = 3, and those are precisely the multipoles an icosahedral start cannot produce. If anyone in this programme later reaches for the twelve-vertex ignition to explain the quadrupole–octopole alignment, this result forbids it. That is worth more than a fingerprint would have been, because a fingerprint at ℓ = 6 stretched through sixty-four e-folds is not an observable anyway — the ignition's angular structure lives at grid-point scale, and 10²⁸ linear stretching carries sub-Planck structure to microscopic scales, nowhere near the CMB. I made a point of saying in the finding that no observable is claimed, because the ℓ = 6 number is exactly the kind of thing that gets quoted later as a prediction if it is not fenced when it is written.

The second is uncomfortable and belongs next to the amplitude problem. A start that is exactly isotropic through ℓ = 5 is a start with no structure in it. The T-3b HALT two patches ago found no source for ζ; this patch shows the initial state is more featureless than anyone had assumed. Those are the same fact seen twice. It does not make the amplitude gap worse in any formal sense — ζ was already zero under C-1 — but it does say that the seed will not be found by looking harder at the ignition's geometry, and the next candidate should not be sought there.
