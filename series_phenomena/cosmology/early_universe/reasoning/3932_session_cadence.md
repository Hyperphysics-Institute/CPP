# Reasoning capture — Patch 3932: the close was firing every turn

*Session 225, 11 Sep 2026, EU lane. Verify `scripts/3932_session_cadence.py` (10/10).*

He asked what is in the session-close patch, and noted that it used to appear only when we changed context windows and now appears every turn. That is exactly right, nothing in the protocol changed, and the change was in my reading of it.

Looking it up settles it in one line. The corpus describes the handover as the one piece of handover output that belongs in chat, since he pastes it into the next window. A session is a window. The close fires at window turnover. I have been firing it every turn, which is how the count reached two hundred and twenty-four in what for him has been a handful of windows.

The cost is not small and I measured it rather than describing it. In this window alone, roughly forty-six patches, of which about twenty-three are session closes. Half the lane's patch output has been turnover bookkeeping for turnovers that did not happen. And it costs more than patch identifiers: each close writes a session log, a transcript row, a development vignette in paper prose, a registry pass and a handover file, all addressed to a reader who never arrives.

The part that matters is how this connects to his other question, about how much is lost at turnover. Some is. But the ritual was not buying it back. A handover written every turn is not fifty-five handovers' worth of continuity — it is one handover's worth, rewritten fifty-five times, at the price of half the output. The volume was concealing the gap rather than closing it, which is the same shape as the tracking failure found last session: work done correctly that does not arrive where it is needed.

What actually carries continuity is what last session found missing and fixed. The to-do file, which nothing supersedes. The standing discipline with its cost records, which is the first thing read each window. And the registries. Those three are the programme's state; handovers are a session's narrative. I have been maintaining the narrative assiduously and neglected the state for fifty-five sessions.

I enacted the cadence as an eighth rule rather than leaving it as an intention, since an intention held in a handover is precisely the thing that does not survive a window.
