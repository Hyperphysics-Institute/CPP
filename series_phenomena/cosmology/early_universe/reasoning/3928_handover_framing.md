# Reasoning capture — Patch 3928: framing the two outbound questions

*Session 223, 11 Sep 2026, EU lane. Finding: `review/eu_outbound_questions.md`. Verify 10/10.*

This discharged what last session's audit found owed: three handovers had reported no in-lane item while leaving both external questions phrased in this lane's vocabulary. Framing a question for the lane that has to answer it is the sending lane's work.

I applied the new rule before drafting, and it paid on the first line. Looking up how the corpus expresses the relevant notions turned up two things I would otherwise have asked for. The particle-physics sector already owns baryogenesis through leptogenesis, with the asymmetry parameter fixed and no free parameters, so the question has to enter through that machinery rather than arriving as a fresh topic. And more usefully, the corpus already states the sea is net-neutral and colour-neutral — the phrase appears in an argument about a muon neutrino being a dipole. So half of what I was about to ask is answered on file, and asking it blind would have spent that lane's time re-deriving something they had already written.

That left a narrower and better question. Net-neutrality is asserted for charge and colour, not for baryon or lepton number. I can see why a pair of opposite-charge points would carry no baryon number — it is particle-antiparticle-like — but that is an inference made in this lane about another lane's objects, and after four definition-substitutions in this arc I am not going to make another one and call it settled. So the question I sent is whether a local enrichment in strong-dominant stacks shifts the baryon asymmetry or the lepton asymmetry, or whether the composition is simply orthogonal to that sector.

I attached the consequence rather than just the question, because a request without a stated stake reads as an errand and gets queued accordingly. If an enriched patch carries net baryon number the perturbation becomes a baryon-isocurvature mode, which survives thermalisation and is tightly bounded, and the candidate dies by about thirtyfold. If it does not, the candidate's adiabaticity holds. That is the single existential gate on the only live amplitude candidate this lane has, and the receiving lane should be able to judge the priority from the request itself.

The second item was acceptance criteria for the remaining exit, which had never had any. I wrote four positive and one negative. The positives are that the coefficient must come out rather than be fitted — with a pointer to the withdrawn circular computation so nobody reinstates it — that the count law must be reproduced, that the logarithm must stay linear in the remaining e-folds, and that the result must be consistent with the cosmological-constant lane's density rather than reproduce it.

Two of those need saying aloud. The linearity criterion matters because a proposal could fix the coefficient and break the tilt, and the tilt is the only confirmed prediction this lane has; that is not a trade to negotiate, it is one to refuse. And the fourth criterion exists because this lane already got that relationship wrong once, reading the horizon radius as the inverse expansion rate and concluding the two objects were the same equation. Writing the retraction into the acceptance test is the only way I know to stop the error being re-committed inside the test meant to catch it.

The negative criterion is the one I think will earn its place. The likeliest near-miss is a derivation that reproduces the proportionality and leaves the constant free — that would look like success and change nothing, because the form is already assigned and the coefficient is the whole of what is missing. Naming a false pass in advance is cheaper than arguing about one afterwards.
