The cancellation followed once I looked at what 3805 had actually computed. Every +stack pairs with a −stack and they co-occupy a floor cell. Two stacks of N at one point: C(N,2) like pairs in each, N² unlike pairs between them, so U = u[N(N−1) − N²] = −uN. The N² self-terms cancel the N² mutual term to the last unit and what is left is one binding per DP. I checked it by enumeration to N = 60 because a formula that clean should be checked against the thing it summarizes. The residual is the DP sea's ground state. The corpus does not count the vacuum's binding as heat anywhere, and it should not start here.

The orders of magnitude are worth recording even though they are crude, because they close a door. αN² E_Pl at N = 10⁷⁴ is 10¹⁴⁷ E_Pl; the observable universe is about 10⁶² E_Pl. Eighty-five orders. Even the released binding, 6Nα E_Pl, is 10⁷³, eleven orders over. A number that exceeds everything it could heat by that much is not a candidate budget, and I should have run it before registering the item. The swarm bound is the useful residue: whatever is held per CP is at most E_univ/12N ≈ 10⁻¹³ E_Pl, about 10⁶ GeV. That is not a derivation, it is what the universe's own energy content says any release must respect; it puts the target eleven orders below Coulomb work at the floor, which is consistent with the picture that only a fraction of a floor step of the pairing *motion* was clipped.

That is the surviving candidate, and it is where 3706 says D4-held energy can be: the flux that arrived during the ≤ 15-Moment pairing and could not be acted on.
