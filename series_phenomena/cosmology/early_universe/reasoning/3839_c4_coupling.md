# Reasoning capture — Patch 3839: C-4's coupling found

*Session 179, 9 Sep 2026, EU lane. Verbatim at-patch capture. Finding: `eu1_derivation/c4_coupling.md`. Verify 8/8.*

C-4 was left with one debt: a coupling by which the sea's orientation shifts when inflation ends. I went at it expecting to close the candidate, because the obvious check kills it. SSV_abs is defined as a sum of magnitudes, and a sum of magnitudes discards direction, so orientation should not reach the perceptual radius at all.

That reading is right at the receiver and wrong overall, and the difference is one clause in AP-4. Each arriving magnitude |E| is itself a vector sum performed at the *source*. So the receiver's magnitude-sum is blind to receiver-side geometry but inherits the source's orientational order through the length of each arriving vector. With twelve neighbours the length runs from √12 to 12 between disordered and aligned — a factor 3.46, an order-unity lever rather than a marginal one. The coupling was sitting in the ratified definitions and needed only to be read with the source and receiver clauses together.

From there the chain closes quickly. Under the branch that preserves the tilt, ε enters the e-fold count linearly, so a fluctuation in ε is a fluctuation in N, which is ζ. And ε depends on orientational correlation, which is a field obeying no conservation law — so the no-go from two sessions ago simply does not apply to it. That is worth being precise about: the no-go is not evaded by some clever argument, it is escaped by falling outside its premise, which only applies to conserved densities.

The tilt then survives without any adjustment, and this is the part I checked hardest because it is what killed Branch B1 and everything before it. The count keeps ln n̄ linear in the remaining e-folds, so the background is untouched and ε is a constant offset rather than a distortion. The spectator's fluctuation rides H, so P_ζ ∝ H² ∝ N_rem², giving exactly −2/N_rem. PRED-C-96 comes out unchanged and is now carried by the spectator instead of by the count. That is precisely what 3837 §6 said would happen if the driver and the carrier were separated, and it is satisfying to have it work rather than merely be plausible.

The amplitude relation is the piece I am most pleased with, because it runs the right way. Requiring the observed ζ fixes f/M_Pl = ε/14.9. That is not a fit — it says that once ε is derived, and ε is the PSR floor the lane already owes, the stiffness scale has no freedom left. And it lands sub-Planckian for ε below about one, which is where a lattice order parameter's stiffness ought to be. I did not choose either number and said so.

Then the objection that should kill it, which I raised against myself rather than waiting for a reviewer. A Goldstone needs a broken continuous symmetry, and the 600-cell has already broken rotations to the icosahedral group, so this is a pseudo-Goldstone and its mass comes from the residual lattice anisotropy. On any ordinary lattice that anisotropy is order unity and the mode is heavy — the register spring's death, repeated.

It does not repeat here, and the reason is a result I wrote three sessions ago and explicitly labelled as having no observable consequence. T-1 showed the icosahedral census is isotropic exactly through ℓ = 5, with the first anisotropic invariant at ℓ = 6. The symmetry-breaking term that would give this mode its mass is therefore sixth-order rather than O(1). The lattice's exceptional isotropy — the thing that made the CMB quadrupole attribution impossible — is what lets the only viable ζ candidate be light. I did not see that connection coming, and I want it on the record that T-1's grade changes: it was a symmetry curiosity and it is now load-bearing.

I was careful to keep two things straight there. The ℓ = 6 result still does not give an observable fingerprint — 3820 §4 refused that claim and the refusal stands — because this is a consequence for the mode's *mass*, not for the sky. And the suppression is qualitative until someone computes ω/H from the ℓ = 6 term. That bound is C-4's principal risk and I put it first in the owed list rather than burying it, because if it fails the candidate dies exactly as the spring did and everything else here is decoration.

So: derivation candidate, not adopted, four debts named. It is the first candidate in this arc to clear every registered wall at once, and after five negative sessions the temptation to overstate it is real, which is why the scope paragraph names the branch assumption, the normalisation assumption, and the qualitative status of the mass suppression.
