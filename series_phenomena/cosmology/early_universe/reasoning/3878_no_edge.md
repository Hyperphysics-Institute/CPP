# Reasoning capture — Patch 3878: no edge, and the error that suggested one

*Session 198, 9 Sep 2026, EU lane. Finding: `eu1_derivation/no_edge_correction.md`. Verify 10/10.*

The founder read my last patch as implying that the universe has an edge, that inflation ends when matter reaches it, and that our galaxy might one day be crushed against it. He said it was a very different concept from anything he had considered, and asked whether it was reasonable and whether the edge could be calculated.

It is not what the physics says, and the misreading is mine. At 3876 I wrote that his phrase — the entirety of the universe that will ever be populated — *is* the end condition, and let it stand without qualification. He had offered it tentatively; I adopted it as though it were exact. The phrase invites a boundary reading and the end condition has no boundary in it at all: one Conscious Point per Planck sphere is a density, intensive and local, satisfied everywhere simultaneously, and it says nothing about where anything stops.

Worse, the phrase conflates two lattice scales that differ by an enormous factor, and I should have caught it when I did the arithmetic. One point per Planck sphere is not one point per grid point. A Planck sphere contains something like four times ten to the ninety-six grid points, so at the end of inflation the occupied fraction is about one in ten to the ninety-seven. I wrote "all grid points that will ever be populated" while my own numbers said "one grid point in ten to the ninety-seven." The lattice was already almost entirely empty when inflation stopped.

And it has become emptier since by another eighty-seven orders, because inflation ending is not expansion ending. The threshold at one per Planck sphere was crossed nearly fourteen billion years ago and expansion carried on through the radiation, matter and dark-energy eras. The density falls monotonically and always has. So there is no filling up, and consequently no squashing — nothing in the picture compresses against anything, and no galaxy approaches a limit.

Having corrected that, I wanted to separate the two things that could reasonably be called an edge, because the founder's question is not empty even though its premise was. One is the boundary of the occupied region, which is his own picture from 3822 and is entirely real: the ball of matter has a surface, and the points near it have vacancy on one side. But it is a matter boundary rather than a wall, and it is comoving — it expands along with everything inside it, so nothing within ever approaches it. Under the only self-consistent reading of the expansion budget it sits at or beyond our horizon and recedes, which makes it unobservable in principle rather than merely in practice.

The other is whether the scaffold itself is finite, and here I applied D-1 and came up empty. I searched the glossary, the programme orientation and the founder's vision document for anything fixing the lattice's extent and found nothing. The corpus does not say. That is a genuine gap rather than an oversight on my part, and it is exactly the kind of thing his question is good at surfacing, so I registered it as an open item. I marked it non-blocking and said why: nothing in the inflation paper depends on it, because the end condition is a density and the occupied boundary is comoving.

I also wrote the corrected picture out positively rather than only saying what it is not, because the true version is still worth having. The lattice was always there and essentially empty; a tiny dense knot one Planck sphere across spreads out across it; inflation is that spreading; it ends when the knot has thinned to one point per Planck sphere; and the spreading continues afterwards into a scaffold that stays overwhelmingly vacant. The universe does not run out of room.

The thing I want noted for the next worker is how the error propagated. I adopted a founder's tentative phrase, praised it as reproducing the count law — which it does — and in the same breath let its looser implication ride. The praise was earned and the looseness was mine, and the two travelled together into a finding and a handover before he caught it.
