\subsection{The $H$-theorem (LEMMA-NS-HTHEOREM)}

\begin{equation}
H(t) = \sum_{\{n\}} P(\{n\},t)\,\ln\frac{P(\{n\},t)}{\pi(\{n\})} \;\ge\; 0

which is $O(1)$ in ZBW-rate units and does not vanish with system size.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Reversibility of the symmetric constant-rate ZRP with respect to $\pi$ is the standard detailed-balance
identity $\pi(\{n\})\,W(\{n\}\to\{n'\}) = \pi(\{n'\})\,W(\{n'\}\to\{n\})$, verified to machine
precision on the exact $L=3$, $N=6$ generator. Given a stationary $\pi$, the KL divergence is a

one that would give the $\ns = 1$ cliff --- is \emph{not} a stationary measure of this dynamics within
the A1 occupation-number state space. A finite, non-vanishing gap secures ``fast enough.''
\end{proof}

\section{The Long-Range Debye Residual is a Phantom (LEMMA-NS-BATH)}\label{sec:debye}
% ============================================================

After the leading mean-field cancels under neutrality, the next occupation-dependent residual is the
Debye--H\"uckel term $\mu_{\mathrm{excess}}/kT \propto -\sqrt{\nbar}$. Naively extrapolated to
$\nbar\sim 10^{74}$ this $\sqrt{\nbar}\sim 10^{37}$ would dwarf $\ln\nbar\approx 170$ by $\sim\!35$
orders and sink the prediction. It does not survive, for a structural reason.

The CPP CP--CP interaction is Coulomb-like: the SSV (Space Stress Vector) sources a static potential
$V(r)\propto q^2/r$ (the DP Sea renormalizes the coupling dielectrically but preserves the $1/r$ form;
mobile $\pm$ charges self-screen --- the Debye mechanism itself). For a Coulomb plasma the excess
chemical potential is governed by the dimensionless plasma coupling $\Gamma = q^2/(a\,kT)$ through the
identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma}
\frac{|\mu_{\mathrm{excess}}|}{kT} \;=\; c\,\Gamma^{3/2}, \qquad c = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\ \text{(DH)} .
\end{equation}
Within the weak-coupling (DH) regime $\Gamma\lesssim 1$, the right-hand side is $\lesssim 0.6$ and can
\emph{never} reach $\ln\nbar\approx 170$. The ``$\sqrt{\nbar}\sim 10^{37}$'' scare held $q^2/kT\sim
O(1)$ while extrapolating to $\nbar=10^{74}$, i.e. $\Gamma\sim 5\times 10^{24}$ --- deep strong
coupling, where DH is invalid in the first place. There is no regime in which the $\sqrt{\nbar}$ term
is both present and large.

\begin{lemma}[Bath-temperature closure]\label{lem:bath}
The chemical potential entering the tilt chain is evaluated with respect to the ZBW/substrate bath, not
the macroscopic de Sitter temperature. With $\Gamma = \alpha/\kappa$ and
$\kappa\equiv kT_{\mathrm{bath}}/\EP$, the bath clause (\S\ref{sec:leg1}) places the occupation
ensemble in equilibrium with the substrate at $kT_{\mathrm{bath}}\sim\EP$, so $\kappa\sim 1$,
$\Gamma\sim\alpha\approx 7.3\times 10^{-3}$, and $|\mu_{\mathrm{excess}}|/kT\sim c\,\alpha^{3/2}\approx
3.6\times 10^{-4}\ll\ln\nbar\approx 170$. The long-range Debye corner is closed PASS, conditional on
the bath clause.
\end{lemma}

The only way to fail is a \emph{cold, strongly coupled} ($\Gamma\sim$ tens) tilt-epoch plasma ---
precisely the opposite of the hot early universe in which the tilt is set. The residual is therefore
coupling-bounded, not a generic killer.

\subsection*{How we review}

\noindent Results in this programme are checked by an \emph{AI review panel}: several large language models from different vendors are given the same paper and the same fixed protocol, and asked to find errors and raise objections independently. Objections are recorded and either answered in the text or registered as open problems under the identifiers glossed above.

\noindent This is a \emph{verification method the authors apply to their own work}. It is not journal peer review, it is not equivalent to it, and agreement among panel members is not evidence that a result is correct --- only that no member found a specific fault. Where individual models are named in the text, they are named for reproducibility of the method; model versions change, and a reader should treat the named models as a record of what was run rather than as an endorsement.
