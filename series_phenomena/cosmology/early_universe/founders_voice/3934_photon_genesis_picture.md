## 2. On the standard story

> The question is whether high qCP regions might emit more photons than eDP photons. I think the postulate that matter and antimatter collided, annihilated, and released photons is plausible. The idea that the remaining matter excess drove the matter asymmetry is plausible, but the mechanism is not certain. *(Worker note, for the record: Standard-Model CP violation — the CKM phase, kaons included — is understood to fall short of 6×10⁻¹⁰ by roughly ten orders of magnitude, which is why beyond-SM mechanisms such as leptogenesis are invoked; leptogenesis is the machinery the Capotauro arc borrows as its empirical anchor.)*

## 3. The photon-genesis picture (the registrable mechanism)

> But, as far as the disparity in the numbers of photons emitted from baryon-rich (qCP) environments vs. lepton-rich (eCP) environments, there may be another explanation than matter-antimatter annihilation. Since the photons that were emitted in the early universe were plausibly from qDP-rich and thus were higher in qDP collisions. Galaxies (regions with higher photon emission in the CMB) congeal around DM, forming the DM ring. But the DM ring is not the only form of qDP grouping/aggregation/species. There is an entire spectrum of qDP-entity species, all of which will gravitate and form differentially at higher concentration in volumes with higher DM/qDP-entity concentration. In the early universe, many species could plausibly collide with enough energy that their collisions produce a collision-generated shockwave, which could be the source of the photons seen radiating from high-qDP-concentration seas and transmitted via the DP Sea throughout the universe. Such collisions would have a frequency distribution with a peak. The photon-producing collisions from high-qDP areas (regions where qDP-entities were highly populated) would have been more numerous due to their higher mass. The eDP entities, being in volumes depleted by the preferential aggregation of the qDPs in the inflationary environment, will be less massive and smaller, with a lower cross section and less attractive to each other, and as a result, their collisions will be less frequent and less energetic (average lower-mass species), resulting in a differential in the number of eDP-produced photons, and hence a paucity of photons arriving from the darker/baryon-poor volumes and thus a lower intensity of the cosmic microwave background from those areas.

**Registrable core (worker's reduction, checked against the text above):** early-universe photon energy is released by qDP-entity collisions (collision-generated shocks in the DP Sea, thermal distribution) and by binding; release is greater in qCP-rich volumes because the qDP-entity species are heavier, larger in cross-section, and more mutually attractive; eDP-side collisions contribute less. The last two sentences of the passage (CMB brightness read as transmitted emission from baryon-rich volumes) are recorded verbatim but **not carried into the registrable core** — see `photon_genesis_condition.md` §3 for why.
