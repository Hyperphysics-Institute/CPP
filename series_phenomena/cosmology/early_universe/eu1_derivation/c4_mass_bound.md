## §1 What sets the mass (verify T1, T2)
A pseudo-Goldstone's mass follows from the ratio of its symmetry-breaking potential to its stiffness. For a director field ordered by a coupling J acting over a range R:

> stiffness f² ~ J/R,  anisotropy Λ⁴ ~ λJ/R³,  hence **m² = 36 λ/R², i.e. m ≈ 6√λ / R.**

Two things to note before the numbers. The mass is set by the **inverse interaction range** — not by any Planck scale, not by the bath temperature, and not by f, which cancels. And the ℓ = 6 harmonic contributes its curvature 6² = 36, which is an **enhancement**: the ℓ = 6 structure suppresses the *number* of surviving invariants (T-1's result) but not the curvature of the one that survives. It would have been easy to read 3839 §5 as though "ℓ = 6" were itself the suppression. It is not; the suppression must live in λ.

Everything therefore turns on R, and the scale hierarchy makes that question decisive rather than technical: H l_P = 1.93×10⁻⁵, so the horizon is 5.2×10⁴ l_P, while the GP spacing required by the e-fold budget is 9.9×10⁻³³ l_P — **32 orders below l_P**.

## §2 The two ranges (verify T3, T5)

| range R | m/H at λ = 1 | λ required for m/H ≤ 1 |
|---|---|---|
| GP spacing, s = 9.9×10⁻³³ l_P | 3.1×10³⁷ | **1.0×10⁻⁷⁵** |

The first is unreachable by any suppression the corpus contains and would end C-4 in one line. The second is a real requirement — demanding, but of a size that mechanisms do reach.

## §4 The bound, and why it is not absurd (verify T6, T7)
> **C-4 is light iff λ ≤ 1.0×10⁻¹¹.**

For calibration: a coherent O(1) crystal anisotropy (λ ~ 0.15) gives m/H = 1.2×10⁵ — **the same wall the register spring hit at 1.3×10⁵** (3812). So C-4 is not automatically better than the candidate that died; it lives or dies on roughly eleven orders of suppression in λ.

**Why that is not hopeless.** At leading order the coupling found at 3839 is *manifestly isotropic*. SSV_abs sums the **magnitudes** of arriving bits, and the arriving magnitude |E| depends on the scalar orientational correlation C — a rotational invariant — not on the director's absolute direction. Rotating the whole sea rigidly changes nothing at that order. **So λ = 0 at leading order**, and the anisotropy is a subleading effect entering only where the *displacement* is constrained to the twelve lattice directions (A1′/AP-3: a CP displaces to a neighbouring GP, and there are only twelve). The bound asks whether that subleading effect is suppressed by eleven orders. That is a computation, and it is not made here (PD-007).

**Honest statement of where this leaves C-4:** the mass bound is **not cleared**. What has changed is that it is no longer a verdict awaiting a modelling choice but a single computable number λ, with the leading term known to vanish and the target known to be 10⁻¹¹.

## §5 An exponential tension, averted (verify T8)
Worth recording because it nearly happened and because it constrains future work. Had the range been the GP spacing, then since the e-fold budget fixes s through N = ln(l_P/s) + 1.31, the mass would scale as

> m/H ~ 6√λ · e^N / (H l_P):  N = 64.5 → 3.2×10²³;  N = 75 → 1.2×10²⁸;  N = 85 → 2.6×10³².

**More e-folds would mean exponentially heavier modes** — the e-fold budget (which wants N ≈ 75, and is still ~10.5 short) and the amplitude would have been in direct exponential conflict, and no resolution of one could have avoided worsening the other. The shell clause averts this entirely: with R = l_P the mass is independent of N, and the two problems are decoupled.
