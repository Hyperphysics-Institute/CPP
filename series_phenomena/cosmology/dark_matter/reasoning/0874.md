## Inputs (read from the corpus, not invented)

## The calculation

**Electric vdW contact depth** (deepest the eDP residual can be, at the well bottom):
V₀_elec = f_color·(3α/α_s)²·E_eDP ≈ **34–94 keV** (central ~57 keV) = **~2–5× kT_amb**. The excess eDP
rests at the well bottom (contact), so the **full** contact depth is in play — there is **no standoff
reduction** (an earlier draft wrongly applied a g(s) standoff fraction; corrected).

**The real suppression is the density contrast.** V_surf is the *net* excess over the bulk Sea. In the
bulk an eDP already sits in electric-vdW wells with neighbouring Sea eDPs (the vacuum Sea is a dense,
space-filling substrate lattice); at the spine it trades those for wells with the spine. So
**V_surf = V₀_elec · Δ**, with Δ = (spine-surface vdW − ambient-Sea vdW)/V₀_elec = the spine-vs-ambient-Sea
**density/vdW contrast** — the same ρ_Sea/ρ_spine as 0873. Because the vacuum Sea is *itself* dense
(0873: ρ_Sea/ρ_spine ~ O(1)), Δ is plausibly small — the quantitative form of "bulk-Sea texture, not
bound mass": the spine presents little density contrast to a dense Sea.

**Honest correction to 0872/0873:** the residual is induced-dipole vdW → **always attractive** → the
"promiscuous-edge orientation cancellation" (cosh, second-order) that 0873 leaned on **does not apply**.
The excess is first-order, m_coat/m_spine = G·(e^(V_surf/kT) − 1), G ~ 1.6–6. So Δ — not orientation —
must do all the suppressing, and the effective safe bar tightens to **V_surf/kT ≲ 0.3** (for
m_coat/m_spine < 1 at G~3).

## Result

**Safe iff Δ ≲ 0.1–0.15**, i.e. **ρ_spine/ρ_Sea ≲ 1.15** — the nucleated spine at most ~15% denser than
the ambient vacuum Sea. Near substrate uniformity (ρ_spine/ρ_Sea ~ 1.05) → V_surf/kT ~ 0.15 (safe);
a compressed knot (ρ_spine/ρ_Sea ~ 1.5–2.5) → V_surf/kT ~ 1.5–4.5 (dilutes).

## Verdict (Layer C, SF-grounded — MARGINAL, leaning SAFE; NOT a clean closure)

The corona reduces to **one sharp substrate number, ρ_spine/ρ_Sea ≲ 1.15.** This is **plausible** under
substrate uniformity (both the spine and the vacuum Sea are the dense 600-cell lattice substrate, so a
nucleated knot need not be much denser than its surroundings), which is why the route **leans safe** —
but it is **not established**, and a strongly compressed spine would dilute. The cross route's entire
survival now rests on this one strong-sector / eDP-Sea packing question. Reserved lemma stays
**unregistered**.
