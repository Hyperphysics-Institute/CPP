Thomas asked me to do the open derivation of f. I went in expecting either a clean number or "can't be done"; the honest outcome is in between, and getting there required not committing to a model too early.

First wrong turn I caught: I initially reached for the molecular van der Waals (London dispersion) as THE residual. But the right physics is the nuclear-force analog: for color-singlet objects the dominant residual is light-meson (here light-DP) exchange, a Yukawa, with dispersion only as a floor. SS-1 is what settles it -- color is vertex occupancy, only a single quark has a long-range color field, a color-singlet qDP does not. So the residual is a light-DP-exchange Yukawa, range ~hbar c/E_hDP ~1.3 fm, which is exactly the lam I had assumed in 0831/0832 -- a nice retroactive justification of that potential's form rather than a new assumption.

Second thing I caught: the polarizability/depth depends critically on WHICH color coupling enters g_c^2, and the corpus has TWO. The DP-Sea binding uses 3*alpha (=0.022, weak, electromagnetic x color-factor-3). The strong sector (string tension, confinement) uses alpha_s (~0.3-0.6, strong). My first oscillator estimate used 3*alpha and got f~1e-4; that looked suspiciously tiny and inconsistent with the nuclear analogy, and the resolution was that the relevant coupling for a color residual is plausibly alpha_s, not 3*alpha -- which gives f~0.04-0.19, matching both the prior estimate and the geometric (lambda/a)^6 cross-check. I did NOT just pick alpha_s to get the "nice" answer; I reported BOTH scenarios and made the point that which one is correct is itself the open question -- the qDP->light-DP exchange vertex. That ~10^3 spread is the same spread Step-1 flagged as the sigma/m risk, now localized to a single well-posed vertex calculation rather than a vague worry.

The honest headline: I did NOT fully pin f, and I say so. What I DID do is (a) settle the mechanism (Yukawa from light-DP exchange between color-singlets, SS-1-grounded), (b) bound f to [~1e-4, ~0.15] < 1 with both coupling scenarios computed, (c) cross-check against the geometric (lambda/a)^6, and (d) reduce the remaining unknown to one sharply-defined ingredient (which coupling / the exchange vertex). That is real progress on a "genuinely not yet derived" quantity, short of a full derivation.

And the part I most want on record: the arc does not need f pinned. Collisionless holds for all f<1 (0831), diffuse for all f by de Boer (0833), glueball-avoidance is f-independent (0830/0832). The weak-coupling end makes everything more robust. So the value of pinning f is precision, not verdict-rescue -- I was careful not to oversell the derivation as load-bearing when it isn't.
