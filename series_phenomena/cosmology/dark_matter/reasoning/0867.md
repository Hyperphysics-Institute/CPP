## The honesty trail (I am keeping the dead end in the record)
My first model placed the bonded-pair opposite charges ON the hinge axis, equidistant from both free tips at every bend angle. Output: cancellation fraction **exactly 0** for all screener offsets — κ_full = κ_bare = 0.125 q²/L throughout. Diagnosis: axial screeners are at distance √(L²+s²) from each tip *independent of the bend angle*, so their energy is constant in the bend coordinate and contributes zero curvature. The minimal symmetric placement accidentally decoupled the very screening that is supposed to create the near-cancellation. That is itself a real result (IF the opposite charges sit axially/symmetric, the hinge stiffness is the un-screened free-vertex repulsion — robust, restoring, no cancellation), but it misses Thomas's physics. I rebuilt v2 with the opposite charges on the **concave side** (offset y_s toward where the tips bend), so screening couples. I kept the v1→v2 trail in the script header because the decoupling is a genuine geometric fact, not just a bug.

## The decomposition and the new failure mode
v2 gives the clean decomposition **κ_full(y_s) = κ_bare − κ_screen(y_s)**:
- **κ_bare = +0.125 q²/L** — the like-charge e:e free-vertex (1-3) repulsion curvature, placement-independent, always restoring. This is the "stiffening" Thomas describes, and it is real.
- **κ_screen(y_s)** — curvature *removed* by the bend-coupled opposite charges. With equal charge magnitudes, even a modest concave offset (y_s ≈ 0.25) drives κ_screen **past** κ_bare, so **κ_full < 0: the straight chain BUCKLES** (prefers to pre-bend). The prior framing (0863, Thomas: "repulsed in both directions → genuine restoring well") assumed restoring is generic. The explicit model shows it is **conditional**: restoring holds only if the screening stays curvature-subdominant (κ_screen < κ_bare). **Buckling is a new, real way the extended-aggregate channel can die** — if the hinge buckles, there is no straight stiff ribbon, σ/m ∝ N collapses to the floppy constant, and the whole magnitude argument fails.

## "Medium" stiffness is a knife-edge next to the buckling cliff
Tuning the opposite-charge magnitude q_s at fixed concave offset shows the restoring window: κ_full/E_ee ranges ~[0, 0.72] and **shrinks toward 0 as q_s approaches the buckling edge**. So a *restoring hinge with "medium" (sub-bare) stiffness* — exactly what the DM target wants (κ_θ "medium," not covalent-deep, not free) — necessarily sits **close to the buckling instability**. The softer you want the hinge (to land ℓ_p in band rather than over-stiff), the closer you push it to κ_full → 0⁻, i.e. to buckling. This is the deep reason G1 is the make-or-break: the desired regime is intrinsically the sensitive one. "Medium" is not a comfortable middle; it is the edge of a cliff.

## Anharmonic ceiling (valid harmonic reference, axial restoring case)
For the restoring case the harmonic approximation holds to ~7% at 45° bend and breaks to ~34% by 90°, stiffening as the free vertices approach superposition — the ~90° anharmonic ceiling Thomas flagged. Consequence (mildly favorable, unchanged from 0863): tight loops are extra-suppressed, pushing the population toward larger loops the cross-section wants.

## The bridge, and the two-pronged make-or-break
κ_θ/kT_form = (κ_θ/E_ee)·(E_ee/kT_form). The model shows κ_θ/E_ee is (i) **sign-conditional** (buckling cliff) and (ii) **shrinks to 0 at the cliff** — so it is NOT bracketable to an in-band number from geometry alone, unlike G2/G3.
