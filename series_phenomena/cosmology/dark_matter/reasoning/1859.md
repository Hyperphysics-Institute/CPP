## The question

Paper §5 (`sec:xsec`, v1.0 notice): cluster collisions (~1.95 MeV) exceed the bond window ⇒ the Cross-Rod
fragments ⇒ collisionless in clusters. Thread 1855–1858: the same ½μv² at the Cross-Rod's own pins gives
~0.04–0.5 MeV < E_ee = 0.9 MeV ⇒ intact ⇒ over-cores clusters. Both cannot be right; the handover flagged the
4–15× discrepancy as unexplained ("what mass/velocity/fraction actually participates").

## The resolution — simpler and worse than the handover guessed

The discrepancy is not a subtle effective-collision-energy question. **The paper's §5 figures are the 0860 hoop
ledger's numbers, imported verbatim and never rescaled to the Cross-Rod.** The verify script reproduces the
paper's "~1.95 MeV" and "~0.78 keV" *exactly* (1.955 MeV, 0.782 keV) from ½μv² at the hoop parameters N = 1183,
m_rung = 264 MeV — a 312 GeV rod. The Cross-Rod paragraph that quotes those figures sets, in the same sentence,
N_dwarf ≈ 5–60 at m_element = 1408 MeV — a 7–85 GeV rod. Same formula, same velocities, stale (N, m). The 4–44×
"discrepancy" is exactly the rod-mass ratio. Both windows computed correctly; the paper stitched the old numbers
onto the new object.

## The honest numbers (Cross-Rod pins: m_el = 1408 MeV [0886], E_ee = 0.9 MeV [1813])

Under the paper's own criterion (total COM KE = ½·(N·m/2)·v² vs one bond):

- **Typical cluster (1500 km/s): KE = 0.044–0.53 MeV across N = 5–60 — below E_ee for the entire band.** The
  clean above-window switch §5 describes does not occur.
- **v_thr(N) = 6780 / 3915 / 1957 km/s at N = 5 / 15 / 60** (∝ 1/√N, confirming the thread's 1855–1858 estimate).
- **Maxwellian tail:** in a rich cluster (σ_1D = 1000 km/s, v_rel Maxwell with a = √2σ), the fraction of
  encounters exceeding threshold is ~0% (N=5), ~5% (N=15), ~28% (N=30), ~59% (N=60). Fragmentation is *partial
  and N-dependent* — it prunes the long end of the rod population and barely touches the short end.

## The criterion ladder (all rungs point the same way)

- **Total-COM-KE vs one bond** (the paper's criterion) is already an *upper bound* on fragmentability: a fully
  inelastic merger thermalizes ½μv² over ~2N bonds, so breaking any one bond needs KE comfortably *above* E_ee,
  not merely equal.
- **Per-contact** (μ = m_el/2): 8.8 keV at 1500 km/s; E_ee needs ~15,000 km/s (~0.05c). On this rung nothing
  astrophysical fragments anything.
- **Bond assignment:** E_ee = 0.9 MeV is the weakest link (side/coat bond). Cutting the rod axially breaks the
  E_qq-class end-bond, which 1855 classifies as deep/irreversible; if that is the operative bond, fragmentation
  is excluded outright at any rung of the ladder.

Every relaxation of the generous criterion strengthens the same verdict.

## VERDICT — outcome 2, with a partial-3 rider

**Fragmentation fails as the cluster-safety mechanism for typical clusters at the paper's own N = 5–60.** The
Maxwellian tail fragments only the long end (N ≳ 40) at tens-of-percent per-encounter rates — at most an assist
that prunes the population toward short N, not the suppression mechanism. **Cluster safety must come from the
capture falloff (1857/1858)** — the steep 1/r-residual Rutherford falloff, robust for any R_s — which was already
the thread's robust headline. The thread's capture detour was NOT a garden path driven by a wrong estimate; it
was driven by the *correct* estimate. The garden path was the paper's: §5's velocity-dependence-by-fragmentation
sentence rests on numbers belonging to a retired morphology.
