## The question

The σ/m(v) discriminant is only as strong as the **N-contrast** between dwarfs and clusters. Does the mechanism
generate enough contrast, and what pins it?

## What the calculation shows

**1. The threshold brackets the velocity range — the contrast generator is real.** v_thr(N) = 1720·√(28/N) km/s
(two-coat, ∝1/√N) sits at **~550–900 km/s** for N ~ 100–380 — **between** dwarf dispersions (~30–150 km/s) and
cluster dispersions (~1000–2000 km/s). So dwarfs are **sub-threshold** (encounters bounce, rods stay at their
formation length) and clusters are **super-threshold** (encounters catch, rods self-limit). Catch numbers over
cosmic time confirm the split cleanly: N_catch ≪ 1 in dwarfs (they never fuse), ~14–68 in clusters (they fuse to
the self-limiting stall). **This is a genuine, non-tuned success** — the penetration threshold, computed from the
two-coat physics, lands exactly where it must to make the discriminant work.

**2. The cluster branch is σ/m ∝ 1/v² — a near-parameter-free prediction.** A super-threshold rod fuses until its
rigid segment shortens to the point where v_thr(segment) = v_cluster, i.e. **N_seg(v) = 28·(1720/v)²**, so
**σ/m_cluster(v) = K·N_seg(v)·ε(v) ∝ 1/v²** (no N_form dependence). Numerically: σ/m ≈ 0.86 (1000 km/s) → 0.43
(1500) → 0.25 (2000) → **0.08 at the Bullet (3600 km/s)** — landing inside the observed cluster band and
**comfortably under the tight Bullet bound (~0.5–1).** This is a distinctive shape (not the v⁻⁴ of light-mediator
SIDM), and it is the strongest positive result of this pass: the high-velocity suppression the tightest
constraints demand comes out **for free** from the self-limiting geometry.

**3. The dwarf branch — and the honest weak point — is the normalization.** Below the knee, rods stay at N_form,
so σ/m_dwarf = K·N_form·ε_bounce. Because dwarfs *bounce* (sub-threshold), the per-encounter transfer ε_bounce
(~0.4) **suppresses** the dwarf value: with N_form ~ 250 the model gives σ/m_dwarf ~ 1.3, **below the ~2–5 that
dwarf cores want.** Reaching the observed dwarf cores needs **N_form ~ 400–1000 elements** — larger than the naive
150–380, because the bounce-suppression has to be overcome by more length.

## The verdict

- **Direction + cluster shape: SECURED.** The threshold brackets the velocity range; σ/m ∝ 1/v² at cluster scales
  is a near-parameter-free prediction that lands in-band and passes the Bullet bound. The velocity discriminant is
  real and distinctive.
- **Dwarf normalization: OPEN, and it is the make-or-break.** σ/m_dwarf = K·N_form·ε_bounce lives on two pinnable
  quantities: **N_form** (the formation size distribution, which needs the relic/epoch **kT_form**) and
  **ε_bounce** (the dwarf-regime transport fraction). Getting the dwarf cores requires N_form ~ 400–1000; whether
  PCD aggregation produces rods that long is exactly the **formation-size dynamics** — now confirmed, from the
  phenomenology side, as the single question the candidate stands or falls on.
- **Honest headwind:** the ε(v) transport opposition (bounces at dwarf, catches at cluster) compresses the
  low-velocity contrast, and the bounce-suppressed dwarf value is the specific place it bites. The candidate is
  **not** a comfortable global fit at a stroke; it is a real discriminant with a secured high-v branch and an
  open low-v normalization.

## What this pins down for the campaign

The σ/m(v) direction, the cluster shape, and the dwarf normalization **all reduce to N_form** (with ε_bounce as a
secondary transport knob). The cluster branch is already a prediction; the dwarf branch waits on formation. So the
formation-size dynamics is not just "the next make-or-break" — it is the quantity that sets whether the one open
branch (dwarf normalization) lands. That is the calculation to do next: **does PCD equilibrium aggregation, at the
relic-epoch kT_form, produce a Cross-Rod population peaked at N ~ 400–1000 elements?**
