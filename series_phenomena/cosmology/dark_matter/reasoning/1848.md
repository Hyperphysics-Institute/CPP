## The choice: B (nucleation-limited), decisively

Asked to pick the path that is the proper gradient, I choose **B — reframe the formation-size make-or-break around
nucleation density**, over A (make the DM sector ~100× colder than radiation). The physics decides it three ways,
and they agree:

**1. The over-shoot is a regime error, not a mistuned parameter.** The natural freeze-out gives ⟨N⟩ ~ 10⁴–10⁶ at
E_bond/kT ~ 50. That is exactly what the *equilibrium* size law (⟨N⟩ = √(φ·ν/H)) predicts when binding dominates
thermal energy — "essentially everything polymerizes." But real strong-binding aggregation is **not**
detachment-equilibrium-limited; once E_bond ≫ kT, monomers do not come off, so the size is set by
**nucleation-and-growth kinetics**, not by the freeze-out temperature. The 2–3 order over-shoot is the *symptom*
that the equilibrium formula is being applied outside its regime of validity. **B addresses the cause; A patches
the symptom** while keeping the wrong lens.

**2. B is the standard physics; A is forcing the wrong framework.** In dust nucleation, polymer growth, and
cosmological aggregation, when the bond dominates the thermal scale the size distribution is set by the
nucleation-site density (seeds per volume) and the monomer budget: N_form ~ n_monomer/n_seed. This is textbook.
Route A keeps the equilibrium framework — structurally the wrong one for E/kT ~ 50 — and rescues it with an
unpinned decoupling temperature.

**3. B is robust where A is fragile (the sensitivity argument).** This is the sharpest discriminator:
- **A is exponential.** ⟨N⟩ ~ exp(E_bond/2kT_form): a factor ~1.7 in kT_form spans ~3 orders in ⟨N⟩, so the
  decoupling temperature (T_DM/T_rad) would have to be pinned to ~10–15% to land N ~ 400–1000. Even if it
  "works," the dwarf branch stays a **fine-tuned** prediction.
- **B is linear.** N_form ~ 1/ε_nuc (ε_nuc = seed fraction): the band 400–1000 corresponds to ε_nuc ∈ [1.0, 2.5]
  ×10⁻³ — a factor-2.5 window in a *mild* parameter. "Approximately right" nucleation efficiency (order 10⁻³)
  lands in-band. No exponential tuning.

So the gradient points at B: it is the physically-correct regime, the standard treatment, and the non-fine-tuned
one. A is a genuine but tuning-flavored escape that leaves the equilibrium mis-framing in place.

## Honest caveat (B is the right framework, not a guaranteed rescue)

Choosing B does **not** assert the dwarf branch is saved. It asserts the make-or-break is now the **right
question**: does the CPP substrate nucleation efficiency come out ~10⁻³ (seeds-per-monomer), giving N_form ~
400–1000? That is a real substrate-cosmology calculation (the dimer/seed nucleation rate vs the growth rate at the
aggregation epoch), and it could still land outside the band. But because the size law is *linear* in ε_nuc, an
order-of-magnitude-right nucleation efficiency gives an order-of-magnitude-right N_form — so unlike A, B does not
require a coincidence, and its answer is a candidate for a genuine (non-tuned) prediction.
