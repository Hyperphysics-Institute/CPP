# Reasoning fragment — Patch 2851 (a derivation that closed everything, and was wrong)

**STATUS: verbatim, captured at patch. Session of 2026-07-27.**

## The most tempting five minutes of the week

The random-walk argument gives v/c ~ 1/sqrt(N), needs only N > 44 per
Planck sphere, and the physical Sea has that by astronomical margins.
It uses CPP's own primitive, it is two lines, and it closes the last
gap in the programme. I wrote it, computed the table, and then ran it
against the only data we have -- and the measured ratio moves the wrong
way with occupancy. Not off by a factor: off in direction.

Two readings were available and I could have taken the flattering one
(the automaton numbers are artifacts, which I myself proved at 2810 and
which L-1 forbids citing). Instead the honest reading is the one that
kills the derivation: C26 commits the Sea to dedicated bonded pairs,
and the sqrt(N) argument assumes independent contributions. The premise
is not merely unverified, it is one the framework explicitly denies. I
built an argument on the negation of a committed axiom because the
argument was short and landed where I wanted.

Recording it as a failed attempt matters more than usual here, because
the next session will face the same temptation with the same two lines.

## The structural finding

Scoping the three routes surfaced something I had not seen: two of them
run into the same FEM wall that blocks PR4-COMPLETED and the new
transverse-validation gate. Only the correlated-Sea derivation avoids
it. So the programme's remaining obstacles may be one obstacle -- the
arc-inertia specification -- appearing under three names. That is worth
the founder knowing before he decides where to spend effort, and it is
better news than it sounds: one wall is easier to plan against than
three.
