# Reasoning fragment — Patch 2864 (bias on a ringing carrier)

**STATUS: contemporaneous, captured at patch. Session of 2026-07-29.**

He slept on it and came back with the right frame, and it retires an
objection of mine rather than a claim of his. My 2863 damped-spring
analysis treated the arc as a freshly excited oscillation that has to
ring down. The substrate never sits still. The ZBW carrier is always
running, and the current does not create an oscillation, it offsets one.
Ring-down mathematics does not apply to a bias on a driven system. My
zeta-conditionality result was correct mathematics on a superseded
picture, and I want that recorded as my objection retiring rather than
as his position moving.

The coincidence in section 3 is the thing I most wanted to get on paper
before anyone else noticed it. Take "each Moment" literally and k = 1,
which puts the v/c bar at 0.15 -- exactly the number I used in error at
2855 and corrected at 2861. A corrected analysis landing back on the
value I originally had wrong is precisely the pattern that looks like a
quiet walk-back, and it would be reasonable for a reader to suspect
one. So I named it in the artifact, in the commit, and here. The 2861
correction is still right: 0.15 is the epsilon_mem threshold, k is the
converter, and k = 1 is downstream of this ruling rather than a
restoration of the mistake.

My objection this time is the microscopic-versus-collective one.
Molecules collide every picosecond and sound absorption is orders of
magnitude slower. PCD renews every CP each Moment; that does not make a
collective organization spanning a volume renew each Moment, and his own
words say volume and say arcs plural. So N, the number of Moments the
organization is held before restoration, is the successor to zeta. I
have not eliminated an unpinned quantity, I have replaced it with a
better-posed one.

The inertia inversion is the real content and I nearly under-weighted
it. If extraction and restoration balanced perfectly each Moment there
would be no inertial force at all -- a closed exchange delivers nothing
to a decelerating body. So the imbalance IS the inertia, and the lag is
not merely bounded by inertia, it is what makes inertial mass nonzero.
That converts my 2863 proposal from an empirical ceiling into a
functional dependence that inverts, which is a different evidentiary
animal: measured inertial mass as a calibration input has the standing
of m_e, not the standing of an empirical substitute for a derivation.

That is three consecutive turns in which a route to closing the
programme's last gate has appeared. I have not verified that f exists
in closed form, have not derived it, and have said so in the artifact.
I also wrote the instruction not to compute tau_Sea or N or any v/c
bound until the panel answers, because three good turns in a row is
exactly the condition under which I would produce a number and find it
satisfactory.
