## The decomposition that makes it easy
0863 gave f_glue = 1/(1+ρ), ρ ≈ ([hTetra]/[ribbon])·(σ_accrete/σ_appose), and flagged the suppression as "checkable, conditional on σ ratio ~ O(1)." Write **ρ = C·S** with **C = [hTetra]/[ribbon]** (concentration) and **S = σ_accrete/σ_appose** (geometry). The load-bearing observation: ρ carries **one full power of C**, and C is a *Sea-fraction over relic-fraction* — vast. So the suppression's robustness doesn't actually depend on S being O(1); it's carried by C.

## (A) C — the concentration hierarchy (dominant, robust)
hTetras are ~50% of the DP-Sea (the substrate vacuum); fully-formed ribbons are a dilute, transient relic species assembled *from* Sea constituents. Accretion is a ribbon+hTetra process (rate ∝ [hTetra]); apposition is a ribbon+ribbon process (rate ∝ [ribbon]). The per-ribbon rate competition is therefore R_cross/R_appose = C·S with C = [hTetra]/[ribbon] ≫ 1 by many orders. (Epoch caveat: the *exact* C depends on formation-epoch densities — an SF/cosmology input — but hTetra-as-Sea-fraction vs ribbon-as-relic makes C ≫ 1 at every epoch.)

## (B) S — the geometric σ-ratio, bracketed not assumed
- **σ_accrete**: a single hTetra captured onto the ribbon's exposed q:q **center channel** — a *local per-site* capture, area ~ π(a few ℓ_rung)² ≈ 3–28 fm², **unsuppressed** (every encounter, any of the ~N center sites).
- **σ_appose**: two **extended** ribbons collapsing lengthwise — a large geometric target (~ribbon projected area, ~10⁴ fm² for a hoop) but **alignment-suppressed** (collapse needs lengthwise registry over many rungs; effective area = projected × p_align, p_align ≪ 1) and needs a rare second ribbon.
- Computed S for a hoop-scale appose target spans ~1×10⁻⁴…3×10⁻¹. For *short-overlap* collapse (appose target ~ a few rungs rather than the whole hoop) S climbs toward O(1)–O(10). I kept the honest bracket **wide: S ∈ [10⁻⁵, 10²]** rather than picking a convenient value.

## (C) The pass condition is trivially afforded
f_glue < 10% needs ρ > 9 ⇒ **C ≥ 9/S**. Across the wide bracket, the required C ranges from 9×10⁻² (S=10²) to 9×10⁵ (S=10⁻⁵). **Even the worst bracketed S needs only C ~ 10⁶**, while the Sea-vs-relic hierarchy is many tens of orders of magnitude. So C ≥ 9/S holds across the *entire* σ-ratio bracket with enormous margin. **This upgrades 0863's conditional ("suppression holds if S~O(1)") to robust-to-S**: the suppression is over-determined by concentration, and S would have to be wrong by ~30 orders (or [hTetra]/[ribbon] catastrophically inverted) to break it.

## (D) The resulting dilution correction is small and bounded
With σ_ext = 1.0 cm²/g and the compact-glueball floor σ_glue = 0.11 (0859): at ρ ≳ 30, f_glue ≲ 3% and σ_eff ≈ 0.97; at ρ ≳ 100, σ_eff ≈ 0.99. So the surviving population's effective cross-section is the extended-aggregate value to within a few percent — the 0862 "dilution tax" is paid down to a small correction, now robustly rather than conditionally.

## (E) The cocoon-arrest radius — the hard residual (flagged, not solved)
The compact glueball endpoint's arrest size is the OTHER half of G3, and it is genuinely hard (OPEN-SS-39 / OPEN-SS-6 strong-sector kinetics). Two cases: arrest ≳ 100s fm ⇒ a **second size-setter** that must agree with the ℓ_p-driven loop size (a new consistency the population must satisfy); arrest ~ few fm ⇒ a compact σ/m ≈ 0.11 dilutant — but its **mass-fraction is the same f_glue** that (A)–(C) bound small. Either way the danger is **gated by f_glue**, which this patch makes robustly small. So the suppression result de-risks the arrest-radius unknown: it can stay open without threatening the population, as long as ρ ≳ 9 (which (A)–(C) secure). I did not attempt the arrest radius — it needs the glueball formation/growth kinetics that OPEN-SS-39 registers.
