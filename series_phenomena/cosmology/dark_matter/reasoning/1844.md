## Computation 1 — the two-coat δ* is a near-cancellation residual (my √2 estimate retracted)

Modelling each eCP coat face as an **alternating-charge square** (the Cross-Rod transverse cut: charges of
alternating sign at the corners) and bringing two faces together at separation δ:

- **The interaction is registry-dependent.** Aligned registry (like-facing) is a **repulsive barrier** (~3.1 MeV
  at ~1 fm contact); anti registry (opposite-facing) is an **attractive well** of equal depth. So a single
  registry is *not* the operative quantity.
- **Registry-averaged over continuous lateral offset**, the barrier is a **near-cancellation residual ~0.32 MeV
  (1→4 fm) ≈ 0.36 × the single-coat E_ee scale** — the alternating charges largely cancel. The operative v_thr
  shift is therefore **~√0.36 ≈ 0.6× single-coat**, i.e. **lower, not higher.**
- **And the coats can relax toward the attractive registry as they approach** (the attractive branch is a real
  ~1.3 MeV well), which would *aid* penetration further.

**So the two-coat δ* makes penetration at least as easy as — probably easier than — the single-coat model
assumed.** This **retracts the √2 "both coats double the barrier" estimate I put in 1842**: two coats do not double
the barrier; because they are alternating-charge Madelung faces, the two-coat interaction is a near-cancellation
residual ≲ the single-coat scale, sign-dependent on registry. Net for strip-then-fuse: **favorable** — v_thr is
comparable-or-lower, so catches are at least as accessible at cluster velocities as before, and the penetration
gate is not the obstacle the doubling estimate feared. (Honest caveat: Layer-C, a 4-charge-square model with an
E_ee-anchored scale; the residual's exact value needs the true coat charge geometry, but the *near-cancellation
character* and the *retraction of the doubling* are robust to the model.)

## Computation 2 — σ/m(v): two velocity effects that OPPOSE (reported straight)

Convolving the transport cross-section over halo velocity surfaces a tension I will not paper over. There are
**two velocity dependences, and they push opposite ways:**

1. **Accumulated self-limiting (falls with v).** High-velocity environments drive more catches over cosmic time →
   more fusion → shorter rods → smaller σ_geo/m = K·N. This is the strip-then-fuse velocity signature (the
   observed, data-preferred direction) and the one 1825's convolution captured.
2. **Per-encounter transport ε(v) (rises with v).** A single present-day encounter transfers *more* momentum at
   high v (more catches, ε→1) than at low v (more glancing bounces, ε→bounce-floor). This *opposes* the
   self-limiting.

In the crude model (K = 0.013, N_form ~ 200, self-limiting floor ~0.25, bounce-floor ~0.35, v_thr ~ 1200 km/s),
σ/m runs ~0.9 (dwarf) → ~0.7 (cluster): still **falling, but weakly** — the ε(v) rise flattens the low-v end that
the self-limiting alone would have lifted. **The finding: the falling direction is not guaranteed by the mechanism
alone; it holds only if the self-limiting N-contrast dominates the per-encounter ε(v) rise.** 1825's clean
3.1→0.8 fall used the self-limiting *without* the opposing ε(v); including the transport weighting shows the
velocity dependence is **softer** than that suggested.

Two things keep it viable rather than broken, and I state them as conditions, not conclusions:
- The velocity dependence needs **v_thr to sit between dwarf (~30–150 km/s) and cluster (~1000–2000 km/s)**
  velocities, so dwarfs bounce (rods stay long) and clusters catch (rods shorten). The two-coat v_thr (~0.6× the
  single-coat knee → ~600–900 km/s) still sits in that window, so the N-contrast survives.
- The **absolute** dwarf σ/m in the crude model (~0.9) is below the ~2–5 wanted for dwarf cores, but that rides on
  N_form and K (factor-few bands, and K's element crossing length is unpinned). The *structural* finding (ε
  opposes self-limiting) is robust; the *absolute* curve is not yet pinned.
