# Reasoning fragment — Patch 2871 (DM-1 sweep; the shallow-clone trap)

**STATUS: contemporaneous, captured at patch. Session of 2026-07-29.**

The contradiction I reported at 2859 does not exist. The v1.4 notice
says "Governance (revised, superseding the v1.3 sentence)" and then
closes with "the v1.3 notice below is retained per house discipline,
with its governance items superseded as stated." Section (vi) is
labelled Governance. So the supersession is explicit and the stacked
notices are house discipline working correctly. I read two notices as
concurrent when the later one says in terms that it replaces the
earlier.

Fifth time this session. I am not proposing a fourth mitigation --
three are written and none held. What actually changed the rate is the
2865 order to search before forming a position, and this patch is what
that looks like when it works: the claim got checked and died before it
reached him as an action item. That is the difference between five
errors and six.

Then the real finding, which is worth more than the retraction. v1.4
made the OSF condition "the stability-cycle clock starts at Patch 1890;
OSF on its completion." Four commits hit the paper three days later,
ending in "THE SECOND KILL, FOUNDER-ATTESTED -- ARC PIVOT". A kill and
a pivot are load-bearing by any reading, so the clock was void from 9
July, and nothing in the paper says so. The status line still reads as
though the condition were running. Twenty quiet days since then is not a
cycle either, because the current text is v1.5 and has never been
ratified -- the paper says so itself, "NOT YET RE-SHIPPED". A stability
cycle needs a ratified version to be stable; an unreviewed draft sitting
still is just an unreviewed draft.

The process finding is the one I want flagged hardest, because it nearly
went the other way. My first pass used the bootup clone at --depth 50
and it reported exactly ONE commit ever touching DM-1's tex, Patch 2804
on 26 July. That would have supported "clock intact, one recent touch"
-- the opposite conclusion, delivered with a git log behind it. It was
the shallow boundary commit, which appears to introduce every file in
the tree. The four commits that matter were invisible.

I caught it because one commit for a paper with five shipped versions
looked absurd, not because I checked the clone depth. That is luck
sitting on top of a bad default. The CLONE-FIRST GATE says clone before
registering, placing, or computing -- it says nothing about depth, and a
shallow clone does not refuse history questions, it answers them wrongly.
Every provenance claim I have made this session came off that clone.

I am not editing the paper. The only edit worth making, a note that the
clock was reset, belongs in the v1.5 re-ship notice, which is the panel
round's work product. Drive-by amendment of a shipped notice is how
stacked-notice discipline gets broken, and I have just spent this patch
retracting a claim that the discipline was already broken.
