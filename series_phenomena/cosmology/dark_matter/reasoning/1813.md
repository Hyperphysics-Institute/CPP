## (A) Virial moderation — the binding is half the Coulomb depth

The ZBW-stabilized opposite-charge bond is a **Coulomb bound state**: the kinetic energy of the ZBW oscillation
is what prevents the opposite charges from collapsing (exactly as the electron's kinetic energy holds it off the
proton in hydrogen). The **virial theorem** for a 1/r potential gives binding = ½|⟨V⟩|, so the bond realizes
only **half** the static Coulomb depth as net binding:

  **f_ZBW = ½ · s**,  s = ⟨r⟩⟨1/r⟩ = 1/√(1−(a/d)²)  (the oscillation radial spread; a/d = ZBW fractional
  amplitude).

Small amplitude ⇒ f_ZBW → **½**. This is the precise content of Thomas's "moderation": half the Coulomb depth is
the oscillation's kinetic energy, half is net binding.

## (B) Alternating-chain Madelung — the same-charge repulsion cuts the count

Along the axial line the eCPs alternate **+−+−**: the nearest axial neighbors (±1 plane) attract, but the
next-nearest (±2) are **same charge and repel**. The longitudinal binding is therefore the alternating Madelung
sum, not the naive nearest-2 count:

  **M_chain = 2·(1 − ½ + ⅓ − …) = 2 ln2 = 1.386**  (vs the naive nearest-2 = 2.000).

The same-charge ±2 repulsion **cuts the effective coordination from 2.0 to 1.386** — the second, "expansion/
repulsion" half of Thomas's moderation, and it too is principled, not fitted.
