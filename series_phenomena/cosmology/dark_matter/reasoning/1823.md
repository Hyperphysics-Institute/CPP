## (2) The per-fusion σ/m drop

DM-1: σ/m = 0.11·N (rigid rod, N elements). A fusion takes two N-rods into one X-cross (2N elements, four arms
of ~N/2 from a central glueball). The drop σ/m_X / σ/m_rod brackets by the junction stiffness:
- **Rigid X** (welded junction): ~the same interactive extent as a single rod but with 2× the mass → **drop ~
  1/2** (factor 2).
- **Flexible X** (single-point glueball → arms pivot/fold to ~half extent): **~1/4 – 1/8** (factor 4–8).

So **per-fusion drop ∈ [2, 8]**. The junction is a *single* glueball fusion point, which favors the
**flexible** end (the arms can pivot around one contact) — and the flexible end is also what the full SIDM fall
needs. Rigid (×2) alone is too weak, but it **compounds** over repeated fusions at high velocity (a rod that
fuses k times drops by ~2^k even if each junction is rigid). So the effective drop runs from ~2 (one rigid
fusion) to ~8+ (flexible or multiply-fused).

## The first σ/m(v) curve

Combining the per-fusion drop with the velocity-dependent fusion fraction from the 1822 gate:

  **σ/m(v) = σ/m_0 · [1 − p_fuse(v)·(1 − drop)]**, with **p_fuse(v) = √(1 − (v_thr/v)²)** for v > v_thr
  (the perpendicularity acceptance, isotropic impact angle, 1822), else 0; v_thr ≈ 2500 km/s (central of the
  1822 band 1700–3900); drop ∈ [1/8, 1/2].

For N = 20 (σ/m_0 = 2.2 cm²/g): flat 2.2 from dwarf through *typical* cluster (2000 < v_thr), then **Bullet
(4000) → 1.34 (rigid) / 0.70 (flex); high tail (6000) → 1.20 / 0.45.** For N = 10 (σ/m_0 = 1.1): cores at
dwarf, Bullet → 0.70 / 0.41.

**The shape is the prediction: a velocity THRESHOLD at v_thr ~ 2500 km/s — σ/m flat (= σ/m_0, cores) below,
falling above — not a power law.** A threshold is distinctive and falsifiable, and it is what the strip-then-fuse
gate produces (penetration switches on at v_thr, not gradually). This is the discriminating signature of the
corrected (uniform-core, strip-then-fuse) mechanism.

## The honest tension

With v_thr ~ 2500 km/s, **typical clusters (~2000 km/s) sit *below* threshold and are *not* suppressed** — only
Bullet-like mergers and the high-velocity tail fall. That creates a real tension: a candidate with N ~ 20
(σ/m_0 ~ 2.2) would leave typical clusters at ~2.2 cm²/g, **above the cluster bound (≲ 0.1–1).** A viable window
exists but it is **constrained**: it needs **smaller N (~10, σ/m_0 ~ 1.1)** and/or **v_thr at the low end of the
band (~1700–2000)** so that typical-cluster velocities clear the threshold, and/or **multiple-fusion
compounding** to deepen the fall. So the curve is not yet a clean success — it is a distinctive shape with a
constrained viable region.
