# Reasoning — Patch 2622 (the gate was guilty, the engine innocent, and the proof cost 68 seconds)

STATUS: verbatim, at-patch.

The scaling test is the whole argument: a defect in the η = 0 path would hold Edrift
flat under dt refinement, and instead the drift halves on schedule while Sea stays
pinned at machine zero across three decades of step size. The engine's OFF member is
exactly what the prereg claimed it was — the split algebraically removed — and the
failure belongs entirely to a threshold I borrowed from the G-SP battery without
asking whether small-oscillation-at-equilibrium licenses full-transit-through-the-
well. It does not: D3 shows the settled square drifting at 1 MeV while the transit
cell drifts at 25 — the baseline is generated where the incident dives through 60-MeV
wells at finite dt, and no registered transit cell has ever gated on FLOOR for
precisely this reason. I checked the lineage before writing that sentence rather than
after; that ordering is the 2612 lesson operating.

The corrected gate follows from the numbers, not from convenience: convergence-order
on the OFF null (the property a healthy instrument must have) plus a baseline-
exposure guard on every gated deposit (the property a fair comparison must have,
given both instruments carry the same 10–30 MeV encounter noise). The guard matters
more than it looks: S_1pass readings in the hundreds of MeV clear 3× baseline
comfortably, but the funnel-edge cells at large b may not, and I would rather declare
the exclusion rule now than improvise it when a marginal cell begs to be read.

Banking the failure class felt right: borrowed-gate transfer is going to recur in
this programme every time a battery-born threshold meets a new regime, and naming it
next to fixed-column/drift gives the N3 prereg two entries on its hazard list before
N3 exists.
