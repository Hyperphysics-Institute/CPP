## The fork

Tracing the 400–1000 target: it is **not** the retracted point-scattering result. It is the **stiff-hoop
enclosed-area inversion** (0860): σ/m ∝ N via area πR² ∝ N², giving N ∈ [355, 2366] for σ/m ∈ [0.6, 2].
Carried into the Cross-Rod era by 1818 ("long Cross-Rod") and 1846. Meanwhile 1823's σ/m = 0.11·N (geometric)
wants N ~ 15. 0860 flags that 0.11·N is an **upper bound** on the *transport* cross-section (rods pass through /
align / deposit little), so N~15 is a floor; a transport efficiency ε<1 raises it. Two live strands:
**long** (hoop/long-rod, N~500, velocity trend from fragmentation) vs **short** (rod, N~15, trend from
strip-then-fuse). The decider: does a long rod's momentum-transfer cross-section (what cores a halo) track its
geometric area (ε~1 ⇒ σ_T/m ∝ N ⇒ long over-cores ⇒ short wins), or is it suppressed enough to hide (ε ∝ 1/N)?

## The gate (pk_closure / free-streaming) is clean

Neither imposes a lower bound on N. PK-EU1 (2001) constrains only the primordial spectrum shape
(n_s, turnover, transfer) — never rod length. Free-streaming/coldness (0706) rests on the **GeV constituent
mass**, not on N (a rod is cold because its constituents are heavy, however many). So nothing outside
self-interaction requires a long rod — the retirement is not blocked by a second home for 500.

## The number (`1856_rod_transport_efficiency.py`)

Hard-capsule elastic frictionless rigid-body scattering (thin-rod inertia), orientation- and impact-averaged
momentum-transfer cross-section σ_T = ⟨(1−cosθ)⟩·area vs geometric σ_geom. **Sanity: sphere (A=0) ⇒ ε = 1.03**
(hard-sphere σ_T = σ_geom). Then:

| N (~4A) | ε = σ_T/σ_geom | σ_T/mass (arb.) |
|--------|----------------|-----------------|
| 4  | 0.20 | 1.34 |
| 16 | 0.26 | 1.48 |
| 64 | 0.35 | 3.18 |
| 256 | 0.40 | 10.4 |
| 512 | 0.35 | 16.9 |

**ε collapses to ~0.3 the moment the body is a rod, then stays FLAT** across every aspect ratio. The rescue
hypothesis (ε ∝ 1/N, long rod transport-invisible) is **false**: suppression is a constant ~3×, not
length-growing. Hence σ_T/m still **grows** with N (~N^0.7 here), and the unit-independent statement is
**a 500-element rod has ~11× the momentum-transfer cross-section per mass of a 15-element rod** (16.9 vs 1.48).

## Resolution: SHORT, and the transport-corrected normalization

A long rod that survives to scatter over-cores dwarfs by ~an order of magnitude and blows the cluster bound.
**The scattering population must be short.** Applying the flat ε ≈ 0.30 to the geometric calibration
σ/m = 0.11·N shifts the dwarf normalization from the naive ~15 up to **N ~ 30–60** (σ/m 1–2 cm²/g ÷ ε·0.11) —
still firmly short, ~3× the geometric value because momentum-transfer counts only ~⅓ of geometric collisions.

**N = 500 (the long/hoop strand) is RETIRED** — not as stale point-scattering, but because a rod that long
over-cores by ~11× on the honest transport cross-section. OPEN-SS-41/41a/42 (the ΔG*_nuc → N_form~500 barrier
program) are superseded: the dwarf normalization is set by the short transport-N, not by a formation barrier.

## Honest residuals (registered, not swept)

1. **Absolute normalization** (r in fm, per-element mass) unpinned — same input 0860 already flagged; ε and the
   N-scaling are the robust outputs, the exact cm²/g is not.
2. **Cross-section functional form:** the MC rod–rod σ scales ~N^1.6 (σ_T/m ~ N^0.7), which does **not** match the
   corpus σ/m = 0.11·N (linear, the L² perpendicular limit). Reconciling the convention is follow-on work; it does
   not change the fork (every reasonable scaling grows with N ⇒ short).
3. **Fusion channel** (inelastic strip-then-fuse) not in this elastic MC — but fusion is the *high-v* channel;
   the dwarf σ/m ~ 1–2 constraint lives at low v where elastic scattering is the right physics.
