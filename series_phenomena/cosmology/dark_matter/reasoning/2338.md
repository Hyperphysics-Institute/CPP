# 2338 — the door that was real, and what was behind it

Three things I want the record to carry from this one.

First: the door was real, and I want that stated without hedging because the
temptation after five closures in a row is to treat the sixth question as a formality.
It was not. The well supports a bound state sitting near threshold, and sweeping the
unregistered strength across its own uncertainty band swings the dwarf-velocity
cross-section by a factor of four hundred — through a Ramsauer minimum where the
candidate becomes nearly invisible to dwarfs, and up through a threshold resonance
that reaches the dSph window's interior. Every classical result in this campaign,
including my own family closure from this morning, is structurally blind to that.
If the resonance had been narrow enough to sit between 63 and 176 eV, the suite would
have opened and the texture of the 20-July decision would be different. It is not
narrow enough — s-wave threshold features have no barrier to sharpen them, and the
measured maximum of the dSph/pin ratio across five hundred and twenty evaluations is
3.40 against a bar of 4 — but the question deserved the computation it got, and the
founder's instinct to gate it open rather than let me argue it away classically was
correct.

Second: the validators earned their keep again, and this time before any physics ran.
The engine's first Born check failed, and the failure decomposed into a hard-sphere
test whose exact answer I could compute by hand; the log-derivative I was feeding the
matching formula was of the reduced wavefunction u rather than of R = u/r — a
one-line, textbook-grade bug that would have shifted every phase and survived casual
inspection, because the numbers it produced were plausible. I also corrupted the
results file mid-session with a careless numpy-bool dump and had to repair the JSON
by truncation-search; the repair recovered all 448 scan rows intact, and the
refinement was recomputed. I record both because the pattern of this whole arc —
2336's truncation, 2337's b-grid, tonight's matching bug — is the same lesson three
times: no number was trusted until something independent agreed with it, and every
number that survived did so by that route.

Third, the part that matters for the founder: the quantum picture changes what the
killed candidate looks like one more time, and in its most defensible reduction the
object now fails exactly one anchor. The soft-coat variant at the registered point
passes the pin, LSB, the group prediction, and the whole cluster ladder, and misses
only the classical dSph cores — the anchor DM-1 itself always called heterogeneous
and the shape's weakest edge. I have not moved the verdict and will not: the suite is
the suite, pre-registered, and it fails. But six flanks are now closed around a
candidate that dies by one window, and the difference between that sentence and
"reverts to a flat relic" — which is where this arc started two days ago — is the
entire yield of the founder's three instincts: record it either way, there is
something we haven't looked at, and run the measurement first. The panel gets all of
it, pointed at the joints I know are load-bearing.
