## The one number that decides the candidate

An extended scatterer has σ ~ R_g² and mass m ~ N; for a fractal aggregate N ~ (R_g/a)^d_f, so

    (σ/m)_agg / (σ/m)_monomer = (R_g/a)^(2 − d_f).

- **d_f < 2** → σ/m **grows** with size → can climb from the 0.11 floor into the 0.6–2 cm²/g band. **WORKS.**
- **d_f = 2** → σ/m flat at the floor. **Dilution threshold.**
- **d_f > 2** → σ/m **falls** below the floor. **DILUTES → candidate dies.**

So "is the fluffy ball a viable DM candidate?" reduces to one measurable number: the fractal
dimension of the formed aggregate. Caveat: σ ~ R_g² is the geometric-silhouette estimate; very
open (d_f<2) objects may be partly collision-transparent, trimming σ — but the d_f=2 threshold itself
is robust.

## What I built, and the two honest dead-ends along the way

Faithful on-cubic-lattice growth encoding Thomas's PCD rules: monomer = one hTetra ~ ℓ_rung; **ee/qq
bonds propagate linearly (coplanar)**; **qe hinge → perpendicular (90°) branch** with per-site
probability p_branch; **like-on-like (plus-plus/minus-minus) vertex contacts = repulsive excluded
volume** ("fluffy"); growth runs to N_max (depletion of the pool share for one aggregate).

**Dead-end 1 (kept in the record): broken d_f estimator.** My first estimator read d_f from the
running R_g(N) log-log slope as the aggregate grew. It returned d_f ≈ 4–5 — **impossible** (a spatial
object in 3D cannot exceed d_f = 3). The running-R_g slope conflates the early growth transient with
late-time R_g saturation and is not a fractal dimension. Replaced with the **static mass–radius
estimator** N(<r) ∝ r^d_f on the final object, **validated on known shapes**: line → 0.99/1.00,
disk → 1.99/2.05, solid cube → 2.77/2.86 (the ~0.2 high-end underestimate is finite-size/window; it
only makes a >2 reading *more* firmly >2). The decision line d_f = 2 is safely resolved by this tool.

**Dead-end 2 (kept in the record): in-house cluster-cluster toy did not converge.** I attempted a
lattice cluster-cluster aggregation (hierarchical pairwise merges) to measure the working-regime d_f
directly. Three variants (footprint-overlap-forced contact; randomly-oriented seed chains; wide
transverse-offset edge-sticking) all failed to give a clean, physical d_f on accessible cluster
sizes: results scattered across d_f ∈ [2.0, 3.0] with ±0.3, the merge rule over-densified
(too head-on → compact), and degenerate cases produced unphysical d_f<1 (colinear seeds → filaments)
or d_f>3 (fallback stacking). **I did not ship a number from this toy.** The working-regime value is
therefore **cited from established aggregation physics, not asserted in-house** (see below). This is
the same discipline as 0867 v1 (axial-screener decoupling kept as a documented wrong turn).

## The robust in-house result (validated)

**Monomer-fed branched growth — the picture as described (hTetras adding one at a time to a growing
ball) — gives d_f ≈ 2.4–2.7 for every branch probability 0.2–0.65.** Interior like-on-like screening
(λ, biasing growth toward surface tips) does **not** lower it (probe: λ=2 → 2.46, λ=5 → 2.21; if
anything noisier, not helpfully lower). This sits in the Eden→DLA band (known: Eden/ballistic ≈ 3,
DLA ≈ 2.5), consistent and expected. **Conclusion: the monomer-fed fluffy ball is ABOVE the dilution
threshold → it DILUTES.** Branching probability is *not* the rescue knob, and "fluffiness" as a local
screening parameter does not cross the line.

## What reaching d_f < 2 actually requires (cited)

Standard 3D cluster-aggregation d_f ladder: Eden/ballistic ≈ 3.0 (dilutes), DLA monomer→cluster ≈ 2.5
(dilutes), RLCA ≈ 2.05 (~threshold), **DLCA (diffusion-limited cluster-cluster) ≈ 1.8 (works)**. Only
DLCA — **pre-formed chains/loops/sub-balls random-walking and merging** — crosses below 2. Monomer
feeding (DLA/Eden territory) cannot. At d_f ≈ 1.8 the σ/m band needs R_g/a in the hundreds-to-
thousands (reachable via depletion-set sizes); at the monomer-fed d_f ≈ 2.5 **no** size reaches the
band.

## The sharpened (partly sobering) verdict

The fluffy ball works **only if d_f < 2**, and **d_f is set by the growth CHANNEL, not by branch
probability or screening**:

- **monomer-fed** (the described picture): d_f ≈ 2.4–2.7 → **DILUTES** [in-house, validated];
- **diffusion-limited cluster-cluster of extended sub-units**: d_f ≈ 1.8 → **WORKS** [cited].

So the ball is **not an independent morphology** — it is the **chain/loop population coalesced**, and
it works **only because those sub-units are extended/anisotropic**. Its viability is therefore
**coupled to the same chain physics** (including the G1 hinge of 0867): you cannot bypass the chain
channel by appealing to balls. The deciding question is reframed from Thomas's "how branched?" to
**"does the ball assemble monomer-by-monomer (dilutes) or by chain-cluster-cluster coalescence
(works)?"** — a substrate-kinetics/SF question, now sharply posed.
