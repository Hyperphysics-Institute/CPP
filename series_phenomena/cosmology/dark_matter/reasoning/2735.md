Two nights, two of my own explanations executed by their own
preregistrations. Yesterday the error bars were supposed to be loose;
they were tight. Tonight the windows were supposed to be mismatched;
matching them doubled the significance. I notice the temptation to
feel embarrassed and I decline it: a programme whose convenient
hypotheses die this fast is measuring something, and the shape of
what it is measuring sharpened considerably. HNC hands back an almost
perfect Yukawa; the simulation hands back a profile whose apparent
decay constant climbs the further out you look. That is not a
disagreement about a number; it is a disagreement about a function,
concentrated at small k, at one soft-core value, in defiance of the
association ordering that any textbook mechanism would predict.

The discipline note I want on record: the classification did NOT
update tonight, though I could feel the pull to write "probably HNC
closure" and move on — the ordering paradox forbids it, and the
frozen rule only permitted one specific update on one specific
outcome that did not occur. What the arc has instead is a clean
experimental question with a chartered instrument pointed at it:
X5 measures the susceptibility with an actual external field, no
equilibrium assumption, no fluctuation relation — the same probe for
both instruments' claims. If the driven response sides with the
fluctuations (kappa high), HNC is missing physics and the O(Gamma)
story gets a second order. If it sides with HNC, the fluctuation
spectrum of the 0.04 chains carries a systematic we have not named,
and PR3 will have caught it doing exactly the job GPT designed it
for. Either answer is progress; only an unfrozen analysis could
waste it.
