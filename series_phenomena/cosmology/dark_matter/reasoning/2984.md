# Reasoning fragment — Patch 2984 (campaign runner notebook; the GPU ruling)

The founder offered a GPU. The ruling under PD-006 is no for THIS measurement, and the reason is worth registering because it will recur: the frozen prereg binds the run to "the committed 2902 engine, UNMODIFIED," and a GPU port is a re-implementation of the physics, not a driver-path fix. The usual rescue — "port it and verify equivalence" — is unavailable here specifically because of our own registered 2908 finding: the Sea is deterministically chaotic, so the GPU's differently-rounded floating point diverges trajectories exponentially, and no after-the-fact comparison can distinguish "same physics, different rounding" from "different physics." Equivalence for a chaotic engine has to be established statistically (matched ensembles, distributional tests), which is itself a validation campaign — legitimate FUTURE work that would give later preregs a fast instrument, but not something to bolt onto a frozen measurement to save two days. The freeze exists precisely so that convenience cannot renegotiate the instrument mid-measurement.

What the founder's request CAN have immediately is the notebook: a launcher/monitor wrapper that adds zero evidentiary surface. Design choices to that end: the notebook shells out to the committed driver as a subprocess (never imports and re-executes the physics — also the robust pattern for Windows multiprocessing under Anaconda), the smoke cell asserts the paired-sea bit-identity before any compute is spent, the progress cell reads only filenames and wall-times (no F arrays — nothing statistical crosses the no-interim-looks line), and the git cell prints rather than executes by default. Per the repo convention, .ipynb lives in scripts/.
