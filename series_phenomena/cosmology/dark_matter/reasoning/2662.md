## The substantive reasoning, preserved

**On the discrimination itself.** The founder's first statement was that
SSV(Net and Abs) is modeled as the sum of the spherical totality,
corresponding to the lattice cell. My initial sharpening: FA-C2's ℓ is the
decay constant of a *static* pattern (U(r) is the static pair interaction),
and static screening in a gapped medium is set by the medium's response
granularity, not by transit loss. The per-edge candidate is a
propagation-loss picture — loss per DI-bit hop along an edge — and transit
loss governs retardation and dynamic attenuation, which is not the
observable FA-C2 parameterizes. So the edge scale is the natural answer to
a question FA-C2 is not asking.

**The founder's elaboration upgraded this from a plausibility claim to a
phase-localization claim.** His account places the edge quantity in exactly
one phase of the PCD cycle — Displace, as the executed output's de facto
magnitude and direction — and places the summation (all SSV_abs and SSV_net
from local and distant GPs, spherical integration, holographic overlap of
shells) in Perceive + Compute. The two FA-C3 candidates are therefore
readings of *different phases*: per-edge attributes the decay constant to
the output step, per-cell to the response step. Screening is a response
property. That localization is the decisive form of the argument, and it is
the founder's — my contribution was naming it and testing it against the
2659 registration.

**The isotropy-inheritance observation (supporting only).** f(r) isotropy
in FA-C2 is not free; it is produced by the overlapping-shell summation
that yields the continuum 1/r² behavior. The screening length parameterizes
that isotropized response, so consistency suggests it inherits the
summation's granularity. I flagged this supporting-not-decisive because a
hop picture can be isotropized on average — the mean-hop decay constant of
an edge-loss process need not imprint anisotropy on the far field. I
declined to overreach here.
