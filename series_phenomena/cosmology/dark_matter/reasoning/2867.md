# Reasoning fragment — Patch 2867 (transit asymmetry; the runaway collision)

**STATUS: contemporaneous, captured at patch. Session of 2026-07-29.**

He answered the crux: lopsided, internal. That closes 2866's open
question at reading (ii) and the 2496 conflict dissolves rather than
resolving in anyone's favour -- two accountings of one object. The
F_hold test I specified last turn is mooted, which is the correct
outcome for a test whose interpretation depended on the crux.

His detailed mechanism is better than I expected. He derives the fore/
aft amplitude asymmetry from transit rather than assuming it: the
forward arc is partway through charging, the rear arc is fully charged
and decaying, so at equal distances the rear is larger. That is the
standard wake geometry and he got there from the substrate rules.

Two things stop me short. The sign is the first. Induced polarization
is attracted to the inducing charge, so a cloud stronger behind pulls
backward -- that is why polarization drag is drag, and it is the
opposite sign to what he needs. His flip comes from the discharge step
pushing the receding CP forward. I do not know whether that is right. It
is the single load-bearing move in the whole mechanism and I cannot
settle it from the corpus, so I have put it back rather than picking
the sign that makes the rest work.

The second is that his account is a fixed point with a stability
condition, A(v) = v/mu exactly, and nothing in the mechanism forces the
equality. Above it the CP accelerates itself, below it momentum decays.
An explanation of Newton I that works only on a knife-edge is not yet
an explanation of Newton I.

And then the thing I found by searching first, which I am now doing by
default and which keeps earning its keep. 2496's registered debt (a) is
"the undressed massless point surfs its own retarded wake", observed
empirically at high mobility, filed as the Abraham-Lorentz analog. That
IS his mechanism, seen in the development runs, in the high-mobility
regime where his persistent cloud is meant to live -- and recorded as
the runaway branch, not as the mechanism of coasting.

That is a location, not a refutation, and the location is interesting.
2496's own reading is that a bare CP is not a stable asymptotic object
and that physical particles are dressed standing patterns. So the
mechanism would be stabilised onto the marginal line by closure, not by
tuning. And 2496 already has the anchor: for the closed self-bound SF-1
cage, Laue forces coefficient exactly 1. Marginality that looks
fine-tuned for a bare point is forced for a closed system.

If that holds, k = 1 is the stability condition of a closed pattern
rather than a coincidence, and eps_mem, the SF-6 inertia coefficient
and the SF-1 cage are one object. This is the third independent route
to k = 1 in four turns and it is the most attractive thing anyone has
said in this session. Two links are unproven: the sign, and whether a
Laue-forced coefficient for a closed cage transfers to eps_mem for the
ambient Sea. I have derived neither. The pattern of this session is that
attractive convergences arrive faster than I can check them, and the
correct response to a fifth one is the same as to the first.

One genuinely good sign: the next action -- the SF-1 cage simulation --
is already scheduled work from the inertia pin. First time this session
that something on the critical path was not new work.
