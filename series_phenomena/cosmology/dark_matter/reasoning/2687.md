I committed the statistics before running the comparison because the
[ADJ] demanded it, and I am glad it did, because both headline statistics
FAILED and the temptation to re-derive a friendlier tolerance after the
fact would otherwise have been live. S1 failed on exactly one arena — the
diameter-matched 19-site ball came out entirely positive. My prediction
("any compact arena of I1's proportions staggers") conflated two
different compactness parameters: diameter and site count. In 3D,
matching I1's 1.18 fm diameter buys you 19 sites; I1 has 120 because it
lives on a 3-sphere. The S3 diagnostic settled what the 19-site field is:
median 2.3% deviation from the full extended field on the same sites —
it is just the local positive patch near the source, below the
multiple-scattering depth that generates staggering. So the miss teaches
something real: staggering is a feedback phenomenon with a site-count
threshold, not a pointwise property of the operator. The count-matched
arena, which matches the parameter that actually controls the feedback,
reproduces I1's class exactly.

S2 failed more bluntly: worst chord residual 4.6 log units against a
committed 3.3 ceiling, and the geodesic comparison worse — partly my
commitment's own defect (a 3D chord-space decay constant tested against a
path metric stretches the prediction), partly genuine 4D-vs-3D shell
structure the self-calibrated tolerance could not absorb. I wrote both
failure modes down and did not soften the verdict: the committed test
says NOT-CONSISTENT, R1-PASS is off the table, and whether the
operationalization itself was adequate is the panel's vote, disclosed
with the results as the charter requires.

The result that survives cleanly is qualitative: I1 behaves exactly as
the extended solution predicts a 120-site compact arena should — the
original READOUT-INCONCLUSIVE was the physics, not an instrument fluke.
