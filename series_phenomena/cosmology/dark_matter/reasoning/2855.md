# Reasoning fragment — Patch 2855 (Route 2 step 2; L_field)

**STATUS: contemporaneous, captured at patch. Session of 2026-07-28.**

The charter said compute L_field. I went to fetch the Sea's committed
density first and the answer fell out before any simulation: d_DP =
0.3640 fm, mean inter-pair spacing 0.3243 fm, screening length 0.1820
fm. The bond is longer than the spacing and twice the screening length.
Route 2 needs d_DP ≪ L_field and the committed numbers say d_DP is the
*largest* of the three lengths.

What I want to record is where the error actually lived, because it is
a language error and those recur. The handover says "the bonded dilute
Sea." C26 says "dedicated, semi-persistent DP bonds… NOT a plasma gas."
C26's "dilute" is a statement about **bonding topology** — bonds are
dedicated rather than promiscuous — and it says nothing about
geometry. Route 2's suppression needs **geometric** diluteness. The
same word carried both readings across three patches and nobody, me
included, checked it against κ·d_DP = 2.0, which is a number the
programme has carried since the α1 arc and which states the conflation
directly: the bond length is two screening lengths. It was sitting in
the record the whole time.

This is the same shape as the 2851 √N failure — that one built on the
negation of a committed axiom, this one on a reading of a committed
axiom the axiom does not support — but with the sign reversed, since
this reading was not flattering, it was merely unexamined. Worth
noting that the failure mode has a version that does not require
motivated reasoning at all. Inherited vocabulary is enough.

On method: L_field turned out to be regulator-sensitive, moving 2.6×
across the committed soft-core axis while R_drift moved 0.9%. That is
a real result about the charter's own question — the E²-weighted
correlation length is dominated by close encounters and is therefore
not well-conditioned in a Sea this dense. I could have quoted a_soft =
0.02, gotten d_DP/L = 6.1, and declared the route dead in one line.
I did not, because a number that moves with a regulator cannot carry a
verdict, and because a cheap negative is still a worker deciding the
outcome. The quantity the proxy stood for is computable directly and
is regulator-independent, so I computed that instead.

The thing that makes me confident is that R_drift landed on 1/√2 to
within 0.5%. That is not a fitted number. It is what the ratio equals
when E(+) and E(−) are statistically independent, derivable in two
lines with no simulation, and the simulation reproducing it means the
two CPs of a bonded pair genuinely see uncorrelated environments. The
polarity cancellation from 2853 is real physics and it simply has
nothing to act on at this density.

I built §5 to try to rescue the route — structural ordering could
correlate the two CPs — and it closed on Γ = 0.2251, weakly coupled,
O(Γ) corrections of 22% against a factor 4.7 needed. S4-N's κ_fit =
1.017 κ_D says the same thing from the simulation side. So the escape
hatch is shut on committed numbers rather than on my say-so.

Consequence I am not going to soften: 2851 SS4 predicted that if route
2 failed, the honest conclusion is that PR7 and PR4 are one obstacle
wearing two names. It failed. With OPEN-C23-TRANSVERSE-VALIDATION that
is three names, and the obstacle is the C23/C24 arc-inertia
specification the founder assesses as FEM-class. Six of seven is where
the ledger stays, and the remaining programme work is now a single
identified object rather than three open items.
