## The question

1825 left the cluster floor on a knife-edge: single-fusion (~0.8, viable) vs **runaway over-depletion** (a
marginal-fusion fixed point N_coll(σ/m)·p_pen ~ 1 sits near σ/m ~ 0.07, far under the bound). With N_coll·p_pen
~ 10–40 at clusters, multiple fusions are available — so what stops the runaway?

## The answer — the self-limiting is built into the penetration physics

Fusion happens at **crossing points**, so each fusion **shortens the rigid segment** (the arms between glueball
junctions are shorter than the reactant rods). And the penetration threshold **v_thr(N) = 2√(E_ee/(N·m_el)) ∝
1/√N** — **shorter segments have higher v_thr.** So each fusion (~halving the segment) raises v_thr by ~√2.
After **k ~ 1–2 fusions, v_thr exceeds the local relative velocity and fusion stalls.** The *same* v_thr that
*gates* fusion (1822) also *self-limits* it — no separate mechanism is needed, and the limiter is automatic.

Concretely, from N₀ = 28 (v_thr = 2865 km/s): k=1 → segment 14, v_thr 4051; k=2 → segment 7, v_thr 5730; k=3 →
segment 3.5, v_thr 8103. The threshold runs away from the available velocity faster than fusion can proceed.

## The result — a bounded, velocity-dependent floor

k_max(v_rel) = 2 log₂(v_rel/v_thr(N₀)); σ/m_floor = σ/m₀·drop^k_max (drop ~ 0.25):

| env | v_rel (tail) | k_max | σ/m floor |
|---|---|---|---|
| dwarf / galaxy / group | <  v_thr(N₀) | 0 | **3.1** (cores) |
| cluster | ~3600 | ~1 | **~0.8** |
| Bullet / mergers | ~5800 | ~2 | **~0.2** |
| high merger | ~8000 | ~3 | ~0.05 |

**The floor is bounded and velocity-dependent**, and it sits **far above the no-self-limit runaway point
(~0.07).** Segment-shortening drives v_thr past v_rel after k ~ 1–2 fusions, stalling well before
over-depletion. So **the 1825 cluster floor (~0.8) is protected, not a runaway** — and it is consistent with
1825 at k ~ 1 (the cumulative f_fused → 1 fuses each rod about once, then segment-shortening stalls further
fusion). The secondary backstop (a floppy d_f ~ 2 aggregate has coil σ/m → const, not → 0) holds too, but the
segment-shortening self-limits **first**.

## Honest caveats

- **The cluster floor (~0.8, k ~ 1) sits in the upper part of the cluster band** (≲0.1–1) — the tightest
  constraint. It improves (lower) for smaller N₀, smaller per-fusion drop, or a fatter velocity tail; it
  worsens for the rigid X-junction (drop ~ ½). So the cluster end is where the candidate is most exposed.
- The **segment ~ N₀/2^k halving** is a model approximation: real crossing-point fusion gives a *distribution*
  of arm lengths and a *branched* aggregate, not a clean chain of halved segments. The **self-limiting**
  (v_thr rises monotonically as segments shorten) is robust to this; the **exact k_max and floor** are not.
- The floor uses the rod-length-limited backing v_thr; a stress-wave-dominated backing for large aggregates
  would shift k_max. The qualitative cap (k ~ 1–2) is robust.
