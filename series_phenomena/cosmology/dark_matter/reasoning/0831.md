Thomas asked for the no-near-threshold-resonance calculation -- the kill 0830 flagged and Step-1 named (open #2). The object is the residual color potential between two NEUTRAL qDPs, modeled as the nuclear-force analog: hard core r_c (eDP excluded-volume coat) + attractive color Yukawa (range lam, depth V0). The deliverable is the s-wave scattering length a(V0) and whether a near-threshold bound state (a -> inf) lives anywhere the residual depth can physically reach.

The result that organizes it: an ORDERING. residual depth (<= E_qDP=264) < sigma/m=SIDM crossing (~300 MeV) < resonance pole (~500 MeV). The load-bearing physical claim is that the residual color attraction between two neutral qDPs is the van-der-Waals RESIDUE of each qDP's own internal color binding E_qDP=264 MeV, hence weaker than it (charges screened within each qDP; cf. nuclear force ~50-100 MeV vs GeV internal binding). So V0_residual <= 264, and across that whole range sigma/m <= 0.504 -- below SIDM. The kill needs V0 > ~300 > E_qDP, i.e. a residue deeper than its source: structurally impossible. So 'no near-threshold resonance' is structural, not fine-tuning, and robust even to the unphysical worst case f=1 (residual = full internal binding), which still gives sigma/m=0.50. That is a stronger and cleaner conclusion than I expected going in -- I had been prepared to argue a fine-tuning-fraction (thin dangerous bands near resonances), but the residual-force hierarchy makes the resonance simply out of reach rather than merely improbable.

Two numerics mishaps, both caught and fixed -- recording them because the corrections changed the picture:
1. First resonance-finder located the wrong feature. It looked for 1/a crossing zero, but at a near-threshold resonance a -> inf (1/a -> 0 smoothly), whereas the crossing it caught was a passing through ZERO (Ramsauer point, where 1/a jumps discontinuously through +-inf). It mislabeled the a=0 Ramsauer point (~150 MeV) as the bound-state threshold. Fixed by detecting the actual pole (a jumps large-negative -> large-positive) and, more simply, by reading the sigma/m=SIDM crossing directly.
2. A Numerov rewrite (attempted for speed after a timeout) gave a INCREASING with attraction -- unphysical; attraction must pull a down from the hard-sphere value r_c. It failed the sanity check. Abandoned it for solve_ivp, which passes a(V0=0)=r_c exactly and gives the physical trend (a decreases, crosses zero ~150 MeV, goes negative toward the pole). All quoted numbers are solve_ivp.

The honest caveats are in the doc: the residual depth fraction f is the real input (bounded f<=1, realistic f~0.1 from analogy, not yet derived); the crossing/pole move with r_c, lam, m_qDP (representative hDP-scale values used; heavier qDP widens the margin); two-body s-wave only (identical-qDP statistics and higher partial waves are refinements, but s-wave dominates cold DM). The conclusion is robust to f because even f=1 is safe.
