## The question, made answerable

Does the spine boundary host a **near-zero-energy, charge-neutral collective surface mode**? Such a mode would
evade the V₀²/E_eDP perturbative bound regardless of well depth. It requires one of: (A) a mass-sign domain
wall, (B) chiral/topological protection, or (C) accidental fine-tuning. Test each (code/0884).

## Effective model

The lowest charge-neutral matter excitation is the eDP — a +eCP/−eCP **dipole**, net neutral — gapped at
Δ = E_eDP = 88 MeV. The panel established this sector is **bosonic**. Massive bosonic field, half-space x>0,
Robin boundary ψ′(0)=κψ(0); surface mode ψ ~ e^(−qx) has ω_surf² = Δ² − (ℏcq)², in-gap for 0<ℏc|κ|<Δ;
ω_surf→0 needs deep binding ℏc|κ|→Δ.

## The three closures

- **(A) Domain wall — EXCLUDED.** The eDP mass is positive everywhere; the Cross-Rod is a bound aggregate in
  the **same vacuum**, not a distinct phase. No sign change ⇒ no Jackiw-Rebbi zero mode.
- **(B) Topological protection — EXCLUDED (generic).** A gapped **bosonic** mode has no chiral symmetry and no
  protected boundary zero mode (a protected one needs a nontrivial bosonic-SPT structure the neutral sector
  lacks). The only protected/gapless structure is the **charge-sourced photon**, which the neutral surface
  decouples from at k→0. Protection is in the charge channel, not the neutral one.
- **(C) Fine-tuning — EXCLUDED (non-generic).** The decisive quantitative point, closing via the *same*
  sub-threshold logic at the collective level: a weak attractive surface (V₀ ≪ Δ) binds only **shallowly**
  below the gap top, depth ~V₀²/Δ ≈ 36 eV ⇒ ω_surf ≈ Δ ≈ 88 MeV, nowhere near zero. A near-zero mode needs
  deep binding ~Δ (~1557× stronger boundary) or tuning to within V₀/Δ ~ 6×10⁻⁴ of the critical strength — a
  ~1-in-1557 unforced fine-tuning.

## Result

Lowest charge-neutral surface mode ≈ E_eDP = 88 MeV — a **clean gap ≫ V₀ ~ 50 keV**. The RESTATE's kill route
is closed: a near-zero mode cannot be *produced* by a weak boundary and is not *protected* into existence. The
elegant point: the same "a weak well can't bind a deep state" logic that handled the single particle handles
the collective surface mode — which is exactly the level ChatGPT worried it might not reach.
