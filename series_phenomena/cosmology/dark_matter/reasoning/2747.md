# reasoning/2747.md — X5-FE and the scale surprise (verbatim, at-patch)

STATUS: verbatim, captured at Patch 2747, 21 July 2026.

I wrote the B-sampler expecting it to convict the A-path, and instead
it acquitted it — at sixty-four particles, everything agrees with
everything, including the exact tilt. So the phantom is not a wrong
formula; it is something that GROWS. The un-tilt check makes the
large-N fact undeniable in one line: run the reweighting backward
from the driven chain and it lands at minus one point seven where
zero must sit. A chain whose stationary law is provably not the
Boltzmann law of the Hamiltonian in its own acceptance test, built
from lines that are provably correct at small N, is either a
numerics-at-scale story or a sampler-physics-at-scale story, and I
genuinely do not know which — which is the first time this campaign
has left me without a favored hypothesis to shoot. Candidate (b)
deserves respect: a symmetry-breaking field plus local moves plus a
growing system is textbook territory for equilibration pathologies,
and if that is what this is, the "bug hunt" has been a measurement
of sampler physics all along.

The prefactor catch goes in the record with some relief attached: an
independent implementation that silently sampled half the k-space
energy would have produced a spurious disagreement, F1 would have
fired, and I would have spent a session hunting a defect in correct
code. Inspection before results is cheap; its absence is not.
