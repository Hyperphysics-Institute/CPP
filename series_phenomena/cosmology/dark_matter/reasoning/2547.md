# Reasoning — Patch 2547 (founder bond specification captured; blocker dissolves; ENDBOND-1 closed, ENDBOND-2 named)

STATUS: verbatim, at-patch.

The founder's (c) stopped me short in the best way. I had framed the blocker as "the corpus
never registered a static strong pairwise form," and his answer was that the corpus never
registered one because the category is physically empty — nothing in the rod is static. My
first instinct was to defend the statics framing (the 2544 prereg's own language); the
grep killed that instinct in one pass: the panel already adjudicated this exact question a
week ago, in his favor, in FORK-SWITCH-1. The δ-statics is superseded-by-reading and the
dance is the working functional. The 2544 prereg — written under time pressure at the end
of a long session — inherited the superseded instrument, and 2546 executed it faithfully
and correctly reported that it cannot produce the bond. The system worked: the prereg's
Branch-I limb caught the gap, the founder's physical answer identified WHY the gap exists,
and the corpus check confirmed his answer was already registered doctrine.

The delicate governance point was whether to treat his (a)/(b) as a NEW registration
(reopening ENDBOND-1 under its zero-decision drop-in contract) or as a pointer to EXISTING
registered content requiring a successor campaign. I chose the successor. The drop-in
contract was written for a static pairwise form that adds linearly to the banked electric
partial; what actually exists is a different instrument entirely (trajectory-averaged,
non-additive with the statics partial, different convention axes). Forcing it through the
ENDBOND-1 apparatus would have meant quietly swapping the closed input list after the fact
— the precise move the discipline forbids. A fresh prereg costs one patch and keeps the
record honest.

The G3 demotion had to happen in THIS patch, before the successor prereg exists and before
any number is computed. The ≈85 MeV back-implication came from the same dance functional
the successor will run; calling agreement with it a "gate pass" would be self-confirmation
dressed as validation. Declaring the demotion now, in the capture patch, means no one can
later suspect it was demoted because the result missed it — or kept because the result hit
it.
