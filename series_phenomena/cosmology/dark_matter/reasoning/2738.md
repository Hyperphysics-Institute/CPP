The arbiter did not pick a side; it indicted the court. The driven
probe was supposed to tell us whether the simulation's hard screening
or HNC's soft screening was right, and instead it disagreed with the
simulation's own fluctuation spectrum — which, inside an exact Gibbs
sampler, is not a physics result but an impossibility theorem with a
loophole. I spent the rest of the session walking the loophole list
with cheap decisive runs: the accumulator is exact to 1e-12; the
suppression survives fresh summation in a new box; the harness passes
the one case where FDT is analytic. What survives is time: every
chain we have ever run may simply be SHORT compared to a slow channel
in the charge dynamics, and a variance starves before a mean does.

If X3-LONG confirms it, the aesthetics are almost suspicious: one
mechanism would close the original 10% excess, the X6 shape
disagreement, the contact-ordering paradox, and tonight's FDT
violation simultaneously, and would do it by vindicating GPT's most
pedantic-looking demand — effective sample size for the lowest-k
observable — as the load-bearing criterion of the whole campaign. I
am suspicious of explanations that tidy; that is what the frozen
signature test is for. And if the suppression is instead FLAT in
chain length, then something in the sampled ensemble genuinely
violates the fluctuation relation, and the Moment-rule automaton
stops being a promotion formality and becomes the most interesting
instrument in the programme. Either branch is worth the night it
took.
