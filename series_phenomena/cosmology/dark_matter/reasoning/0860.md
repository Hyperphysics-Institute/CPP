## Why the reverse-calculation is NOT merely curve-fitting (the load-bearing reframe)
Inverting σ/m ∝ N for N is, taken alone, a one-parameter fit — it would just rename the knob from "well depth" (the dead point-scattering picture) to "loop size." What rescues it is **over-determination**: the loop picture brings more constraints than knobs. Two knobs — loop size N and per-rung bond energy E_bond — face **three** independent constraints:
- **magnitude → N** (σ/m sets the size),
- **cosmological lifetime → E_bond floor** (the ribbon must survive ~14 Gyr in the thermal-eDP bath — Thomas's durability argument, made quantitative),
- **coring/velocity trend → an E_bond window** (σ/m rising toward dwarfs requires collisional fragmentation that turns ON at cluster velocities and OFF at dwarf velocities).
Lifetime and the fragmentation-trend are governed by the **same** E_bond, pulling in opposite directions (durable wants E_bond large; fragmentable-at-cluster-v wants it bounded above). If a single (N, E_bond) lands in all three boxes, the fit becomes a genuine three-way consistency test. That is the move only the extended geometry makes available.

## What the cross-section pass computed (numbers from the script)
- **Scaling is the linchpin.** d ln(σ/m)/d ln N = **+1.000** for stiff hoops (face-on and orientation-averaged), **0.000** for a floppy ideal-chain coil, **+0.18** for a self-avoiding coil. So σ/m ∝ N — a real magnitude knob AND the prerequisite for a fragmentation trend — exists **only if the loop is stiff** (persistence length ℓ_p ≳ loop size). A floppy coil gives σ/m ≈ const: no magnitude tuning and no trend. **Stiffness is not optional; it is precisely the property the structure calculation must deliver.** And Thomas's durability argument *is* the stiffness argument: a 4-CP-wide, high-symmetry, strong-bonded ribbon that only rogue-waves can break is, by the same token, a high-bending-rigidity ribbon. The wide symmetric hard-to-break structure is the one geometry that makes all three constraints live at once.
- **The N-target.** Orientation-averaged opaque hoop (Cauchy ⟨A_proj⟩ = πR²/2, R = Nℓ/2π): σ/m ∈ [0.6, 2] ⇒ **N ∈ [710, 2366]**, R ∈ [113, 377] fm. Face-on (max) hoop: **N ∈ [355, 1183]**, R ∈ [56, 188] fm. So the target loop size is **~10²–10³ DP-rungs, R ~ 50–400 fm** — squarely the band 0859 quoted, and a *reasonable* scale (not 10¹, not 10¹⁰), which is itself a mild free consistency check. σ_geo is an **upper bound** on the transport cross-section (loops can pass through / link / deform), so every N here is a **lower bound**; a transport efficiency ε<1 raises the true N by 1/ε.
- **Mass sensitivity.** N-target scales linearly with the per-rung mass. Continuity choice m_rung = 264 MeV (the constituent the point-scattering used) gives N(σ/m=1) = 1183; m_rung = 528 → 2366; m_rung = 1056 → 4731. Pinning the real 2eDP:2qDP per-rung mass moves the target linearly — worth fixing before quoting a final band, but it does not change the order of magnitude.
- **The ledger closes — conditionally.** With N* ≈ 1183 (orientation-averaged, σ/m=1): a loop–loop collision deposits ~0.78 keV at dwarf velocity (30 km/s) and ~1.95 MeV at cluster velocity (1500 km/s). So the **fragmentation window** wants E_bond ∈ [~0.8 keV, ~2 MeV] (fragments in clusters, not dwarfs → trend rises toward dwarfs, the data-preferred direction). The **lifetime floor** (Arrhenius, any-of-N rungs breaking fragments the loop) is E_bond ≳ ~100 kT_amb. These overlap — a single (N, E_bond) satisfies magnitude + lifetime + trend together — **iff the ambient thermal-eDP scale kT_amb ≲ ~19 keV**. That sub-~20-keV condition is the **falsifiable hook**: the substrate thermal scale must be modest enough that a 14-Gyr-durable relic is still fragmentable at cluster velocities. Whether the actual substrate thermal-eDP scale meets it is an **SF-input to check**, not something this pass asserts.

## A subtlety that makes the trend physical (worth keeping)
Exactly-linear σ ∝ N would make fragmentation **conserve** total σ/m (k loops of N/k give back N) — no trend. The stiff *hoop* evades this: circumference ∝ N ⇒ R ∝ N ⇒ enclosed area πR² ∝ N², so σ/m ∝ N²/N = N, and fragmenting a hoop *reduces* σ/m (N² split into k pieces of (N/k)² loses a factor k). High velocity → more fragmentation → lower σ/m → σ/m **rises toward dwarfs**. The right direction falls out of the geometry, but only for stiff hoops — reinforcing that stiffness is the make-or-break structural property.

## Honest layer grading
This makes σ/m a **calibrated** quantity (one fit: magnitude → N) that is then **tested** against lifetime and trend. That is **Layer B/C** ("consistency demonstrated, magnitude calibrated, with a falsifiable thermal-scale condition"), NOT a zero-parameter Layer-A prediction. DM-1 must say so plainly until PCD formation dynamics actually *derives* N. Doing the inversion first is good experimental design, not a retreat: it fixes the precise target — N ~ 10²–10³ rungs AND ℓ_p ≳ loop size — that the formation-dynamics campaign must hit. You cannot tell whether the dynamics "favour the right size" until you have computed what the right size *is*. This pass computed it.
