## The key reframe (why the cross is not "just a stiffer chain")

Bending a cross-bonded bundle does **not** bend each strand at its hinge. Plane-sections beam bending
forces the OUTER strands to STRETCH and the inner to COMPRESS. The restoring energy comes from the
**axial bond-stretch stiffness κ_ax ~ E_bond** — robust and LARGE (the same E_ee/E_qq depth from the
G2/0865 bracket) — times the **width² lever**, NOT from the near-cancelled hinge angular spring that
killed the strand:

    B_beam = κ_ax · a · Σ_i (y_i − y_neutral)²        (Euler–Bernoulli, parallel coupled strands)
    ℓ_p(rungs) = B_beam/(kT·a) = (E_bond/kT) · Σ(dy_i)²     [a = ℓ_rung = 1, κ_ax ~ E_bond/a²]

The cross is stiff for a **structurally different reason** than the strand, and is therefore **immune
to the near-cancellation** that made G1 a make-or-break for the strand. That is the whole result.

## Geometry: why "cross", not flat "ribbon" (confirms Thomas's word choice)

- Flat 4-ribbon: Σdy²=5 (in-plane) but **Σdz²=0** out-of-plane → zero stiffness for one bending axis
  → it COILS out-of-plane. Not viable as a rigid extended object.
- "+" cross (2D section): Σ≈2 in BOTH transverse directions → **isotropically stiff**, will not coil.

So the viable stiff morphology is the cross, exactly as Thomas specified — the flat ribbon is floppy.

## Numbers (Layer C, bracketed — and they land favorably)

ℓ_p(rungs) = c_geom·(E_bond/kT), c_geom ≈ 2 (+ cross, worst/isotropic axis) to 5 (flat ribbon, in-plane).
With the 0865 lifetime-floor bracket E_bond/kT ≳ 100 (E_bond∈[0.8 keV,2 MeV], kT≲19 keV):

- flat-ribbon in-plane at floor: ℓ_p ≈ 500 → in-band (but floppy out-of-plane, so not the real object).

Honest statement: the stiffness is supplied by the **same bond depth the lifetime floor already
demands** — no separate fine-tuning — and lands in/approaching the band. It is over-determined in

not a failure — it just means rigid rods; object SIZE is set by depletion, ℓ_p only needs ≳ object
length for it to stay extended.

## Sign-safety (the beam beats the residual hinge)

A cross of N_w strands inherits N_w hinge residuals (each ~κ_hinge, possibly buckling per 0867/0869).
Stability ratio B_beam/(N_w·|κ_hinge|) = c_geom/(N_w·(|κ_hinge|/E_bond)). For any plausible residual
|κ_hinge| ≲ 0.5 E_bond, the ratio is 1–50× → the cross is **stable even if the bare strand hinge
buckles**: the width-coupled bond-stretch restoring force dominates the near-cancelled hinge term.

## σ/m and the unification

A stiff/semiflexible cross is an extended scatterer: L ≤ ℓ_p → rigid rod (d_f=1, σ/m ~ L); L > ℓ_p →
semiflexible (d_f~1–1.5, σ/m ~ L^(2−d_f) still grows). Either way σ/m rises with size toward the band.
AND the stiff cross is precisely the **extended sub-unit** whose cluster-cluster coalescence gives the
d_f<2 amorphous ball (0868). So the morphologies **unify on the cross**: it is both the standalone
ribbon and the feedstock for the fluffy ball.
