# 0869 — G1 refined: the 4-charge folded-vertex apposition (2+, 2−) + ZBW

**Lane:** DM 08xx, DM-local. No registry/SF/THEO/OPEN/founders edits. DM-1 stays v0.1; no `.tex` touched.
**Layer:** C (bracketed; absolute vertex geometry + arrangement are SF-2/SF-5 pending).
**Goalpost:** G1 — land the hinge so a loop sits in the 0860/0861 band (~300–2500 rungs ⇔ per-hinge
bend θ₀ ~ 0.14–1.2°, since a loop closes at N₀ = 360°/θ₀). Direct refinement of 0867.

## The refinement Thomas posed

0867 modeled the hinge as **2 like tips (+ +) + 2 opposite screeners (− −)** and found the buckling
kill-risk (bend-coupled equal screening subtracts curvature → straight config unstable). Thomas's
correction: the real folded internal vertex is **not** that structure. It is a **4-charge apposition,
2 plus + 2 minus** — a +/− dipole vertex V1 apposing a +/− neighbor pair (V2, V3), in a variety of
plus-minus close/far combinations, with **ZBW oscillation** setting the mean separations (soft spacer
against collapse). Because both attractive (+−) and repulsive (++/−−) pairs sit in the *same*
apposition, the bend coordinate is richer than "restore vs buckle."

## Honesty trail — a draft narrative I built and then had to retract

My first pass wrote an optimistic story into the script: that the 2+,2− structure turns 0867's
buckling into an *in-band* loop feature ("sub-degree preferred angles → hundreds-to-thousands of
rungs," "κ/E ~ O(0.1–1) lands the product in [100,700]," "a narrow balance window collapses θ₀ to
zero"). **Every one of those claims was contradicted by the code's own output** when I actually read
the numbers: the computed θ₀ was ~18°, not sub-degree; κ/E was ~35–42, not O(0.1–1); and the q2
"balance window" left θ₀ pinned at 17.85°. I caught the prose-vs-output contradiction, threw the
narrative out, and rebuilt the analysis to report only what the model computes. (Same discipline as
0867 v1's axial-decoupling and 0868's broken-estimator/non-converging-CCA dead-ends.) The corrected
result is more negative — and it is the real one.

## What the model actually finds (self-consistent with output)

Two sign-families exhaust the 2+,2− apposition.

**Symmetric (++ ‖ −−).** L/R-symmetric; the straight config is a *maximum* (k₀ < 0) → **BUCKLES**
(now seen as a symmetric double-well at ±θ₀) for all but the largest gap; straight-stable only at
large gap. **0867's buckling is confirmed** in this family.

**Alternating (+/− ‖ +/−).** Inherently L/R-asymmetric → a **preferred fold angle θ₀ ≠ 0** (intrinsic
curvature; **no hard buckling** — a genuinely softer outcome, the one real improvement). **But θ₀ is
robustly large: ~18° (and *worse*, to ~50°, in the wide-anvil limit), and is NOT moved below ~17° by
any accessible parameter** — anvil magnitude (0.3–5.0), dipole magnitude (0.3–2.5), dipole length
(0.05–0.9), gap, or separation. θ₀ is a **geometric docking angle** (where the moving +/− vertex docks
onto its attractive anvil partner), not a tunable residual. So the alternating family self-closes into
loops of **~7–20 rungs — ~15–50× too tight** for the 300–2500 band, and it **resists tuning** into the
band entirely. (The stiffness κ at the minimum *is* a tunable near-cancellation residual, spanning
~0.03–300; but the *angle* is the newly-stuck quantity, and the angle is what sets loop size.)

**ZBW** is structurally required: finite a sets the mean separation → finite κ and a well-defined
minimum; as a → 0 the attractive pairs collapse to contact (singular).

## Verdict (Layer C, honest — NOT a pass; a new kill-risk surfaced)

The 2+,2− apposition genuinely changes the risk and is **partly better** — the alternating family
removes 0867's *hard* buckling. But it is **not a rescue**, and it surfaces a **new kill-risk**: in
this generic 4-charge geometry, *neither* family lands a band-sized loop. The symmetric family buckles
(or, at large gap, is straight-stable and then needs external closure); the alternating family curls
far too tightly (~18°/loop) and resists tuning. The make-or-break is **relocated** from 0867's
stiffness-near-cancellation to the **docking angle**, and it is now a *harder, more geometric* tension:
the loop wants a sub-degree per-hinge bend; the 4-charge docking robustly delivers tens of degrees.

**Caveat (the load-bearing one):** this is **one schematic geometry** (radial +/− dipole on an arm vs
a lateral +/− pair). The true SF-2/SF-5 hTetra vertex geometry could be special — e.g. an attractive
partner placed nearly on-axis would give a small docking angle. But the burden is now squarely on SF
to *show* that, because the generic structure does not, and actively resists. That is the refined,
sharpened, and newly more-doubtful open SF question.

## Two prongs, refined

- **PRONG-1 (sign-safety):** the alternating family avoids hard buckling — a real partial win.
- **PRONG-2 (magnitude → now angle):** can the SF vertex geometry produce a **sub-degree docking
  angle** (band-sized loop)? Generic geometry says no; SF must demonstrate a special small-angle
  configuration. This is the live make-or-break.
