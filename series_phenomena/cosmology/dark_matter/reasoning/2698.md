Three design decisions. First, I split the outcome space into two
determinations plus a consequence (D1 object, D2 alpha, D3 readout)
instead of flat classes, because the founder's answer showed the
matching question and the discreteness question are different axes —
2685-2690 taught me what happens when one frozen class has to stretch
over two axes (the SHIFT text straining to hold "no clean revised
value"). Second, the D3 guard: the derivation could plausibly conclude
the staggered structure is a discretization artifact, and I wrote into
the charter that the worker CANNOT enact that conclusion — FG-OTHER
was classified by a five-seat panel twice, and a reclassification is
theirs. Freezing my own lack of authority now, while I do not yet know
what S3 will find, is the cheapest honesty I will ever buy. Third, the
S2 reconciliation requirement names the possible kappa = 2/d_DP lock
without claiming it, with the non-elevation clause explicitly extended
to it — the l(alpha) minimum already tempted me once this session to
read operating-point specialness as support, and if the Debye-type
scale lands on the gap-route kappa, that temptation will return
doubled. The charter now says: a lock is reported, not elevated.

The kinetic scale is the one ingredient S1 cannot derive from the
registered objects alone — what sets the width of the chaotic SSV_net
fluctuations is mechanism, and mechanism is the founder's. Rather than
block, the charter lets S1 proceed to the kernel FORM (which does not
need the scale) and holds the scale question for him if it turns out
to gate S2 numerically. CHI-INCOMPLETE exists so that "cannot close
honestly" is a reportable outcome, not a failure to be papered over.
