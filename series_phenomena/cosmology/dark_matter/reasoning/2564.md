# Reasoning — Patch 2564 (the K1 pre-registration; the gate built from the code, not the wish)

STATUS: verbatim, at-patch.

The prereg's spine is the RP-GATE, and the gate was written by reading the instrument
rather than the charter's summary of it. The home-anchoring is not a vibe — it is one
line, `dest = P[tgt] if out else H` — and that line dictates the entire representational
question: the only extension that isn't a new rule is letting H itself move. Everything
else (drag terms, capture heuristics, modified arrest) would be inventing dynamics the
postulates haven't licensed, which is exactly the risk charter Q3 named. Writing the
minimal extension into the prereg as THE admissible extension, with three controls that
can each kill it, means the panel's question gets answered by the gate's outcome instead
of by my confidence.

C-3 is the control I almost didn't include. C-1 and C-2 test the new thing (free flight,
pass-through); C-3 tests that the classifiers recover the OLD thing (the registered bound
state). Without it, a broken classifier could fail every production cell and read as
"null capture — adverse but reportable," which would be a false adverse manufactured by
instrumentation. The asymmetry matters: C-1/C-2 failures indict the extension; a C-3
failure indicts the classifier; conflating them would send a founder-routed Branch I for
what is actually a threshold bug.

The grids were the tuning temptation. The epoch band [10.2, 17.0] sits interior to the
E_rel grid because capture physics near the formation epoch is the point — but the §5
fence had to say explicitly that capture-at-the-band is not the success criterion,
because that is precisely the output-justified step a tired future session would take.
Declaring σ_cap's b* rule now (largest all-cell-captured grid b, band-reported, no
post-hoc refinement) closes the other tuning door: resolution improvements are fine,
but only pre-declared, uniform, and in a successor prereg where they can't chase a
particular cross-section.
