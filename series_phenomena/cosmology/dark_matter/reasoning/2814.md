## The hypothesis I brought was half wrong, and the record says so first

I proposed this audit on the theory that the NOT-FOR-RELEASE banners
were written against conditions that had since changed. Source
reading says otherwise: the banners are attached to a KILLED
candidate and to founder decision B7, neither of which S4-X's closure
touches. What changed is the banners' upstream dependency — the
Candidate (B) promotion path — not the banners. Recording that
correction at the top of the audit matters more than the audit's
findings, because an audit that flatters its own premise is worthless.

## The finding worth the whole exercise

PR4's frozen text reads: "A direct registered Moment-rule automaton —
OR AN ANALYTICALLY EQUIVALENT DERIVATION FROM THE EXPLICIT MOMENT
TRANSITION LAW." Every session since 2726 has read PR4 as an
automaton requirement, including all of mine; the arc closure declared
the automaton route bounded by FEM feasibility and I very nearly filed
PR4 as blocked-indefinitely on that basis. The disjunction was there
the whole time, in the panel's own frozen wording, and it converts the
release chain's hard blocker from "wait for computing machinery nobody
has" to "attempt an analysis nobody has attempted." Whether the
analysis is tractable is unknown — but scoping it is cheap and the
answer is decisive either way.

PR2's own text asks for kappa_eff consistent with 1 within total
uncertainty <= 3%. The successor returned 1.0349 +/- 0.0194:
consistent with 1 at 1.8 sigma, uncertainty 1.94%. Read against PR2's
letter that looks like a pass; read against my 2795 band [0.97, 1.03]
it is UNRESOLVED. My band was stricter than the requirement it
operationalized. I could argue either way and that is precisely why I
must argue neither: I have seen the number. The audit flags it in
bold, hands it to the panel, and the statistics extension proceeds
regardless so the question may become moot.

## What the audit refuses to imply

Nothing here moves a banner. Founder decision B7 holds all three
papers, and only the founder lifts it. The audit's job was to say
truthfully what stands between here and OSF; the answer is: one hard
blocker with one unexplored door, three cheap executable items, two
desk passes, and then a substantial revision-and-review chain that no
amount of worker diligence can shorten.
