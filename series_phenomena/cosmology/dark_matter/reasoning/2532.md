## How the theorem appeared

Attempting to write the founder's barrier as a state-energy difference: count the tetra's edges against the
separated pair's. The four added cross edges carry charge products qe·qq·(++, +−, −+, −−) — the sum is
(Σ e-charges)(Σ q-charges) = 0·0 = 0 whenever all four cross distances are equal, which the regular tetra
guarantees. So the repulsive edges are exactly paid for by the hybrid attractive edges AT the symmetric
point — the barrier cannot be a state property. It must live on the assembly path, where the four distances
are unequal and the cancellation fails; the sympy check confirms a displaced configuration is net repulsive.
The founder's velocity/activation picture and the geometry agree precisely, and the agreement is
informative: it locates the barrier and simultaneously proves the reaction is near-threshold at leading
electric order (ΔE_b = pure repackaging + color-in-tetra + ZBW; second-order).

The near-threshold consequence is flagged carefully: it makes order-half hybrid fractions *natural*, which
is favorable-direction for the reopening — but it is registered as a derivation target (NB-F-1-T), not a
result. The temptation to say "near-threshold ⇒ f ≈ 1/2 ⇒ reopen at D-strong" was strong and was refused:
that would be the 2529 null-baseline trap wearing a theorem as a disguise. The trap clause governs until
ΔE_b, E_act, and the freeze-out condition are actually computed.
