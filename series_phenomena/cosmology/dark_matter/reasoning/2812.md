# Reasoning fragment — Patch 2812 (arc closure)

**STATUS: verbatim, captured at patch. Session of 2026-07-26.**

## Closing at the boundary rather than at exhaustion

The arc could have continued generating runs indefinitely — more
densities, more PSR values, more proxy variants — and every one would
have characterised the same regime more finely without touching the
question that matters (can the Sea store and return momentum). The
founder identified the FEM boundary and I agree with his read, so the
arc closes where the instrument ends rather than where patience does.
Section 4's successor prerequisites exist so that a future
implementation inherits the lessons instead of rediscovering them:
especially (iv), because mover fraction as a thermalisation proxy
cost this arc two gate generations before the velocity autocorrelation
exposed it.

## L-1 is the limitation most likely to be violated later

Someone downstream — possibly me in a future session — will want a DP
number density, and this arc contains numbers that look like they
could supply one. They cannot: the proxy runs are driven, energy is
not conserved, no temperature exists. L-1 states that in the
strongest available language and corrects the record on the damping
question in the same breath, because a false memory of "we damped it
with a parameter" would make the density extraction look merely
contaminated rather than impossible.

## Why W5 is superseded rather than deleted

The original W5 asked the panel which of three phenomena to chase.
The worker's own diagnostic then showed all three to be regime
artifacts, so asking would waste five seats' attention on a question
already answered. Recording it as superseded WITH the reason — rather
than quietly deleting it — keeps the packet honest about its own
drafting history, and the replacement W5' asks the thing that
actually needs adversarial review: whether the scope statement's
limits are strict enough.
