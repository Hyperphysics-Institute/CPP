# Reasoning — Patch 2772 (C2R-L2 prereg freeze)

**STATUS: verbatim, at-patch.**

The design question for L2 was how to turn the founder's prose
specification into a computation without inventing physics the
ruling does not contain. The specification has three load-bearing
clauses: an inverse-square-law-governed radial polarization gradient
at the single-charge level, superposition of oppositely-signed
curves between adjacent charges, and the Gauss's-law
enclosed-charge criterion on every closed surface. The minimal
realization is to notice that the L1-derived closure already
CONTAINS the single-charge object: the self-consistent linear
response of the occupied medium to a unit charge is the distributed
induced cloud κ²e^(−κr)/(4πr), whose enclosed charge interpolates
smoothly per Gauss and whose near-field is governed by the bare
inverse-square Coulomb field. Nothing new is postulated; the profile
is the closure's own Green-function content, and the superposition
clause is then automatic because the model is linear — the staggered
q_j supply exactly the oppositely-signed adjacent curves the founder
describes. I recorded two rejected readings in the prereg because
both are tempting and both smuggle something in: a hard-cutoff 1/r²
profile re-introduces the exclusion radius the ruling rejects, and a
density-modulated per-site χ is second order in the bias — outside
linear response and outside the charter.

What the correction then DOES to the operator fell out with more
structure than I anticipated when freezing the charter. Distributing
each site's response as the cloud does two things at once: it
softens the off-diagonal kernel (1−e^(−κr))/r < 1/r, and it
generates a finite diagonal term g(0) = κ — the site's own
near-field medium acting back at its GP. That diagonal term is
precisely the responding r < a medium of the occupancy ruling
entering the discrete operator; its size, ακ = 0.45, is not small,
and the homogenized corrected closure has COMPLEX poles
k² = κ²(−1 ± i√3)/2 — the corrected continuum medium screens with
damped oscillation on its own, before any lattice discreteness.
That is a striking consonance with the staggered evanescent finding,
and it is exactly the kind of observation that must stay OBS-class
in the prereg: consonance is not derivation, and the lattice at
κa = 2 is not the continuum. The prereg therefore commits the
analytic pole comparison as a cross-check, non-adjudicative.

The riskiest committed element is the structural report. The
effective stiffness 1 + ακ pushes the effective coupling down ~31%,
and the 2688 map says the staggering exists only above a coupling
threshold. So the corrected operator may exit the staggered regime
entirely. I committed the fallback fit form and the same-font
reporting rule NOW, before knowing, because that outcome would be
the single easiest place to flinch after results exist — a lost
staggering reads like a broken instrument, and the discipline's
whole point is that it would instead be the correction speaking.
The 50% honesty bound serves the same function from the other side:
if the first-order profile moves ℓ that far, the shape should be
iterated at the corrected operator before anyone quotes a number,
and naming that rather than absorbing it keeps the leg from
overclaiming its own order.

Pairing the δ extraction (same arena, same R, same window, both
operators in one script run) was chosen over comparing to the
committed 0.0904 band directly: the band mixes four arenas and
carries instrument spread that would masquerade as correction
error. The paired design isolates the operator change, which is
the thing L2 is about; ℓ_LO per matched variant is re-derived in
the same run, and the campaign-level number still references the
committed envelope through the L4 assembly, where the charter puts
it.
