Prediction-first was tested twice tonight and held twice. The first
run's validation gate failed at 0.59 and I felt the pull to "just
check what the predictions say anyway" — the gate said no, and the
gate was right: the solver had a real bug (the chain function's
Coulomb tail truncated by the finite sine-transform box), the classic
charged-fluid trap, fixed by the standard analytic renormalization I
should have written the first time. The second run's gate failure was
different in kind — 0.9868 against a 0.5% bar is not a bug but the
method's extraction floor, and the honest move was to keep the FAIL
verdict, measure the floor, and let every prediction wear it as a
band. A gate you loosen after it bites is not a gate.

The physics that came out is quietly decisive where it matters and
honestly tense where it does not. The promotion-relevant number is
the GP-limit: HNC walks the soft-core ladder down to kappa_eff =
kappa_D within the floor — the physical Sea screens at d_DP/2, now by
an independent declared method, and the S4-E excess is diagnosed as a
finite-regularization property. The tension at a_s = 0.04 I resisted
explaining away in either direction: the simulation's own error bars
are the previously-conceded weak point, AND HNC's closure error peaks
exactly at that contact strength — so the record names both, routes
the cheap discriminating computation to S4-X, and notes that the
GP-limit conclusion survives either resolution. The Gamma-linear
scaling of the finite-core excess was a small surprise (I had
half-expected 3/2); it is filed as characterization because a fitted
exponent over three points is a hint, not a law.
