# Reasoning — Patch 2754 (gate FAIL; A-path self-pair defect localized)

The gate was designed to certify B ≡ A and instead certified B ≡ H
and A ≡ H + g(|δ|). The first run showed all five discrepancies
positive and move-size dependent — wrong shape for a prefactor error
(which would scale with the k-space share and not flip magnitude by
a factor of sixteen between moves), right shape for a spurious pair
term keyed to the displacement itself. The hypothesis wrote itself
from reading run_A's increment with fresh eyes: `d_n = pos − newp` is
taken against the array in which particle i still sits at its OLD
position, so row i of the new-distance table is the old→new
displacement — small, nonzero, and inside the erfc mask's window. The
1e-12 guard was written for `d_o`, where row i is exactly zero, and
was silently trusted for `d_n`, where it does nothing. I tested the
hypothesis quantitatively before believing it: subtracting
Q²·f(|old−new|) from dE_A − dE_B leaves residuals of 1–3×10⁻¹⁴ on
all five moves. That is not a correlation; it is an identity.

The genealogy mattered more than the identity. If the defect were
mine (a transcription slip in the 2753 gate reference), the campaign
would be untouched. It is not mine: the same two lines sit verbatim
in 2749, 2746 run_A, 2743, 2740, 2737, and 2714. And the S4-N code
one act earlier — 2709 — is clean, with `eo[i]=0; en[i]=0` explicit.
So this is a refactoring regression with a birthday: the S4-E Ewald
rewrite (2714) replaced index zeroing with a magnitude mask, and the
mask is correct for exactly one of the two tables it was applied to.
Every Ewald-line Metropolis ensemble since — including the N =
432/686 chains where the B2 anomaly was first read, and all twelve
X7 series that mapped its plateau — ran with a displacement-dependent
penalty of 2–28 MeV against Θ = 35 MeV on every proposed move.

What I refused to do in the record is the thing the moment invites:
declare the anomaly solved. The penalty applies to driven and
undriven chains identically; a symmetric per-proposal penalty breaks
detailed balance (in the |ΔH| < g regime the acceptance ratio squares
the Boltzmann factor), but its NET effect on the driven-mean/tilt
ratio is not signed by inspection, and the strongest counter-fact in
the file is X5-FE itself: at N = 64 the defective A path agreed with
the correct B sampler to 0.27σ. The defect's observable-level bite is
demonstrably small somewhere. Whether it is what switches on across
(64, 80] is a measurement, and the amended act is built to make it:
fix A with one line (`mn[i] = False`), certify fixed-A against B at
the increment level, then let three clean ensembles (A-FIX-UND for
the uncontaminated tilt, A-FIX-DRV, B-DRV) and one frozen J-fork say
whether the enhancement dies with the defect, survives it, or the
implementations disagree.

Two housekeeping resolutions fell out. The 2753 prereg's open
question about the committed 2746 B file is closed: with the full
k-space prefactor my B matches A's k-space increment to 10⁻¹⁴, so
full PREF is A's convention and the committed half-prefactor line is
a stale pre-fix snapshot, as suspected. And F3's prose — "no
line-level defect of the wrong-formula kind exists" — is impeached as
a mechanism claim while its numbers stand as measured; the
corrections ledger gets both this and the stale snapshot. The gate
consumed seed 1 only; 20260795 is still virgin for the production
run. The frozen prereg said FAIL blocks production and the session
reports the defect. Blocking a run I wanted to run, because the
document I froze an hour earlier says so, is the discipline working
exactly as designed — the prereg's I-fork never executes, and its
successor is chartered in the open, before any new data exists.
