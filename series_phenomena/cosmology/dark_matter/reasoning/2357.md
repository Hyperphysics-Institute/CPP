## What I decided and why
1. **Answered the questions before writing the documents.** The founder asked what
   these decisions ARE. Clause 1: he could not approve it as it stood — the ratified
   text would have been the mechanism-form proposal that 2346 already ruled must be
   re-drafted. Telling him "not yet, and here is the text you CAN approve" is the
   only honest answer to "should I approve."
2. **Held the line between my approval and his ratification — while making
   ratification one line of work.** He authorized me to approve; I approve F3,
   band-reporting, and R2, and said so on the record. But these decide what the
   papers say under his name, and the house grammar (founder verbatim; the 2352
   scope line) makes his one-line ratification the thing that turns my approvals
   into the programme's record. The consolidated slate exists so that costs him a
   single sentence.
3. **Clause 1 population form: exits, not conditions-to-win.** Four independent
   exits — S(N) rent, formation rent, completion audits, direct-detection/F1
   discriminant — each already registered elsewhere as a live check, so the clause
   invents nothing; it binds what exists. The no-softening-after-adverse-results
   sentence and the add-only amendment rule are the teeth; a falsification clause
   without teeth is decoration.
4. **The DM-1 section is written per-reading, not per-my-preference.** The
   three-row pin table carries the steep edge as live and prices each pass (α, p)
   in the same breath as the pass. Decision slots are marked [SLATE-n] so the
   founder's ratification drops in without rewriting physics.
5. Section delivered as a standalone insertion draft (completed-document-in-hand,
   per the founder's stated preference) rather than surgery on the shipped DM-1
   tex — the full rewrite proceeds after the slate lands.
