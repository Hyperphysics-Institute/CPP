## Why this dissolves the two-sided N-squeeze (1856's near-kill)

The squeeze was an artifact of the *threshold* frame: a flat plateau σ₀~1.6 held to v_thr meant clusters below
threshold sat at 1.6, forcing v_thr low → long rods → conflict with the transport-short requirement. With no
threshold, the curve is **σ/m(v) = σ_floor + (1/r² capture, ∝1/v²)**:
- **σ_floor** = the *elastic rod-bounce* momentum transfer (1856 σ_T, velocity-independent) = the **cluster floor**
  (want LOW → short N ✓). 1856's σ_T was never the dwarf value; it is the high-v floor.
- **capture tail** = the 1/r² gravitational-focusing capture (this is the origin of the "parameter-free 1/v²"),
  large at low v → **dwarf coring**, dying by clusters.
Clusters see the low floor, not a high plateau; dwarfs are cored by the capture. **Short N is consistent on both
ends; the squeeze is gone.** Coring = momentum redistribution, so soft (reversible) and hard captures both feed
σ/m — justifying the single-channel collapse.

## Cost: the prediction shape changes (threshold → smooth)
