**STATUS: verbatim, captured at patch. Session of 2026-07-27.**

## Why prereq 5 is the one that mattered

Items 2, 4 and 6 are essentially bookkeeping: continuity is a mixed-
partials identity, the magnetic force term is second order by
construction because B itself carries a v/c, and C_2 falls out of the
Darwin bracket by inspection. Prereq 5 is the substantive claim -- that
every first-order term actually cancels -- and it is checkable. The
ratio test settles it: halving beta divides the deviation by 4.093,
4.023, 4.006, converging on exactly 4. A surviving first-order term
would have given 2. That is the cancellation exhibited rather than
asserted, and it is the first time this week I have been able to hand
the panel a number that closes a question instead of opening another.

## The ceiling I did not round in my favour

At 2838 I called C_2 "O(1), natural range 0.5-1, conservative cover 2."
The derivation gives a hard maximum of exactly 1, attained for parallel
velocities along the separation. That is BETTER than my earlier guess,
and the temptation was to lead with it -- v/c <= 0.387 instead of
0.274. I have stated it plainly but put it in the prerequisite table
rather than the headline, because the number only matters if the
conditional in section 5 holds, and leading with a relaxed bar would
invite exactly the misreading the panel has twice had to correct.

## The conditional I am handing back

Everything in prereqs 4, 5, 6 is electrodynamics. It applies to CPP if
CPP's field sector is Maxwellian in general -- which S1's R1 amendment
deliberately left open, and which I cannot settle by adding more
identities. I have now supplied every ingredient the panel named, and
the residue is precisely the thing they flagged at the start. That is
either convergence or a very well-organised circle, and the founder
should be the one to say which.
