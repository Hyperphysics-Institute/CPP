The override failure is the event worth the most words. When the duty "re-run" came back
matching the registered record to four decimals across three dt, the pleasant reading was
"perfectly invariant!" and I nearly wrote it. Four-decimal identity across trajectories
that SHOULD differ (different reach lists change targets, targets change trajectories)
is not invariance -- it is identity, and identity meant my override never bound. The
namespace of an exec'd chain holds its own names; my module-level assignment was
decoration. The accident banks as a genuine asset -- a bit-exact from-scratch
reproduction of the 2514 record -- but only because suspicion beat satisfaction by about
thirty seconds. Rule of thumb now explicit: a re-run under a CHANGED functional that
reproduces the old numbers exactly is evidence the change didn't take, not evidence of
robustness.

The L=8 finding is the audit doing precisely what the panel bought: the defect nobody
was looking for, in the corrected-era code, bounded to one cell. I want the shape of the
result on record: the wall -- the campaign's falsifiable positive -- is clean at every
tested L; the defect touches only the window's LOWER endpoint, which is the less
load-bearing edge (nothing downstream selects on L=8; the candidate is 16; the prize
question is the upper structure). An honest caveat rider on a low-stakes endpoint,
plus a guard that makes silent binding impossible in the future, is close to the
best-case outcome an audit can deliver.

The old-rule binding at L=16 (max 7) completes the damage accounting in a way that
mildly VINDICATES the correction arc: the retired classifier was wrong twice over --
misclassifying planes AND truncating arbitrarily -- and both defects died in one
adjudication. Grok's "the promotion case rests on corrected data" concern reads
differently with today's additions: the corrected pin reproduces from scratch, the duty
verdict is classifier-invariant, and the corrections keep being in the direction of
MORE fidelity, independently confirmed. That accounting goes to the promotion dispatch
when it fires; today it just accretes.
