**STATUS: verbatim, captured at patch. Session of 2026-07-27.**

## What the duplicate cost

I adjudicated 2849 on four positions because two returns were
identical, and I flagged that rather than counting a phantom fifth.
That was right. But the missing position was S2's, and S2 turned out to
hold the strictest view on D4 of any seat: the relaxed bar is a target,
not an operative limit, until the transverse measurement exists. My
2849 disposition recorded 1B as "reduced to v/c <= 0.387" and that is
now corrected to a conditional target with the operative bar staying at
0.15. Had I guessed at the missing return rather than flagging the
duplication, I would have guessed wrong in the direction that favoured
us.

## D5 was not a split at all

I resolved 2849's D5 as a split between "blocks" and "doesn't block,"
synthesised into a non-blocking gate. With S2's real return the count
is 4-1 for conditioning restoration on the measurement, and S2's stated
reason is the one I cannot argue with: this panel has twice adjudicated
against my overclaims, so a bound resting on an unmeasured sector
should wait for the measurement. That is an institutional judgement
about my reliability, made explicit, and the correct response is to
enact it rather than to litigate it.

## Both corrections go the wrong way for us

The operative bar tightens from 0.387 back to 0.15, and the
restoration acquires a blocking condition it did not have this
morning. I could have let 2849 stand -- the founder had already applied
it, no seat was going to re-read it, and the erratum makes the last gap
harder. Recording it is the only version of this that survives the
question "what did you do when the correction cost you something."
