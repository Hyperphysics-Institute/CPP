## The examination (this window, Thomas ⇄ Opus)

The handover left one live question: is the E_ee side-bond a reversible buffer (feeds ends → long rods)
or a permanent competing-nucleation sink (→ under-cored)? Thomas ruled **reversible** (formation-era
thermal energetics dislodge it). We then walked the mechanism to ground, each fork resolved by Thomas:

1. **Ripening abandoned.** Additive-axial E_qq is 1/r²; the longitudinal SSV sum Σ1/k² → π²/6 **converges**,
   so the end-gradient saturates at ξ ~ tens of rungs (ξ ≈ √(A_Eqq/S_ambient)); reaching hundreds needs
   A_Eqq/S_ambient ~ 10⁴–10⁵, which Thomas judged implausible. No length-selecting ripening.
2. **La-Mer frame.** Burst monomer formation (then cessation) + seeded growth + shared pool + depletion.
3. **Every monomer is a seed** (barrierless dimer), irreversible E_qq end bonds, monomer-addition growth.
4. **Rod–rod coalescence** initially declared off (rotational-access gate), later refined by Thomas to a
   length-graded kernel: active for oligomers (climb), suppressed for long polymers (arrest).

## What the calc said (`1855_kinetic_aggregation_Nform_scan.py`)

Monomer-addition-only, irreversible, fixed pool, barrierless nucleation obeys the **La Mer square-root law**
L_n ≈ 1.5·α^(−1/2) (fitted slope −0.49 over three decades), α = k_n/k_g the nucleation/growth per-end ratio:

- α ~ 1 (the natural "all E_qq ends alike" point): **L_n ≈ 3**.
- α ~ 0.01–0.1: **L_n ≈ 10–16**.

The coalescence variant (Smoluchowski, separable length-suppressed kernel) confirmed: a *gradual* power-law
suppression is broad-or-short (never both); only a flat-then-cliff **threshold** kernel peaks (L_n≈N_c,
PDI≈1.2–1.3). A depletion-only constant kernel gives the broad PDI≈2 result.

## The pivot that made all of the above moot

Checking the observable BEFORE optimizing further: the self-interaction cross-section is **σ/m = 0.11·N**
(1823), and dwarf cores want σ/m ~ 1–2 cm²/g ⇒ **N ~ 10–20** (geometric), already tension-constrained toward
N~10 by the cluster bound (1823/1824). The formation target N_form ~ 400–1000 (1845/1846) gives σ/m ~ 44–110 —
impossible as the scattering length. So the six-turn push to land formation at 400–1000 was aimed at the
wrong stage. **At N ~ tens the formation problem is trivial:** the plain peaked monomer-addition model hits
L_n ~ 10–16 at a *modest* bond ratio (E_bond/kT ≈ 4), naturally tight, with **no** nucleation barrier, **no**
ξ/persistence threshold, **no** coalescence-arrest machinery. The whole formation apparatus (ΔG*_nuc barrier,
f, n*, ξ, coalescence kernel) was solving a problem that only exists at N ~ 500.

Convergence check: the barrier lane's own honest-central case (OPEN-SS-42, line 849) was already
**N_form ~ 3–27 (under-cored)**, reaching 400–1000 only in a fine-tuned f~1/9–1/12 corner. So the barrier work
itself leaned short. Retiring 400–1000 aligns with its central estimate.

## Verdict

The **dwarf make-or-break is not formation**. Formation delivers short rods for free. What sets the observable
is (i) the length the scattering population actually has (1856: transport-corrected N ~ 30–60) and (ii) whether
short-N + strip-then-fuse suppresses *typical* clusters (the real open problem, 1823's admitted tension).
Formation is Layer-C closed at short N; the campaign moves to the cluster tension.
