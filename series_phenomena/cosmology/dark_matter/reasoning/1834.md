## Why — Earnshaw, and why it was hiding

This is **Earnshaw's theorem**: a static configuration of point charges has no stable equilibrium. By Laplace's
equation the curvatures of a Coulomb potential over orthogonal directions sum to zero, so they cannot all be
positive — every static charge cluster has at least one unstable direction, and here the dihedral is one of
them. My 1833 estimate (κ_θ ≈ 0.27 MeV, positive) was a **two-term subset** (one repulsive pair + one cancelling
pair) that happened to give a positive curvature by leaving out the rest of the sum, which is net destabilizing.
The full sum exposes that. **The 1833 static number is wrong physics and is retracted.**
