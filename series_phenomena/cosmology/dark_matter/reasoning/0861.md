## The PCD → polymer-physics bridge (the energetics, made concrete)
Thomas's "PCD aggregation energetics" maps cleanly onto reversible equilibrium polymerization once the PCD primitives are identified:
- A **bond** between adjacent rungs is an **SSV binding well of depth E_bond**.
- The **attempt / detachment clock** is the **PCD cycle rate ν_PCD** (Perceive–Compute–Displace) — the substrate's fundamental frequency, and the same ν₀ that appeared in the 0860 lifetime floor.
- **Thermal-eDP fluctuations** at the formation-epoch scale kT_form drive reversible rung addition/removal — exactly the rogue-wave bond-breaking Thomas invoked for durability, here running in *both* directions during formation.
So formation is reversible linear aggregation in a thermal bath: an equilibrium-polymerization length distribution that **freezes out** when the per-bond detach rate ν_PCD·exp(−E_bond/kT(t)) drops below the Hubble rate as the bath cools.

## The central simplification: two inputs, one source
The whole campaign reduces to **two substrate numbers**, and both come from the **same** object:
- **E_bond** = DEPTH of the 2eDP:2qDP rung-bond SSV well → sets the length distribution, the 14-Gyr lifetime (0860), and the fragmentation window (0860).
- **ℓ_p** = ANGULAR STIFFNESS (κ/kT, bending modulus) of that same well → sets the loop size (via ring-closure) and the cross-section scaling regime (0860's σ/m ∝ N requires ℓ_p ≳ loop size).
Both the depth and the angular stiffness fall out of the **one** 2eDP:2qDP rung-bond SSV potential. **Derive that single potential and the entire DM picture is determined.** This is the campaign's deciding calculation, now stated as one well-posed SF-2/SF-5 object rather than a vague "loop-size distribution."

## What the kinetics computes (numbers from the script)
1. **Length distribution.** Equilibrium polymerization gives ⟨N⟩ ~ exp(E_bond/2kT_form), an exponential (Flory) distribution P(N) ∝ exp(−N/⟨N⟩) — a **broad** population, not a delta, which is the right qualitative shape for a coring spread across systems. Reaching ⟨N⟩ ~ 10³ needs **E_bond/kT_form ~ 13.8**.
2. **Ring closure picks the loop size.** Semiflexible cyclization (Shimada–Yamakawa stiff-chain J-factor, validated in-script to peak at L* = 3.37 ℓ_p — matching the DNA-calibrated optimal-cyclization length ~3.4 ℓ_p) peaks the loop population at **contour length L* ≈ 3.4 ℓ_p**. Loops smaller than ~ℓ_p are bending-suppressed (exponential); larger ones are entropy-suppressed (power law). So **stiffness selects the loop size**: N* ≈ 3.4 ℓ_p/ℓ_rung, R* ≈ 0.54 ℓ_p.
3. **The convergence (the load-bearing result).** Requiring the formation peak N* to land in the 0860 cross-section band [355, 2366] needs **ℓ_p ∈ [105, 702] fm**. The resulting R* = 0.54 ℓ_p ∈ [57, 379] fm. And here is the non-trivial part: at the closure peak L* ≈ 3.4 ℓ_p, the loop is **~3.4 persistence lengths around**, i.e. **ℓ_p/R ≈ 1.9 > 1** — automatically on the *stiff* side of 0860's σ/m ∝ N requirement (ℓ_p ≳ R). So ring-closure does not merely place the size in the band; it places it at a stiffness ratio that **satisfies the cross-section requirement by construction**. Two independent constraints — the formation peak and the cross-section scaling — point to the **same** ℓ_p ~ 10²–7×10² fm. **The loop-size knob collapses onto ℓ_p.**
4. **Two-temperature consistency.** Length wants E_bond/kT_form ~ 13.8 (for ⟨N⟩ ~ 10³); lifetime wants E_bond ≳ 100 kT_present (0860). Consistent iff **kT_form/kT_present ≳ 7** — the formation bath a few × hotter than the present substrate scale, trivially satisfied by cosmological cooling. (Absolute scales need the substrate thermal history — SF-input.)
5. **Supply-vs-closure caveat (computed).** When ⟨N⟩ is comparable to the closure-preferred size, the linear-chain supply term pulls the loop peak below 3.4 ℓ_p (representative ℓ_p=300 fm, ⟨N⟩=10³ → peak at L≈2.7 ℓ_p, N_peak≈820). So the loop size is roughly min(supply ⟨N⟩, closure 3.4 ℓ_p/ℓ_rung) — a real coupling between the two inputs worth tracking.

## Honest caveats held
- The cyclization-preferred loops are **semiflexible** (contour ~3.4 ℓ_p), NOT rigid hoops. So 0860's rigid-hoop σ ∝ N² is an upper estimate; the true σ/m for a semiflexible loop sits between rigid-hoop and floppy, the effective scaling exponent is somewhat below 1, and the true N-target is therefore somewhat **higher** (consistent with 0860's "σ_geo is an upper bound ⇒ N is a lower bound"). The loop is stiff *enough* (ℓ_p/R ~ 1.9), not infinitely stiff.
- The R-band agreement (0861's [57, 379] vs 0860's [56, 377] fm) is partly **definitional** — both use the same R = Nℓ/2π map — so it is an *internal-consistency* confirmation, not a second independent measurement. The genuinely independent content is that **one ℓ_p ~ 10²–7×10² fm simultaneously (a) lands the closure peak at the target and (b) satisfies the σ/m ∝ N stiffness requirement.**
- The J-factor used is the SY stiff-chain *shape* (right limits, right peak), for population-shape illustration — not a precision transport cross-section.
