# 2341 — the brief that asks to be broken twice

**STATUS:** verbatim, at-patch (Opus, DM lane 23xx, 8 July 2026; founder-directed:
"Please assemble the brief for the panel").

The design problem was keeping two commissions from contaminating each other in one
document. Part 1 must invite destruction without hedging — if a panelist senses the
kill is being defended rather than exposed, the review is theater. Part 2 must invite
construction without implying the kill is soft — if a panelist senses the specification
is a consolation prize, the scouting is charity. The wall between them is the same wall
the charter built between the verdict and the hypothesis: the parts are declared
independent, and the brief says out loud that a BREAK in Part 1 is a welcome outcome
and that SPEC-1 survives most local repairs of the kill. Both statements are true, and
saying them costs nothing except the appearance of invulnerability, which the programme
has never been in the business of purchasing.

Two disclosures I made deliberately. The brief hands the panel L3-a — our own
self-identified soft spot in the quantum scan — rather than waiting to see if they find
it. The precedent is the whole arc: every error this week was caught by giving an
independent method the chance to disagree, and the panel is the next independent
method. Concealing a known weakness to see if they catch it would test the panel;
disclosing it tests the physics. We are testing the physics. And the brief states
Clause 1's true status — PROPOSED, pending founder ratification — because the panel
should see the discipline as it stands, including the part still in the founder's
hands. A review package that airbrushes its own governance is advertising, not science.

The scout commission is the experiment inside the experiment. The swarm has only ever
been pointed at finished work; Q1–Q4 point it at an open problem with a measured
specification, a rent rule, and a ranked-deliverable format. If it works, the
methodology grows a second mode. If it produces noise, we will have learned that
adversarial calibration does not transfer to generative work for free — which is
itself worth a line in the methods catalogue. Either way the dSph window gets four
more sets of eyes, and the founder's framing — enroll them in the exploration — gets
its first honest trial.
