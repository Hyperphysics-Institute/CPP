## The corrected gate (Thomas's physics)

Fusion vs recombination is decided **at the instant of collision**, not over cosmic time. A collision strips
the contact (E_ee) and the question is whether the impact drives the two cores through the mutual eDP coat into
**E_qq (color) range** before the stripped eDPs recombine. Color beats recombination decisively *if* the cores
are brought within range (≈73× in energy, ~300× in coupling²) — so the whole gate reduces to a **penetration**
question: does the impact clear the eDP-coat repulsion and bring the cores into color range?

My 1816 v_pen (~1.1×10⁴ km/s, "coat holds at all DM velocities") answered this with the **local-patch mass**
(~1 element) — too coarse. Thomas's correction supplies the right inertia: for a **perpendicular, central** hit
on a **long** rod, the **rod-tail inertia** backs the contact (the tails resist acceleration, providing the
reaction force that converts the full relative KE into penetrating work), and because the collision KE is
**held in the DP Sea (SF-6)** the relative velocity is **sustained** — no instantaneous rebound — so the stress
wave recruits that backing mass during the penetration. This is the answer to the rebound-vs-closure timing I
could not resolve: closure has time because rebound is the *slow* build of E_ee counter-EMF, not an instant
bounce.

## The threshold lands in the cluster band

Penetration condition ½ m_eff v_perp² = E_ee, with v_perp = v·sin θ and m_eff the rod-tail backing mass. Two
estimates of m_eff (bracketing), all inputs pinned (E_ee = 0.9, E_qq = 66, m_element = 1408; v_sound =
√(E_qq/m_element) = 0.22c, the E_qq core setting the axial sound speed):
- **(A) stress-wave recruitment** m_eff = (v_sound/v_perp)(δ/d)m_el → v_thr = **1770–3540 km/s** (δ/d = 1→0.5);
- **(B) fixed rod-fraction** m_eff = f_back·N·m_el → v_thr = **1960–3910 km/s** (N = 60→15, f_back = 0.5–0.25).

Both models give **v_thr ≈ 1700–3900 km/s — squarely in the cluster band, and ~30–60× above dwarf velocities
(~60 km/s).** The naive local-patch v_thr (~1.1×10⁴) was 3–6× too high; the rod-tail-backed value reaches into
where clusters live. **So the penetrating tail is real and populated at cluster velocities, and absent at dwarf
velocities.**

## The velocity dependence (right sign, threshold-like)

Penetration needs v·sin θ ≥ v_thr, so the **perpendicularity acceptance widens with velocity**: at v_thr ≈ 1740
km/s, a v = 2000 collision penetrates only within ~30° of perpendicular, a v = 3000 within ~55°, a v = 4000
within ~64°. Combined with the threshold itself (no penetration below v_thr even perpendicular), the
penetrating — hence fusing — fraction rises **steeply** with velocity:
- **dwarf (60 km/s):** v ≪ v_thr → no penetration → no fusion → rods intact → high σ/m → **cores** ✓;
- **typical cluster (~2000 km/s):** near threshold → only the most perpendicular hits on the longest rods fuse
  → small fusion fraction;
- **high cluster / Bullet (~4000 km/s):** well above → wide acceptance → substantial fusion fraction.

So **σ/m falls with velocity**, via the fraction of collisions clearing the (geometry-dependent) penetration
threshold — no accumulation, no free knob. The three controlling variables are exactly Thomas's: **velocity**,
**perpendicularity** (v sin θ), and **centrality + length** (the backing mass), each raising penetration toward
clusters and long rods.

## Relation to 1816 (refined, not wrong)

1816's "coat holds at all DM velocities" is correct **on average** (a generic head-on/glancing collision does
not penetrate at cluster velocities), but it **misses the braced-perpendicular penetrating tail** that the
strip-then-fuse mechanism rides on. 1816's local-patch mass was the right object for the *average*; the rod-tail
backing is the right object for the penetrating *tail*. The "qq-contact channels are suppressed" reading of 1816
is likewise refined: suppressed for generic collisions, **accessible for the perpendicular/central/long tail at
cluster velocities** — which is precisely what revives Thomas's mechanism.
