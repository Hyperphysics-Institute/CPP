# 2343 — the resonance that showed up and couldn't do the job

**STATUS:** verbatim, at-patch (Opus, DM lane 23xx, 8 July 2026; same session as the
2342 adjudication — the panel's unanimous priority, run before the ink dried).

The satisfying thing about this patch is that everyone was right about something. The
panel and my own L3-a disclosure were right that the door was real: the ℓ = 1
crossings exist, six of them across the band, two sitting almost exactly where SPEC-1
needs them — 83 and 108 eV, as if the potential were trying. DeepSeek was right that
the scan couldn't exclude them and wrong about why and by how much, and the grading
stands. And the kill was right too, for the reason that has decided every flank of
this campaign: the feature is broad. Γ ≈ E_res. A centrifugal barrier at 100 eV in
this potential is a speed bump, not a wall; the resonance leaks, the pin rides up
with the dSphs, LSB overflows, and the suite fails at both hits by the same signature
the s-wave showed. What the energy-axis scan bought over the strength-axis scan is
finality: you cannot argue a pole hid between grid points when the pole was found,
sat in the window, and was measured failing.

Method note for the record: this is the third time this arc has converted a
could-be-hiding argument into a there-it-is-and-here's-why-it-fails measurement, and
each time the conversion cost one bounded session. The pattern generalizes: when a
closure rests on scan granularity, trace the axis the feature lives on. The ℓ = 2
completeness item is queued but I graded it low without apology — the coupling
suppression is stronger and the 2338 all-ℓ transport was smooth there; if the panel
disagrees, the trace is cheap. The kill now has exactly one open flank, and it is the
one the panel found and I missed: the monodisperse assumption. 2344 decides it.
