## What the panel got right (and I got wrong)

The v1.0 §7 closure asserted: the eDP coat is at the same chemical potential as the bulk Sea →
no accumulation → σ/m undiluted. Three reviewers independently identified the flaw: **"same bulk
chemical potential" rules out *unlimited* deposition, but NOT a finite surface excess or a kinetically
trapped layer, unless the spine's residual SURFACE potential well V_surf is actually bounded.**
"Promiscuous ee-edge, no deep specific bond" ≠ "zero attractive well." I had not computed or bounded
V_surf. The criticism is correct; the closure overreached.

## The fix: one deciding quantity, two channels, both made explicit

The whole question reduces to **V_surf/kT** — the residual, Sea-screened, orientation-averaged surface
potential an ambient eDP feels at the cross spine. The coat dilutes σ/m only through:

**Channel 1 — kinetic trapping (the "slow Gyr deposition" worry).** A coat eDP escapes by thermal
activation: τ_res = τ_0·exp(V_surf/kT). It is σ/m-diluting dead mass only if τ_res ≳ t_halo. Threshold:
(V_surf/kT)\*_trap = ln(t_halo/τ_0). For t_halo ≈ 10 Gyr and *any* plausible substrate attempt time
τ_0 ∈ [10⁻²³, 10⁻¹⁰] s, this is **60–93**. With E_bond/kT ≳ 100 (0865 floor), trapping requires
**V_surf ≳ 0.6–0.9 E_bond** — the coat traps only if a *bare* ambient eDP binds to the spine at
60–90% of a *full hTetra bond depth*. This is the computed, robust half: trapping is avoided unless
the residual eDP→spine well is nearly a full bond. The promiscuous-edge argument (no deep specific
bond to a bare eDP) supports V_surf ≪ E_bond, so this channel is plausibly safe — but it is now an
**explicit bound (V_surf/E_bond ≲ 0.6)**, not an assertion.

**Channel 2 — equilibrium surface excess (the "wetting layer" worry).** Even at steady state a
localized excess over uniform bulk gravitates with the aggregate: m_coat/m_spine = G·(⟨e^(V/kT)⟩−1),
with G ~ (ρ_Sea/ρ_spine)·(perimeter·λ_D/A_spine) a geometric/density prefactor. Two things help and
one is pending: (i) the promiscuous edge flips V's sign across orientations, so the orientation
average is ⟨e^(V/kT)⟩ → cosh(V/kT), and the excess is **second-order**, (1/2)(V/kT)², not first-order
— orientation cancellation suppresses thin-coat mass; (ii) the prefactor **G is SF/substrate-pending**
(it needs the Sea-vs-spine density ratio and the screening length). For G ≲ 0.4 (ρ_Sea/ρ_spine ≲ 0.1)
and V_surf/kT ≲ 1, m_coat/m_spine ≲ 0.2 (safe-to-marginal); larger ρ_Sea/ρ_spine is the failure corner.

## Honest status (this is the panel's requested fix, delivered)

§7 is **downgraded from "closed by reasoning" to an explicit conditional bound**:

> The corona is σ/m-safe iff **(1)** V_surf/kT ≲ 60–90 (no Gyr kinetic trapping — robust, computed)
> AND **(2)** G·(cosh(V_surf/kT)−1) ≪ 1 (thin equilibrium excess — needs the SF prefactor G).

Both are controlled by the same residual V_surf/kT. The promiscuous-edge + Sea-screening +
orientation-cancellation arguments make small V_surf/kT **plausible**, and the trapping channel is
robustly bounded; but constraint (2) also needs ρ_Sea/ρ_spine. So the corona is **BOUNDED and
PLAUSIBLE, not CLOSED** — two explicit SF/substrate numbers (V_surf/kT, ρ_Sea/ρ_spine) now decide it,
replacing the uncomputed assertion. This is exactly the RESTATE-with-fix the panel asked for.
