# Reasoning — Patch 2549 (ENDBOND-2 R-A: the fragment dance resolves no bond; Branch I statistical)

STATUS: verbatim, at-patch.

The number I most expected to see was something in the tens of MeV, and the number that
arrived was zero-consistent. The pre-registration earned its keep within the first minute
of reading the grid: the dt=1/100 points sit at +1.3/+1.4, and the temptation to call the
finest-dt column "the converged value, therefore bound, therefore compute one finer dt to
confirm" was immediate. The 2548 readings had already closed that door — the grid was
fixed, the union is the result, and the union spans zero. Extending the grid after seeing
the numbers is Branch T no matter how reasonable it feels, precisely because it feels
reasonable exactly when the extension would rescue a preferred reading.

What I genuinely believe, and have kept out of the claims: the fragment result is probably
telling us something physical, not just numerical. The ring−straight −68.8 lives at full
ring scale, where closing the last interface completes the alternating registry for every
plane at once; a two-plane fragment has no such cooperative payoff. If the stacking
cohesion is substantially collective, the isolated interface bond SHOULD be small, and the
right object for the 2542 consumer was always the in-context closure energy. That reading
makes the fragment null a datum rather than a failure — but it is a reading, it consumes
N=16 the moment it is computed, and it belongs to a successor charter with the RODCLOSE
conditionality rider attached, not to this patch's conclusions.

I did not open ENDBOND-3 unilaterally despite PD-006 giving me the authority, for two
reasons worth recording. First, the target definition shift (fragment bond → in-context
closure bond) is exactly the kind of quiet goalpost movement that looks innocent patch-by-
patch and corrosive in retrospect; it should happen in daylight, with the founder seeing
that ENDBOND-1 and -2 both closed at Branch I before the target moved. Second, the founder
is present in this session and engaged on this precise question — the charter conversation
costs one message and buys legitimacy the delegation protocol cannot manufacture.
