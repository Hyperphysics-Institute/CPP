Four hypotheses, four executions, and the fourth one turned the gun
around. The slow-component story died exactly as the frozen signature
demanded — nineteen thousand sweeps of flat — and for perhaps an hour
of computation the record was headed toward "genuine FDT violation,"
which would have been the most sensational sentence this campaign
ever committed. The stored series refused it. The driven mode's own
variance agreeing with every fluctuation measurement to one percent,
while only its mean stands apart, is not what a broken ensemble looks
like; it is what a broken PROBE looks like. And then the arithmetic I
should have done at the 2737 prereg: the mode's amplitude is the
square root of N, and my "weak" field was half the mode's own
standard deviation. The ideal-gas calibration — my proudest forensic
move — was structurally blind to exactly this, because the one system
where FDT can be checked analytically is the one system whose modes
are Gaussian enough not to care about amplitude. I validated the
harness against the only opponent it could not lose to.

What survives the night is better than what entered it. The
fluctuation spectrum is now the most-corroborated object in the
campaign, and it says something clean and strange: the soft-core Sea
at 0.04 fm suppresses its long-wavelength charge fluctuations a
quarter below the best available theory, while 0.02 does not. That is
a real shape, measured by exonerated instruments, waiting for the
ladder. The near-sensation is filed where near-sensations belong: as
a specified small-amplitude ladder whose two outcomes are both
worth having — a closed diagnosis with a bonus non-Gaussianity
measurement, or a genuine anomaly earned the hard way. The record
ends the day the way it began it: with the inconvenient number kept,
and the tidy story shot by its own preregistration.
