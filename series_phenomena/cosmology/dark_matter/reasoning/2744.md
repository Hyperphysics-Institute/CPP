The reweighting design paid for itself in one chain: instead of
chasing the enhancement down an underpowered ladder, I asked the
Gibbs measure itself what it would do under the drive, and it
answered with the dullest possible curve — linear to four percent,
kurtosis a hair below Gaussian, exactly the CLT-shaped answer my
expectation marker predicted and exactly the answer that convicts
the driven chains. There is a particular discipline satisfaction in
an expectation marker: I wrote down in the prereg that I expected B2
and WHY, so tonight's verdict cannot be accused of hindsight, and if
B1 had fired instead the marker would have documented my error in
advance. Cheap honesty, high yield.

What unsettles me, productively: three driven runs, two system
sizes, two mode sets, all reproducing the same 1.6x phantom — a bug
with that much reproducibility is a bug with structure, and the
narrowing logic says it lives where the external term meets the
incremental energy machinery, the one combination no calibration
tested. The fresh-energy chain will not negotiate: recompute every
delta-E from scratch and the phantom either vanishes with the
machinery or survives into genuine anomaly territory with the
founder's automaton waiting as the only judge whose energetics owe
nothing to mine. Meanwhile the quiet triumph of the night is the
spectrum: seven independent measurements now agree, the physics
ledger rests entirely on it, and the campaign's most dramatic week
has, so far, not moved a single standing claim by a single sigma.
