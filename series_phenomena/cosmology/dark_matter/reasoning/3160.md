# Reasoning fragment — Patch 3160 (Route C completion, defect correction, disposition)

**Registered Patch 3160 (K-MEM Route C).** Full reasoning:

## The KeyError and why deletion was the correct repair

The frozen analysis aborted on `KeyError: 'beta_f'` at line 40. Three
candidate repairs existed and the choice among them is not cosmetic, because
one of them would have been a retune.

*Candidate A — add `beta_f` to `pilot_report.json`.* Rejected. The pilot report
is a pre-launch artifact; writing a new key into it after the evidentiary legs
exist is post-launch modification of a committed input, whatever the value.

*Candidate B — re-source `beta_f` from `calibration.json`.* Rejected, and the
reason is instructive: a single scalar β cannot be correct here at all. β is
per-arm (0.10 at the four isolation arms, 0.60 at AK). Any scalar assignment
would either be unused (harmless but pointless) or, if wired into the band,
would silently apply one arm's drive to every arm — the exact failure mode that
would corrupt the S3-C band. Candidate B invites a defect.

*Candidate C — delete the line.* Adopted. The decisive observation is that
`beta_f` is assigned and **never read**. `grep -n beta` returns exactly three
further hits, all of them the per-arm `beta` unpacked from the module-level
`ARMS` tuple, and only one of those enters arithmetic: `band = SUST_REF0 * beta
/ 0.10` at line 63. The band is therefore already correctly per-arm sourced.
Deleting an assignment whose target is never read is provably
behavior-preserving — the strongest available guarantee that the frozen §4–§5
estimator is untouched.

The `3054 → 3055` diff confirms the provenance as a v2-rewrite regression: the
predecessor's flat `ARMS = [(tag, role, n, x)]` carried a single global β
context; the v2 rewrite moved β into the per-arm tuple and orphaned the
pilot-report fetch. This is dead code left behind by a schema change, not a
missing input.

## Why the ordering matters and is disclosed

The repair was diagnosed and applied before any statistic had been computed or
seen. That ordering is not incidental — it is what makes the repair
uncontaminated. Had the analysis run, printed DISP-I3, and only then been found
to carry a defect, any subsequent edit would be indistinguishable from tuning
toward a preferred disposition, and the honest course would have been to report
the failure and stop. The corpus has recorded elicitation-order problems before
(CONV-019 §5, the post-fire ruling) and the lesson taken there was that the
ordering must be stated rather than assumed benign. It is stated here.

## The pre-emption, and the temptation it creates

The frozen tree is evaluated in order. Item 1 fired (all five arms fail S3-C),
so the disposition is DISP-I3 INSTRUMENT. But item 2 — *S1-C SIGNIFICANT in ANY
valid Δ ≠ 0 arm → DISP-T: THE FALSIFIER FIRES* — had a live trigger sitting in
a2 (Δ = +6, S1-C SIGNIFICANT at 5.551e-03 against scale 2.849e-04). Item 1
pre-empted it by position alone.

This must be said out loud: **the instrument verdict is the only thing standing
between the corpus and a fired falsifier against Candidate B requirement 7.**
The worker notes the structural hazard — an instrument verdict that happens to
protect a favoured candidate is exactly the verdict a worker should be most
suspicious of returning, and exactly the one a panel should scrutinize hardest.
The worker's protection against its own bias here is that it made no choice: the
order was frozen in Patch 3055, before any leg ran, and item 1's trigger is
not marginal (five of five arms fail, one by a factor of 100).

The genuine interpretive question is whether the §4 minority clause — *a failing
INFERENTIAL arm is PROSPECTIVELY NON-INTERPRETABLE and drops from inference* —
strips a2 of "valid" status and thereby removes item 2's trigger entirely, or
whether it does not, in which case the falsifier evidence survives the
instrument verdict and awaits a clean re-read. The clause was drafted for a
*minority* of failing arms; all five failed. The worker declines to extend a
minority clause to a unanimity by construction, and sends the question to the
panel as Q3 rather than resolving it in the direction that happens to favour the
corpus.

## The P-κ error, owned

In first reading the output aloud to the founder the worker stated that AK
satisfied P-κ. It does not. The condition is κ_sys^{U99} < 1; AK returns
κ_sys[BOUND] = 0.9928 with 99% CI upper bound **1.0059**. The point estimate
sits below 1 and the bound does not, and the frozen condition is on the bound.
The worker read the point estimate and spoke past the condition. Corrected in
the record before dispatch, and surfaced to the panel as its own question rather
than buried — a 0.6% overshoot on a bound is precisely the kind of margin a
worker is tempted to round in its favour.

## On the S3-C universality

Five arms spanning x_half 16–32, T_END 104–504, and β 0.10–0.60 all undershoot
their bands, none overshoot, none land inside, with shortfall ratios from 0.84
down to 0.0098. A genuine absence of sustained response would be expected to
scatter about the band, not to fail one-directionally across every
configuration. The worker's hypothesis is the fit window — `t_post = t_step +
1.5·x_half + 6`, baseline `max(12, min(48, (T_END − t_post)//3))` — whose
arithmetic already produced one pre-launch defect corrected at v2.1 (Patch
3057), caught by an executable gate rather than by reading, in the same family
as the v1 exit-time error. A second defect in the same arithmetic is a live
possibility.

The competing hypothesis, which the worker rates no lower, is that the band
itself does not transplant: `SUST_REF0 = 2.6e-3` is a Route B reference carried
into Route C geometry and scaled only by β. Nothing in this campaign tested that
transplant. If the band is simply wrong for Route C, then a0 (0.84 of band-low,
a 16% miss) and possibly a0p were never failing arms at all, and the whole
DISP-I3 trigger is an artifact of a mis-sited band.

Neither hypothesis is adopted. Both are named so the panel can attack them, and
neither is acted on: **no retune after launch.** A repair chosen after seeing
which repair would produce which disposition is not a repair.
