# Reasoning fragment — Patch 2989: the ratification enacted — and the small verbatim decision inside it

**"approve AP=2" registered as written.** The tempting shortcut was to quote the founder as
saying "approve AP-2" — cleaner, obviously what he meant. But the verbatim discipline exists
precisely for the cases where interpretation feels safe: the record shows what was said, the
interpretation note shows what was read and why (keyboard adjacency; AP-2 was the only pending
item; the request being answered named it alone). If the reading is ever questioned, the
evidence for it is in the record, not in a silent correction.

**Where AP-2 lives in the registry.** AP-1 amended a row (A1 → A1′); AP-3 became an A3′
definitional clause. AP-2's proposal header says it "amends the DI-bit definitional layer under
A1′/A3′" — so it goes INTO the A1′ row as definitional content, not as a new row and not as a
free-floating note. Axiom count stays 9, which is the amendment-accounting invariant all three
proposals were drafted to preserve. The audit trail written into the row includes the corrected
2986 tallies (4/4, not the originally recorded 5/5) — the registry should carry the corrected
history, not the flattering one.

**The glossary edit paid off the 2982 restraint.** Holding the DI-bit entry un-edited at 2982
(flag only) looked pedantic at the time — the founder had already ruled, why not fix half the
sentence? The answer arrives now: the consolidated edit carries the ruling, the falsifiers, the
reset-per-hop protocol, the pattern-level phase pointer, AND the audit provenance in one
coherent entry with one history note. A 2982 partial edit would have needed a second edit now
anyway, with two history notes to reconcile.

**Tier A closes.** R1 ratification → drafting (2977) → panel (2978/2979, corrected 2986) →
founder (2982 + 2989) → edits (2982 + 2989). Every gate in the 2962a ordering was honored,
including the one the panel added mid-process (audit-before on AP-2) — which turned out to be
the right call: the audit found a real demotion, not a rubber stamp. The process worked because
it was allowed to be slower than convenience wanted.
