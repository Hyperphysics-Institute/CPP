# Reasoning fragment — Patch 2868 (hold-force refinement; 2496 correction)

**STATUS: contemporaneous, captured at patch. Session of 2026-07-29.**

He asked whether he had addressed my two issues. One yes, one no, and
the measurement decided both against my expectations in opposite
directions.

The sign. I argued from standard induced-dipole physics that a lagging
cloud pulls backward, which is why polarization drag is drag, and I said
his flip was the load-bearing step I could not settle. It is settled.
F_hold is +4.688e-5, positive, along the motion. The model pushes the
CP forward during constant-velocity coasting. He was right and my
textbook reasoning did not apply to this substrate. I should note that I
reached for the standard result rather than the model's own numbers,
which is the same reflex that produced four gloss errors earlier today
in a different costume: reasoning from what I expected instead of
reading what was there.

The knife-edge. He addressed it by postulating exact recovery and
calling it a restatement of Newton I. That is honest but it is an
assumption, not a derivation, and the measurement now shows the bare
point is not merely unforced onto the line, it is measurably OFF it:
F_hold/v is about 9.4e-4 and positive, so A(v) exceeds v/mu and the
thing self-accelerates. My objection stands and got sharper.

The unexpected result is the correction to 2496. Its section 4 blames
the 2.9% residual on the integrator's time-staggering floor. Four times
finer in dt: unchanged. Twice as wide in sigma: unchanged. Exactly
linear in v across a factor of four. It is not a temporal artifact and
not a spatial one. The quoted ratio climbs to 6% at sigma 3.0 purely
because the denominator halves while the numerator sits still, which is
a reading trap in the original presentation.

So a shipped Tier-2 result's central compliance claim is wrong in its
own numbers, and it was findable by running the script that was already
in the repository with three parameter values. Nobody ran it. I nearly
did not either -- at 2867 I mooted this exact test on the grounds that
F_hold measures an external force, when F_self is the sea's
back-reaction on the CP, which is precisely the internal cloud force at
issue. That mooting was wrong and I am recording it as wrong.

What I am not doing: this measures a forward self-force on a bare point
in a scalar toy. It does not measure eps_mem, it does not touch the
ambient Sea, and the vector redo owed at 2496 section 7(b) is still
owed. Three routes to k = 1 have now appeared in five turns and this is
not a fourth. It is a correction to an inertia result that happens to
confirm the founder's sign.
