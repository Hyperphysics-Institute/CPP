## S1 wrote the answer into the requirement

The 1B specification said: derive the bound at O(v/c) "or, if the
first-order term cancels by symmetry, at O(v²/c²)." That clause reads
like defensive drafting. It is not — for a field-mediated charge
system the first order DOES cancel, by gauge structure, and the
leading correction is the Darwin term. S1 either knew and left the
door open, or wrote a careful hedge that happened to be exactly right.
Either way the requirement contained its own resolution.

## Why I am hedging harder than the physics might require

Twice now I have converted a control parameter into a value and been
caught. The Darwin result is textbook and I am confident in it AS
ELECTRODYNAMICS. What I cannot assert is that CPP's relay reproduces
the transverse sector at the right order — and that is precisely the
gap that would restore an O(v/c) term if it failed. Emergent Coulomb
tests the scalar sector and says nothing about the transverse one.
So the note states the conditional twice, declines to close 1B, and
names the missing test. Third time is where a pattern becomes a
character, and I would rather under-claim than complete that pattern.

## The founder described the cancellation before it was needed

His fore/aft arc argument of 2026-07-25 — establishment ahead,
discharge behind, cancelling at constant velocity — IS the physical
content of "no first-order longitudinal effect." He produced it to
answer a drag objection, not a memory one, and it turns out to be the
same statement. That is the second time this week his physics has
answered a question posed later in different language, and it is
mild evidence that the picture is more coherent than any single
derivation of ours.

## The measurement worth making

Item 1 — measure the arc field around a uniformly moving charge on
the automaton and compare to Biot-Savart — is the same apparatus that
gave emergent Coulomb, with a moving source instead of a static one.
It would test C23 directly, supply C_2, and close the last conditional
in 1B. If anything in this programme deserves the next block of
compute, it is that.
