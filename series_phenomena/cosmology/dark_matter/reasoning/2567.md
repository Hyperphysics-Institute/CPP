# Reasoning — Patch 2567 (four reviews, one echo; the split that wasn't a split)

STATUS: verbatim, at-patch.

The duplicate paste had to be the consolidation's first line, not a footnote, because it
changes the arithmetic of everything after it. A 2-2 route split among four distinct
reviews is a different evidentiary object than a 3-2 split among five, and the Copilot
probation screen — which the dispatch was explicitly carrying — simply did not happen.
The temptation was to quietly assign the duplicate to Gemini (the thinking-trace
formatting points there) and score Copilot as non-responsive; that would be
manufacturing an attribution the paste channel doesn't support. The record states what
is observable, voids the screen without prejudice, and adds the cheap channel-echo fix
so this class of defect dies here.

The substantive finding is that the R1 ordering split dissolves under one level of
analysis. The O3-first seats and the O1-first seats are giving the same physics answer —
no capture without a sink — and disagreeing only about which honest partial to run first.
Once O3 is re-scoped to a bound and O1 is stripped to its conservative limb, the two
camps' recommendations are literally compatible, and ChatGPT's O1a/O1b decomposition is
the frame that makes the compatibility visible. I want to flag how close I came to
missing this: my own recommendation on record was "O3 first, O1 second," and reading the
reviews as a vote would have confirmed it 2-2 and left me picking by tiebreak. Reading
them as physics instead produced a synthesis better than any single seat's answer —
which is the only outcome that justifies the dispatch's cost, and a data point for the
review-economy rule working as designed: this panel fired on a genuine impasse and
earned its keep.

One line I adopted verbatim into the fence inventory because I could not improve it:
"inertia licenses memory and acceleration, not arbitrary dissipation." That sentence is
the entire K1b discipline in eleven words, and it will stop a tired future session from
writing -gamma*V and calling it SF-6.

The capability-census rule (Grok's observation) I adopted without defensiveness, noting
in the document itself that my own 2564 gate under-anticipated. The gate WORKED — it
fired, nothing false entered the record — but it caught the second defect only through
an unplanned diagnostic, and "the safety net held" is a lower standard than "the net was
where I aimed it." The rule closes that gap for every future prereg.
