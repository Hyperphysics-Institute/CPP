## The founder's mechanism (verbatim sense)

Two velocity-qualified rods push aside their eDP coats on the apposing faces; the 8qDP cores appose at 90°;
**neither core breaks**; a **single eDP is removed from each rod** at the contact; the qDP edges touch at **a
single hinge point with one strong qq edge bond** — like two hTetras bonded on apposed qq edges. The remaining
DPs and the rods' own rigidity **prevent bending into a full-face 8qCP bond**; the other 3 eDPs per rod stay
coated (or recoat fast). The perpendicular rods are then **free to hinge on the qq edge bond and prevented from
tipping into face-to-face bonding.** No glueball: the collision is **below the transverse-plane fracture
energy** (the established energy wall), so the cores never reorganize. The two rods are bonded by an **edge bond
only.**

## Why this corrects 1830 (I had a category error)

1830 estimated the junction angular stiffness by tying it to the **bond depth** — modeling the junction as a
reorganized **knot** and concluding κ_θ ≥ E_ee, hence a "tense" floor ~1.0–1.5. **That was wrong.** For a single
qq **edge** bond, rotating about the edge axis (the hinge motion) **does not stretch the bond.** So the bond
depth (E_qq) sets only the **stretch** stiffness — which is exactly what holds the junction together and forbids
fragmentation — while the **hinge (dihedral) stiffness is decoupled** and set by *secondary* coat/steric effects
(≲ E_ee). 1830 conflated the two stiffnesses; the edge-hinge separates them cleanly. This is the *mechanism-level
realization* of the stretch-stiff + angular-flexible split I asserted in the 1828 panel fold — now grounded, not
assumed.

## Three consequences, all favorable relative to 1830

1. **Hinge stability (face-bond is unreachable).** A one-point edge hinge folds by *rotating about the edge* →
   the rods go coplanar/side-by-side, **not stacked face-to-face.** The full 8qCP face bond (the rigid, deep
   well) needs a *translation* to stack the lengths, which a single-point hinge plus rigid rods cannot supply.
   So the face-bond is **geometrically inaccessible from the hinge** — there is no hinge→rigid conversion
   channel, and the hinge is stable over the collision history. Thomas's "prevented from tipping into
   face-to-face" is geometric, not just energetic.

2. **Self-limiting SECURED (runaway branch closed).** A free hinge **guarantees** the inertial decoupling the
   self-limiting needs: a collision on one arm is backed by that arm's own (shorter, N/2) inertia → v_thr ≈ 4050
   km/s **rises** → fusion stalls. The rigid-junction runaway (whole-X backing, 2N → v_thr falls) required a
   rigidity the edge-hinge does not have. So the worry from 1828/1830 that a rigid junction would run away is
   **removed by the mechanism** — the hinge cannot be that rigid junction.

3. **Cluster floor → ~0.8–1.0.** The kinematic dihedral range is **wide** (90° down to steric ~w/L), so
   orientation-averaging is substantial *if* the hinge is soft. The floor is set by the **residual dihedral
   stiffness** κ_θ: soft (κ_θ ≪ E_ee) → drop ~1/8 → floor ~0.4; mild bias (κ_θ ~ 0.4 MeV) → drop ~1/4 → floor
   ~0.8; strong bias (κ_θ ~ E_ee, pinned near 90°) → drop ~1/2 → floor ~1.6. **Floor ≲ 0.8 as long as κ_θ ≲
   0.4–0.5 MeV (sub-E_ee)** — plausible for a geometric hinge whose restoring torque is secondary coat/steric,
   with one of four eDPs already stripped at the contact.

## Honest residual (what is still not pinned)

The floor still rides on the **magnitude** of the residual dihedral stiffness — the restoring torque of a
*locally-stripped* qq edge-hinge from the remaining coat (3 eDPs) plus steric body-body interaction as the
dihedral swings. The edge-hinge guarantees this is **decoupled from E_qq** and therefore small, but whether it
is comfortably sub-E_ee (floor ~0.8) or near E_ee (floor ~1.0, mild tension) is the remaining number. It is now,
however, a **much more specific and tractable target** than 1830's 4-arm-knot energy: the dihedral restoring
torque of a single qq edge bond with a partially stripped coat — a one-junction geometry/energy calculation, not
a strong-sector knot field problem.
