# Reasoning fragment — Patch 2840 (a test I designed defeated a derivation I filed)

**STATUS: verbatim, captured at patch. Session of 2026-07-27.**

## The shape of the temptation, and why I did not take it

Yesterday I filed the Darwin argument: first order cancels, the bar
relaxes from 0.15 to ~0.3, 1B gets much easier. Tonight the test I
wrote to validate it returned TRANSVERSE-FAIL — and the data is
visibly bad. A lag exceeding full retardation is unphysical; the
scatter spans 0.04 to 1.05 rad; the two velocities err in opposite
directions. Every one of those facts is a lever for saying "the test
was too noisy to decide, so the derivation stands."

That move is exactly the failure the frozen-table discipline exists to
block. I wrote rows 2 and 3 to be reachable precisely so that only-row-1
could not fire, and then row 2 fired. Using the measurement's noise to
discard its verdict while retaining the conclusion the measurement was
built to check would be indistinguishable from never having run it.
So the verdict is enacted, the second-order bound is withdrawn, and 1B
is harder tonight than it was this morning.

## What I actually think happened

The estimator is probably wrong rather than the physics. A source that
teleports one whole GP per Moment is not a uniformly moving charge; it
is a hopping charge, and its field carries the hop. Sampling a single
GP along a lattice axis compounds that. The honest reading is that
tonight's design could not have measured the effect cleanly at any
outcome, which is a design failure of mine and a third one in this
family after 2820's power defect and 2822's naive-error gate.

But "the design was bad" is a claim about the next test, not a licence
to keep yesterday's conclusion. Both statements are in the record, in
that order, separated so they cannot be conflated.

## The state I am handing forward

1B is now: derive C_mem at first order (the cancellation is NOT
established for CPP), bound ambient v/c below ~0.15, and — if anyone
wants the second-order relaxation back — design a transverse test that
can actually resolve it: sub-GP source motion, neighbourhood
averaging, many directions, background subtracted, small v/c. That is
a real experiment, not a patch to tonight's.
