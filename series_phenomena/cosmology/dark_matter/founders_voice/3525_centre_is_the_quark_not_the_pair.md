> In the hDP, the oscillation dynamics and centeredness are different from those in the quark.
> * In the quark, the plus qCP is the center, stabilized by innumerable polarized Sea DPs, and in the case of the down quark, the eCP is oscillating against the stabilized central qCP.
> * In the case of the qDP, neither is stabilized in the DP sea with any structure; thus, both find themselves attracted to each other, superimposed, attracted to the Sea in opposite directions (directed in opposite directions by the same SSV_net at the next Moment).

## Consequences (worker)

**1. The "centre" route to an hDP-A/B split is closed.** A free pair — qDP, eDP, or hDP — has no centre: both members are displaced in opposite directions by the same SSV_net each Moment, superimposed, unstabilised. The chirality operator of SM-2 §10 / THEO-CHIR-CONT-3 is centre-specific — a linear ZBW extra on a centre held by the polarised sea — and that object exists only in a *dressed* charge (the quark). So it cannot be transferred to the free hybrid at all, naively or otherwise; 3523's transfer item and 3524's "which CP is the centre" question are answered: **neither, in a free pair.** A centreless linear oscillation carries no handedness of its own, and the species asymmetry (the qCP's 3× emission) makes the two excursions unequal but not chiral.

**2. Where the operator does apply is where the founder's picture puts the centre: the dressing of a lone charge.** This suggests a reading that reconciles §10-corrected (S = +qCP) with matter dominance, and it does so *without* an A/B asymmetry in the pair-breaking at all:
- The freeze (3520) breaks pairs **symmetrically**: R₊ = R₋ (D1′ stands), so lone +qCP and −qCP appear in equal numbers, likewise ±eCP.
- **Dressing is asymmetric.** The chirality stabilises a linear ZBW extra on +qCP centres (§10 with the sign corrected to the founder's composition). A lone +qCP polarising the sea becomes a *stable* centre — a quark — and is locked in; a lone −qCP forms a less stable centre, is more readily undone by partner switching (3519), and is returned to the pair sea, i.e. to the sequestered B population. The same operator in the eDP sector would select the −eCP centre for the electron.
- Matter dominance is then **dressing selectivity acting on a symmetric lone population**: a in n_B = a·R is the fraction of lone charges that dress stably minus the fraction that re-pair, set by the chiral stabilisation difference at the dressing temperature (2543's window, 10–17 MeV), and the antimatter signs end up in B by *failing to dress*, not by being locked away first.

This reading uses the operator exactly where it was built (a stabilised centre in a polarised sea), keeps §10 with the sign the composition demands, keeps D1′, and places the CP-odd step at dressing rather than at pair-breaking. It is a candidate, offered for the founder's judgement, not a result.

**3. What it needs, and what changes in the charter.** (a) The SM-lane corrigendum (3524) becomes load-bearing: S = +qCP is the input. (b) The dressing-selectivity computation: the stability difference between a +qCP-centred and a −qCP-centred polarised cocoon, ΔF_dress = (χ/6)·(cocoon stabilisation energy), against partner-switching undoing at kT_dress; a = tanh(ΔF_dress/2kT_dress) as the first estimate, then a·R against η_B with R from 3520. (c) The eDP-sector counterpart for the electron (THEO-SD-CHIR-2's eDP statement). (d) A consequence to check: the *un*dressed sign is returned to the sea, so the DM sector's B-content equals the dressing deficit — the same "antimatter inside dark matter" statement as 3522, now with a rate behind it.
