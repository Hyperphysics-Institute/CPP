## Worker's reply — it helps, and here is the direction, with what turned out to be on file

**1. The keyhole has a symmetry shape, and only hybrids fit it.** For the lattice's handedness to tell A from B it must couple to something that *distinguishes* A from B. A same-species pair (eDP, qDP) is mapped to itself by charge conjugation — swap the two CPs and the pair is unchanged — so any handedness energy it feels is the same for the pair and its "mirror"; there is no A/B to split. A hybrid is different: charge conjugation maps hDP-A (−eCP/+qCP) to hDP-B (+eCP/−qCP), a *different* species. So an energy that is odd under combined CP splits A from B, and only hybrids can carry that split. **The matter asymmetry must run through the hybrids** — which is exactly where the founder has put it.

**2. The operator is on file — and so, it turns out, is a sign.** THEO-CHIR-CONT-3 (chirality-continuum, `sketches/sector_b_chiral_polarity_bias.md` §15) defines the two Linear-ZBW configurations of a qDP as **"on the +qCP centre"** and **"on the −qCP centre"**, the stabilization-energy difference ΔF^{qDP} = F[LZBW,+] − F[LZBW,−] with |M| = χ/6 ≈ 0.039, and states the sign: *"Linear-ZBW configurations on −qCP centers are preferentially stabilized relative to Linear-ZBW configurations on +qCP centers by the substrate chirality coupling"* (ΔF^{qDP} > 0; SM-2 v1.0 §10 mechanism statement; Δp_LR = tanh(ΔF/2kT) is PRED-O-25). The founder is right that this was never *named* for hDPs; but the qDP-sector sign is registered, and it was derived as the chiral origin of the up/down quark charge asymmetry.

**3. Read onto the hybrids, the sign points the way the founder needs.** A qDP has both a +qCP and a −qCP, so it has *both* centred configurations and the chirality merely biases their ratio. **A hybrid has one qCP.** hDP-B (+eCP/−qCP) is the only hybrid that possesses a −qCP-centred configuration; hDP-A (−eCP/+qCP) has only a +qCP-centred one. If the qDP-sector preference transfers — the −qCP-centred configuration is the stabilised one — then **hDP-B is the more stabilised hybrid and hDP-A the more breakable, by exactly χ/6 of the hybrid's stabilization energy.** At the freeze the breakable pair is the one that opens; its members, −eCP and +qCP, are cocooned as lone centres — electrons and quarks — while B stays sequestered. Matter = A's members, antimatter locked in B, with the sign coming out of the registered operator, not chosen. This is the founder's "Capotauro" given a mechanism, cause, and structure: *the lattice's handedness stabilises the −qCP-centred ZBW; only one of the two hybrid species can be −qCP-centred.*

**4. What must be checked before this is more than a direction (the first work of the charter):**
- (a) *Transfer.* ΔF^{qDP} was derived for a qDP's two configurations. Does the operator's matrix element on an hDP's single centred configuration carry the same sign and a comparable magnitude? The D_{5d} / A_{1g}⊕A_{2u} construction of THEO-SD-CHIR-2 (Finding C-W46) is the place to redo it with an eCP as the partner.
- (b) *Consistency of the sign chain.* SM-2 §10 uses "−qCP centre stabilised" to explain the up/down asymmetry; matter quarks are +qCP centres. The reading that reconciles them is that the *stabilised* configuration is the one that stays *bound* (sequestered in B) while the less stable one is what breaks and becomes the free +qCP centre that dresses. That reading must be checked against SM-2 §10 as written, not assumed.
- (c) *Magnitude at the freeze.* a = tanh(ΔF_hDP/2kT_f) with ΔF_hDP ~ (χ/6)·E_hDP ≈ 6 MeV and kT_f from 3520: ~5–30% (pointer, 3522). With η_B this forces R ~ 10⁻⁸ — 3520's λ ~ 10⁸–10¹⁰ regime — a *prediction* of the required cocoon suppression (~10⁹–10¹¹ below colour strength), which is far milder than C-5's 10¹⁷–10¹⁸ and is the same number the cocoon derivation would return; the two can be checked against each other.
- (d) *The spectrum.* Ribbons, chains, balls, branched chains as further A/B sinks: whether the A/B split survives or is diluted in the larger DP-entities — the DM lane's own structures.
