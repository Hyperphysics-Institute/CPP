## The end of the Planck era, and what drives the expansion after it

The problem is what happens when all the SCPs are depleted — when we are at Planck spacing and nothing is superimposed any longer, because every CP has a GP of its own and there is room for everyone. The question is what happens to the outward expansion at that point.

The answer is that pluses are now closest to pluses and minuses closest to minuses. There may be plus–minus pairs that are close, bonded, and inclined to stay together, but each is also surrounded by like charges, and the net SSV felt at each of those points is repulsive. Every CP next to a like-charge CP sits in a place of extreme repulsion, while its attraction is to its partner. The DPs will be repelled, each half driven away from the like charge beside it and drawn toward the other half of the pair — and that may become the new DP. The question is which pull is stronger, the attraction to the bonded partner or the attraction toward the opposite charge that is repelling the like charge next to it; the result will be either a partner-switching response or a mutual bonding.

In the case of the qCPs, a qCP next to a qCP will probably attach as a third, oriented on one side or the other of the opposite charge: a plus–minus–plus or minus–plus–minus, held in place. That is a trio — essentially a radical, available to attract an opposite-charge qCP or eCP as a fourth component. So there is not really a problem with a third qCP or eCP landing on a dipole: it is simply added and held at the appropriate distance. If a collision comes along violent enough, it knocks the third off; the third is then free for a while, and may form its own DPs around it as an isolated eCP or qCP. The third member of a bonded DP is not as strongly bound, because of its proximity to the like charge on the far side of the opposite charge in the middle.

So the repulsion of like charges is the driver of the expansion of the universe in this initial phase, right after the end of the Planck era. The Planck era is the era in which everything is massively superimposed, with all the jumping and the expansion outward into the unoccupied space at the edge of the superimposed, high-SCP-concentration ball; going from superimposed to every location being unique is what we are calling the end of the Planck era. After that edge, everything has its own location, its own GP. SCPs are no longer the rule but the rarity — GPs are still multiply occupied whenever a plus and minus qCP or eCP oscillate together in ZBW oscillation, but not beyond two.

## The odd man out, the cocoon, and annihilation

After the Planck era we have the repulsion, and we have the occasional odd man out: the third that was on a DP and got knocked off and separated far enough that it is cocooned — the naked qCP or naked eCP is cocooned by dipoles polarised around it. It does not let itself bond with anything, because it is too cocooned, too isolated from any other naked plus or minus qCP by the boundary of polarised DPs surrounding it.

When two matter–antimatter particles do come together, however, they annihilate — the naked charges can meet their opposites because the outer layer of each cloud is the opposite charge to the other's. Take a quark: the outer layer of a matter up quark is plus, and the outer layer of the antiquark is minus. The two layers of DPs simply combine and organise themselves into a photon, which is an organised form of the dipole sea propagating its effect to the other qCPs and eCPs in the environment and travelling at one Planck-sphere radius per Moment — hence the speed of light. Exactly how two spherically polarised regions of DPs organise into a photon involves some complex asymmetry; I suspect there is a relative velocity between the two, and that the relative velocity determines the direction of the kinetic energy, which is the direction the photon maintains after it forms. That is the matter–antimatter photon-formation piece.

## Depletion of the isolated CPs, and the dressing of quarks

The depletion of the isolated CPs runs through quark formation: the isolated CPs form into quarks, quarks attract each other, and they form on top of an hTetra — the understructure and substance of a proton or neutron, depending on whether the combination is up-up-down or up-down-down.

The issue is whether the isolated charge is fully depleted by its interactions, and the answer is no. These isolated charges are sufficiently separated — possibly by having become part of a trio, a qDP plus a qCP — that they are here and there; when hit, they are knocked off, because they are not as well bonded as the qDP's two members are to each other. The qDP remains, and the third becomes an independent naked qCP which, given enough eDPs and qDPs in its environment, polarises them into a cloud and so forms a quark or antiquark, with all the complex forms of the quarks — strange, with its tetrahedral cage, the hTetra cage, probably formed from qDPs bonding around it. The hTetras are not tetrahedra until they have a naked qCP at the centre to form onto; on their own they are unstable, repulsive, open, flat entities — an HDP-A and an HDP-B. Each pair of HDPs would form an hTetra, but it is just a flat entity until it has a qCP to put in its centre. It puts a down quark in the centre of the tetrahedron, and a down quark is just an up quark with a captured oscillating eCP.

So the depletion scenario at the edge of the Planck era is that we pass into what might be called the repulsive era: a repulsive push between all the like charges, and during that push an expansion. That is when the DP formation starts to become stable, when the trios form, and when the knocking-off of one member of a trio makes it a centre for the nucleation of the quarks, which then precipitate onto the hTetra and, by their bonding with it, become a proton or a neutron. The species that bonds on the hTetra determines its family and type: onto an icosahedron it would be charm, onto a dodecahedron a bottom, onto the 30-vertex figure a top, each with its own stability. There is a certain decay potential between the species, from their stability and their interactions with the environment — collisions and partner-swapping with dipole particles that cause the edges of, say, an icosahedron to decay out and turn into a tetrahedron, or collapse into a dipole or a series of smaller particles, quarks, and neutrinos, which would be the spinning dipoles. Lepton conservation and baryon conservation come from the bookkeeping of the qCPs and eCPs originally present, each forming its own entity as a centre — unless a plus qCP and a minus eCP come together, in which case there is a hybrid, its own kind of entity.

As to whether the reaction of the separate naked eCPs and qCPs goes to completion, turning everything into eDPs and qDPs: that is not likely, because of the sequestration of the qCPs into their own DP entities, and because the eCPs are more on their own. The violence of the thermal collisions is enough to keep most of them from being long-lived, chained, or agglomerated entities. There is too much collision energy in the thermal mix for the eDPs to form enduring entities comparable to the DP entities, which are quark-based, Q-based.

## Registrable content (worker's reduction, checked against the text; short quotations below are from the raw transcript and may differ in wording from the rendering above)

1. **What carries the dilution after the count law's end — answered in mechanism, not in rate.** When every CP has its own GP and superposition has ended ("what we're calling the end of the Planck era"), *"the repulsion of like charges is going to be the driver of expansion of the universe in this initial kind of phase"* — the **repulsive era**: each CP beside a like charge sits in extreme repulsion while attracted to its partner; the net push disperses the ball outward. **This selects 3512's branch (i): something else carries the expansion past n̄ = 1.** The founder gives no rate; the Moments-per-e-fold at the freeze, which 3512 showed decides p, remains open.

2. **A third CP on a DP does not spoil the pair — it is captured.** *"There isn't really going to be a problem with a third QCP or ECP landing on a dipole, it will just simply be added to and held at the appropriate distance"* — a **trio** (+−+ or −+−), *"basically a radical"*, the third bond weaker because of the like charge across the middle; violent collisions knock the third off. **Kernel ruling for the framework:** exactly-a-pair (K1 of 3512) is the wrong reading; a pair forms in the presence of a third (K2 direction), *and the third is bound, not free*.

3. **The naked-qCP residue is produced by knock-off, not by never finding a partner.** *"The occasional odd man out, the third man that was on the DP that got knocked off and separated far enough"*; the isolated charges are *"sufficiently separated, possibly by becoming part of a trio, QDP plus another QCP … when they get hit they get knocked off because they're not as well bonded"*. The **freed third** is then cocooned by polarized DPs and nucleates a quark. Hedged ("possibly") but it is the only source of naked qCPs the founder names. **This refines 3510's "residue frozen at the dilution cutoff": the leftover inventory at the cutoff is sequestered into trios, and the residue that dresses into quarks is the knock-off yield in the repulsive/thermal era.**

4. **The cocoon is what keeps a quark bare (3510 Q4, mechanised):** a naked qCP polarises the surrounding eDPs/qDPs into a cloud that isolates it from other naked charges. **Annihilation still occurs (confirms 3511):** quark and antiquark meet because their *outer* layers are opposite; the two polarised clouds combine and self-organise into a photon propagating one Planck-sphere radius per Moment; the founder suspects the relative velocity fixes its direction. (Flag 3511-ii, the late photon channel, now has a mechanism sketch.)

5. **Pairing does not go to completion.** *"Whether they go to completion and turn into all EDPs and QDPs, that's not going to be a likely situation"* — because of sequestration into DP-entities and the violence of thermal collisions; eDPs do not form enduring entities comparable to the Q-based ones. **This answers 3512's branch (ii) negatively in the founder's picture: the pool is not used up.**

6. **Dressing sequence and conservation (context, step (d)):** hTetras are flat and unstable until a naked qCP is placed at the centre (a down = up + captured oscillating eCP); species by cage (icosahedron → charm, dodecahedron → bottom, 30-vertex → top); lepton and baryon conservation as the bookkeeping of the original CPs; a +qCP with a −eCP is a hybrid entity.
