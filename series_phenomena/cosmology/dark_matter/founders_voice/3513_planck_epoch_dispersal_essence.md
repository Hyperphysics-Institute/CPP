# The end of the Planck era and the birth of the lone charge — the essence of the founder's 12 Sep 2026 walk-and-talk

**Patch 3515, DM block, from this window under PD-006.** This is an interpretation of `3513_planck_epoch_dispersal.md` (edited rendering; raw retained), written at the founder's request. It states what the picture *is*, in the order the physics runs, and then what it *implies* for the open work. Where the text below goes beyond what the founder said, it says so.

---

## 1. The picture in one paragraph

Inflation, in CPP, is the era in which Conscious Points are massively superimposed and jump outward into empty lattice. It ends the moment there is room for everyone: each CP on its own Grid Point, no stacks left. But the end of superposition is not the end of expansion. Once every charge has its own address, the arrangement is a crowd of like charges pressing on each other — pluses beside pluses, minuses beside minuses — and the net field each CP feels is repulsion. That repulsion is the new driver. It pushes the ball outward through what the founder calls the *repulsive era*, and during that push the sea organises: opposite charges settle into dipoles, spare charges hitch onto dipoles as loosely bound thirds, collisions shake the thirds loose, and each loose charge wraps itself in a cocoon of polarised dipoles. Those cocooned lone charges are the seeds of quarks. The rest of matter formation — quarks onto hTetra cages, protons and neutrons, the heavy flavours, annihilation into photons — follows from what the sea does with those seeds.

## 2. The stages, as the founder runs them

**(i) Superposition ends.** The Planck era is defined by superposition, not by a temperature: it ends when every CP can have a GP of its own. After that, a multiply occupied GP is the exception — two CPs in ZBW oscillation as a dipole — and never a stack.

**(ii) The repulsive era begins.** With like charges adjacent, each CP is in "a place of extreme repulsion" from its neighbours and in attraction to its own partner. The question the founder poses, without settling it, is which wins: the bond to the existing partner, or the pull toward the opposite charge that is repelling one's like-charge neighbour. Either partner-switching or mutual bonding results; either way the like-charge repulsion, summed over the ball, is an outward push, and that push is the expansion of this era.

**(iii) Dipoles and trios.** Where a qCP finds itself beside a qCP, it does not compete with the pair; it attaches as a third, on one side of the opposite charge — plus-minus-plus or minus-plus-minus — and is held there. The trio is "basically a radical": open to a fourth. The third is bound less strongly than the pair, because it sits within reach of the like charge on the far side. So a third landing on a dipole is not a problem for the dipole; it is a feature of the sea.

**(iv) Knock-off.** Thermal collisions violent enough to break the weak third bond do so. The third is then free for a while — the "odd man out." The dipole survives; the third does not rejoin it.

**(v) The cocoon.** A free lone charge polarises the eDPs and qDPs around it into a shell. That shell isolates it: it can no longer reach another lone charge of opposite sign to bond with, because both are wrapped. This is the answer to why a quark stays bare — not because nothing wants to pair with it, but because the sea itself has cocooned it. *(This mechanises the founder's earlier Q4 answer at 3510.)*

**(vi) Annihilation is still possible, and makes photons.** A cocooned quark and a cocooned antiquark can meet, because the *outer* layer of each cloud is the opposite charge to the other's. The two shells merge and self-organise into a photon — an organised form of the dipole sea, propagating one Planck-sphere radius per Moment. How two spherically polarised regions become a directed wave is left open; the founder's suspicion is that the pair's relative velocity fixes the photon's direction.

**(vii) Dressing.** The lone qCP is the centre onto which an hTetra collapses; the hTetra is an unstable flat pair of HDPs until it has a centre. A down quark is an up quark with a captured oscillating eCP. Which cage the lone charge lands in fixes the flavour — tetrahedron, icosahedron (charm), dodecahedron (bottom), the 30-vertex figure (top) — and each cage's stability sets its decay. Lepton and baryon conservation are the bookkeeping of the original lone eCPs and qCPs, each keeping its identity as a centre.

**(viii) It does not go to completion.** The lone charges are never all paired off, for two reasons: the qCPs are sequestered into DP-entities, and the thermal violence keeps the eDPs from forming anything enduring. The sea keeps a population of lone charges.

## 3. What is new against the record, and what it changes

Three things move.

**The expansion has a second driver.** The count-law engine (H ∝ μ = kT ln n̄) drives dilution *while* there is superposition, and stops when there is none. The repulsive era is what dilutes the lattice *after* that. 3512 showed that the exponent p the C-5 candidate depends on is fixed by how fast dilution runs at that hand-over; the founder has now named the mechanism, and the rate is the one number still missing.

**The residue is made by knock-off, not by failure to pair.** Earlier (3510) the unpaired inventory was "frozen at the dilution cutoff." In this picture, the leftover charges at the cutoff are not left over at all — they are absorbed into trios — and the lone charges that become quarks are produced later, by collisions breaking thirds off. The residue is a *yield* of the repulsive/thermal era, not a *remainder* of the Planck era. Any computation of p must now count trios and their breaking, not partnerless hops.

**A third on a dipole is captured, not repelled.** The pairing kernel used at 3512, which treated a third qCP as spoiling the pair, is the wrong reading. The right one has the pair form regardless, with the third bound.

## 4. What the picture leaves open (the founder's own hedges, and the worker's questions)

- *The rate.* Nothing here says how many Moments the repulsive era takes to thin the crowding by a factor e, nor whether the interior of the ball thins at all or only its surface. p — and therefore C-5 — turns on this.
- *Partner-switching versus mutual bonding* in stage (ii) is posed, not answered.
- *Trio formation:* whether trios form at the moment superposition ends (from what happens to land together) or during the repulsive era (from what drifts together), and how the knock-off rate depends on local composition. Both enter p. The founder's "possibly by becoming part of a trio" is a hedge and is recorded as one.
- *Photon formation* from two merged shells is a mechanism sketch, with the direction-from-relative-velocity idea explicitly a suspicion.
- *Cross-lane:* the like-charge push named as the driver is the same stored self-energy that the EU lane found cancelled pairwise by the dipole sea's binding (3805; reheat_budget_recut). Whether the repulsive era's push survives that cancellation — perhaps because it acts on the *unpaired* fraction and not on the bound pairs — is a question for the EU window, not answered here. *(Worker's framing; the founder did not raise it.)*

## 5. The one-sentence version

When superposition ends, like charges pushing apart take over the expansion; in that push the sea pairs into dipoles, spare charges ride along as loosely bound thirds, collisions shake them loose, and each loose charge cocoons itself in polarised dipoles — and those cocooned lone charges, never fully paired away, are the seeds of every quark.
