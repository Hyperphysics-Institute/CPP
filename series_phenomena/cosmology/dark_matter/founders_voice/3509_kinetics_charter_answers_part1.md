## Follow-up — does the DI-bit signal bias where a hopping CP lands?
> Yes, as you said, the qSCP (q-dominant SCP) emits a signal that biases the response of the qCPs in the other qSCPs to migrate toward each other and bond as qDPs, and seed the formation of the qDP-entity family. The qSCPs bias the formation of qCPs because of their greater affinity (both stronger than eCP-eCP attraction/SSV_net displacement, and universally attracted to other qCPs within other qSCPs, regardless of polarity). The qSCPs-to-qSCP attraction biases qCPs toward bonding as free qCPs into qDPs. Their transient residence in a qSCP merely makes the opportunity to bond externally more rapid; every Moment is an opportunity, rather than a metered, sequestered release. Their transient location does not diminish their differential effect on qDP formation or on elongation/aggregation.

## Registrable content (worker's reduction, checked against the text)
1. **An SCP has no cohesion.** Every CP on a stacked GP hops every Moment; an SCP at the next Moment is whichever CPs landed together. Recapture is a landing event, not a capture by a bound object.
2. **The landing is affinity-biased.** A q-dominant SCP's per-GP emission (AP-4) biases the migration of qCPs in other SCPs toward it; the bias is the strong channel — universal among qCPs regardless of polarity and stronger than the e–e displacement. Affinity therefore enters step (a) of the chain through the landing probability. **3902's Q–Q/E–E asymmetry and f_E/f_Q = α_s/α are consistent with this; the ε = 2.70 anchor of the 3936 p-band stands.**
3. **Release is not metered.** Every Moment is a bonding opportunity; transient residence accelerates rather than throttles external bonding and does not dilute the differential. The chain is rate-limited by encounter and affinity, not by release.
