The audit item said: recount the census, two unit electric dipoles
per 4-CP unit, jointly with the reopened d_s question. The "jointly"
turned out to be the substantive content. The factor itself looked
trivial — two dipoles per site instead of one, φ₁ = 2 — until I asked
what "per site" means in a Sea whose site spacing is the very unknown
the next derivation must produce. If d is the inter-unit spacing, the
census factor is 2. If d is the inter-DP spacing — the literal
wording of the 3076 re-anchoring — then at fixed DP density the units
sit at 2^{1/3}·d and the factor is 2^{−1/3} ≈ 0.79. Same physics,
opposite directions, purely a change of variable. Left ambiguous,
this is precisely the kind of convention seam through which a silent
factor of 2.5 slips into an assembly that has sworn to multiply its
factors once. So the note's first real act is a declaration: the
canonical variable is the inter-UNIT spacing, because the unit is the
ruled object and the relic that dilutes, and D-DS-RELIC is bound to
produce that variable or convert explicitly.

The second decision that needed naming rather than assuming: the
receiving unit's sibling dipole. It sits at the unit extent ξ, and a

excluded it, and the justification is not convenience: the sibling's

double-counts binding as vacuum energy across the A-1/A-2 boundary.
R-CC-DIST-CONT independently says the engine is the inter-species
distance that dilution increases; the intra-unit separation does not
dilute. And the reductio: an included sibling ties ρ_Λ to the binding
scale, contradicting every registered scaling of the arc. The
boundary statement went into the note in binding form because the
factorization discipline only works if no quantity can appear on both
sides of it.
