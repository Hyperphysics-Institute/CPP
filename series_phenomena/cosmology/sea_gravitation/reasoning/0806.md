# Reasoning — Patch 0806 (DM-2 Step 2(a): ZBW bound / gradient-control)

STATUS: verbatim, at-patch (Opus, Session 156, 8 June 2026).

This is the kill-risk Step 1 flagged, so I went in expecting it might be the wall and tried to make it fail honestly. It did not fail, and the reason is cleaner than the weak-field story I told in Step 1.

First the bound, grounded in c08's own facts rather than my assumptions: k*Delta|SSV| = GM/rc^2 exactly, bounded to [0,1] by CP exclusion. At the ZBW (Compton) scale r = hbar/mc this is exactly (m/m_P)^2. So every sub-Planck constituent — all matter, all DM candidates, GeV-scale and below — is weak-field by >= 35 orders. The only thing that is O(1) is the Planck-scale Sea ground state itself. That O(1) was the worry.

The resolution is that the O(1) ground-state amplitude is not dangerous, because gravitation is gradient-controlled, not amplitude-controlled. I checked this symbolically instead of asserting it: expanding F around an O(1) background u0 with a slow fluctuation gives F_lin = -2 k^2 q^2 u0^2 sin(qx)/(1+k u0)^3. The source carries q^2 — the gradient squared — and the background u0 sits only in the bounded factor 2(k u0)^2/(1+k u0)^3, which maxes at 1/4 at the ceiling k u0 = 1. So the nonlinear term, even at the CP-exclusion ceiling, is a bounded number times the gradient. OP1 is a strong-AMPLITUDE statement (does the full nonlinear F reproduce Einstein at large field); but a uniform ground state has small GRADIENT regardless of amplitude, so the amplitude-nonlinearity is never exercised. This is just curvature ~ d^2(metric), not |SSV|_abs, made explicit in the F structure.

So condition (a) passes, and it retires the kill-risk I had honestly left open: the O(1) value occurs only for the uniform ground state, exactly where gradient-control neutralizes it. The large-gradient sources are the sub-Planck localized excesses, weak-field by 1e-39, where the truncation and the parity argument hold with enormous margin. D2 does not bottom out on OP1.

I want to be careful not to overclaim. What is NOT yet closed is condition (b): whether the discrete Planck-scale zero-point has a net parity-broken gradient that survives coarse-graining. Gradient-control says a uniform ground state is harmless, but the zero-point is not perfectly uniform at the lattice scale; its non-uniform part has O(1) amplitude AND nonzero gradient. The saving structure is (i) lattice discreteness (fixed scaffold, founders L33 — no sub-l_P propagating mode) and (ii) the parity cancellation of Step 1. Whether those fully kill the net coarse-grained gradient is condition (b), and it is exactly the residual<->Lambda question, so I handed it to 0807 rather than hand-waving it here. That is the honest seam: (a) closed by gradient-control; (b) open, local, and not OP1.
