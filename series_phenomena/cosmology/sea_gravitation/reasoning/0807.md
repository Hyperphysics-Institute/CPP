Two things Thomas asked me to "run": the residual<->Lambda identification, and (folded into it) his hypothesis that this is the same single-sign question as chirality emergent-vs-primitive. I did both and kept them honestly separated.

The residual<->Lambda mechanism. Step 1 said symmetric zero-mean fluctuations cancel by parity. The natural question is what breaks the parity, and the finite causal Sea answers it: parity cancellation needs each fluctuation's antisymmetric partner to be present in the averaging region. Sub-horizon modes have many periods in the patch, partners present, exact cancellation; the horizon-scale mode has its partner beyond R_H, so cancellation is incomplete and a residual survives. I tested this numerically rather than asserting it: window-averaging the cubic source over [0,R], integer-period modes give 0 to machine precision, and the horizon mode q=pi/R gives <s>*R^2 = const, i.e. residual ~ 1/R^2 — which is exactly the 5b Lambda scaling rho_Lambda ~ c^4/(8 pi G R_H^2). So Step-1's "what survives parity" and 5b's "largest gradient the finite Sea can't cancel" are the same physics, and the IR boundary is *why* R_H enters. That felt like a real result, so I was deliberate about its grade: I claim the scaling and the mechanism, NOT the coefficient or the Hubble-vs-event-horizon choice (those are 5b/D3, untouched). And I flagged the genuine gaps — 1-D single-mode demo, needs the 3-D mode sum and an all-modes cancellation proof. The handover's warning about promoting elegant identifications to proofs is exactly on point here, so it is filed as conjecture-with-mechanism.

The chirality link. Thomas thinks the residual sign is the chirality emergent-vs-primitive sign. So there is a shared substrate-parity root, and I wrote out the two branches (symmetric substrate -> chirality emergent AND Lambda purely a geometric IR residual; handed substrate -> chirality primitive AND an intrinsic Sea skew). The not-the-same part: the chirality verdict moves on the mu^2-sign via the eta-susceptibility, bottomed out at the H-NESS gap in the other window; the DM-2 residual moves on the ZBW skew via parity of <F>. Different sectors, different observables, different gaps. DM-2 Step 2(b) will not resolve the chirality sign and vice versa.
