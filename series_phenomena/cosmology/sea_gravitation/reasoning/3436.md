# Reasoning — Patch 3436 (rate extrapolation, after the Picture-B ruling)

I asked the fork deliberately as a binary because I could not settle
it from the corpus and did not want to settle it by preference. The
founder ruled B and gave the mechanism rather than the label, which is
the part that determines the instrument: entities evaporate CPs, CPs
bind into DPs, DPs bond into new entities. That is a cycle with
throughput, and the important structural consequence is that the cycle
runs at zero drive too — with zero net. The net is what expansion
produces, and the net is the lag. So "not in equilibrium" here does
not mean "chaotic" or "unmeasurable"; it means the steady state
carries a drive-dependent offset, which is a well-behaved thing to
measure.

I want to record that I checked this against the corpus rather than
accepting it because the founder said it, since the lane's own
calibration note warns about proposing without reading. It is
consistent with 3401 — the Le Chatelier resolution already
established the net formation rate as a restoring response tracking
expansion rather than an independent constant, and Picture B is that
statement viewed from the measurement side. It also fits 3419's
structural finding better than I expected: entity count nearly
invariant across 64× in temperature while binding within entities
moves 35% is what a robust population with internal throughput looks
like. Three independent places in the corpus point the same way. If
they had not, I would have said so and pushed back rather than
amending a budget I froze one patch ago.

The instrument consequence is that F-C1 was not merely too strict, it
was pointed the wrong way. A guard requiring rate-independence would
have failed a correctly functioning engine, and the failure would have
looked like an INVALID verdict rather than like a wrong guard. That is
the worst class of error in this lane — a frozen rule that discards a
true result — and it is worth noting that freezing the rules early did
not protect against it. What protected against it was asking a physics
question before running, and getting an answer that contradicted the
rule. The lesson I would carry forward is that freezing rules early is
necessary but not sufficient; the rules have to be frozen against a
physical picture, and if the picture is unsettled the question goes to
the founder before the runs, not after.

On the extrapolation direction, which I initially had backwards in my
own head. My first reaction to "never in equilibrium" was that the
instrument was in trouble, because the physical rate is ~59 orders
below anything simulable and no amount of slowing down closes that
gap. But we are not trying to reach the physical rate. We are trying
to reach the intercept, and the physical rate is close enough to zero
that the intercept IS the physical answer to a precision nothing else
in this budget can match. Extrapolating downward is also the safe
direction for linear response, which improves as drive weakens. The
real hazard is the reverse one — the whole accessible ladder sitting
above the linear regime — and that is a testable condition rather than
an assumption, which is why F-C1' checks curvature explicitly instead
of the design assuming linearity.

On the ladder choice. The result that surprised me is that the
intercept inflation factor for K=4 spanning 8× is 0.860, below unity:
four rates extrapolated to zero give a BETTER number than one rate
measured directly, at 3× the runs. I had expected extrapolation to
cost precision and was prepared to argue for spending it. It does not,
because the four points buy more than the extrapolation lever costs.
The binding constraint turned out to be wall-clock rather than run
count, and specifically the slowest rung — duration scales as L/rdot,
so a 27× span spends 19.5× wall-clock to save runs. That is why I
rejected the wider span despite its better inflation factor. Anyone
re-optimising this later should optimise wall-clock, not runs.

On F-C3, which I had to amend rather than keep. The old null mode
(freeze expansion, reproduce the fixed-box result) no longer describes
anything, because at zero rate there is no trajectory and no slope to
compare. The replacement is better anyway: check that the cycle shows
zero net at zero drive. That is the founder's mechanism at its own
fixed point, and an engine that shows a net at zero drive has a defect
that would present exactly as dark energy — which makes it the single
most important thing to test before believing any intercept this
instrument returns.
