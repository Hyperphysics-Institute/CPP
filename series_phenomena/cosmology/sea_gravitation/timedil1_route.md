## §1 — The proposal (founder, 26 Aug, summarised from verbatim in the conversation record)

Distant supernovae sit in denser ancient Sea (higher m̄, higher
SSV_abs). Higher SSV_abs → slower local passage of time → fewer
photons emitted per Moment. The explosion takes more Moments to
release the same photon budget, so the distant candle is dimmer per
unit received time — proposed as the origin of the excess dimming now
attributed to accelerated expansion. Photon frequency/energy is
unchanged (prior lane reasoning); only the emission *rate* is
affected.

## §2 — The refutation argument (worker, 26 Aug)

**(a) Slowed emission is a light-curve stretch, and stretch is
measured.** Spreading a fixed photon budget over more time widens the
light curve beyond the kinematic (1+z). The standard luminosity
distance already contains both powers of (1+z) — energy redshift and
arrival-rate dilation — so the acceleration signal is dimming *beyond*
those, and this route must supply it as *additional* stretch.
Magnitude required: ~0.25 mag excess at z ≈ 0.5 → flux ratio 0.79 →
extra stretch ×1.26 → combined exponent
(1.5)^b = 1.5 × 1.26 → **b ≈ 1.57**.
Measured (DES, 1504 SNe Ia, 0.1 ≲ z ≲ 1.2, 2024):
**b = 1.003 ± 0.005 (stat) ± 0.010 (sys)**.
The route's required exponent is excluded at roughly **50σ**.

**(b) The dichotomy that closes the escape.** Either the slowing is
*differential* (physical rates in the past ran observably slower
relative to comoving clocks) — excluded by (a) — or it is *uniform*
(all local processes, including local clocks, slow together), in
which case it is unobservable by construction, produces **no**
differential dimming, and explains nothing. There is no third
branch: a slowing that dims must stretch, and stretch is measured to
1%.

**(c) Monotonicity.** Independently of (a): the route dims
monotonically with distance, but the Hubble diagram turns over —
SNe beyond z ≈ 1 are *brighter* than the low-z dimming trend
extrapolates (the decelerating epoch preceding acceleration). No
monotonic mechanism reproduces a turnover. This same argument closed
grey dust (route's postulate 2, confirmed eliminated per the
founder's request), and it applies here with equal force.

## §3 — What survives regardless

The **mass/number split** stands on its own: Λ carried by number
concentration (constant), SSV_abs carried by mass per entity
(falling). Nothing in §2 touches it. What §2 excludes is only the use
of the SSV gradient to explain the *supernova acceleration signal*.

**Noted, not chartered:** an SSV-gradient account of the cosmological
*redshift itself* would — like gravitational redshift — automatically
carry matching clock dilation and thus predict b = 1, consistent with
the DES measurement. But it would then leave no residual to explain
acceleration, so it is a candidate account of redshift, not of dark
energy, and it belongs to a different route if ever opened.

## §5 — Triangulation record (compiled at closure, at the founder's request)

Independent probes of "ancient clocks ran slower," by class:

**5.1 Duration comparisons (is ancient physics slower?).**
- SN Ia light-curve widths: b = 1.003 ± 0.005 (stat) ± 0.010 (sys),
  1504 SNe, 0.1 ≲ z ≲ 1.2 (DES, 2024). No slowing beyond (1+z).
- SN Ia spectral aging: aging rate ∝ 1/(1+z) from multi-epoch spectra
  (Blondin et al. 2008) — independent systematics, same answer.
- Caveat, on record: GRB durations show weak/scattered dilation and
  quasar variability studies have often returned null (a standing
  interpretational puzzle; hierarchical reanalyses recover a QSO
  signal). SNe are the clean clock; QSO/GRB intrinsic clocks are not
  standardised.

**5.2 Gearing comparisons (is ancient physics *ratioed* differently?).**
Dimensionless constants are immune to uniform time rescaling, so they
probe differential (species-dependent) slowing:
- α: Δα/α = 1.3 ± 1.3 ± 0.4 ppm at z = 1.15 (ESPRESSO, HE 0515−4414);
  weighted mean −0.5 ± 0.5 ± 0.4 ppm over 28 clean absorbers,
  z = 0.6–2.4 (Murphy et al. 2022).
- α, geological: |Δα/α| < 7×10⁻⁸ at z ≈ 0.15 (Oklo); < 3×10⁻⁷ over
  4–5 Gyr (meteoritic ¹⁸⁷Re).
- μ = m_p/m_e: |Δμ/μ| < 1.8×10⁻⁶ at ~half the current age of the
  universe (NH₃, B0218+357).
- No credible surviving detection; earlier α-drift claims traced to
  wavelength calibration and line-structure systematics.

**5.3 Atomic-vs-dynamical time.** The only handle on a *uniform*
slowing: compare atomic clocks against gravitational orbits over
history. Ġ/G bounds from lunar laser ranging and binary pulsars
(~10⁻¹³/yr) would register any sector-dependent SSV coupling as an
apparent drifting G.

**5.4 The surviving corner, stated precisely.** Everything beyond
(1+z) is excluded. The (1+z) itself is interpretation-underdetermined:
"photon stretched in flight" and "emitting-epoch clocks slower by
exactly (1+z)" give identical redshift AND identical b = 1 stretch
(as with gravitational redshift). If CPP ever asserts the second
reading, the gearing nulls impose: **SSV_abs must couple to the PSR
universally — every process slowed by the same factor — to ~10⁻⁶
across 13 Gyr.** Universal coupling derived from the mechanism would
be a point in the theory's favor; any predicted species dependence is
falsified by α(z) at ppm. This is a derivation target, not a route,
and is not chartered here.
