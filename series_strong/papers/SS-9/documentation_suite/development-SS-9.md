## Vignette 1 — OPEN-SS-24 handover authored — closure as highest-leverage strong-sector open problem (26 April 2026, Session 1)

OPEN-SS-24 was registered in SS-7 v1.0 (20 April 2026) as the first-principles derivation of assumption C4 (alpha clusters in bound strict-N=Z nuclei arrange as vertices of simplicial convex 3-polytopes). C4 is the structural hypothesis underlying both SS-7's $(3N_\alpha - 6) B_\text{pair}$ edge formula and SS-8's $(2E/V) B_\text{pair}$ interstitial scaling. Session 1 produced the OPEN-SS-24 handover document framing closure as the highest-leverage open problem in the strong-sector series: 54 of 55 conditional D-N predictions in the cumulative swarm tally promote from conditional to unconditional if C4 closes from programme-level axioms (A1–A11).

Three physical intuitions registered in SS-7 §2.1 were inherited as candidate components of the derivation: (1) triangular faces from rigid-tetrahedral base-to-base contact, (2) maximal contact reinforcement from thermodynamic selection, (3) convexity from rigid-packing constraints. Session 1 deliberately did not attempt the derivation itself; it scoped the problem and identified the inheritance structure for Session 2. The Session 1 handover document (`session_logs/OPEN-SS-24_handover.md`) is preserved as historical bootstrap; it is pre-§4-discipline genre and will not be updated.

## Vignette 2 — Steinitz pivot, conditional-C4 closure scaffold v0.2, deltahedra-gap insight (26 April 2026, Session 2)

Session 2 opened with a bootup-protocol failure (assistant attempted `web_fetch` rather than `git clone`, producing a partial view of the repo state and a long retracted analysis); the recovery was clean and the methodological lesson was captured in the session log preface. The substantive work pivoted from "direct programme-level closure of C4 from A1–A11" to "C4 as a conditional theorem from C1+C2+C3+C5+C6," where C5 (ground-state energy minimization) and C6 (cluster surface-realization, no interior alphas) were new paper-level hypotheses surfaced in the session and registered as OPEN-SS-29 and OPEN-SS-30 candidates respectively. The pivot followed SS-8's Level-1/2/3 methodology: deliver Level-1 (algebraic) and Level-2 (functional) closure under stated paper-level hypotheses; Level-3 (physical-principle) closure deferred to follow-up open problems.

The session's substantive insight was the **Steinitz pivot**: the cleanest formal hammer for C4 closure runs through Steinitz's theorem (1922) — a graph $G$ is the 1-skeleton of a convex 3-polytope iff $G$ is simple, planar, and 3-vertex-connected — combined with the planar-graph edge bound from Euler's formula (giving $E \leq 3V - 6$ with equality iff every face is a triangle, i.e., the polytope is simplicial). The pivot is non-trivial because SS-7 §2.1's prose treatment frames the question as physical reasoning about rigid-tetrahedral geometry, but the formal hammer is graph-theoretic. Lemma A (pairwise triangular contact under C1+C2) and Lemma C (energy minimization picks max edges under C3+C5) were delivered clean; Lemma B (rigid-packing → planar 3-connected contact graph) was delivered with two registered argumentative gaps (forward direction: supporting-hyperplane argument at the shared face $F_{ij}$ needs sharpening; reverse direction: implicit C5 dependency needs explicit acknowledgment).

The second substantive insight was the **deltahedra-gap clarification**: the Freudenthal–van der Waerden enumeration of convex deltahedra has exactly eight members at $V \in \{4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 12\}$, with no convex deltahedron at $V = 11$ or $V \in \{13, 14, \ldots\}$. SS-7 Table 1 successfully predicts ${}^{44}$Ti, ${}^{52}$Fe, ${}^{56}$Ni at residuals of $-0.26\%, -0.57\%, -0.73\%$ at exactly the deltahedra-gap $N_\alpha$ values. This means the binding formula does not actually require deltahedral realization (the stronger condition); it requires only graph-simpliciality (the weaker condition: maximal planar 3-connected contact graph). The deltahedra-gap was registered as OPEN-SS-31 candidate with three resolution options. Session 2 closed with a parallel Template-B section codifying the Session-Log-as-Handover-Backbone Discipline (operating_system.md §4) and the §15/§4 reconciliation discipline (operating_system.md §15 reconciliation subsection).

## Vignette 3 — Off-track investigation: strict-C1 inconsistency surfaced, multi-faceted-rigidity reframing, refined C1 v1.3 patched (26 April 2026, Session 3)

Session 3 opened with the continuation handover from Session 2 directing immediate work on Lemma B gap closure. Before pushing on the supporting-hyperplane argument, a structural concern surfaced from cross-referencing the v0.2 working draft against the strict reading of SS-7's C1: at $N_\alpha \geq 7$ all deltahedra have vertices of degree $\geq 5$, but strict 4-face C1 cannot host a vertex of degree $\geq 5$ (no alpha can present 5 face-coincidence contacts on a 4-face tetrahedron). The v0.2 Theorem's claim of clean conditional closure at $N_\alpha \geq 7$ rested on a strict reading of C1 that is geometrically impossible at exactly those vertex counts. Closing Lemma B as the previous Opus framed it would have tightened a proof of a statement that was internally inconsistent.

Thomas's call: pivot to resolve the alpha-rigidity question off-track from the Lemma B gap-closure path, because the inconsistency is foundation-level (touching SS-5/SS-7/SS-8 inheritance, not just SS-9). The investigation followed five steps: (i) read SS-5 directly (finding that SS-5 v6 explicitly characterizes alpha rigidity as leading-order with ~5% LO band, not strict); (ii) map C1's load-bearing surface across downstream calculations (finding the K_3 mode value is graph-topological per SS-5 v6 §9, not face-shape-dependent — the asymmetric SS-2 nucleon's first-order shift vanishes by trace-free structure); (iii) read the SS-7 Table 1 residual fingerprint (computing effective excess contact count by regime and finding a clean Regime A ≈ 0, Regime B flat $+1.32$ plateau, icosahedron suppressed $+0.71$, Regime C variable pattern); (iv) synthesize the refined C1 with three structurally independent accommodation modes; (v) patch SS-7 §2.1 to v1.3.

The methodological pattern that produced the breakthrough was **Thomas's reframing of "side tracks" as facets**: what Opus had initially worried about as scope creep — the four candidate sub-questions (K_3 eigenvalue sensitivity, SS-5 → SS-7 inheritance, deltahedra-gap edge-length tolerance, SS-8 D2 inter-alpha coupling) — were not separate adjacent questions but the same alpha-rigidity question viewed through four different probes, each diagnostic in a different cluster regime. Under that reframing, "scope discipline" became "characterize the rigidity structure across all four diagnostic windows" — a single substantive question, not four loosely connected ones. The empirical record (SS-7 Table 1 residual pattern) became the fingerprint that selected which facet was doing what work where. The refined C1 patched into SS-7 v1.3 has three facets: (a) internal LO rigidity (load-bearing for $B_\alpha$ only, non-load-bearing for K_3 mode value); (b) vertex-hosting accommodation at degree $\geq 5$ cluster vertices (mechanism TBD; small per-vertex cost in LO band); (c) cluster-level collective oblate-deformation mode at belt/seam-supporting cluster shapes (provisional, OPEN-SS-32 candidate). The refined C1 dissolves the strict-4-face/degree-5 inconsistency: the Lemma B gap closure can now resume on a stable foundation.

## Vignette 4 — OPEN-SS-32 mechanism reading via slip-plane intuition; cluster-physics literature consilience; SS-8 H3' cross-paper structural identity (26 April 2026, Session 3)

The Regime B residual fingerprint ($+1.32 \cdot B_{\text{pair}}$ flat across $N_\alpha \in \{7, 8, 9, 10\}$ despite degree-5 vertex count varying from 2 to 8) initially defied Opus's per-vertex-cost reading. Thomas's slip-plane intuition supplied the correct mechanism: the excess is a *gain* the cluster captures because, at sufficient size, a qualitatively new bulk degree of freedom becomes accessible — a tectonic-plate-style adjustment, a slip plane, a configuration force that activates discontinuously when the cluster shape can support it and persists once activated. The flatness of the Regime B plateau (insensitive to degree-5 vertex count) and the icosahedron suppression at $+0.71 \cdot B_{\text{pair}}$ (full $I_h$ symmetry quenches the axial-symmetry-breakable mode) together selected the slip-plane reading over per-vertex-cost or DP-sea-screening alternatives.

Cluster-physics literature consilience confirms the reading at the SS-7 scale: ${}^{28}$Si (the smallest J-solid case at $N_\alpha = 7$) is "uniquely oblate shaped" with "pentagon shape interpretable as static one-dimensional density wave at the edge of the oblate state" (KanadaEn'yo 2011); ${}^{44}$Ti realizes ${}^{40}$Ca + α core+halo with parity-doublet bands; ${}^{56}$Ni shows alpha-gas behavior with multiplicity up to 7 in inelastic scattering (GANIL 2013); Tohsaki & Itagaki 2018 explicitly study α-cluster structures as polytope configurations on a hollow sphere and find that closed shapes (icosahedron, fullerene) are structurally privileged with "prominent hollow structure" relative to others. The slip-plane mechanism — cluster-level collective oblate-deformation mode — is in the literature already, by various technical names (oblate density wave at edge of cluster, parity-doublet bands). What CPP adds is *quantization*: the mode contributes approximately $+B_\text{pair} = +M_0/\varphi$ of additional binding when active, with a cluster-shape-dependent attenuation factor.

SS-8 cross-paper consilience is the structural payoff: SS-8 v1.0's H3' provisional opposite-polarity pair-bonus mechanism (operating at the interstitial scale with a $1/\varphi^2$ attenuation factor and OPEN-SS-28 as the forward-looking derivation target) is *structurally identical* to the slip-plane reading at the SS-7 cluster-shape scale — same $+B_\text{pair}$-attenuated form, same provisional-tier registration, same forward-looking derivation question. SS-8 line 278 makes the K_3 scale-recurrence framing explicit: $B_\text{pair}$ is "the fourth-scale occurrence" of the K_3-mode quantum (after SS-5 nucleon-pair, SS-5 A=4 closure bonus, SS-7 alpha-alpha edge K_3, SS-8 D2 interstitial-host K_3). The slip-plane reading at the SS-7 cluster-shape scale is the candidate fifth-scale instance, registered as OPEN-SS-32 and connected explicitly to OPEN-SS-28 via methodological parallel. Three forward-looking predictions (PRED-O-16, PRED-O-17, PRED-O-18 — single-cluster slip-plane extension, single-to-hierarchical regime transition, hierarchical slip-plane additivity) extend the reading to alpha-chain nuclei beyond SS-7's $N_\alpha = 14$ ceiling and are testable against AME 2020 data.
