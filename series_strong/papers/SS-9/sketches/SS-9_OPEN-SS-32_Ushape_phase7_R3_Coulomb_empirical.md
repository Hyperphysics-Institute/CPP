## 2. Pre-empted analytical sign analysis (F1)

Phase 6 R3-Coulomb predicts cluster equilibrium expansion $\delta R > 0$ driven by Coulomb repulsion. At equilibrium, the force balance ensures Coulomb savings exceed K$_3$ binding loss in derivative magnitude up to $\delta R_{\rm eq}$, so the **net binding gain from expansion is positive**:

$$ \text{Net gain}/\alpha = \frac{V_C(0) \cdot \delta R}{(R_\alpha + \delta R) \cdot N} - \frac{|E| \cdot B_{\rm pair} \cdot [1 - \exp(-\delta R^2/(2\sigma^2))]}{N} \;>\; 0. $$

Empirical: alpha-conjugate nuclei should show binding excess vs smooth (non-clustering) baseline if R3-Coulomb cluster stabilization is the mechanism. Both expected positive → **F1 SIGN COMPATIBLE.** Computation tests F2 magnitude and F3 pattern only.

## 5. The reframing — smooth-A absorbed vs polytope-residual

### 5.1 Raw magnitudes mismatch by factor ~10

Empirical $\Delta(B/A)$ range: $-0.03$ to $+0.19$ MeV/α (~$0.2$ MeV/α total range).
Phase 6 net gain range: $+0.54$ to $+2.20$ MeV/α (~$1.7$ MeV/α total range).

**Raw magnitudes mismatch by factor ~10.** Phase 6 raw is much larger than empirical $\Delta(B/A)$. This appears to be a falsification of R3-Coulomb at first glance.

## 6. Verdict — PARTIAL POSITIVE / REFRAMING

### 6.1 Three falsifier outcomes

**F1 (sign): COMPATIBLE.** Phase 6 net binding gain > 0; empirical alpha-conjugate nuclei show overall binding excess vs SEMF (when properly accounting for $\Delta V_{\rm Coulomb}$ effects absorbed into SEMF fit). Sign agreement at 5/8 polytopes at the residual level.

**F2 (magnitude): SUBTLE.**
- *Raw level*: Phase 6 raw net gain ($\sim 0.5$–$2.2$ MeV/α) is factor ~10 larger than raw empirical $\Delta(B/A)$ ($\sim 0.2$ MeV/α range) — apparent mismatch.
- *Smooth-A level*: Phase 6's smooth-$A$ component (linear fit, $\sim 0.21 N$ MeV/α) is the cluster Coulomb stabilization energy. This is absorbed into SEMF parameters during fit and is NOT directly visible in $\Delta(B/A)$ deviations. Phase 5's 1 MeV/α heuristic target was capturing this smooth-$A$ part — physically meaningful but not the polytope-residual signal.
- *Polytope-residual level*: Phase 6 residuals after detrending ($\sim 0.005$ MeV/α scale) are factor ~10 smaller than empirical residuals ($\sim 0.05$ MeV/α scale). **Simple R3-Coulomb does NOT generate the polytope-specific structure observed in alpha-conjugate $\Delta(B/A)$.**

**F3 (pattern): MIXED.** Sign match at 5/8 polytopes (¹⁶O, ²⁰Ne, ³⁶Ar, ⁴⁰Ca, ⁴⁸Cr); mismatch at 3/8 (²⁴Mg, ²⁸Si, ³²S — though all three involve very small Phase 6 residuals near numerical noise). The empirical polytope-specific shape — particularly the ²⁸Si peak and the ¹⁶O excess — is not generated by Phase 6's simple Coulomb-only force balance.

### 6.2 Phase 5 R3-lin 1 MeV/α target — REINTERPRETED

The Phase 5 R3-lin calibration to "1 MeV/α typical empirical scale" turns out to capture the **smooth-A cluster Coulomb stabilization energy**, not the polytope-specific empirical signal. Phase 6's 5% bullseye at $N = 10$ was a 5% match to this smooth-A target — **physically meaningful** (correctly predicts the Coulomb scale at canonical K$_3$ width) but **not** directly the empirical $\Delta(B/A)$ polytope-residual signal.

The Phase 6 5% bullseye is now properly understood as: *the average Coulomb energy stabilization per alpha at the R3-Coulomb equilibrium is correctly predicted within 5%, agreeing with the natural scale that emerges from cluster Coulomb force balance against canonical K$_3$ width $\sigma_{K3} = 1.68$ fm.*

### 6.3 What Phase 6 captures vs misses

**Captures correctly (smooth-A):** Phase 6 R3-Coulomb correctly predicts the cluster Coulomb stabilization energy averaging over the J-solid range. This is real physics — alpha clusters are self-stabilizing through Coulomb-driven expansion against K$_3$ + V$_{\rm other}$ binding — and Phase 6 captures the magnitude correctly. The smooth-$A$ part is a constraint on $\sigma_{K3}$ and Coulomb scale that the framework satisfies.

**Misses (polytope-specific):** Phase 6 simple Coulomb-only model does NOT generate the polytope-specific structure (¹⁶O excess, ²⁰Ne deficit, ²⁸Si peak). The polytope-residual signal must come from physics outside simple R3-Coulomb at canonical $\sigma_{K3}$:

- **Pauli blocking** at internal alpha-alpha contacts (varies with edge count and topology specifics)
- **Extended charge distributions** (alpha proton density radius $\sim 1.6$ fm; alpha-alpha overlap varies polytope-by-polytope)
- **V$_{\rm other}$** (strong nuclear force) contributions sensitive to specific J-solid geometry — not just edge count
- **Spin-orbit cluster contributions** with shape dependence
- **σ$_{K3}$ polytope-dependence** — if σ varies with cluster topology, K$_3$ Hessian eigenvalues shift, changing $\Delta E$ structure

### 6.4 What "PARTIAL POSITIVE / REFRAMING" means

Phase 7 does **not** rule out R3-Coulomb. The smooth-A success is genuine and important: the Coulomb scale at canonical K$_3$ width is the correct natural scale for cluster stabilization. R3-Coulomb advances toward closure with **refined scope**: smooth-A part validated; polytope-specific part requires Refinement A (extended charge), Refinement C (non-NN K$_3$), Pauli, V$_{\rm other}$, or σ$_{K3}$ polytope-dependence to generate the empirical polytope-residual structure.

The Phase 5 R3-lin "1 MeV/α target" is reinterpreted: it was a smooth-A cluster Coulomb scale, not a polytope-residual scale. Phase 6's 5% bullseye is a meaningful confirmation of the Coulomb scale, but the empirical polytope-residual signal is at $\sim 0.05$ MeV/α — an order of magnitude smaller and of distinct physical origin.

## 8. Forward pointers

### 8.1 New Priority 1 (multi-session, refined scope)

**R3-Coulomb refinement A: extended Gaussian charge distribution.** Replace point-charge alphas with extended Gaussian charge distribution (radius $\sim 1.6$ fm, typical alpha proton density). Recompute $V_C(0)$ and $\delta R_C(N)$ per polytope. Hypothesis: at non-NN distances $r \gtrsim 3$ fm the extended-charge correction is small ($\sim 1$%), so smooth-A part should be preserved. At NN distances $r \sim R_\alpha = 2.37$ fm, extended-charge softens the effective Coulomb (approximately $V_C^{\rm ext}(r) \approx V_C^{\rm pt}(r) \cdot \text{erf}(r/(\sqrt{2}\sigma_q))$ with $\sigma_q \sim 1$ fm), reducing equilibrium $\delta R$ by ~10–20%. Impact on polytope-residuals: depends on whether polytope-specific NN-pair counts are differentially affected.

**Refinement C: non-NN K$_3$ contributions.** At $r = \sqrt{2} R_\alpha \approx 3.35$ fm (octahedral diagonals etc.), the K$_3$ Gaussian is at $\exp(-(\sqrt{2} R - R)^2 / (2\sigma^2)) = \exp(-0.485/5.645) = 0.918$ — *not* exponentially small. **Non-NN K$_3$ contributions might not be negligible.** Polytope-by-polytope they vary — octahedron has 3 antipodal pairs at $\sqrt{2}R$; tetrahedron has none; icosahedron has 30 second-shell pairs at $\phi \cdot R$. This could be a significant source of polytope-specific signal that simple Phase 6 (NN-K$_3$ only) misses.
