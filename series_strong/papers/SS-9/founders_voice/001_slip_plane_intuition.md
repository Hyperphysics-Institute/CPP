## The structural insight, distilled

The slip-plane intuition has three parts that work together:

**(1) Sign correction.** The +1.3 MeV Regime B excess is a *gain*, not a cost. The cluster *underbinds* in the SS-7 formula, meaning the actual binding is *stronger* than the formula predicts. Therefore the excess is an unlocked degree of freedom that the leading-order calculation misses, not a strain cost paid for hosting degree-5 vertices. The physical picture is: a new force becomes accessible at sufficient cluster size, contributing additional binding rather than subtracting from it.

**(2) Bulk character, not local.** A per-vertex cost story cannot be flat across N_α = 7 → 10 (where the number of degree-5 vertices doubles from 2 to 8). A bulk-distortion story can: the distortion is a single qualitative event — a slip plane, a tectonic-plate adjustment, a seismic shift — and once it's there, the cluster can grow without re-activating it. Hence the flat plateau at +1.3 MeV across the four J-solid deltahedra. Once the cluster has the qualitative shape that supports the new force, growing the cluster doesn't activate any *more* of the same force; it activates *the same amount, once*.

**(3) Mode quenched at full closure.** The icosahedron at N_α = 12 shows a smaller excess (+0.7 MeV) because its full I_h symmetry forbids the symmetry-breakable axial-deformation pathway that the J-solid deltahedra access. The "slip plane" requires a belt-or-seam structure that lets the cluster shift collectively along an axis; the icosahedron has no such belt. Closure suppresses the mode.

## Connection to existing CPP framework

The slip-plane reading sharpens refined-C1 facet (c) from a vague "edge-length tolerance" into a specific structural claim: the third facet of alpha rigidity is a **cluster-level collective oblate-deformation mode** that activates at belt-or-seam-supporting cluster shapes. The cluster-physics literature has independently identified this structure under different names — oblate deformation, density wave at edge of cluster, parity-doublet bands (e.g., KanadaEn'yo 2011 on ²⁸Si pentagon shape; Tohsaki & Itagaki 2018 on hollow polytope shapes).

What CPP adds is *quantization*: the slip-plane mode contributes approximately +B_pair = +M_0/φ of additional binding when active. This is the K_3 quantum recurring at a fifth identified scale across the strong-sector papers (after SS-5 nucleon-pair, SS-5 A=4 closure bonus, SS-7 alpha-alpha edge K_3, SS-8 D2 interstitial-host K_3). Pattern 6 K_3 scale-recurrence operates at the cluster-shape scale via the slip-plane mechanism.

## The hierarchical-regime extension

Thomas's second articulation extends the slip-plane reading to higher N_α than SS-7's current Table 1 (N_α ≤ 14) covers. Three nested predictions:

**(P1) Single-cluster slip-plane extension.** For some range N_α ∈ [15, N_α^crit], each new alpha addition gains a slip-plane bonus *if* the resulting cluster shape supports belt/seam structure. Subject to: (a) the cluster doesn't grow so large that secondary structures prevent slip-plane formation; (b) slip-plane modes don't interfere destructively. Binding excess scales with the number of slip planes the cluster shape admits.

**(P2) Single-to-hierarchical regime transition.** At some critical N_α^crit (estimated 16 ≤ N_α^crit ≤ 25 from cluster-physics literature), the residual pattern shifts discontinuously. The cost of supporting one large cluster exceeds the cost of fragmenting into sub-clusters. Heavier nuclei form as hierarchical organizations of sub-clusters, each of which is itself an alpha-cluster polytope.

**(P3) Hierarchical slip-plane additivity.** In the hierarchical regime, total binding excess ≈ Σ_i (B_pair × number-of-slip-planes for sub-cluster i). Each sub-cluster contributes its own slip-plane bonus per refined-C1 facet (c).

## The deeper structural claim about Pattern 6

Thomas's hierarchical-regime prediction makes a programme-level claim: the K_3 scale-recurrence isn't just "the same scale recurs at every level." It's "the same scale recurs at every level *that can support the relevant collective mode*." The transition between regimes happens at the geometric size where a higher-order organization becomes energetically favored over continuing the lower-order one.

This is testable: comparing SS-7 single-cluster predictions to AME 2020 data at N_α ∈ [15, 25] should diagnose where the transition occurs and whether the hierarchical-additivity rule holds.
