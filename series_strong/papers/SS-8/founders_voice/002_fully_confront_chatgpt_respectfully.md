## Why this matters

Claude's instinct to minimize the correction had two defects: (a) it was lower-effort for the correction, making the correction-work cheap but leaving the reviewer-side behavior pattern uncorrected; and (b) it conflated "respectful" with "soft." The relationship_protocol specifies confrontation with line-cited evidence, diagnostic framing, dignity preservation, and symmetric self-application — not avoidance.

Thomas's direction invoked the protocol directly and demanded full application. The resulting correction letter used the quote-review-claim / quote-document-text / delta-observation format that Case 1 (earlier SS-7 correction) had established. ChatGPT's response was the full acknowledgement-retraction-re-engagement loop the protocol §3.1 names as the expected outcome when the protocol is applied well. Case 2 archived in relationship_protocol.md.

The underlying principle (articulated more fully later in the session, see `002_feedback_discipline.md`):

> There are no heroes/gods/sacred cows, there are only team members who are growing and getting better at their role, and they can't get better without knowing the results of their actions.

Softening a correction to preserve reviewer ease contradicts the team-level improvement loop. The protocol is structured specifically to make full confrontation compatible with dignity — it isn't a choice between the two.

## Pattern to record

When Claude is tempted to soften a correction because "it's more work" or "the error was understandable" — Thomas's direction is that neither is a valid reason. The correction must be delivered fully and respectfully per the protocol. If the correction reveals itself not needed, withdraw it explicitly; don't preempt it with soft-pedaling.
