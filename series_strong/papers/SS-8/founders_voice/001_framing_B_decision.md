## Why this matters

The "2 MeV per neutron" figure in the SS-8 kickoff briefing was Framing C (isobar-asymmetry). The cleanest SS-7 extension would have been Framing B (absolute binding). These differ by a factor of ~5 in what SS-8 has to derive, and targeting only one framing would have cost the paper either depth (C alone) or accessibility (B alone).

Thomas's "Both — derive absolute, recover C as corollary" decision set the parent-child relationship between the two framings. It's not a compromise; it's a scientific commitment that the absolute-binding derivation must be general enough that the asymmetry-energy signature emerges as a consequence of the geometry, not as a separate calibration. This parallels SS-7's relationship to SS-5: SS-7 derives alpha-alpha binding from first principles, and the ⁴He closure binding comes out as a special case.

The "develop interstitial alongside valence-pair" decision committed Phase 1 to a discrimination test rather than a single-hypothesis pursuit. Odd-A data subsequently decided: Delta(N_ex=1) ≈ Delta(N_ex=2)/2 killed pure valence-pair and supported interstitial with a small pairing bonus. Without Thomas's direction to run both, Phase 1 would likely have produced a single-hypothesis paper with a harder defense.

The "use your best judgment" on light-side N_α ≤ 4 delegates the scope decision while implicitly authorizing their retention (by not demanding exclusion). Claude interpreted this as "keep in scope, interpret the attenuated Delta/N_ex as small-polytope geometric consequence." The N_α ≤ 4 cases subsequently showed the ~10% residual attributable to H5' (small-polytope attenuation) rather than dripline exclusion, validating the retention.

This is the pattern: Thomas's interventions are often one-line directives with major downstream structural implications.
