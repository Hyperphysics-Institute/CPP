isn't. Once you write the one-loop relation with `β₀ = 7` (already an exact CPP theorem), `Λ_QCD`

the substrate." That reframing is the main content of step 0, and it changes how 1002 should be

## Why I did not just declare 5/(8φ) the UV input

The tempting shortcut: `α_s = 5/(8φ) ≈ 0.386` is the "lattice coupling," the lattice is at `l_P`,
so set `α_s(E_P) = 0.386` and run. I checked it: that puts the Landau pole at ~10¹⁸ GeV, nowhere
near 0.218 GeV. The resolution is that `5/(8φ)` is not a UV value at all — running it down to
`Λ = 0.218` with `β₀ = 7` lands it at `Q ≈ 2.2 GeV`, a charmonium scale where physical `α_s ≈ 0.39`.
So `5/(8φ)` is an **IR anchor**. Recognizing that (a) explains why the naive UV identification fails
and (b) turns the IR end into a *consistency check that already passes* rather than an open
question. This is the encouraging half of the report and is locked in by the verify script.

## Why Route B over Route A

Route A asks for a substrate reason that `α_s(E_P) ≈ 0.020`. The number 0.020 is suspicious: it is
what the *log* inversion demands, and there is an obvious risk of reverse-engineering it from the
target. The honest objection is that the log inversion itself is illegitimate near `l_P`, because
the 600-cell is discrete there — the continuum RGE cannot hold in the top decade. SS-1's own
summary table already flags "non-log running at Planck scale: lattice discreteness correction to
RGE." So the right move is Route B: don't seek a magic 0.020; compute the discreteness-corrected
flow from `PSR_eff → l_P/2` and see what `Λ_QCD` it yields. The lattice is the regulator — this is
CPP-native and dodges the reverse-engineering trap. A is kept as a secondary check, valid only if
0.020 falls out of the `l_P/2` geometry independently.
