## §1 Problem statement

Extend THEO-DSL-11 (face $\mathcal{O}(\delta^3)$ closure in $\sqrt 3\cdot\mathbb{Q}[\phi]/3$) to $\mathcal{O}(\delta^4)$ by computing the closed-form coefficients of the fourth-order substrate-current vector $\vec j_4^{\,\text{face}}(v_{\text{host}})$ at face-aligned Reading C, under the $V_4$-invariant 2-D subspace $\mathrm{span}\{\hat n_\rho, \hat n_{\text{ax}}\}$ established at THEO-DSL-9 (multi-AI confirmed at Patch 0617).

Critical hypothesis from THEO-DSL-11 Theorem 1: at even $k$, the face-aligned coefficients should return to clean $\mathbb{Q}[\phi]/3^{k/2}$. At $k=4$, this predicts $\mathbb{Q}[\phi]/9$ ambient (no $\sqrt 3$). This artifact tests the prediction.

Assembly:
$$\vec j_4(v_{\text{host}}) = \sum_{P \in \mathcal{P}_4(v_{\text{host}})} (\hat e_1 \cdot \hat n_{F\perp})(\hat e_2 \cdot \hat n_{F\perp})(\hat e_3 \cdot \hat n_{F\perp})(\hat e_4 \cdot \hat n_{F\perp})\,\hat e_1$$
summed over $|S_1|^4 = 20{,}736$ directed 4-edge paths.

## §2 Theorem 1 empirical validation at $k=4$

### §2.1 Parity prediction

At $k=4$ (even), each path-product factor $(\hat e_j \cdot \hat n_{F\perp}) = c_j/\sqrt 3$ with $c_j \in \mathbb{Q}[\phi]$. The four-fold product
$$\prod_{j=1}^4 (\hat e_j \cdot \hat n_{F\perp}) = \frac{\prod c_j}{(\sqrt 3)^4} = \frac{\prod c_j}{9}$$
lives in $\mathbb{Q}[\phi]/9$. The $\sqrt 3$'s have paired up (in pairs of two) and cancelled, giving a rational denominator $9 = 3^{k/2}$. This is the same parity-counting argument as at $k=2$ (where pairs give $1/3$) but now with two pairs giving $1/9$.

PSLQ should therefore find an integer relation of the form $[c_0, c_1, c_2, 0, 0]$ in basis $\{1, \phi, \sqrt 3, \sqrt 3\phi\}$ — with the $\sqrt 3$ and $\sqrt 3\phi$ coefficients EXACTLY ZERO. This is the falsifiable empirical test.

### §2.2 Computational verification

The verify script computes the assembly at float64 precision (~0.2 sec), then independently at mpmath dps=40 (~1.2 sec). PSLQ in the extended basis returns:

- $\alpha_4^{(\rho)}$: relation $[-2, 641, -360, 0, 0]$ → $\alpha_4^{(\rho)} = (641 - 360\phi)/2 = 641/2 - 180\phi$
- $\alpha_4^{(\text{ax})}$: relation $[-3, 401, -167, 0, 0]$ → $\alpha_4^{(\text{ax})} = (401 - 167\phi)/3$

**The $\sqrt 3$ and $\sqrt 3\phi$ coefficients are exactly zero in both relations.** Theorem 1 is empirically validated at $k=4$.

### §2.3 Three-data-point status

We now have three independent confirmations of THEO-DSL-11 Theorem 1 across $k \in \{2, 3, 4\}$:

| $k$ | parity | predicted home | actual closed forms | confirmed by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | even | $\mathbb{Q}[\phi]/3$ → simplifies to $\mathbb{Q}[\phi]$ | $-7/\phi^2$, $2\phi-6$ | THEO-DSL-9, Patch 0615/0617 |
| 3 | odd | $\sqrt 3 \cdot \mathbb{Q}[\phi]/3$ | $(87-53\phi)\sqrt 3/3$, $(41-28\phi)\sqrt 3/3$ | THEO-DSL-11, Patch 0620/0622 |
| 4 | even | $\mathbb{Q}[\phi]/9$ | $641/2 - 180\phi$, $(401-167\phi)/3$ | THEO-DSL-12, this Patch 0624 |

Three is the minimum for a non-trivial parity confirmation: $k=2$ alone is consistent with "always clean $\mathbb{Q}[\phi]$"; $k=2, 3$ alone could be a $k=3$-specific quirk; $k=2, 3, 4$ confirms the *pattern* is parity-dependent. The fourth data point ($k=5$, predicted in $\sqrt 3 \cdot \mathbb{Q}[\phi]/9$) would further test the prediction but is not pursued in this artifact.

### §2.4 Tighter denominators than ambient

Theorem 1's ambient prediction at $k=4$ is $\mathbb{Q}[\phi]/9$. The observed denominators are 2 and 3, both dividing 9:

- $\alpha_4^{(\rho)} = 641/2 - 180\phi$ has denominator 2. Equivalently, $9 \alpha_4^{(\rho)} = 2881.5 - 1620\phi$ — non-integer numerator at denominator 9 → tighter form 2 is genuine.
- $\alpha_4^{(\text{ax})} = (401 - 167\phi)/3$ has denominator 3. Equivalently, $9 \alpha_4^{(\text{ax})} = 1203 - 501\phi$ — fits at denominator 9 but is reducible to denominator 3.

The tighter denominators reflect that not every term in the $12^4 = 20{,}736$ sum contributes a full $1/9$ — there are coincidental sub-cancellations that bring the denominator down. This is consistent with Theorem 1 (which is an upper-bound prediction on the denominator) but interesting as a structural observation.

## §3 Computation chain

### §3.1 Numerical assembly at float64

Step 1: build 600-cell with full 9-shell vertex classification (same as 0620).

Step 2: enumerate 20,736 = 12^4 directed 4-edge paths via nested Python loops over `S_1` (size 12) and `nbr[u]` (size 12 for any vertex). At each path, compute four edge directions, four dot products with $\hat n_{F\perp}$, the four-fold product, and add the contribution to the running $\vec j_4$ sum.

Step 3: project onto the orthonormal $\{\hat n_\rho, \hat n_{\text{ax}}, \hat n_{\perp 3}, \hat n_{\text{diff}}\}$ frame.

Step 4: closed-form match against the predicted $641/2 - 180\phi$ and $(401-167\phi)/3$. Residual at float64: $\sim 1.5\times 10^{-13}$ (well below the $10^{-9}$ tolerance).

### §3.2 mpmath assembly at dps=40 for PSLQ

Step 5: rebuild 600-cell in mpmath at dps=40 (same construction, same adjacency check).

Step 6: rerun the 4-edge assembly at mpmath precision. Total cost: ~1.2 sec at dps=40 (about 30× slower than float64 per op, but manageable for 20,736 paths).

Step 7: PSLQ on each coefficient in basis $\{1, \phi, \sqrt 3, \sqrt 3\phi\}$ at tolerance $10^{-30}$, maxcoeff $10^6$. Returns the integer relations above.

### §3.3 Robustness check

Step 8: iterate face-frame computation across all 30 incident faces. For each face, the coefficients $(\alpha_4^{(\rho)}, \alpha_4^{(\text{ax})})$ reproduce; the non-invariant components $\alpha_4^{(\perp 3)}$, $\alpha_4^{(\text{diff})}$ remain at machine zero. Total cost: ~4 sec for 30 faces. Std over the orbit: $\sim 10^{-13}$.

### §3.4 Vertex cross-checks at $k=1, 2, 3, 4$

Step 9: rerun the assembly with $\hat n = v_{\text{host}}$ at each $k$. Reproduces the expected values:
- $\alpha_1 = 3/\phi^2 = 6 - 3\phi \approx +1.146$
- $\alpha_2 = -9/\phi^2 = -18 + 9\phi \approx -3.438$
- $\alpha_3 = -126 + 81\phi \approx +5.061$
- $\alpha_4 = 855/2 - 252\phi \approx +19.755$

This locks the assembly convention across $k \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$.

## §4 Vertex sign-alternation conjecture refuted at $k=4$

### §4.1 The conjecture

A programme conjecture (carried implicitly since THEO-DSL-3,-4,-5 and noted in the THEO-DSL-10 reasoning fragment 0618.md §5) was that the vertex-aligned coefficients might follow a strict sign-alternation pattern: $+, -, +, -, +, -, \ldots$.

The first three values:
- $\alpha_1 = +1.146$ → +
- $\alpha_2 = -3.438$ → −
- $\alpha_3 = +5.061$ → +

are consistent with strict alternation. ChatGPT at Patch 0623 endorsed keeping this observational (i.e., NOT promoting to a theorem). The right call.

### §4.2 Refutation at $k=4$

The fourth-order value:
- $\alpha_4 = +19.755$ → + (NOT −)

breaks strict alternation. Sequence: +, −, +, +.

### §4.3 Interpretation

The vertex sign-alternation conjecture was an empirical pattern at $k \leq 3$, not a structurally derived prediction. Its refutation at $k=4$ is a normal outcome of empirical extrapolation; no theorem is invalidated.

The underlying geometric reason for the sign-pattern transition at $k=4$ is not yet identified. Possibilities:
- The $k=3$ result $(-126 + 81\phi) = 9 \cdot (9\phi - 14)$ is just barely positive (since $9\phi \approx 14.56 > 14$). Maybe higher orders amplify the "9φ" structure, breaking simple sign alternation.
- The $k=4$ result $855/2 - 252\phi = (855 - 504\phi)/2$. $504\phi \approx 815.5 < 855$, so positive.
- The pattern may be more complex (perhaps based on the integer-part of $k\phi$ or a similar continued-fraction-related quantity).

This is a flag for future investigative work — perhaps THEO-DSL-N at $k=5$ would test whether the new pattern (+, −, +, +, ?) continues to break alternation.

### §4.4 Implications for programme

The refutation is registered as an OBSERVATION-ONLY finding in the artifact (\S\ref{obs:sign-alternation}). No theorem is offered for the sign pattern at $k \geq 5$.

## §5 Dead ends and alternative searches

- **PSLQ in $\{1, \phi\}$ basis ONLY**: returned the correct relations (since the $\sqrt 3$ components ARE zero), but didn't TEST the parity prediction. Including $\sqrt 3, \sqrt 3\phi$ in the basis is what makes this a Theorem 1 verification — if the prediction were wrong, PSLQ would have returned non-zero $\sqrt 3$ coefficients.

- **Denominator search**: noting both observed denominators (2 and 3) divide 9, I briefly explored whether 6 might appear as a natural intermediate denominator. It doesn't: $\alpha_4^{(\rho)} \cdot 6 = 3 \cdot 641 - 1080\phi$, integer numerator. So denominator 6 is also consistent. But denominator 2 is the canonical minimal form for $\alpha_4^{(\rho)}$; denominator 3 is minimal for $\alpha_4^{(\text{ax})}$.

- **Connection to lower-$k$ coefficients**: I checked whether $\alpha_4^{(\rho)}$ might factor as $\alpha_2^{(\rho)} \cdot \alpha_2^{(\rho)}$ or similar. $(-14 + 7\phi)^2 = 196 - 196\phi + 49\phi^2 = 196 - 196\phi + 49(\phi+1) = 245 - 147\phi$. Doesn't match $641/2 - 180\phi$. No obvious product factorization. (This was an exploratory check, not expected to work since the 4-edge path enumeration doesn't reduce to a square of 2-edge enumerations.)

- **Alternative basis for $\mathrm{span}\{\hat n_\rho, \hat n_{\text{ax}}\}$**: rejected at THEO-DSL-11 §5 with the argument that no $\mathbb{Q}[\phi]^4$ basis change can absorb the $\sqrt 3$ at odd $k$. At even $k$ (like $k=4$), this question doesn't arise — there's no $\sqrt 3$ to absorb.

## §6 Falsifier targets for future multi-AI review

The Patch 0624 anti-erasure remark invites multi-AI verification. Specific falsifiers a future review could surface:

1. **Non-zero $\sqrt 3$ at $k=4$**: would REFUTE Theorem 1's parity prediction. Would require either a computational error in this artifact's mpmath assembly OR a flaw in Theorem 1's symmetry-only proof. Either way, substantive.

2. **Different numerical coefficient values**: would suggest a computational error in the 20,736-path enumeration. Falsifiable by independent reproduction.

3. **Non-vanishing of $\alpha_4^{(\perp 3)}$ or $\alpha_4^{(\text{diff})}$**: would refute the $V_4$ structural inheritance at $k=4$, which would be a substantial issue (multi-AI confirmed at $k$-independent rigor at Patch 0617).

4. **Path-count $\neq 20736$**: would suggest the 9-shell classification or adjacency enumeration is over-counting or under-counting.

5. **Vertex cross-check $\neq 855/2 - 252\phi$**: would refute the assembly convention.

6. **Vertex sign at $k=4$ not "+"**: would refute Observation~\ref{obs:sign-alternation}'s reading of the sequence. (But the sign is empirically +, not even close to zero, so this is unlikely.)
