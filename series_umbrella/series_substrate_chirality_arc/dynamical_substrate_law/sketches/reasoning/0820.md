# Reasoning — Patch 0820 (coarse-graining probe: effective η-field)

The probe's job was to find out whether deriving the effective η-field's d.o.f.-structure resolves mechanically or bottoms out at Thomas's PCD layer. It resolved -- toward primitive -- and I want to be careful about how strongly to say that, because it is verdict-adjacent and it is the favorable direction, which is exactly when I should be most disciplined.

The argument has three legs. The geometric one: the canonical local enantiomorph is the orientation of the whole vertex figure (the 12-neighbour icosahedron), a symmetric function reading all 12 edges -- not a 4-subset det. So m_eff=12 by construction for the canonical object. The computational one: I built an explicit geometric pseudoscalar (orientation signs from a real det against an n-hat frame) and measured C_nn directly -- it came out -0.054 for m=12, |K_lift|/K_c~0.65, matching 0819's arcsin estimate, and bias-robust (delta=0 vs 0.10 barely moved it). The structural one: the bias shifts means (tilt) but not reading weights, so it polarises without driving criticality.

Two honest things I made sure to surface rather than bury. First, the m=4 explicit-pseudoscalar case came out C_nn~0, NOT the arcsin model's 0.16 -- because the geometric orientation signs and the top-4 selection partially cancel / may not even share the edge. That means the coupling is genuinely model-dependent in detail, and I should not lean on the arcsin numbers for the local case; the robust claim is only for the canonical m=12 object. Second, the coupling came out antiferromagnetic. I noted that frustration on this lattice disfavours ordering (supporting primitive), but I deliberately did NOT make that load-bearing, because the sign depends on my orientation convention; the load-bearing result is just |K_lift|<K_c.

The thing I was most disciplined about: this resolves toward primitive, and it would be easy to write "chirality is primitive, done." I did not. It is a defensible LEAN at a stated rigor level (geometric-canonical eta + weak bias + mean-field), it is the chirality lane's verdict to move (DG-3), and I named the three residual gaps plainly -- the dynamical=geometric eta identification (the one spot insight could still enter, though it looks tractable), the mean-field vs true K_c, and the frustrated-AFM non-ordering. None of those is the "imagine a new PCD mechanism" wall Thomas feared, and that is the real news for his question: the probe got to a defensible primitive lean WITHOUT the PCD layer. I said that plainly but kept it a lean, not a theorem.

So the honest answer to "can we finish without Thomas": we got much further than stuck -- to a defensible primitive lean with a concrete derivation -- and the residual gap is sharp and probably closable as rigor work, not clearly his layer. The chirality lane takes K_lift from here.
