\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We produce an exhaustive, classified inventory of every point in the current
Conscious Point Physics (CPP) framework where chirality is referenced,
presupposed, or implicitly used. The framework inputs audited are: the
canonical nine-axiom set (A1--A11 in the post-consolidation numbering, with
A6$'$ consolidating four earlier axioms and A7/A9 retired); the 600-cell
geometric substrate; the Perceive--Compute--Displace (PCD) per-Conscious-Point
per-Absolute-Moment cycle; and the existing chirality-sector derivations
(THEO-CHIR-CONT-1/2/3, THEO-SD-CHIR-1/2, THEO-DSL-1 through THEO-DSL-12,
under the OPEN-SD-CHIR-PRIMITIVE umbrella). For each chirality-touching
element we record its \emph{source} (axiom / geometry / dynamics / derivation),
its \emph{operational sense} (spatial / temporal / CP-asymmetric), and its
\emph{category} (primitive / emergent / unregistered). The result
(Theorem~\ref{thm:audit}) is a 27-entry classification map. Its central finding
is that \emph{spatial} chirality enters the framework at a single irreducible
primitive --- the substrate primitive 4D direction $\nhat$ (FI-C-RC-1) --- with
all other geometric handednesses either gauge-removable or reducing to
$\nhat$ plus the 600-cell (the reductions \emph{established} where a registered theorem
carries them and \emph{provisional} --- registered derivation targets --- otherwise);
that \emph{temporal} chirality is sourced by the PCD
sequence (carried by A1 + A4), whose \emph{ordering} is primitive while its
time-reversal \emph{asymmetry} is presupposed rather than asserted; and that
\emph{CP-asymmetric}
chirality is uniformly emergent, requiring $\nhat$, the dynamics, and the matter
content to combine. The audit registers the downstream OPEN-CHIR-* derivation
programme. This is a structural-cataloging theorem at publication-grade Layer~3
unconditional: it uses the current axiom set and geometric primitives as given
and introduces no new framework axiom. Falsification is by exhibiting a
chirality-touching element absent from the inventory, or by showing an inventory
entry is misclassified. \emph{(v1.1: multi-AI-review calibration of labels ---
emergent graded established/provisional, E20 reclassified
unregistered-conditional, ZBW registered as deferred exclusion X5/OPEN-CHIR-2e; no
entry added or removed, no falsifier produced. See} \texttt{reviews-CHIR-AUDIT-1.md}.\emph{)}
\end{abstract}

\subsection{The three primary questions}

The audit produces an exhaustive enumeration of every point in the current CPP
framework where chirality enters --- as an explicit axiom clause, an implicit
geometric construction choice, a dynamical coupling, or an unregistered
assumption --- and a classification of each.

\begin{description}
\item[Q1 (Inventory).] What are all the points in CPP A1--A11 (canonical
9-axiom set) $+$ 600-cell geometry $+$ PCD-cycle dynamics $+$ existing
CHIR-sector derivations where chirality is referenced, presupposed, or
implicitly used?
\item[Q2 (Classification).] For each inventory entry, is it (a) \emph{primitive}
(already axiomatized in the current set or in a registered foundational input),
(b) \emph{emergent} (reducible to geometry $+$ dynamics without independent
assumption), or (c) \emph{unregistered} (currently assumed but neither
axiomatized nor derived)? \emph{(v1.1 calibration.)} The emergent category is
further graded by the \emph{epistemic state of the reduction}, to avoid claiming
derivations that are not yet written: \emph{emergent (established)} --- a
registered derivation exists at some layer (the row's chirality is produced by an
existing CHIR theorem); \emph{emergent (provisional)} --- the entry is reducible
to $\nhat$ $+$ the 600-cell $+$ the dynamics with no second primitive apparent,
but the derivation is \emph{owed} and is registered as an OPEN-CHIR target. A
provisional-emergent row is a derivation \emph{hypothesis with a target}, not a
completed reduction; it differs from \emph{unregistered} in that no second
primitive or new axiom is apparent, only the explicit derivation is missing.
\item[Q3 (Operational sense).] For each inventory entry, which operational
sense(s) of chirality (\S\ref{sec:senses}) does it produce --- spatial,
temporal, CP-asymmetric, or a combination?
\end{description}

The audit does \emph{not} answer the primitive-vs-emergent question for any
specific element by derivation; it produces the classification map that names
every such element and assigns it provisionally, with the substantive
derivation work becoming the registered downstream OPEN-CHIR-* programme.

\subsection{Three operational senses of chirality}
\label{sec:senses}

\begin{description}
\item[(a) Spatial chirality.] The existence of non-superposable mirror images
--- handedness in 3- or 4-dimensional space. Operationally: there exists an
orientation-reversing isometry $\phi$ (e.g.\ a 4D reflection) such that $\phi$
applied to the configuration yields a configuration not superposable onto the
original by the rotation group of the substrate. Spatial chirality enters CPP if
any geometric or dynamical construction selects between left- and right-handed
mirror-image realisations.

\item[(b) Temporal chirality.] An arrow of time --- distinguishing forward
dynamics from time-reversed dynamics. Operationally: there exists a
time-reversal operator $T$ that is \emph{not} a symmetry of the substrate
dynamics. Temporal chirality enters CPP if the substrate's dynamical evolution
(the per-CP per-Absolute-Moment cycle) is not invariant under $t \mapsto -t$.

\item[(c) CP-asymmetric chirality.] The combined charge--parity asymmetry of the
weak interactions in the Standard-Model sense. Operationally: there exists a
combined $CP$ operator that, applied to a process amplitude, yields a different
amplitude. This is the sense in which the observed universe is chiral at the
deepest empirical level (CP violation in kaon and B-meson decay; the
matter--antimatter asymmetry of the cosmos).
\end{description}

These senses are operationally distinct but logically interrelated. The downstream
derivation work differs sharply by sense: spatial chirality from geometry is
typically a Wythoff-construction or partition choice; temporal chirality from
dynamics typically requires showing the cycle has irreversible coupling;
CP-asymmetric chirality typically requires a combined argument involving both
geometry and dynamics interacting with matter content.

\section{Axiom-level pass: A1 through A11}
\label{sec:axioms}
% ============================================================

We walk the nine canonical axioms in order. For each: (i) does it reference
direction, orientation, sequence, sign, or handedness in any form? (ii) if so,
does it \emph{impose} a chirality choice or merely \emph{permit} one? (iii) which
operational sense does it touch? Most axioms read ``no chirality reference''; the
discipline is to check each explicitly.

\paragraph{A1 (CP existence).} Two clauses bear on chirality.
\emph{Polarity/type.} Polarity ($\pm$) and type (electric/quark) are sign-valued
and binary; a sign is not a handedness, so polarity alone sources no chirality.
But the \emph{coupling} between polarity and the substrate dynamics --- which
polarity captures which partner, in which orientation --- is a candidate
spatial/CP-asymmetric source, deferred to the dynamics pass (D3,
\S\ref{sec:dynamics}).
\emph{Perceive-and-respond capacity.} A1 endows the CP with the capacity to
perceive its environment and respond by deterministic rule. Together with A4 this
carries the PCD cycle, whose Perceive $\to$ Compute $\to$ Displace ordering is a
\emph{temporal} sequence; see A4 and D1. \textbf{Touches: temporal (via PCD),
spatial/CP (via polarity coupling, deferred).}

\paragraph{A2 (600-cell topology).} A2 fixes the substrate as the 600-cell. As a
\emph{static} polytope the 600-cell is achiral: its full symmetry group $\Hfour$
contains orientation-reversing isometries (reflections), so any chiral
sub-structure is matched by its mirror image and no handedness is imposed at the
axiom level. A2 therefore \emph{permits} chirality (it hosts chiral internal
structures, \S\ref{sec:geometry}) but does not \emph{impose} it. \textbf{Touches:
spatial (permissive only).}

\paragraph{A3 (DI-bit propagation).} A3 carries a complex amplitude
$\psi = \sqrt{\rho}\,e^{i\varphi}$. Complex conjugation $\varphi \mapsto -\varphi$
reverses the phase winding; if the substrate dynamics treat $+\varphi$ and
$-\varphi$ asymmetrically, a CP-asymmetric handedness is sourced (complex
conjugation is the substrate analog of charge conjugation). A3 as stated does not
itself assert such asymmetry --- it permits both windings. Whether the dynamics
break the $\varphi \mapsto -\varphi$ symmetry is a dynamics-pass question (D3) and
a downstream target (OPEN-CHIR-3). \textbf{Touches: CP-asymmetric (permissive,
deferred).}

\paragraph{A4 (the Nexus).} A4 enforces global consistency at each
\emph{Absolute Moment}. The Absolute Moment introduces a discrete time cadence; in
conjunction with the PCD cycle (A1) it gives the substrate a forward dynamical
ordering. If the canonical specification asserts the forward Perceive $\to$
Compute $\to$ Displace sequence and does not also assert time-reversal symmetry,
A4 + A1 jointly source \emph{temporal} chirality at the axiom level. This is the
strongest axiom-level chirality candidate. \textbf{Touches: temporal.}

\paragraph{A5 (propagation efficiency $\eta = 1/\phi$).} A scalar ratio. $\phi$ is
achiral. No direction, orientation, or sequence. \textbf{No chirality reference.}

\paragraph{A6$'$ (Walk-Dimension Gauge Principle).} A6$'$ specifies that walk
dimensionality plus coordination geometry on the 600-cell determines gauge
structure, coupling pattern, and mass enhancement. ``Walk'' carries a notion of
oriented traversal; however, the gauge structure A6$'$ produces is parity-blind
at this level (it counts coordination, not handedness), and the principle is
stated symmetrically over walk orientations. A6$'$ \emph{permits} an oriented walk
but does not impose a handedness. \textbf{Touches: spatial (permissive only).}

\paragraph{A8$'$ (Cage-Volume Scaling).} A power-law mass-scaling relation. No
direction, orientation, sequence, or sign-of-handedness. \textbf{No chirality
reference.}

\paragraph{A10 (colour attraction).} Fixes the \emph{sign} of the colour
self-energy ($\varepsilon_S < 0$). A sign is not a handedness; a negative scalar
contribution to an eigenvalue has no mirror partner to be distinguished from.
\textbf{No chirality reference.}

\paragraph{A11 (Lattice-Scale Grounding).} Fixes the lattice-to-physical length
conversion ($\ell_{\text{unit}} \approx 0.589$ fm). A scale, not a handedness.
\textbf{No chirality reference.}

\paragraph{Axiom-level summary.} Of the nine axioms, four are chirality-silent
(A5, A8$'$, A10, A11). Two are permissive without imposing (A2, A6$'$ --- spatial;
A3 --- CP-asymmetric). Two jointly impose the only axiom-level chirality the
framework currently carries: A1 + A4 source the temporal sense via the PCD
sequence and the Absolute-Moment cadence. The polarity-coupling of A1 and the
$\varphi$-winding of A3 are deferred to the dynamics pass.

\section{600-cell geometric pass}
\label{sec:geometry}
% ============================================================

The static 600-cell is achiral ($\Hfour$ contains reflections). But it carries
multiple \emph{internal} structures that become chiral once a choice is made
among them. We enumerate them.

\paragraph{G-1: the $5 \times 24$-cell partition.} The 120 vertices partition into
five disjoint 24-cells, and this can be done in two enantiomorphic ways. Choosing
one partition introduces spatial chirality. \emph{Audit determination:} if the
programme uses one partition canonically, the choice is either constrained (and
should be registered as the constraint that fixes it) or arbitrary (an
\emph{unregistered} spatial-chirality entry that must be eliminated or registered).
\textbf{Spatial; unregistered.}

\paragraph{G-2: the binary icosahedral $2I$ cover.} The 600-cell is the quotient
of $S^3$ by the binary icosahedral group $2I$. $2I$ has chiral irreducible
representations (the two spinor representations are exchanged by an outer
automorphism). Whether the substrate dynamics distinguish the left- and
right-spinor representations is the question the W-bracelet sector
(THEO-SD-CHIR-1) and the qDP/eDP sector (THEO-SD-CHIR-2) implicitly answer ---
they consume a definite spinor handedness. \textbf{Spatial; emergent (from A2 +
$\nhat$), consumed by SD-CHIR.}

\paragraph{G-3: the icosahedral subgroup chain.} $\Hfour \supset \Hthree \times
A_1 \supset \Icoh \supset I = A_5 \supset \cdots$. The icosahedral \emph{rotation}
group $I = A_5$ is a chiral subgroup of the icosahedral \emph{symmetry} group
$\Icoh$ (which contains the inversion). When the substrate dynamics select for
rotations only --- e.g.\ a K3-stabilizer $D_{3d} = D_3 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$
retaining a $C_3$ rotation axis --- chirality enters via the choice of rotation
sense. The Capotauro vertex-aligned resolution $\nhat = \vhost$ breaks $\Hfour \to
\Hthree = \Icoh$, retaining the full icosahedral symmetry; further breaking to
chiral subgroups happens in specific sector closures. \textbf{Spatial; emergent
(from $\nhat$ + sector-specific symmetry breaking).}

\paragraph{G-4: the Wythoff construction orientation.} The polytope built from the
Coxeter diagram $\Hfour$ admits orientation choices that produce mirror-image
embeddings. As with G-1, the choice is gauge-like at the level of the static
polytope (the two embeddings are isometric); chirality enters only if a dynamical
quantity selects one. \textbf{Spatial; emergent / gauge-removable.}

\paragraph{G-5: the golden-ratio coordinate sign conventions.} The standard
600-cell vertex set $\{(\pm 1, \pm 1, \pm 1, \pm 1)/2,\ (\pm\phi, \pm 1,
\pm 1/\phi, 0)/\text{perms}\}$ involves sign and permutation conventions. $\phi$
itself is achiral; the sign choices are coordinate gauge, removable by an
$\Hfour$ transformation. \textbf{Spatial; emergent / gauge-removable.}

\paragraph{G-6: the substrate primitive direction $\nhat$ (FI-C-RC-1).} Per the
Reading-C trajectory (Capotauro v2.0), $\nhat$ is a substrate-primitive 4D
direction registered at Layer~3 as a foundational input. Its mere presence breaks
the achiral $\Hfour$ down to $\Hthree = \Icoh$ at the host vertex --- an
orientation-reversing isometry of the polytope generically does not fix $\nhat$.
This is the irreducible spatial-chirality source of the framework: it exists at
the foundational-input level (not yet derived, not removable by gauge). Every
emergent geometric handedness above (G-2, G-3) traces its definiteness back to
$\nhat$. \textbf{Spatial; primitive (foundational input FI-C-RC-1).}

\paragraph{Geometric-pass summary.} The static 600-cell imposes no chirality.
Of the six internal structures, two are gauge-removable (G-4, G-5), two are
emergent from $\nhat$ + the polytope (G-2, G-3), one is unregistered pending a
constraint determination (G-1), and exactly one is primitive (G-6, the direction
$\nhat$). The geometric sector's chirality reduces, up to gauge and derivation, to
the single primitive $\nhat$.

\section{Dynamics pass: the PCD cycle}
\label{sec:dynamics}
% ============================================================

The dynamics couple to the geometry. We examine four sub-questions.

\paragraph{D1: does the PCD temporal sequence impose an arrow of time?} The
Perceive $\to$ Compute $\to$ Displace sequence at each Absolute Moment is, on its
face, not time-reversible: time-reversed, the cycle would Displace $\to$ Compute
$\to$ Perceive, a different operational dynamics. If the canonical specification
asserts the forward sequence and does not assert time-reversal symmetry, the cycle
sources \emph{temporal} chirality at the axiom level (this is the dynamical content
of the A1 + A4 finding of \S\ref{sec:axioms}). \textbf{Temporal; primitive (the \emph{ordered} sequence, in A1
+ A4), with the time-reversal-\emph{asymmetry} (irreversibility) assertion currently
presupposed rather than explicit --- a logically distinct property registered as the
load-bearing gap OPEN-CHIR-2a.}

\paragraph{D2: does the perception step distinguish left from right?} The Perceive
step has the CP detecting its local SSV environment. If perception is a vector
inner product against an oriented sensor frame (distinguishing $+\hat{x}$ from
$-\hat{x}$ only), no chirality is added. If perception is an oriented bivector or
pseudovector that distinguishes left- from right-handed coordinate frames, spatial
chirality is added. The canonical specification does not yet fix which. \textbf{Spatial;
unregistered (depends on the perception-implementation choice).}

\paragraph{D3: does the capture/partnering step prefer one handedness?} This is the
deepest question. CPs form Dipole Pairs by partnering with neighbouring CPs. If the
partnering rule prefers one handedness --- e.g.\ electric-positive CPs preferentially
partnering with quark-negative CPs in a specific orientation --- then chirality is
sourced at the dynamics level, not the geometry level. The W-bracelet V--A coupling
work (OPEN-FP-SF-2-CHIR) and the Capotauro K3-doublet chirality work both
\emph{depend} on this preference; they consume a definite partnering handedness via
the pairing-convention generators $\zeta^W$, $\zeta^{qDP}$ (the latter a combined
$CP$ operation). The preference is currently consumed but not derived from a
registered axiom. \textbf{Spatial/CP-asymmetric; unregistered (consumed by
SD-CHIR-1/2; the load-bearing dynamical chirality source).}

\paragraph{D4: does the PCD cycle carry an orientation pseudovector $\wPCD$?} The
F.1 sub-question asks whether $\wPCD(\vhost) = \sigma \cdot \nhat$ for a sign
convention $\sigma$. Its resolution determines the classification: Scenario~A
($\wPCD$ derived from $\nhat$ via a substrate mechanism) places this entry under
\emph{emergent}; Scenario~B ($\wPCD$ an independent primitive) places it under
\emph{primitive} alongside $\nhat$. The audit does not pre-commit; pending resolution
the entry is classified \emph{unregistered (conditional)} rather than emergent, so
the table does not silently adopt the Scenario-A presumption (v1.1 calibration).
\textbf{Spatial; unregistered (conditional: emergent under Scenario~A, primitive
under Scenario~B) --- registered as OPEN-CHIR-F1-LINK.}

\paragraph{Dynamics-pass summary.} The dynamics carry the temporal arrow (D1,
primitive in A1+A4 but with the asymmetry presupposed), one unregistered spatial
entry (D2, perception implementation), the deepest unregistered entry (D3, capture
handedness, consumed by SD-CHIR), and the $\nhat \mapsto \wPCD$ link (D4, emergent
or primitive pending OPEN-CHIR-F1-LINK).

\subsection*{How we review}

\noindent Results in this programme are checked by an \emph{AI review panel}: several large language models from different vendors are given the same paper and the same fixed protocol, and asked to find errors and raise objections independently. Objections are recorded and either answered in the text or registered as open problems under the identifiers glossed above.

\noindent This is a \emph{verification method the authors apply to their own work}. It is not journal peer review, it is not equivalent to it, and agreement among panel members is not evidence that a result is correct --- only that no member found a specific fault.
