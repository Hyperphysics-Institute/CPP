\section{The disanalogy with parity: a $\Te$-even geometry, no breaking chain}
\label{sec:disanalogy}
% ============================================================

STATUS-2 found a rich breaking chain for parity: the achiral 600-cell isometry group $\Hf$ breaks to
its rotation subgroup $\Hfp$ (index 2, quotient $\Ztwo$), with the det-pseudoscalar $\sgn(\nhat)$ as
order parameter. The temporal case is structurally \emph{lighter}, and the reason is itself the result.

\begin{lemma}[The substrate geometry is $\Te$-even; no $\Te$-odd geometric quantity exists]
\label{lem:teven}
Time reversal $\Te$ acts on the substrate \emph{dynamical} variables (the current $\jnet$, the cycle
progression $\wPCD$, the rate-asymmetry $\delta$), not on the static lattice. The full set of
static-geometric invariants of the 600-cell is, with $(\Po,\Te)$ character:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item vertex coordinates $\{v_i\}$ --- $(\Po\text{-frame-dependent},\,\Te\text{-even})$;
\item the Gram matrix $G_{ij}=v_i\!\cdot\!v_j$ --- $(\Po\text{-even},\,\Te\text{-even})$;
\item the full pairwise-distance spectrum --- $(\Po\text{-even},\,\Te\text{-even})$;
\item the unsigned volumes --- $(\Po\text{-even},\,\Te\text{-even})$;
\item the signed $4$-volume $\det[v_1;v_2;v_3;v_4]$ --- $(\Po\text{-odd},\,\Te\text{-even})$;
\item the orientation pseudoscalar $\sgn(\nhat)=\text{FI-C-9}$ --- $(\Po\text{-odd},\,\Te\text{-even})$.
\end{enumerate}
\emph{Every} entry is $\Te$-even (the $\Te$ column is uniformly even, including the two $\Po$-odd
entries (v),(vi)). Consequently the substrate geometry supplies \emph{no} $\Te$-odd quantity, and time
reversal admits \emph{no} finite-group breaking chain analogous to $\Hf\to\Hfp$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The 600-cell is a fixed (time-independent) configuration; its coordinates and all functions thereof
(Gram, distances, signed/unsigned volumes, the det-orientation) are unchanged when the direction of
time is reversed --- they carry no time index --- hence $\Te$-even. (This is the $\Te$-character row
of the MERGE-2 table: $\sgn(\nhat)$ is $\Po$-odd but $\Te$-\emph{even}; machine-checked,
\texttt{verify\_tarrow\_1\_arrow\_status.py} CHECK 2a.) For the no-chain claim: a finite-group
breaking chain (as in STATUS-2) requires the broken symmetry to be a \emph{point-group} element
acting on the geometry; $\Te$ is an antiunitary operation on the dynamics, not a point-group element,
and the geometry it would act on is $\Te$-invariant. So there is no index-2 spatial-symmetry quotient
to break: the ``$\Te$-breaking'' is the \emph{dynamical onset of a nonzero arrow}, not a geometric
symmetry reduction.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The sharp contrast]
\label{rem:contrast}
For parity, the achiral geometry still hosts a $\Po$-odd order parameter ($\sgn(\nhat)$): a candidate
chirality \emph{exists} in the framework, and the question is whether the dynamics select it. For time
reversal there is \emph{no} $\Te$-odd geometric candidate at all; the only $\Te$-odd object in the
entire registered framework is the dynamical arrow itself (§\ref{sec:placement}). This is why the
temporal status is even more tightly constrained than the spatial one.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Only a $\Te$-odd quantity distinguishes the two arrows]
\label{lem:todd}
A quantity $Q$ can take different values on the two time-orientations (forward/backward arrow) only if
$Q$ is $\Te$-odd. Every $\Te$-even quantity is invariant under $\Te$ and so agrees on both.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The two arrows are $\Te$-images of each other. A $\Te$-even quantity is by definition $\Te$-invariant,
so it agrees on the two and cannot distinguish them; only a $\Te$-odd quantity flips under $\Te$ and so

place of $\Po$.)

inherited direction).

\begin{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:teven} the geometry supplies none, and by MERGE-2 (conditional on MERGE-$\alpha$) the

$\jnet$ arrow; $\wPCD$ is not independent of it --- $\wPCD=\scyc\,\nhat$ and the $\Te$-odd part of
$\scyc$ is exactly $\sgn(\delta)$). The only available $\Te$-odd sign-fixer is therefore
$\sgn(\delta)$ --- circular, not a derivation. So the direction cannot be explicitly derived at the
axiom level; by Theorem~\ref{thm:partition}, $\mathcal{V}_\Te\in\{\textsf{spontaneous},\textsf{free}\}$
at axiom level, so the $\mathcal{C}_\Te=\textsf{Yes}$ outcome is W1. (Machine-checked,
\texttt{verify\_tarrow\_1\_arrow\_status.py} CHECK 2b/2c.)
