## Why the merge is the right next step (the recommendation)

Thomas asked whether to keep following the roadmap in the drive to determine whether chirality is
emergent or primitive. The honest strategic read: that determination is now *mostly done* at the
structural level. The audit + the three derivations reduced chirality to `{n̂, FI-C-9, σ_cycle}`,
with everything else emergent. Two reductions remain: (a) the E19/E20 merge (is `σ_cycle =
sign(n̂)`? — reduces the sign count from 2 to 1), tractable; (b) 1d-β (the FI-C-9 dynamics —
would eliminate the magnitude+sign inputs entirely), deep. So the merge is the last *tractable*
piece of the primitive-count question, and it's directly teed up by CAP-1 (which left it as the
explicit open hypothesis R2). 1d-β is the deep capstone (multi-session, SS-corpus); the other
OPEN-CHIR entries (1a/1b, 2-series, 3, 4) are more peripheral to the core count question. Hence:
scope the merge.

## What the reads changed

Before reading, I half-expected the merge to bottom out immediately in 1d-β. It does not. Two
findings made it scopable:
1. **THEO-DSL-3 already sources a temporal arrow from `n̂`.** The F.1/DSL arc closes
   manifestation (iv), the thermodynamic causal arrow, via the substrate current `∥ n̂`. And the
   chirality-continuum architecture *explicitly* aims to unify spatial (i–iii) and temporal (iv)
   handedness through the shared `n̂` + the shared `−1/(2φ)`. So there IS a registered route from
   `n̂` to a temporal chirality — the merge is not a priori blocked.
2. **But the sign carries a `sign(δ)` from Mechanism A.** The Phase-1 current is `j ∝ δ·n̂`, so
   the arrow's direction is `sign(δ)·n̂` — the temporal sign is `sign(δ)·sign(n̂)`, NOT simply
   `sign(n̂)`. Unless `sign(δ)` is tied to `sign(n̂)`, the temporal sign stays independent. This
   is the load-bearing subtlety, and it's why M2 (two signs) is a live outcome, not a strawman.

## The decision I made: leave the merge open, scope it honestly

The temptation is to declare M1 (one sign) because it's the elegant, unifying answer — one frozen
enantiomorph sourcing all chirality. I refused. The merge requires a registered link
(MERGE-α identification + MERGE-β sign + the OPEN-CHIR-2a T-asymmetry), and the `sign(δ)` question
is genuinely open. Asserting the merge for elegance would be the exact false-reduction the CHIR
arc has avoided throughout. So the scope:
- Decomposes into MERGE-α (cycle = DSL-3 arrow; near-term reachable) + MERGE-β (the sign;
  entangled with `sign(δ)` and OPEN-CHIR-2a).
- Carries three honest outcomes: M1 (one sign), M2 (two signs), M3 (undetermined pending 2a).
- States plainly the merge is NOT closable now without OPEN-CHIR-2a, and that the likely
  near-term artifact resolves MERGE-α and reports MERGE-β as M3-conditional — a partial result
  that narrows the merge to the sign question without forcing M1. This mirrors the staged closures
  of E21 (ratio reachable / dynamics deferred) and E19 (involution reachable / FI-C-9 consumed).

## On FI-C-9

Either way the merge does not eliminate FI-C-9: M1 routes `σ_cycle` *to* `sign(n̂) = FI-C-9`
(consuming it), it does not derive the enantiomorph. The merge changes the chirality *count*
(one sign vs two), not FI-C-9's input status. The only reduction that touches FI-C-9's status is
1d-β. Kept this distinction explicit so a future reader doesn't mistake a merge for a deeper
reduction than it is.
