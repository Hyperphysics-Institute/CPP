## Was 0991 right?

Under its own rule, yes. The rule was pre-committed at 0989 and applied as written: λ_min/floor = −3.2×10¹⁵ at
dps 30 against a −10⁶ threshold, eight digits stable across 30/45/60. The verdict stands.

The critique is about the *object*. The probe diagonalises S = sym(Π e^{2tQ}). That is the whole of reflection
positivity only for **real** observables. VW-2 Thm A writes the pairing with the conjugate, on L²(π):

  ⟨Θ_OS(Ā) A⟩ = ⟨A, T A⟩_π ≥ 0  — a Hermitian condition (and the proof calls T "positive self-adjoint").

For a single-time A this is F(z) = z†Mz, M = Π e^{2tQ}. Split M = S + K. F is real for every complex z only if
K = 0, and K = 0 iff Π e^{2tQ} is symmetric iff the chain is reversible. So at δ ≠ 0 the probe tested a
necessary condition on the real subalgebra, while the condition VW-2 actually states can fail with S ≥ 0 — at
t ≤ 0.75 as well, where 0991 found S positive.

## The recorded caution, and why it does not save RP here (D-1)

0685 and 0983 (and TARROW-2's scope) hold that **RP ≠ T-symmetry**: irreversibility removes Thm A's guarantee
without, by itself, refuting RP. That caution is right in QFT, where Θ_OS carries an internal antiunitary map.
So the honest question is not "is the chain reversible" — TARROW-2 already says no — but "does some internal
involution J make Θ_OS∘J Hermitian-positive?"

On this measure that loophole is finite. The automorphisms of the single-time observable algebra (functions on
120 vertices) are the vertex permutations. A Hermitian pairing needs J with π∘J = π and J Q Jᵀ = Q̂ = Π⁻¹QᵀΠ.
Q̂ has the same edge support as Q (π > 0), so J must be a 600-cell graph automorphism: H4, 14,400 elements.
Exhaustive.
