## The key realization that shaped the scope

Reading the F.1 sub-question sketch closely, the Scenario-A structure is *already derived*
at viability level (F.1 §12): $\vec{\omega}_{PCD}=\sigma_{cycle}\cdot\hat{j}^{net}=\sigma_{cycle}\,\hat{n}$,
where $\hat{j}^{net}\parallel\hat{n}$ (Phase 1 / DSL-4) and $\sigma_{cycle}$ is the cycle's TI-odd
handedness. The temptation was to scope THEO-CHIR-PCD-ORIENTATION-1 as "re-prove the F.1 Phase-2
result at Layer 3." That would be wrong on two counts:

- It would relitigate F.1 Phase-2 foundations work, which is a separate trajectory with its own
  three open commitments (F.1 §14.17). Duplicating it here is scope creep.
- It would overclaim: the F.1 result is viability-level (Layer 2.5), not Layer 3. A CHIR theorem
  claiming Layer 3 on top of a Layer-2.5 input would violate the calibration discipline the recent
  AUDIT-1 review cycle just reinforced (and that ChatGPT's caution explicitly flagged).

The correct, narrower scope: the audit's E20 question is a **primitive-count** question, not a
magnitude question. "Is $\vec{\omega}_{PCD}$ an independent primitive, or does it reduce to
registered primitives?" That question is answerable *more robustly* than the full F.1 closure,
because the decomposition $\vec{\omega}_{PCD}=\sigma_{cycle}\,\hat{n}$ shows the cycle-orientation is
a product of two ALREADY-REGISTERED primitives — the spatial primitive $\hat{n}$ (E16) and the
temporal primitive's handedness $\sigma_{cycle}$ (E2/E5/E17) — and the three open F.1 commitments,
even if all fail, do not reintroduce an independent direction or handedness. They affect the ansatz,
the explicitation, and the magnitude — none the primitive-count. So the classification is firm even
where the magnitude is provisional. That economy is the reason E20 is the right first theorem.
