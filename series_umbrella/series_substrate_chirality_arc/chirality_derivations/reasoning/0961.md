# reasoning-0961 (verbatim, captured late — see 0942 header note; same provenance)

Scoping piece 1. The founder asked whether it is a day or a year, and the useful answer turned out to be a *route eliminated*.

I would have bet on the symmetry argument: the lattice is regular, the vertex stabiliser acts on the twelve edges, symmetry forces equal weights within each orbit, orbit structure bounds p from below. Built it to check.

**It fails.** Four of the nine n̂-shells — the 48 vertices at v·n̂ = ±0.809 and ±0.309 — have edge orbits **[1, 1, 5, 5]**. Those singletons mean a symmetry-covariant observable can put all its weight on one edge and reach p = 1. **Symmetry permits precisely the collapse piece 1 must exclude**, and permits it near the poles, which is roughly where 0924 feared it.

So no orbit-counting bound can close piece 1, and knowing that before spending a month on it is the value of the session.

**What the natural candidates give:** sign weights p = 12 everywhere — the maximum on twelve edges — and |det| magnitude weights p ∈ [5.33, 12]. Reaching the floor takes ~70% of the norm on one edge. So they clear comfortably; they are just not *established* as the dynamical η.

**Correction to the stated worry:** 0924 named "degenerating at n̂-extremal vertices". The extremal vertices are the best case (poles: full I_h, transitive on all twelve, p = 12). The exposure is the near-polar singleton-orbit shells instead. Said so rather than quietly aiming at the right ones.

Reframed the problem accordingly: piece 1 is the **η-identity** question — not "is some observable non-degenerate" (CAPACITY-1 already quantifies over the class) but "which observable does the dynamics single out". Recommended one bounded step rather than opening it blind.
