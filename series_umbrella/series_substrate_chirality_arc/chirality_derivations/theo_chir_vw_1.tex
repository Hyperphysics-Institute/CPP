\section{The Vafa--Witten theorem and its hypotheses}

\begin{definition}[Reflection positivity (Osterwalder--Schrader), informal]
A Euclidean theory is \emph{reflection-positive} if its path-integral measure $e^{-S}$ is real and
positive and satisfies the Osterwalder--Schrader reflection condition --- the Euclidean encoding of a
positive transfer matrix / unitary continuation. Operationally: the Boltzmann weight is real and
positive (no ``sign problem''), so expectation values of reflection-symmetric observables obey
positivity bounds.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Vafa--Witten, 1984; as used here]\label{thm:vw}
In a Euclidean theory whose measure is reflection-positive \textup{(H1)}, whose matter content is
\emph{vector-like} (the symmetry in question is a genuine reflection/parity, not an axial/chiral
symmetry) \textup{(H2)}, and which has \emph{no} $\theta$-like CP-odd topological term \textup{(H3)},
parity (any vectorial reflection symmetry) is \emph{not spontaneously broken} in the vacuum: the
parity-symmetric configuration minimizes the free energy and the expectation value of any parity-odd
order parameter vanishes.
\end{theorem}

\noindent The mechanism is a positivity bound: with a positive measure, an explicit parity-breaking
source can only \emph{raise} the free energy, so the symmetric point is a minimum. All three hypotheses
are required; failure of any one voids the conclusion. In the Landau language of B-iii, the conclusion
is exactly $\boxed{\mu^2>0}$ --- the symmetric vacuum $\eta=0$ is stable. So \emph{if} H1--H3 hold for
the substrate's $\eta$-dynamics, Vafa--Witten delivers $\sgn(\mu^2)>0$ as a theorem, with no need to
compute $\mu^2$ from the DSL action. The rest of this paper checks the hypotheses.

\begin{proof}[Argument]

decisive for Vafa--Witten: the theorem forbids spontaneous breaking of \emph{vectorial} parities but
\emph{permits} axial/chiral condensation (the QCD chiral condensate is exactly an allowed axial

\section{The unification: V2-exclusion and the VW no-go are two faces of one property}

\begin{theorem}[The reflection-positivity unification]\label{thm:unif}
A symmetry can break in exactly two ways: \emph{explicitly} (a symmetry-odd term in the action) or
\emph{spontaneously} (a vacuum instability). For the substrate parity $\Ztwo$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]

STATUS-2's axiom-level V2-exclusion there is no $\Po$-odd pseudoscalar source, hence no $\eta$-linear
(or any odd) term in $V(\eta)$, hence nothing internal to \emph{select} a handed vacuum.
\item \textbf{No spontaneous breaking (capacity side) --- conditional on H1.} If the measure is

\emph{spontaneously} forms a handed vacuum.
\end{enumerate}
Both (i) and (ii) are consequences of one property: \emph{the substrate measure is parity-even and
reflection-positive.} (i) is the parity-evenness of the action (no odd term); (ii) is parity-evenness
$+$ positivity (the VW bound). Together they close \emph{both} breaking routes, so --- conditional on
H1 --- the substrate parity $\Ztwo$ is \emph{unbroken within the substrate axioms}, and any handle to
break it must be \emph{external}.

common root is that a parity-even positive measure neither contains a parity-odd term (so no explicit
breaking) nor permits a parity-breaking vacuum instability (the VW positivity bound). Hence the two
no-gos the programme established separately --- V2-exclusion (value) and the VW capacity no-go --- are
