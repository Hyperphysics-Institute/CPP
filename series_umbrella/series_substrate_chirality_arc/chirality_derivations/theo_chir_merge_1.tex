\section{Setup: the merge question and the primitive-count stakes}
\label{sec:setup}
% ============================================================

After the three audit-downstream derivations, the programme's irreducible chirality content is
$\{\nhat,\ \text{FI-C-9},\ \scyc\}$: the spatial primitive direction $\nhat$; the FI-C-9 datum
(the magnitude $|\chi| = \varphi^{-3}$, value-derived by THEO-CHIR-CHI-1, and the frozen
enantiomorph sign $\mathrm{sign}(\nhat)$); and the temporal cycle handedness $\scyc$ (E20; from
A1$+$A4). THEO-CHIR-CAP-1 (verdict R1) pinned the \emph{spatial} capture-handedness sign to
$\scap = \mathrm{sign}(\nhat) = \text{FI-C-9}$. OPEN-CHIR-MERGE asks whether the \emph{temporal}
$\scyc$ is the same sign:
\begin{equation}
\textbf{Is } \scyc = \mathrm{sign}(\nhat)\,?
\label{eq:merge-question}
\end{equation}
A confirmed merge (M1) means one frozen substrate enantiomorph fixes spatial capture handedness,
temporal cycle direction, and the $\nhat$-orientation alike --- \textbf{one} chirality sign. A
refuted merge (M2) means $\scyc$ is an independent sign primitive --- \textbf{two}. This is the
last tractable piece of ``is chirality emergent or primitive'' (the only deeper reduction being
1d-$\beta$, the FI-C-9 dynamics).

We decompose \eqref{eq:merge-question} into \textbf{MERGE-$\alpha$} (is $\scyc$ the same temporal
orientation as the DSL-3 thermodynamic arrow?) and \textbf{MERGE-$\beta$} (does that orientation's
sign reduce to $\mathrm{sign}(\nhat)$?). No false merge: one sign is more elegant than two, but
elegance is not a derivation; M2 and M3 (undetermined) are honest outcomes.

\label{sec:alpha}
% ============================================================

The candidate registered route is THEO-DSL-3, which closes OPEN-SD-CHIR-PRIMITIVE manifestation

\begin{quote}
asymmetric DI-bit arrival at the host vertex $\to$ capture-phase bias in the PCD cycle $\to$

\begin{proposition}[MERGE-$\alpha$: identification]
\label{prop:alpha}
The PCD-cycle orientation $\wPCD$ (whose sign is $\scyc$) and the THEO-DSL-3 thermodynamic causal
arrow are the \emph{same temporal orientation}. The substrate current $\jnet \parallel \nhat$
biases the PCD cycle's capture phase, inducing the preferred $\wPCD$; the macroscopic arrow is the
coarse-grained manifestation of that same cycle bias. There are not two temporal chirality data
(a microscopic cycle sense and a separate macroscopic arrow) --- there is one, $\wPCD$, with the
arrow as its large-scale face.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Layer and inherited ceiling]
Proposition~\ref{prop:alpha} is the DSL mechanism narrative read as an identification. It holds at
the \emph{mechanism-narrative / structural} level (Layer 2.5), inheriting the THEO-DSL-3 viability
ceiling: THEO-DSL-3 establishes the substrate-locality \emph{structure} (the closed-form current)

arrow at the same level at which DSL-3 supports the arrow --- no stronger. The emergence layer is
deferred (it is OPEN-CHIR-2a; see \S\ref{sec:2a}).
\end{remark}

So MERGE-$\alpha$ \emph{resolves (provisionally)}: the temporal chirality is one orientation. The
remaining question is its sign.

\section{MERGE-$\beta$: the sign is undetermined (verdict M3)}
\label{sec:beta}
% ============================================================

Does the one temporal orientation's sign reduce to $\mathrm{sign}(\nhat) = \text{FI-C-9}$? Two
registered facts say it is undetermined at current rigor.

\subsection{Reason (i): the orientation sign is a Layer-3 convention}
\label{sec:beta-i}

THEO-DSL-3 states plainly that the global sign $\sigma$ in $\wPCD = \sigma\nhat$ ---
\emph{whether $\wPCD$ aligns with $+\nhat$ or $-\nhat$} --- is \emph{a convention fixed at the
framework level}, and that \emph{the two sign choices correspond to time-reversal-symmetric
framings of the same physics and are not distinguishable at Layer 3 rigor}. The sign becomes
consequential \emph{only} when coupled to the F.2 trajectory's substrate-Wigner-Eckart datum
(Capotauro v2.0 \S20), where it couples to the thermodynamic-causal-arrow direction via the
cage-shell factor $1/6$. So at the present (DSL Layer-3) level, $\scyc$ is not a physical sign that
could equal $\mathrm{sign}(\nhat)$; it is a convention awaiting a physical coupling.

\subsection{Reason (ii): the arrow sign carries an independent $\mathrm{sign}(\delta)$}
\label{sec:beta-ii}

The current \eqref{eq:current} has direction $\mathrm{sign}(\delta)\,\nhat$: with $6/\varphi^2 > 0$,
$\jnet(\delta>0) = +(6/\varphi^2)\nhat$ and $\jnet(\delta<0) = -(6/\varphi^2)\nhat$ (verified,
\texttt{verify\_merge\_current\_sign.py}, using the uniform host-to-first-shell projection
$\hat{u}_i\cdot\nhat = -1/(2\varphi)$). So the temporal-arrow sign is
$\mathrm{sign}(\delta)\cdot\mathrm{sign}(\nhat)$, carrying the Mechanism-A parameter sign
$\mathrm{sign}(\delta)$ \emph{in addition to} $\mathrm{sign}(\nhat)$. THEO-DSL-3 is explicit that
$\delta$ is an \emph{independent framework input}: \emph{the relationship between $\varepsilon$
[the spatial chirality / Reading-C edge perturbation, controlled by $|\chi| = \varphi^{-3}$] and
the Mechanism-A propagation-rate parameter $\delta$ is not pinned at this paper's scope; both are
taken as independent framework inputs.} Tying $\delta$ to the spatial chirality (and hence
$\mathrm{sign}(\delta)$ to $\mathrm{sign}(\nhat)$) is exactly the Layer-4 Mechanism-A derivation
(OPEN-FP-F1-2). Until then, $\mathrm{sign}(\delta)$ is free, and the arrow sign
$\mathrm{sign}(\delta)\cdot\mathrm{sign}(\nhat)$ does \emph{not} reduce to $\mathrm{sign}(\nhat)$.

\begin{theorem}[MERGE-$\beta$: the sign is undetermined; THEO-CHIR-MERGE-1]
\label{thm:beta}
Under CPP axioms A1--A11 $+$ THEO-DSL-3 (Reading~C $+$ 600-cell $+$ Mechanism~A, sketch-document
Layer~3), the reduction of the temporal-orientation sign to $\mathrm{sign}(\nhat) = \text{FI-C-9}$
is \textbf{undetermined (verdict M3)}. It is not M1 (the sign is a Layer-3 convention, \S
\ref{sec:beta-i}, and carries an independent $\mathrm{sign}(\delta)$, \S\ref{sec:beta-ii}) and not
M2 (neither reason \emph{establishes} independence; $\mathrm{sign}(\delta)$ may yet be tied to
$\mathrm{sign}(\nhat)$ at Layer 4). The merge sign is gated on two registered closures: the F.2
substrate-Wigner-Eckart coupling that makes $\sigma$ physical, and the Layer-4 Mechanism-A
derivation (OPEN-FP-F1-2) that would tie $\mathrm{sign}(\delta)$ to the enantiomorph.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof.]
By Proposition~\ref{prop:alpha} the temporal chirality is one orientation with sign carried by
$\sigma$ (equivalently by $\mathrm{sign}(\delta)$ via \eqref{eq:current}). \S\ref{sec:beta-i}:
$\sigma$ is a Layer-3 convention with the two choices T-reversal-symmetric, so no physical equality
$\scyc = \mathrm{sign}(\nhat)$ is available at this level (rules out asserting M1).
\S\ref{sec:beta-ii}: the arrow sign is $\mathrm{sign}(\delta)\cdot\mathrm{sign}(\nhat)$ with
$\mathrm{sign}(\delta)$ an independent input (rules out asserting M1 a second way). Neither reason
exhibits a $\scyc$ degree of freedom \emph{provably} independent of $\mathrm{sign}(\nhat)$ (the
Layer-4 derivation could tie them), so M2 is not established either. The remaining verdict is M3,
with the two named gates.
\end{proof}
