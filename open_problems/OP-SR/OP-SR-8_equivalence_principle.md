# OP-SR-8: Equivalence of Inertial and Gravitational SSV Contributions

**Priority:** MEDIUM  
**Status:** OPEN — stated in SR paper; not derived from CPP mechanics  
**Series:** SR main paper; GR companion  
**Last updated:** 23 March 2026

## Statement

Prove from CPP mechanics that the SSV contribution from kinetic
energy (inertial) is locally identical to the SSV contribution from
gravitational potential energy, recovering Einstein's equivalence
principle:

$$\Delta\text{SSV}_\text{kinetic}(v) = \Delta\text{SSV}_\text{gravitational}(\Phi)$$

when $\frac{1}{2}mv^2 = m|\Phi|$ (same energy, different source).

## Current Status

The SR paper adds one sentence: "Since both acceleration and
gravitational potential contribute identically to ΔSSV, the
equivalence principle emerges naturally."  This is stated, not derived.

## The Derivation Path

Both inertial and gravitational SSV arise from the same underlying
mechanism: the CP's interaction with the surrounding DP Sea. The key
claim is that the DP Sea stores energy the same way regardless of
whether it came from kinetic or potential energy:

$$\Delta\text{SSV}(E_\text{stored}) = k \cdot E_\text{stored}$$

independent of the *source* of $E_\text{stored}$.

**Show explicitly:**  
1. Kinetic energy $\frac{1}{2}mv^2$ increases local SSV by $k \cdot \frac{1}{2}mv^2$
2. Gravitational energy $m|\Phi|$ increases local SSV by $k \cdot m|\Phi|$
3. Both produce identical PSR reduction: PSR_eff = l_P/(1 + k·E)
4. Therefore, a freely-falling observer in a gravitational field
   is locally indistinguishable from an accelerating observer
   in flat space.
