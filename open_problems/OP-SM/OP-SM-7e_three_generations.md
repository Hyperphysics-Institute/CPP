## Statement

Derive from CPP first principles why the colour cage base has exactly
three vertices ($N = 3$), and hence why there are exactly three charged
lepton generations.

---

## What the K3 Theorem Shows

The K3 Spectral Theorem (Paper 3) establishes: for the complete graph
$K_N$, the Koide ratio is:

$$K(K_N) = \frac{N+1}{2N}$$

The observed charged-lepton Koide ratio $K = 2/3$ requires $N = 3$.

| $N$ | Graph | $K$ | Physical meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | $K_2$ (edge) | $1/2$ | Two-generation lepton sector |
| **3** | **$K_3$ (triangle)** | **$2/3$** | **Three generations (observed)** |
| 4 | $K_4$ (tetrahedron) | $5/8$ | Four-generation sector |
| $\infty$ | $K_\infty$ | $1/2$ | Infinite generations |

Only $N = 3$ gives $K = 2/3$. But **why $N = 3$** in the 600-cell cage
is not derived — it is the observation that the tetrahedral cage base
has three vertices, encoding three colour states.

---

## The Deeper Question

The tetrahedral cage has four vertices: $\{V_1, V_2, V_3, V_4\}$.
The base $\{V_1, V_2, V_3\}$ has three vertices because:
- SU(3) colour has three states (proved in the strong sector from the
  tetrahedral hopping operators — Paper 1 of the series)
- The cage base must accommodate exactly three colour states

This shifts the question: why does SU(3) — and not SU(2) or SU(4) —
emerge from the 600-cell tetrahedral cells?

The strong sector answer (already established): the tetrahedral hopping
generates exactly 8 Gell-Mann generators, giving SU(3)$_c$. Three colours
$\Leftrightarrow$ three base vertices. But why the 600-cell produces
tetrahedral cells specifically (not cubic or icosahedral cells with more
or fewer vertices) requires the deeper 600-cell group theory.

---

## Connection to the 600-Cell

The 600-cell consists of 600 regular tetrahedra — each with exactly 4
vertices (1 apex + 3 base). The 3-vertex base is a consequence of
the tetrahedron being the simplest 3D regular solid.

A derivation of $N=3$ from CPP would show:
1. The 600-cell's tetrahedral cells are the unique structure compatible
   with CPP interaction rules (proved in the strong sector — why tetrahedra?)
2. The tetrahedral base has $N=3$ vertices by the geometry of the
   tetrahedron (trivially true once tetrahedra are established)
3. Therefore $N=3$, hence $K=2/3$, hence three lepton generations

Step 1 is the non-trivial part — why tetrahedra, not octahedra or cubes?
