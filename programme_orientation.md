## Part I: The Foundations

### Chapter 1: The Two Postulates

CPP rests on two foundational claims:

**Postulate 1: Consciousness is fundamental.** The universe is made of Conscious Points (CPs) — entities that perceive their local environment and respond according to rules. [SS-1, Axiom AXIM-2]

From these two postulates, everything else follows.

### Chapter 35.5: The Conditional-Closure Framework and Cross-Sector Closure (Programme-Level Methodology)

The SF-4 v4.0-v4.3 ChatGPT review trajectory crystallized two programme-level methodology patterns into explicit convention, both of which are now expected to govern future flagship papers and are codified at `templates/conditional_closure_framework.md` and `theorem-dependency-graph.md`.

**The conditional-closure framework.** Every flagship-paper closure in CPP to date is conditional on a named stack of foundational inputs (FIs) plus a named subset of CPP axioms, not full derivational closure from CPP primitives alone. The distinction matters because "RESOLVED" can be misread as "full closure" when the closure is in fact conditional. The framework establishes: (1) every conditional closure declares its closure level explicitly via a paper-level Remark setting the framing globally; (2) FIs are accounted for at the closure boundary by name, traceability, load-bearing identification, and count; (3) "RESOLVED" is read in the conditional sense by default unless explicitly qualified as "full derivational closure" (rare; not currently used in any flagship paper); (4) the choice between inheriting an FI vs re-deriving from CPP primitives follows the default convention "inherit when the FI is itself at theorem-level closure in its source; re-derive when the FI would otherwise depend on an ansatz or unresolved open problem." The framework was first instantiated in SF-4 v4.2 via Remark `rem:conditional_closure` and generalized to programme convention at patch 0340.

**Cross-sector closure as structural pattern (Finding β-10).** A cross-sector closure is a theorem-level closure in which a single derivation chain simultaneously resolves open problems in two or more distinct papers. The first instance in CPP is SF-4 v4.0's Composite K3-Cage-Shell Coupling Theorem (THEO-SF-4-5), which resolves both OPEN-FP-SF-4-2 (in SF-4) and op:nu_id (in SM-5) jointly. The second instance is **THEO-SD-CHIR-1** (Cross-Sector Substrate Chirality Unification Theorem, registered Patch 0434 Session 132; Chapter 22g), which establishes that the K3-doublet mass-mixing sector (Capotauro) and the W-bracelet electroweak V-A sector (SF-2) inherit substrate chirality from the same primitive 4D direction at the same substrate-physics magnitude $|M^{K3}| = |M^W| = \chi/6 = \phi^{-3}/6 \approx 0.0394$ — substrate-level closure of the OPEN-FP-SF-2-CHIR ↔ OPEN-SM-4 sub-claim (b) cross-sector pair (Chapter 41 + Chapter 41.5). The two cross-sector closures sit at structurally distinct scopes: THEO-SF-4-5 is **sector-internal mutual closure across two papers** (each paper's closure depending on the other; single derivation chain resolves both simultaneously); THEO-SD-CHIR-1 is **substrate-level unification under a programme-level umbrella** (substrate chirality mechanism shared across multiple sectors; cross-sector identification at substrate-physics level, with sector-specific Layer 4 EFT projections remaining as separate closure targets per sector). ### Chapter 35.6: The Series Umbrella (SU) Container and the Substrate-Chirality Arc — Two-Axis Taxonomy (Programme-Level Methodology)

The Session 144 conversation between Thomas and Claude surfaced a programme-level taxonomic question: were Capotauro, Chirality Continuum, and F.1 Dynamical Substrate Law in a category distinct from sector-flagship papers? The answer is yes, and the answer matters because the existing paper-container taxonomy at `/CPP/` root level could not express the distinction. The result is the Series Umbrella (SU) container established at Patch 0571d, the first sub-umbrella Substrate-Chirality Arc (SSCA) holding the three papers, and the §15.13 OS codification of the two-axis taxonomy + regrouping audit discipline at Patch 0571f. This chapter records the programme-level structural conclusion the arc led to.

**The two-axis taxonomy.** Papers organize themselves along two operationally distinct axes. The first axis is phenomenology-sector: which observed part of physical reality the paper describes. The existing sector series (`series_strong/`, `series_standard_model/`, `series_electroweak/`, `series_quantum_mechanics/`, `series_relativity/`, `series_foundations/`) plus the sector-flagship container (`flagship_papers/`) sit along this axis, and they group papers the way physics-as-discipline has been organizing itself for roughly a century. The second axis is problem-arc: which open umbrella problem the paper attacks. Papers in a problem arc may sit in different phenomenology sectors but converge on closing the same programme-level umbrella problem, with the closure ordering driven by which mathematical machinery has matured rather than which manifestation is most foundational. SSCA — Capotauro + Chirality Continuum + F.1 — is the first arc to make the second axis structurally visible: three papers spanning SM, SEW, and SD territory all sit under OPEN-SD-CHIR-PRIMITIVE and its five named manifestations.

The two axes cannot be collapsed into one without information loss. Placing Capotauro in `series_standard_model/` because its primary observable is in SM territory would hide its membership in the chirality arc, would separate it from the other two arc papers that close different manifestations, and would make the OPEN-SD-CHIR-PRIMITIVE umbrella structurally invisible at the filesystem level. The information loss is real and compounds as additional arcs emerge. The SU container makes the second axis explicit: phenomenology-sector folders at root level, problem arcs inside SU.

**What the Substrate-Chirality Arc is.** Three flagship papers organized under the OPEN-SD-CHIR-PRIMITIVE umbrella (the first programme-level umbrella entry in CPP, opened Patch 0434 Session 132). The umbrella scopes the substrate's primitive chirality as a cross-sector unification target across five named observable manifestations: (i) K3-doublet mass-mixing chirality (CLOSED by Capotauro v1.0 via THEO-CAP-1); (ii) electroweak V−A coupling (CLOSED at substrate level by Capotauro v2.0 via THEO-SD-CHIR-1; Layer 4 EFT closure by Chirality Continuum via THEO-CHIR-CONT-2); (iii) electromagnetic-handedness (OPEN; no current closure-trajectory machinery); (iv) thermodynamic causal-arrow direction (CLOSED at sketch-document Layer 3 by F.1 via THEO-DSL-3 + four publication-grade Layer 3 hardened-theorem inputs); (v) cosmological-vacuum asymmetry (OPEN; registered as OPEN-FP-F1-4). Three of the five manifestations are closed at varying rigor levels; two remain open with no current closure-trajectory machinery. The arc continues; arc retirement when all five manifestations close at minimum sketch-document Layer 3 rigor.

The arc's closure ordering is opportunistic, and the framework is honest about that. The five-manifestation enumeration is registered (not invented post hoc to fit closures); but which manifestation gets attacked next depends on which mathematical machinery has matured. Capotauro v1.0 came first because the Wigner-Eckart factorization machinery was the most mature substrate-level tool available. Capotauro v2.0's cross-sector extension came next because THEO-SD-CHIR-1 extended the same machinery without requiring new tools. Chirality Continuum's Layer 4 EFT closure came after because the topological-projection machinery had to be developed. F.1's substrate-locality work came fourth because the perturbation-theory + first-shell-locality machinery only matured over Sessions 137-142. The remaining two manifestations stay open partly because their machinery hasn't matured. This is how legitimate physics opportunism works; the discipline against degeneration is the umbrella registration itself — manifestations (iii) and (v) stay on the books with explicit names. They don't disappear if the framework keeps finding easier surfaces; the umbrella's existence is the commitment to honest completion.

**Three paper-container kinds at programme level.** The post-SU taxonomy at `/CPP/` root recognizes three structurally distinct paper-container kinds, each with its own organizing principle:

1. **Sector series** (`series_<sector>/`) — papers that close a phenomenology sector via a sequence of related results within that sector. Identifier `S<short-sector-code>` (SS, SM, SEW, SQM, SR, SD). Examples: SS-7/SS-8/SS-9 in `series_strong/`; SM-3/SM-4 in `series_standard_model/`.
2. **Sector flagship** (`flagship_papers/<sector>/`) — single-paper flagship that closes a sector phenomenology at flagship-level generality. Identifier `SF-N`. Examples: SF-2 in `flagship_papers/electroweak/`; SF-4 in `flagship_papers/neutrinos/`. With the SSCA migration at Patch 0571d, `flagship_papers/` contents are now exclusively sector-flagship in this sense; the deferred-beautification rename to "Flagship Papers" noted at Capotauro v2.0 entry resolves without explicit renaming because the contents now match the label.
3. **Problem-arc paper** (`series_umbrella/[<sub-umbrella>/]<paper-name>/`) — paper that closes a manifestation of an umbrella problem or otherwise contributes to a cross-sector closure programme. Identifier varies: F-N for F-line flagships closing OPEN-SD-CHIR-PRIMITIVE manifestations (F.1 is the first; future F.2, F.3 etc. will close manifestations (iii), (v), or both); paper-name-only for F-line precursor flagships that predate the F-line numbering (Capotauro, Chirality Continuum).

The three categories are not mutually exclusive at paper-content level (a phenomenology-sector paper can contribute to a problem arc; a problem-arc paper can have phenomenology-sector content) but they are mutually exclusive at paper-container-membership level. Each paper lives in exactly one folder, and the folder reflects the paper's primary taxonomic identity.

**The accumulate-then-group workflow.** A new paper that doesn't fit a phenomenology sector goes into SU without requiring an arc-membership decision at paper-creation time. When two or more accumulated papers share a stable organizing principle, a sub-umbrella folder is created and the papers migrate in via `git mv` at a regrouping Patch. The cost is occasional regrouping overhead (estimated every few months at current corpus-growth cadence); the benefit is that paper creation has no taxonomic gate, and groupings emerge organically from the work rather than being imposed in advance. The regrouping audit discipline (codified at OS §15.13) fires at paper-completion-state Patches touching SU papers: if the count of ungrouped papers exceeds 3 and a shared organizing principle is identified across 2+ papers, a sub-umbrella creation Patch is proposed. The audit prevents the SU container from degenerating into a permanently-ungrouped accumulation while keeping paper creation friction-free.

**Methodological observation.** The SU mechanism is the second piece of programme-organizing infrastructure to crystallize from a Thomas-Claude conversation about a structural ambiguity that had been sitting in the corpus unresolved. The first was the OPEN-SD-CHIR-PRIMITIVE umbrella itself (Patch 0434 Session 132, Chapter 22g) — a programme-level umbrella that resolved the ambiguity of where the cross-sector chirality work belonged in the OPEN registry. The pattern in both cases is the same: a structural ambiguity sits in the corpus for some time (between the two cases: the umbrella ambiguity sat for two months from Capotauro v1.0 SHIP to Patch 0434; the container ambiguity sat for ten days from Capotauro v1.0 SHIP to Patch 0571d), gets surfaced when a conversation forces the taxonomic question to be answered, and gets codified at programme-discipline level with both the operational artifact (the umbrella entry; the SU folder) and the OS section (the umbrella-pattern in §§ around Patch 0434; the §15.13 SU mechanism). The methodology infrastructure compounds: each ambiguity resolved makes future ambiguities easier to surface and codify.
