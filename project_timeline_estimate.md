## Why this file exists

The CPP programme is large enough that the closure rate across any single session feels both substantial (a theorem registered, a paper shipped) and small (the total scope of "all physics that needs to be derived from CPP axioms" is enormous). Without an explicit tracker, the "are we making progress?" question is hard to answer with calibration.

This file gives a scoreboard. It is honest about uncertainty — the numbers are calibrated guesses, not measurements. It tracks meta-markers that can move, so future versions of this file can show motion rather than restate the same snapshot.

The intent is **not** to manufacture confidence about reaching an endpoint. The intent is to make the field-of-play visible while playing.

## Three endpoints

The question "is there an endpoint?" depends on what completion means. The programme has at least three distinct endpoints with different distances and different definability:

### Endpoint 1 — Layer 4 Standard Model parity

Every observed Standard Model phenomenon has either (a) a CPP-derived substrate handle propagated through to observable scales via Layer 4 EFT projection, or (b) a registered open-question entry explaining what would close it. The dimensionless ratios the Standard Model takes as inputs (fine-structure constant, weak mixing angle, fermion mass ratios, CKM + PMNS mixing matrices, neutrino mass scale, CP-violating phases, $\alpha_s$, Higgs vacuum-expectation-value in CPP units, baryon asymmetry, dark-matter abundance) each have a Layer 4 closure passing the four-condition test the programme has codified.

**Plausibly reachable.** This is what the SS, SM, EW, SR, QM, SD, SF flagship lines are converging toward.

**Diagnostic for approaching Endpoint 1:** the open-question registry at Layer 4 starts shrinking faster than new entries arrive; the methodologies catalogue stabilizes; reviewer engagement stops surfacing new substantive gaps.

### Endpoint 2 — Layer 1 substrate-dynamics derivation

Every FI-X entry promoted from Layer 2 substrate-physics input to Layer 1 theorem-level consequence of CPP primitive axioms. The Q1$'$+Q1$'$.A programme for the substrate primitive 4D direction $\hat{n}$ is the canonical first instance.

**Genuine bottleneck.** All three external reviewers at chirality continuum v1.0 SHIP independently identify this as the defining next gate for the Capotauro programme. The dynamical-substrate-law gate is the most load-bearing open theorem-level item in the entire programme.

**Diagnostic for approaching Endpoint 2:** the foundational-input stack contracts to a small number of axiom-level CPP primitives; conditional-closure discipline becomes unnecessary because theorems become unconditional.

### Endpoint 3 — Cosmological + gravitational + quantum-foundations integration

The framework reaches from substrate dynamics through quantum mechanics through the Standard Model up to cosmology + general relativity + dark sector + measurement-problem closure. "Theory of everything" framing in the strict sense.

**Most ambitious; may never have a clean endpoint.** Physics has historically rewarded humility about what we don't yet know we need to explain.

**Diagnostic for approaching Endpoint 3:** harder to define. Within stated scope, the trajectory is tractable; new sub-domains may emerge along the way.

### What the estimate is and isn't worth

**Worth:** A calibrated sense of where the programme sits on its trajectory. A scoreboard.

**Not worth:** A timeline prediction. The numerator (what's closed) is countable; the denominator (what CPP aims to cover) is partly defined by what gets discovered along the way. The closure ratio for any given sub-domain depends on what counts as "closed" — Layer 4 vs Layer 1 vs phenomenological-match-only.

### The pace observation

The rate of closure has been **accelerating** across the past few months. Five flagship SHIPs in seven weeks (SS-9 7 May; SF-4 v4.4 11 May; SF-2 v1.0 14 May; Capotauro v1.0 16 May; Capotauro v2.0 v1.0 19 May; **chirality continuum v1.0 20 May**) — six SHIPs if chirality continuum is counted separately from Capotauro v2.0. The remaining work is increasingly **gated by the same handful of structural items** (dynamical substrate law; Layer 1 promotion; cosmological connection) rather than scattered across the programme. Architecturally the programme may be further along than the 20-30% number suggests, because the work that remains funnels through fewer gates than the work already done.

## Diagnostic: open-question registry rate

The most useful framing isn't "how close to the end" but **"is the open-question registry shrinking faster than it's growing?"**

### Current state

The registry is still growing — every closure surfaces new sub-questions. But the **rate of new-question surfacing is slowing** as the framework's architecture stabilizes. New questions are increasingly clustering around the same gates (dynamical substrate law; Layer 1 promotion; cosmological connection) rather than scattered across the framework.

### Inflection-point criterion

When the new-question rate drops below the closure rate **and stays there for a few months of session time**, that's the diagnostic that we're past the inflection point. **We are not there yet.** But:

- New questions are increasingly clustered (good — suggests structural rather than scattered gaps)
- Closure rate has accelerated (5+ flagship SHIPs in 7 weeks)
- Methods catalogue infrastructure suggests acceleration will continue
- Conditional-closure framework gives a clear path forward

**Estimated time to inflection point: 6–18 months at current cadence, assuming dynamical-substrate-law gate doesn't unlock multiple promotions simultaneously (which it might).**

## Update protocol

This file is updated at:

1. **Every flagship paper v1.0 SHIP** — refresh meta-markers (theorem count, prediction count, problem registry counts); add row to "what just happened" log; reconsider area-estimate column.

2. **Every programme-architecture event** — cross-sector closure (CRO at SF-4 v4.0 + Capotauro v2.0 three-way + chirality continuum); new methodology framework (joint-paper format; methods catalogue introduction); sector synthesis (Endpoint 1 sub-domain consolidations).

3. **Every ~10 sessions as drift-audit refresh** — even without a SHIP event, periodic recalibration of the area estimate.

4. **Whenever a meta-marker drifts in a way that changes the trajectory** — e.g., if Q1$'$+Q1$'$.A closes, this file's "you are here" dot moves substantially, and the entry should reflect that with both the moved dot and a "this is what happened" anchor.

### What to NOT do at update time

- Do NOT replace the sub-domain breakdown columns wholesale without preserving the prior estimates — past estimates are the trajectory record.
- Do NOT round the area estimates to single numbers; the calibrated range is the honest answer.
- Do NOT remove the "what just happened" entries — they accumulate as the programme's progress narrative.
- Do NOT use this file to make timeline predictions in months/years — that's a different kind of estimate and the data doesn't support it. The closest this file gets to timeline is the "estimated time to inflection point" line above, which is a rough order-of-magnitude estimate.

## Notes on what could falsify this estimate

The estimates above are calibrated guesses. They could be wrong in either direction:

**Could be too high (we're further from completion than I estimate):**
- Endpoint 3 (cosmology + GR + measurement) may be far larger than I'm crediting; if the framework needs to derive general-relativistic curvature from substrate-dynamics at full rigor, that alone could be 30–50% of total scope.
- Dynamical-substrate-law gate may be much harder than 5–15 sessions — if Layer 1 derivation requires substrate-dynamics machinery the programme hasn't built yet, this could be 30–100 sessions.
- New sub-domains may emerge along the way that aren't in the current scoping.

**Could be too low (we're further along than I estimate):**
- If the substrate + group-theoretic foundations work transfers cleanly to remaining sectors (cosmology, gravity, dark sector) via the established cage-shell + cross-sector unification pattern, those sectors close faster than 5–15% per major closure.
- If Q1$'$+Q1$'$.A closes and unlocks multiple Layer 1 promotions simultaneously (which is structurally possible — $\hat{n}$ + $|\chi| = \varphi^{-3}$ are both registered as future-window from the same gate), this could be +15–25%.
- If the joint-paper format generalizes to other cross-sector closures (manifestations (iv)+(v); SM-5 cooperation; etc.), the closure rate continues accelerating.

The middle estimate of 20–30% is my best honest read. The 15% to 40% range is the calibrated confidence interval. We will know more after the next 5–10 flagship SHIPs.
