Before proceeding I read SF-2 v1.0 §sec:Wbracelet_thm directly. The W bracelet is *not* an H$_4$-orbit on unit vectors in $\mathbb{R}^4$. It is an orbit of **induced hexagonal 6-cycles in the 600-cell vertex graph**. Each bracelet is a 6-vertex subgraph isomorphic to $C_6$, with all 6 vertices at uniform radius $r_\mathcal{B} = 0.58779$ from a common centroid $\bar{v}$, and the centroid at $\|\bar{v}\| = \phi/2 \approx 0.809$ from origin.

This is a different geometric object from a unit vector at a face centroid direction (radius 0.934 on the face-centroid sphere; different sphere, different object).

## §4 The new geometric result

Computing bracelet centroid directions (unit vectors $\bar{v}_k / \|\bar{v}_k\|$ for each of 1200 bracelets) and comparing with face centroid directions:

- **The 1200 bracelet centroid directions reduce to exactly 120 distinct unit vectors on $S^3$.** Multiplicity 10 per direction. $10 \times 120 = 1200$ ✓.
- **Those 120 distinct directions are exactly the 120 vertex directions of the 600-cell.** Intersection cardinality 120, symmetric difference empty.
- **For each of the 10 bracelets at a given vertex direction $v_i$, the 6 hexagonal-cycle vertices are all edge-neighbors of $v_i$.** Verified by direct adjacency check on all 1200 bracelets.
- **The closest face centroid direction to any bracelet centroid direction sits at cosine exactly $\sqrt{(1+\phi)/3} \approx 0.934172$** — the cosine between any 600-cell vertex and the centroid of any face containing it (closed-form identity verified by elementary calculation).

The bracelet and face direction orbits on $S^3$ are **disjoint**. The casual cross-sector hypothesis ("same orbit") is refuted by computation. The W bracelet orbit and the face orbit are different H$_4$-orbits on $S^3$.

The structural identification follows: **each W bracelet is a Petrie hexagon of an SM-1 first-shell icosahedron** around one specific 600-cell vertex. The 12 edge-neighbors of any 600-cell vertex form a regular icosahedron (the SM-1 first-shell icosahedron, $z=12$ coordination); the regular icosahedron admits exactly 10 inscribed regular hexagons (one per pair of antipodal triangular faces of the icosahedron, of which there are 10); these are the bracelets. $10 \text{ bracelets per icosahedron} \times 120 \text{ icosahedra} = 1200$ bracelets total — matching SF-2's orbit count.

## §5 Implications for Q1' — the structural trade-off

Under Reading C, the substrate has a primitive 4D direction $\hat{n}$. The Q1' question is whether $\hat{n}$ is aligned with a vertex direction (substrate residual symmetry $H_3$) or with a face direction (substrate residual symmetry $D_6$).

**Vertex-aligned case (Finding C-W36 cross-sector argument):**
- 10 W bracelets sit on-axis with the substrate primitive direction at radius $\phi/2$.
- Their D$_6$ stabilizers are 10 Petrie-polygon subgroups of substrate residual $H_3$.
- The SM-1 first-shell icosahedron is preserved as the substrate's local geometry.
- Cross-sector unification with SF-2 is natural: the W bracelet emerges as a sub-stabilizer structure within the substrate's full residual icosahedral symmetry.
- The K3-doublet sits off-axis: if the K3 base is a triangular cluster at a face direction of the 600-cell substrate, its centroid is at cosine $\sqrt{(1+\phi)/3}$ from $\hat{n}$ (the vertex-to-containing-face cosine).

**Face-aligned case (Finding C-W35 from Patch 0417):**
- Substrate residual symmetry $D_6 = S_3 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$ exactly matches the K3-doublet stabilizer used in the Capotauro paper §6.4.
- K3-doublet sits on-axis with the substrate primitive direction (under the standard assumption that the K3 base is realized at a face direction).
- The W bracelet sits off-axis at cosine $\sqrt{(1+\phi)/3}$.
- The match between substrate residual symmetry and the paper's $D_6$ machinery is exact at the group level.

**The structural trade-off:** under either reading, *exactly one* of the two substrate objects (W bracelet vs K3-doublet) is on-axis with the substrate primitive direction, and the other is off-axis at cosine $\sqrt{(1+\phi)/3}$. The two readings *cannot* both put both objects on-axis simultaneously — they are at different H$_4$-orbits on $S^3$.

This is the substantive content of Finding C-W36: cross-sector unification between SF-2's W bracelet and Capotauro's K3-doublet is *not* a single-Reading-C alternative; the two substrate objects split the alternatives between them. Vertex-aligned puts W bracelet on-axis; face-aligned puts K3-doublet on-axis. Neither produces convergent cross-sector unification with both objects.

## §6 Q1'.A — the sub-question registered

The face-aligned case relied on an implicit assumption: that the K3 base of the Capotauro paper is geometrically realized as a triangular cluster of CPs whose centroid points in a face direction of the 600-cell substrate. The Capotauro paper §6.4 specifies the K3-doublet's algebraic structure ($S_3 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$ stabilizer) but does not pin down the geometric position of the K3 base in the substrate.

An alternative geometric realization: the K3 base could sit at a **3-fold axis triangle of the first-shell icosahedron** around a vertex. The icosahedron's 10 3-fold rotation axes each correspond to a triangle of 3 vertices among the 12 first-shell vertices; the $S_3$ stabilizer permutes those 3 vertices, and the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ acts as the antipodal map. This gives the same $D_6 = S_3 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$ algebraic structure as the face-realization, but at a different geometric locus (a 3-fold axis off the vertex direction, rather than a face direction off the vertex direction).

Under the face-realization, the K3 base is on-axis under face-aligned Reading C. Under the first-shell-3-fold-triangle realization, the K3 base is one of 10 sub-stabilizer-locations within the substrate's $H_3$ residual symmetry under vertex-aligned Reading C.

Q1'.A asks: *which K3-base geometric realization is the physical one*? Resolution of Q1'.A resolves Q1' immediately:
