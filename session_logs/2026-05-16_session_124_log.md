## §3 Confrontation with prior theory

Standing back, the closure is mostly mechanical: orbit-stabilizer + parabolic identification via the Coxeter diagram + $D_6$-subgroup-embedding checks across the four possible orbits. The mechanical work delivered the expected refutation of the §2.2 Claim and the expected identification of $H_3$ as the vertex-direction stabilizer. The surprise — the new-physics insight that emerged from doing the work — came in two pieces.

**First surprise.** The face-direction stabilizer in $H_4$ (the parabolic obtained by removing the second node of the H_4 Coxeter diagram, $o_2$) is $\mathbb{Z}_2 \times S_3 \cong D_6$, of order 12. This is exactly the K3-doublet stabilizer $D_6 = S_3 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$ used throughout the Capotauro paper's derivation. The $S_3$ factor of the face-stabilizer permutes the three vertices of the chosen face (i.e., acts on the K3 amplitudes); the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ factor is the perpendicular reflection (i.e., acts on the $\zeta$-parity of perpendicular wavefunctions). Both factors land exactly where the Capotauro paper's §6.4 K3-stabilizer decomposition places them. Under face-aligned $\hat{n}$, the substrate's residual symmetry is *not just consistent with* the paper's $D_6$ machinery — it *is* the paper's $D_6$ machinery. This is a strikingly elegant structural coincidence and a leading argument for the face-aligned reading of Reading C.

**Second surprise.** Even though Reading C posits a primitive 4D direction without specifying its orientation, the requirement of consistency with the paper's $D_6$ machinery **rules out** the cell-aligned and edge-aligned readings. The cell-direction stabilizer in $H_4$ is $S_4$ (order 24), whose only subgroup of order 12 is $A_4$, and $A_4$ has no element of order 6 — so $D_6$ does not embed in $S_4$. The edge-direction stabilizer is $D_5 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$ (order 20), whose element orders are $\{1, 2, 5, 10\}$ — no element of order 3 or 6, so $D_6$ does not embed there either. Reading C's geometry is thus structurally narrowed by group theory to two specific orbit-orientation choices, vertex or face, with the others *forbidden* by consistency with the paper's downstream machinery.

The pair of findings turns the Q1+Q2 closure from a routine verification into a genuine structural refinement of Reading C: the orientation of $\hat{n}$ is no longer an unconstrained parameter; it is narrowed to a binary choice (vertex vs face) with one option already providing a near-perfect structural fit to the paper's algebraic machinery.

## §4 Findings

1. **§2.2 Claim refuted.** The $H_4$-stabilizer of a unit vector $\hat{n} \in \mathbb{R}^4$ is *not* isomorphic to $I_4 = H_4^+$ (order 7,200). No $H_4$-orbit on $\mathbb{R}^4$ has stabilizer of that order. By orbit-stabilizer, the stabilizer's order is $|H_4|/|\text{orbit}| \in \{120, 20, 12, 24, 1\}$ for the vertex, edge, face, cell, and generic orbits respectively.

2. **Stabilizer identifications via Coxeter parabolic theory.** The four special-orbit stabilizers are: $H_3$ (vertex, order 120), $D_5 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$ (edge, order 20), $\mathbb{Z}_2 \times S_3 \cong D_6$ (face, order 12), $S_4$ (cell, order 24). These are the parabolic subgroups obtained by removing one node from the $H_4$ Coxeter diagram $o_1 - 5 - o_2 - o_3 - o_4$, with the removed node corresponding to the relevant fundamental weight.

3. **Origin of the §2.2 error: Operation A vs Operation B conflation.** Operation A is the chirality (enantiomorph) reduction $H_4 \to H_4^+$ (= $I_4$ in the paper notation), index 2, independent of any specific $\hat{n}$. Operation B is the primitive-direction reduction $H_4 \to \mathrm{stab}_{H_4}(\hat{n})$, which depends on the orbit of $\hat{n}$ and is at most $H_3$ (vertex case). Operation B implies Operation A (any specific $\hat{n}$ picks one enantiomorph), but the §2.2 Claim incorrectly identified the result of Operation B with the result of Operation A.

4. **Q2 closure: $D_6$ consistency narrows $\hat{n}$ to vertex or face.** Only vertex-aligned ($H_3 \supset D_6$ via $D_{3d}$) and face-aligned ($D_6$ exactly) orientations satisfy the requirement that the substrate's residual symmetry contain $D_6$. Cell-aligned and edge-aligned orientations are excluded by group theory.

5. **Finding C-W35: face-aligned $\hat{n}$ produces substrate residual symmetry exactly equal to K3-doublet stabilizer.** Under face-aligned $\hat{n}$, the substrate's residual symmetry $\mathbb{Z}_2 \times S_3 \cong D_6$ matches the K3-doublet stabilizer $S_3 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$ of the Capotauro paper §6.4 exactly, with the $S_3$ factor naturally identified as the K3-amplitude permutation symmetry and the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ factor naturally identified as the perpendicular-wavefunction $\zeta$-parity symmetry. The K3-doublet stabilizer is not merely a sub-stabilizer at a special location under this reading; it *is* the substrate symmetry. This is the leading structural argument for the face-aligned resolution of Q1' (vertex vs face).

6. **Q1' sub-question registered.** Vertex-aligned (maximum residual symmetry $H_3$, minimum perturbation from idealized $H_4$) vs face-aligned (substrate residual symmetry exactly equal to K3-doublet stabilizer, strikingly natural match). Both are consistent with the paper's machinery at v1.0. Selection between them requires further structural analysis or empirical input. Estimated 1-3 sessions to closure.
