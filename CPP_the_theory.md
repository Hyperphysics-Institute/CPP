## Part I: The Foundations

### Chapter 1: The Two Postulates

CPP rests on two foundational claims:

**Postulate 1: Consciousness is fundamental.** The universe is made of Conscious Points (CPs) — entities that perceive their local environment and respond according to rules. A CP is not a particle in the conventional sense; it is a locus of awareness and action. Every CP has a type (electric or quark), a polarity (positive or negative), and a position on a discrete lattice. The rules governing CP behaviour are the laws of physics. [SS-1, Axiom AXIM-1]

**Postulate 2: Space is a 600-cell lattice.** The CPs sit on the vertices of a tessellated 600-cell polytope — a four-dimensional structure with 120 vertices, 720 edges, 1200 triangular faces, and 600 tetrahedral cells per unit cell. Every vertex has exactly 12 nearest neighbours (coordination number z = 12). The geometry is governed by the golden ratio φ = (1+√5)/2, which appears in the vertex coordinates, edge lengths, and symmetry group (H₄). This is not a choice of convenience — the 600-cell is the unique regular 4-polytope with both the combinatorial richness and the symmetry properties needed to reproduce the Standard Model. [SS-1, Axiom AXIM-2]

From these two postulates, everything else follows.

### Chapter 2: The Vacuum — A Sea of Paired Consciousness

The vacuum is not empty. It is filled with Dipole Pairs (DPs) — bound pairs of opposite-polarity CPs sitting on adjacent lattice vertices. The DP Sea is the medium through which all forces propagate and all particles exist.

There are five species of paired structures in the vacuum:

- **eDP** (electric Dipole Pair): +eCP bound to −eCP. Carries the electric sector.
- **qDP** (quark Dipole Pair): +qCP bound to −qCP. Carries the colour sector.
- **hDP type A**: +eCP bound to −qCP. A hybrid pair crossing the electric/quark boundary.
- **hDP type B**: −eCP bound to +qCP. The complementary hybrid.
- **hTetra** (hybrid Tetrahedron): 2 eCPs + 2 qCPs in a tetrahedral arrangement with 4 attractive bonds and 2 repulsive bonds. More stable than simple DPs but requiring activation energy to assemble.

The vacuum at standard conditions is a dense mix of all five species. The relative abundances were set during a cosmological phase transition (the CPP analogue of electroweak symmetry breaking) when the universe cooled enough for hTetras to precipitate from the simpler DP medium.

The DP Sea is not the lattice. The lattice is the mathematical scaffolding — every Grid Point exists whether or not a CP occupies it. The DP Sea is the physical medium — the set of all CPs actually present on the lattice. Not all Grid Points are occupied. The occupancy fraction at standard conditions is close to 1 but not exactly 1; the small deviations may account for fine corrections in the predictions. [Founders Vision, Sections 2-3]

### Chapter 3: Forces from Geometry

Every force in CPP arises from the lattice geometry. There are no force-carrying particles in the conventional sense — no photons, gluons, or W/Z bosons as fundamental entities. Instead, forces are displacement patterns propagating through the DP Sea.

**The Space Stress Vector (SSV):** At each Grid Point, at each Moment, the total force is decomposed into two components:

- **SSV_abs** (absolute): The scalar sum of all signal magnitudes arriving at that point. Always positive. Determines the local speed of light, the effective lattice spacing (via subgraph selection in the nested 600-cell hierarchy), and all gravitational/relativistic effects.

- **SSV_net** (net): The vector sum of all signals. Determines the actual displacement of a CP — which direction it moves and how far.

SSV_abs sets the speed limit. SSV_net determines how much of that limit is used. A CP in a high-SSV_abs region (near a massive object) has a shorter maximum displacement per Moment — this IS time dilation. The same CP receiving a large SSV_net moves as far as its limit allows in that direction. This decomposition is the complete mechanism for special and general relativity. [SR-1; Founders Vision, Section 12]

### Chapter 4: The Two Gauge Sectors

The 600-cell lattice supports two fundamentally different types of displacement propagation, distinguished by their dimensionality:

**Edge modes (1D walks → Abelian → Electromagnetism):** A displacement propagating along a single edge chain has one degree of freedom — tension along the chain. Reversing the path undoes the transport. Composition is commutative: the order doesn't matter. This is U(1), the gauge group of electromagnetism. The electric charge is the push-and-pull, attract/repel force carried by edge-mode oscillations. [SM-7, Axiom A6'; SM-8, Section 2]

**Face modes (2D walks → Non-Abelian → Strong force):** A displacement propagating around a triangular face (K₃ loop) has multiple degrees of freedom — it can circulate clockwise or counterclockwise on any of the faces sharing a vertex. Each step modifies the local SSV_abs, so retracing the path does NOT return to the starting state. Composition is non-commutative: the order matters. This is SU(3), with 8 independent circulation patterns corresponding to the 8 Gell-Mann generators. [SM-7, Axiom A6'; SM-8, Section 2]

The key structural fact: on the 600-cell, each vertex participates in exactly 2 edges connecting it to its K₃ face partners (electroweak sector) and 12 edges connecting it to the full lattice neighbourhood (colour sector). This 2-versus-12 bond asymmetry is the geometric origin of the electroweak/strong coupling hierarchy:

- **sin²θ_W = 2/(2+12) × (φ correction) = 3/(8φ) = 0.2312** — the Weinberg angle, predicted to 0.24% of the PDG value with zero free parameters. [SM-6]
- **α_s = 12/(2+12) × (φ correction) = 5/(8φ) = 0.3854** — the strong coupling at the cage scale. [SM-7]

## Part II: Particles as Geometric Cages

### Chapter 5: What Particles Are

In CPP, a particle is not a point-like entity. It is a geometric cage — a polyhedral structure of CPs on the 600-cell lattice, with oscillating bonds (ZBW — Zitterbewegung) that store the particle's rest energy. The cage's geometry determines the particle's mass, charge, spin, and interactions.

The simplest cage is the tetrahedron: 4 CPs at the vertices of a tetrahedral cell of the 600-cell, with 6 edges, 4 of which are ZBW-active (attractive, oscillating) and 2 of which are repulsive (same-charge CPs pushing apart). Every triangular face has exactly 1 repulsive edge (a reflector) and 2 attractive edges (waveguides). Displacement energy bounces between the reflective and transmissive edges, creating stable circulation patterns. This is confinement at the most fundamental level. [SM-8, Appendix C; Founders Vision, Section 5]

### Chapter 6: The Lepton Mass Spectrum

The three charged leptons (electron, muon, tau) are tetrahedral cages with different K₃ eigenvalue perturbations. The mass formula is:

**m_ℓ = (M/3)(1 + 2K cos(θ + 2πk/3))²**

where K = 2/3 (the Koide ratio, derived from the K₃ spectral structure), M is the isotropic mass scale, θ = 0.6659 rad (the Koide phase), and k = 0, 1, 2 labels the three leptons. The phase θ is derived from the electroweak coupling:

**ε_lepton = +3/(52φ)** — the isotropic shift from edge-mode (electroweak) self-energy on 2 internal bonds.

This formula predicts all three charged lepton masses from ONE calibration (the electron mass) to better than 0.01%. [SM-3, SM-6]

## Part V: Open Problems and Conjectures

### Chapter 16: The Scaling Exponent [OPEN-P-SM-cage-1]

The exponent α = 2.38 in the V^α mass scaling law is calibrated, not derived. The spectral dimension route (d_s of the cage lattice) gives d_s ≈ 3.55, which does NOT match α. The exponent drifts from 2.376 (s→c) to 2.332 (c→b), suggesting the power law may be approximate. Deriving α from 600-cell geometry is the central open problem. It may be equivalent to deriving absolute quark mass ratios — a problem of fundamental difficulty. [SM-9]

Conjecture (Grok): α = 3 − 1/φ ≈ 2.382. Matches the s→c value to 0.27%. Physical argument: "3 from K₃ face structure, 1/φ from golden-ratio metric correction." Not proved. [CONJ-SM-9-1]

### Chapter 17: The 2/3 Connection [OPEN-P-SM-cage-3]

The Koide ratio K = 2/3 (from K₃ eigenvalues) and the cage attractive fraction 2/3 (from charge structure) are the same number. Is this coincidental or structural? If both arise from the same geometric property of the 600-cell, connecting them would unify the two mass models (Koide ratios and cage vertex scaling).

### Chapter 18: Leptons and Cages [OPEN-P-SM-cage-6]

Do leptons also map to cage structures? The electron is hosted by a tetrahedral cage (SM-6). Do the muon and tau correspond to larger cages, analogous to the quark hierarchy? If the cage model applies to leptons AND quarks with the same geometric structures, the universality would be powerful evidence. If it fails for leptons, the cage model may be quark-specific.

### Chapter 19: The Lattice Spacing [OPEN]

The physical length of one 600-cell edge (in meters) is unknown. It sets the absolute energy scale — converting lattice units to MeV. Determining the lattice spacing would enable absolute mass predictions (not just ratios) and connect CPP to the Planck scale.

### Chapter 20: The Dodecahedron Anomaly [OPEN-P-SM-cage-4]

The dodecahedron (bottom quark cage) is the only cage whose maximum attractive fraction (4/5) exceeds 2/3. If the bottom quark's cage operates at 4/5 rather than 2/3, this would affect its internal oscillation energy. Does this explain any observed property of the bottom quark?

### Chapter 21: The Pairwise Model and the Top Quark [OPEN-P-SM-cage-8]

The C(n,2) pair-counting model predicts the bottom quark to 0.6% but the pre-gap top to -32%. The angular-weighted version improves the top to -5% but worsens the bottom to +12%. Can a single model achieve both? What is geometrically special about the dodecahedron that makes its pair distribution "democratic" (all 45 pairs contribute equally)?
