## The Nexus Is Not a Hidden Variable — It Is a Global Constraint

The most philosophically important result in QM-3 is the no-signaling proof (THEO-QM-5) alongside the Bell violation derivation (THEO-QM-4). The Nexus maintains the non-separable singlet state |Ψ⁻⟩ globally at every Absolute Moment, which is what allows |S| = 2√2. But because the Nexus operates atemporally — outside the spacetime it coordinates — it transmits no causal signal between the two measurement sites. The correlations are real and non-local in the standard sense, but no information travels.

This resolves the tension with relativity that makes the EPR/Bell situation philosophically puzzling in standard QM. In CPP, the Nexus is neither a local hidden variable (Bell's theorem forbids those) nor a superluminal signal (no-signaling is proved). It is a third category: an atemporal global constraint that maintains correlations without sending information.


## Superdeterministic at the Foundational Level, Not Required for Operational QM

CPP is superdeterministic in the strict Bell-technical sense: the Nexus is a global constraint that co-determines both the microscopic CP histories (the hidden variables λ) and the macroscopic apparatus configurations (the measurement settings a, b). Therefore the measurement independence assumption P(λ | a, b) = P(λ) is violated. This is established rigorously in the SD series (SD-1 through SD-5).

However, at the operational QM level, the standard Bell violations, Tsirelson bound, and no-signaling derived in QM-3 arise purely from non-separable amplitudes on the CP graph. The three theorems of QM-3 are proved in the ε = 0 effective theory — treating measurement settings as free parameters — without invoking measurement-setting correlations. The superdeterministic corrections are O(ε) ~ 10⁻²⁶ and do not affect leading-order QM phenomenology.

The Nexus is therefore necessary for everything — it is the underlying constraint structure that defines both the CP dynamics and the apparatus histories. But the *superdeterministic character* of the Nexus (its violation of measurement independence) is not what drives Bell violations at leading order. The violations come from the entanglement-like non-separability on the CP graph; the SD correlations are an automatic, subleading byproduct of the same global law.

This is the same hierarchy that appears throughout CPP: the Nexus is one mechanism producing consequences at two scales. At the QM scale (ε = 0), it maintains non-separable states and produces standard quantum correlations. At the foundational scale (ε ~ 10⁻²⁶), it also correlates hidden variables with measurement settings — a correction that is present but 22 orders of magnitude below current experimental sensitivity. Thomas Abshier's characterisation of the Nexus as "the Mind of God synchronizing the Moment" captures this unity: the same clock tick that maintains the singlet also correlates λ with θ. One act, two consequences at different scales.


## The Three Levels of Bell in CPP

Bell's 1964 theorem rests on three assumptions: outcome independence, parameter independence (together = locality), and measurement independence (= free choice of settings). Standard nonlocal QM keeps measurement independence and gives up locality. Superdeterministic models keep locality and give up measurement independence.

CPP occupies a distinctive position: the Nexus is non-local (it is a global constraint), and it also violates measurement independence (it correlates λ with settings). CPP therefore violates *both* of Bell's assumptions simultaneously — but for different purposes. The non-locality (global constraint) is what produces the standard Bell violations. The measurement-independence violation (SD correlations) produces only the O(ε) correction.

This explains why QM-3's theorems are exact despite CPP being superdeterministic: the leading-order physics uses only the non-locality of the Nexus, not its measurement-dependence structure. The SD corrections are real but negligible at macroscopic detector scales.
