## Part 2: Mathematical Preliminaries — H₄ and the Golden-Ratio Field

**Step 3 — The 600-cell has H₄ symmetry.**
The 600-cell is a regular 4-dimensional polytope with 120 vertices, 720 edges, 1200 triangular faces, and 600 tetrahedral cells. Its symmetry group is H₄, the largest of the non-crystallographic Coxeter groups, with order |H₄| = 14,400. H₄ contains the subgroup chain:

    H₄ ⊃ H₃ ⊃ H₂ ≅ I₂(5)

where H₃ is the icosahedral group (order 120) and H₂ is the dihedral group of the regular pentagon (order 10).

**Step 4 — All 600-cell coordinates lie in the golden-ratio field ℚ(φ).**
The vertex coordinates of the 600-cell lie in ℚ(φ) = ℚ(√5), the quadratic extension of the rationals generated by the golden ratio φ = (1+√5)/2. All inner products between pairs of vertices, and hence all angles between vertex vectors, have cosines in ℚ(φ). The distinct inner product values are {0, ±1/2, ±1/(2φ), ±φ/2, ±1/φ², ±1}. An angle θ is H₄-special if and only if cos θ ∈ ℚ(φ).

## Part 3: D₅ Projected Symmetry (THEO-SD-1)

**Step 5 — The measurement angle lives on a circle S¹.**
In a Bell experiment, the physically relevant variable is the relative measurement angle θ = θ_A − θ_B. This angle is a coordinate on the circle S¹ = [0°, 360°). The angular correction f_{H₄}(θ) is a function on this circle.

**Step 6 — H₄ projects to D₅ on the measurement-angle circle.**
THEO-SD-1: When the full H₄ symmetry group (acting on the 4D 600-cell) is projected onto the 1D measurement-angle circle S¹, the residual symmetry is the dihedral group D₅ — the symmetry group of the regular pentagon, order 10.

The proof works through the subgroup chain: H₄ contains H₃ (icosahedral, 120 elements), which contains H₂ ≅ I₂(5) (pentagonal, 10 elements). The projection from 4D to the 1D angle circle collapses H₃ to its largest abelian subgroup compatible with the circle topology, which is the cyclic group C₅ (5-fold rotation). Including the reflection symmetry (θ → −θ, corresponding to swapping Alice and Bob) gives D₅ = C₅ ⋊ ℤ₂.

**Step 7 — D₅ symmetry forces the leading Fourier component to be cos(5θ).**
D₅ has a 5-fold rotation axis. Any function on S¹ that is D₅-invariant must have Fourier components at multiples of 360°/5 = 72°. The lowest-order non-trivial D₅-invariant function on the circle is cos(5θ), corresponding to the 5-fold icosahedral structure of the 600-cell.

## Part 4: Fourier Decomposition (THEO-SD-2)

**Step 8 — f_{H₄} has a specific two-component Fourier structure.**
THEO-SD-2: The angular correction function has the Fourier series:

    f_{H₄}(θ) = A₅ cos(5θ) + A₃ cos(3θ) + A₁₀ cos(10θ) + ...

Two independent symmetry sources contribute:

The 5n-fold terms (cos 5θ, cos 10θ, ...) arise from the 5-fold icosahedral symmetry of H₄ — the pentagonal structure of the 600-cell projected to S¹.

The 3-fold term (cos 3θ) arises from the independent tetrahedral cell symmetry of the 600-cell. Each of the 600 cells is a regular tetrahedron with C₃ rotational symmetry about each vertex-to-opposite-face axis. This tetrahedral structure produces a separate cos(3θ) contribution that is not a subharmonic of the pentagonal series.

Higher harmonics (cos 10θ, cos 15θ, ...) are suppressed by φ⁻ⁿ factors from the golden-ratio geometry of the vertex coordinates.

## Part 6: The CHSH Blind Spot (THEO-SD-4)

**Step 11 — 45° is not an H₄-special angle.**
THEO-SD-4: The CHSH-optimal angle θ = 45° is not an H₄-special angle. Proof:

cos(45°) = 1/√2. Suppose 1/√2 ∈ ℚ(√5). Then √2 ∈ ℚ(√5), which requires ℚ(√2) ⊆ ℚ(√5). Both are degree-2 extensions of ℚ. But √10 = √2 × √5 ∉ ℚ(√5), since 10 is not of the form 5k² for any rational k. Therefore ℚ(√2) ⊄ ℚ(√5), contradiction. QED.

Since cos(45°) ∉ ℚ(φ), 45° is not an extremum of f_{H₄}. The CHSH test measures at its least informative points.

**Step 12 — All four CHSH angles are non-H₄-special.**
The standard CHSH experiment measures E(θ) at {0°, 45°, 90°, 135°}. None of these is an H₄-special angle with a guaranteed extremum. The CHSH test provides the weakest possible test of the CPP angular correction — it was designed to test QM, not to detect departures from QM.

**Step 13 — The optimal experiment is a continuous angular scan.**
The CPP signal is maximal when θ is scanned continuously across [0°, 180°] with angular resolution better than ~5° (to resolve the 36° periodicity of cos(5θ)). The ratio test δE(36°)/δE(120°) ≈ −1.065 is a dimensionless prediction that does not depend on the unknown amplitude A₅ — it tests the angular structure alone. This ratio is the most direct falsifiable prediction of the SD series.
