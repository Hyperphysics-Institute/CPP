## II. Free Will and the Red Herring

The most common objection to superdeterminism is philosophical: if the measurement settings θ_A and θ_B are correlated with the particle state λ from the Big Bang onward, then the experimenter's "free choice" of settings is illusory. This objection confuses two things.

The first is metaphysical free will — the question of whether human choices are ultimately determined by prior physical states. This is a 2500-year-old philosophical debate (Aristotle, Augustine, Hume, Kant, Libet) that has nothing specifically to do with quantum mechanics. Every deterministic physical theory — Newtonian mechanics, general relativity, classical electrodynamics — makes the same "prediction" that choices are physically determined. The fact that quantum mechanics appeared to rescue indeterminism was an accident of 20th-century physics, not a settled philosophical conclusion.

The second is experimental free choice — the requirement that the choice of measurement settings be statistically independent of the hidden variables, which is a technical assumption in Bell's theorem. CPP violates this assumption while leaving the philosophical question of free will entirely untouched. The experimenter's choice of θ is determined by the experimenter's brain state, which is a macroscopic DP Sea configuration. The Nexus correlates all DP Sea configurations — the particle's, the apparatus's, and the experimenter's. This is not a violation of free will; it is a statement that all physical subsystems are connected through the Nexus constraint. The experimenter still deliberates, reasons, and chooses — the choice is simply not statistically independent of the particle's history, because both are constrained by the same global conservation law.

The honest philosophical position: CPP is a deterministic theory. Whether determinism is compatible with meaningful human agency is a philosophical question that CPP neither settles nor needs to settle. What CPP does is give the correlations a physical mechanism — the Nexus — rather than leaving them as an unexplained cosmic coincidence.

## IV. Atemporality and the Nexus

The Nexus operates "atemporally" — it enforces the global DI-bit conservation constraint at every Absolute Moment simultaneously, without any signal propagating between events. This raises a deep question about the nature of physical law.

In standard physics, conservation laws are consequences of symmetries (Noether's theorem): conservation of energy follows from time-translation symmetry; conservation of momentum from spatial translation symmetry. The Nexus constraint — global DI-bit conservation — is different: it is not a consequence of a continuous symmetry but a discrete constraint on the information content of the lattice. It is closer in spirit to a holographic constraint (the total information content of any closed surface is fixed) than to a Noether current.

The atemporality of the Nexus is not action at a distance. It does not transmit information between spacelike-separated events. THEO-QM-5 (no-signalling) proves that Alice's marginal probability is independent of Bob's measurement setting — the Nexus correlation is a constraint, not a signal. This distinction is the same as the distinction between the constraint "total charge is conserved" (which applies atemporally to every physical process) and the claim "charges at point A send information to charges at point B." Conservation laws are atemporal; CPP's Nexus is a conservation law.
