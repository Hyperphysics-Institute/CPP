# PART 2: FAQ

### Q1. "Doesn't Bell's theorem rule out superdeterminism?"

Bell's theorem rules out *local* hidden-variable theories with *statistically independent* hidden variables and measurement settings. Superdeterminism denies the statistical independence assumption. Bell's theorem therefore does not rule it out — it assumes it is ruled out by "free will" or "free choice of settings." CPP's Nexus provides a specific physical mechanism for the correlation between λ and θ that does not require free-will violation in any philosophical sense.

### Q2. "If the correction is 10⁻²⁵, can CPP ever be tested?"

The angular structure and N_q scaling are more accessible than the absolute magnitude.
