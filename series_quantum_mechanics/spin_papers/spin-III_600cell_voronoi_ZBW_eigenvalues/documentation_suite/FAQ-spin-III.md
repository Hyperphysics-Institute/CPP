# FAQ — spin-III

**Q1. Didn't the earlier papers already derive spin-½? What does this add?**
Spin I and II worked in continuum space. CPP space is a lattice; this paper
proves the lattice cell selects — rather than merely tolerates — the mode
those papers used. Without it, the arc's foundation assumed what CPP denies
(a continuum).

**Q2. What exactly is the "true Voronoi cell," and why a dodecahedron?**
The 600-cell's dual is the 120-cell, so each lattice vertex's territory is
the corresponding regular dodecahedron — twelve pentagonal faces whose
normals point at the twelve icosahedral nearest neighbors. Founder ruling A1
retired the earlier 24-cell domain as a prior worker's initiative.

**Q3. The lattice spacing is Planck-scale; why does any of this matter?**
It almost doesn't — that's §4's point: discreteness corrections enter at
(l_P/r_th)² ≈ 7×10⁻⁴⁵. What the lattice owes is SELECTION (which mode
family exists), not corrections. Selection is exactly what the theorems
supply.

**Q4. What was wrong with the original March computation?**
Two things. It never encoded the central node (CP Exclusion), so it solved
the closed resonator; and a free boundary on ψ is not Spin II's free
boundary on u = rψ (they give different spectra: tan kR = kR vs (2n−1)π/2).
The corrected instrument discretizes the u-equation directly, closing both.

**Q5. Isn't the measurement circular — you built the instrument that
confirmed your expectation?**
The verdict rule (MODE2-RECOVERED windows, density-stability gate) was
frozen and committed one patch BEFORE the domain ruling that enabled the
run, and the instrument was validated on a sphere where the answer is
exact. The expectation was on record before the data existed.

**Q6. Why is the second radial mode the TENTH global mode? Doesn't that
mean eight competitors beat it?**
Yes — and identifying them is the theorem's fingerprint. They are exactly
the l=1 (3 states) and l=2 (5 states) multiplets, which the icosahedral
group excludes from the invariant sector: no I_h-invariant harmonic exists
below l = 6. They compete in the spectrum, not in the sector the ZBW
scalar breathing mode occupies.

**Q7. Is the trivial-irrep restriction an assumption?**
It is inherited from Spin II, whose resonator equation was radial (scalar)
from the start. The panel adjudicated this exact question (Q7, 3–2
JUSTIFIED-AS-INHERITED). The theorem answers what the lattice does within
that sector; it does not claim to prove why nature selects the sector.

**Q8. How strong is the "20% agreement" between theorem and measurement?**
Deliberately understated: the paper calls it consistency, not
decomposition, because the instrument's own declared approximation enters
at the same O(ε₆²) order. The panel's stricter wording is adopted verbatim,
and the residual's discharge path (propagate instrument approximations;
show the uncertainty envelope contains the residuals) is registered.

**Q9. What would falsify this result?**
A demonstration that the invariant sector of the true cell contains a mode
between the radial Modes 1 and 2 (breaking the Selection Theorem), or a
measured node/antinode shift at O(ε₆) rather than O(ε₆²) (breaking
Protection), or an I_h-invariant harmonic with 1 ≤ l ≤ 5 (breaking the
Lemma — this one is exact arithmetic, so it would break group theory).

**Q10. What remains open?**
Two registered residuals: a fully non-perturbative-in-ε₆ bound (rigor
upgrade; discharge path specified) and robustness under dynamic
symmetry-breaking (lattice defects, boundary fluctuations) — a genuinely
new physical question, Gemini provenance.
