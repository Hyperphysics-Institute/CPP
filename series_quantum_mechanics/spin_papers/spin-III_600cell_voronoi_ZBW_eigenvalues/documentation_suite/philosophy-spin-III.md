# Philosophy — spin-III

**Discreteness without corrections.** The paper's quietest lesson is a
two-scale argument: the lattice is 10⁻²² of the resonator, so its
corrections are invisible (7×10⁻⁴⁵) — yet its SYMMETRY is fully load-bearing
at every scale. Discreteness matters not by perturbing the answer but by
dictating which answers exist. This inverts the usual lattice-artifact
anxiety: the 600-cell geometry is not a numerical nuisance to be
extrapolated away; it is the reason the mode family is what it is.

**Selection by exclusion.** The Selection Theorem's structure — symmetry
removes competitors from the SECTOR, not the spectrum — is a recurring CPP
motif (compare the icosahedral l=4 exclusion in the lattice-isotropy arc).
The universe, on this picture, does not fine-tune the winning mode; it
forbids the alternatives.

**Instrument honesty as method.** Two semantic traps (the missing node;
ψ-Neumann vs u-Neumann) were found by the programme's own diagnosis, not by
reviewers, and both are documented with reproducible demonstrations rather
than silently fixed. The frozen-verdict discipline (readings declared
before data) and the two-legs-cross-confirm structure (measurement and
theorem, neither knowing the other's number) are the epistemology the
multi-AI review methodology is built to reward.

**What "proved" means here.** The paper distinguishes exact arithmetic
(the character Lemma), theorem-conditional results (Selection at the
actual cell's ε₆; Protection perturbatively), measurement under frozen
rules, and inherited premises — and says which is which in its own ledger.
The claim "the lattice must choose Mode 2" is earned at stated strength,
with the two residuals registered rather than waved away.
