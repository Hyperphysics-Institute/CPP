# Tier-4 reasoning fragment — Patch 2999 (CONV-014: the re-ground review dispatch)

**Captured at-patch, producing window.**

The dispatch's design followed the CONV-013 skeleton's core lesson:
freeze the questions while outcome-ignorant, and pre-register the
decision tree so the bar cannot move on vibes. The QM arc's version
of "outcome-blind" is weaker than the DM campaign's (there is no
pending dataset whose values I don't know), but the same discipline
applies to the RULING: the conditions under which the citation bar
lifts are written down before any reviewer speaks, so adjudication is
a table-lookup against verdicts rather than a persuasion exercise.

The honest-disclosure section (§3) took the most deliberation, and
two entries deserve explanation:

**D-4 (plane leakage) is a weakness I found while writing the
dispatch, not before.** The unitarity Proposition treats
multiplication by −i as a quarter-turn in the distinguished plane —
exact for planar rotations. But nothing in the shipped Proposition
verifies that the per-Moment GP refresh keeps SSV_net IN the plane.
If contributions arriving from patterns with different ZBW axes tilt
the local vector out of plane, the U(1) reduction (a planar angle)
is only approximate, and the error term is unquantified. I could
have quietly added a paragraph to QM-1 v2.0 patching this; instead
it goes to the panel as a disclosed weak point, because (a) the
symmetric-honesty discipline says worker-found defects are disclosed
before reviewers find them, and (b) a reviewer-independent assessment
of its severity is worth more than my patch. If the panel sustains it
as serious, it becomes a named residual (R-4) with the objection as
its charter.

**D-5 (grade self-check) is an invitation to attack my own grading
choice.** I graded unitarity as Proposition-at-sketch rather than
Theorem precisely to avoid the v1.x overclaim class — but
under-grading's mirror error is treating a self-assigned modest grade
as self-certifying. The panel is explicitly asked whether even
sketch grade overclaims given the named inputs.

The execution-verification note is the round's structural difference
from the DM dispatches: no scripts, no keys, no SCRIPT-EXECUTED
credit available. Stating this up front prevents the failure mode
where a reviewer manufactures a script-execution claim for status
(the DeepSeek fabrication class) — there is nothing to execute, so
any such claim is self-indicting. REASONED-VERIFIED per step, with
an explicit checked-vs-accepted declaration, is the round's currency.

Question design notes: Q1 splits the attack between A-1 and A-2
because they fail differently — A-1 could be "permissive not
selective" (U(1) compatibility isn't U(1) necessity from THIS
register) without A-2 being numerology, and vice versa. Q2 names the
circularity risk with QM-5 explicitly because I flagged it myself in
the 2997 fragment (the bridge "will be derivable there rather than
here") — if the panel judges that deferral circular rather than
merely pending, the decision tree routes B-QMRG-1 to mandatory
derivation before any bar movement. Q5 separates revision-integrity
(a) from the ruling (b) so a reviewer can endorse the texts while
holding the bar, which I expect to be the modal sophisticated
position.

Copilot's fresh-conversation condition carries over from CONV-013's
diagnosis (window replay), applied per-round; the third-strike
consequence is restated so the seat's status is unambiguous. The
integrity ledger is quoted in the dispatch header itself so the
founder does not have to track it separately when adjudication-time
comes.

One deliberate omission: the dispatch does not ask the panel whether
FI-QMRG-1 "feels right" as physics — it asks whether the arguments
select it. The founder's uncertainty ("I am not certain what the
quantum phase phi is") is part of the registered record; the round's
job is to test whether the worker's adjudication of that uncertainty
was sound, not to poll intuitions.
