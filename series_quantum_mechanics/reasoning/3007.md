# Tier-4 reasoning fragment — Patch 3007 (CONV-016 dispatch, the potentially terminal round)

**Captured at-patch, producing window.**

The dispatch's central design problem was the meaning of "terminal."
A round that could resolve the sector's conditionality invites two
opposite failure modes: a tree so demanding that resolution is
unreachable regardless of merit (conditionality as a ratchet), or a
tree so eager that momentum substitutes for closure (the W-6 worry,
which I disclosed as a standing license for suspicion through every
question). The pre-registered E-6 mapping is the answer to both: the
CONV-015 adjudication already SET the trigger (T1 resolved + T2
disposed), so this round's tree simply instantiates that promise —
four simultaneous majorities (Q1, Q2, Q3, Q5) with the conditionality
question asked directly rather than inferred. If the panel judges
the trigger met, the freeze honors the prior round's own terms; if
not, the RETAIN verdict must state the NEW trigger precisely, so
conditionality can never become an undated debt.

Section 4 exists because "resolve" would otherwise be misread in both
directions. Resolution is an exit from SPECIAL status, not a claim of
completeness: eta's lattice derivation, UNIQ, and the watch condition
persist as ordinary open items either way. Writing this down before
outcomes are known prevents the two post-hoc temptations — inflating
resolution into "the QM sector is finished" or deflating a RETAIN
into "nothing was achieved."

The Q5 arc-integrity question is new to this round and deliberately
global: the previous rounds audited packages; this one audits the
ARC as shipped at HEAD, because a terminal ruling should not be
issued over a corpus that still contains an overclaimed line
somewhere in a registry or glossary entry. Its remediation clause
(fix, then re-poll Q3/Q4 only) is pre-registered so that a single
citable overclaim triggers repair rather than either a cover-up
incentive or a full-arc restart.

Key design was the round's second problem, and W-7 makes the panel
part of its audit. The 3004 flaw taught that keys derived from
printed outputs are theater; KEY-I and KEY-J are post-checks
RNG-stream sentinels — their values depend on the generator's state
AFTER all printed draws, are on no printed curve, and were computed
into variables that nothing prints. I have not evaluated them; the
adjudicating session reruns. The dispatch also says plainly that
stdout reproduction proves nothing this round (both stdouts are
embedded in committed records), which converts last round's
embarrassment into this round's explicit rule: the keys carry the
whole burden. Inviting the panel to attack the key DEFINITIONS
themselves (any residual reconstructibility route) is the cheapest
insurance available — if a route exists, better a reviewer finds it
in the dispatch phase than after credit is granted.

The Copilot third-event rule is frozen here prospectively, exactly
as the CONV-015 adjudication flagged: two events in two rounds in
two distinct fabrication classes is a pattern, and the frozen
stale-redelivery rule does not cover it; extending a frozen rule
retroactively was refused at 3004, and this dispatch does what CAN
be done — freeze the new rule for future conduct, disclosed to all
seats identically. The full integrity ledger sits in the header as
in both prior rounds; the discipline that a seat's history is
context-not-prejudice travels with it.

One structural note for the adjudicating session: if the round DOES
resolve, the enactment patch must handle the conditional-note
mechanics carefully — notes are removed only for claims whose
obligations closed; the persisting items (eta, UNIQ, W-MULTILINK-1)
keep the mechanism available. The failure mode to avoid is a global
find-and-delete of conditional notes on a resolution verdict; the
notes were always per-obligation, and their removal must be too.
