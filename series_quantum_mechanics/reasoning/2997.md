# Tier-4 reasoning fragment — Patch 2997 (QM-1 v2.0: the re-grounded revision)

**Captured at-patch, producing window.**

The revision's discipline was set by one sentence from the 2988 audit:
the theorem never consumed per-bit phase. That meant the correct
revision touches the grounding layer and ONLY the grounding layer —
the theorem statement, its proof, the T = ℏ²/(4mΔs²) remark, the
Madelung proposition, and the Appendix A Laplacian are byte-identical
to v1.1. Any temptation to "improve" the surviving mathematics while
in the file was resistance-tested and declined; a re-grounding that
also edits the math would blur exactly the separability the audit
established.

The three residuals closed at the grades they honestly reach:

**R-1 (the distinguished plane) closed by letting the pattern supply
it.** The worry was that reading φ as a planar orientation smuggles in
a plane field as hidden structure. The resolution: a massive pattern
executes ZBW at ω_C (corpus-established), and any nondegenerate
periodic orbit of a vector observable sweeps a 2-plane. The plane is
not an input; it is a property of the orbit the pattern already has.
The pleasant surprise was the degenerate case: where the periodic
component vanishes, φ is undefined — and that locus is ρ → 0, the
nodes, which is exactly where standard QM says phase is undefined
(vortex cores). A potential embarrassment turned out to be a
consilience check: phase singularities occur precisely where the
register carrying the phase vanishes. I put that in the paper because
it is the kind of thing that is either a coincidence or a sign the
identification is right, and reviewers should see it.

**R-2 (linearity) closed via a realization about what SSV_net's
definition already says.** SSV_net is DEFINED as the vector sum of
arriving contributions. Vector addition of planar vectors with
magnitudes √ρ_j and angles φ_j IS complex addition of the ψ_j — the
substrate's summation rule and the Hilbert-space superposition rule
are the same operation once the identification is made. What remains
input-dependent is the amplitude–count bridge: |ψ|² = ρ requires
|SSV_net,⊥|² ∝ ρ, the coherent-mode amplitude-∝-√N relation. I
registered that as B-QMRG-1 rather than deriving it, because it is a
regime statement (coherent, weak-field — where the Schrödinger limit
lives) and because it is the same harmonic structure QM-5's mode
quantization presupposes, so it will be derivable there rather than
here. Side effect worth recording: B-QMRG-1 dissolves the F-SW-5
glossary tension — under it, "amplitude² of the field" and "count of
DI-bits" are the SAME number, so the glossary's Born Rule language
was not wrong, just stated in the dual variable. The glossary edit
(next patch) can say both and mean one thing.

**R-3 (the potential term) closed by inheritance.** ω_local =
(mc² + V_SSV)/ℏ is the first-order modulation of the rotation rate
under E = ħν_C — which SF-6 ships at Tier 1 (companion-grade
identification). The same SSV→tick machinery that gives time dilation
gives the potential term. One remark in the paper, citing the
machinery rather than re-deriving it.

The unitarity proposition is the revision's one genuinely new formal
object, and its grading cost me the most thought. Clauses (i) norm
conservation and (ii) reversibility are corpus-level facts. Clause
(iii) linearity consumes B-QMRG-1 and the small-signal regime. An
invertible norm-preserving linear map is unitary — that final step is
mathematics. So the honest grade is derivation-sketch conditional on
named inputs, stated as such in a Grade remark inside the paper. The
alternative — calling it a theorem — would repeat the exact sin the
audit convicted v1.x of: a grounding claim stronger than its inputs.
The −i-as-quarter-turn reading I kept, because under FI-QMRG-1 it is
not a metaphor: multiplication by −i literally rotates the planar
register by π/2, and "generator of rotation" is what the Schrödinger
−iH/ℏ is.
