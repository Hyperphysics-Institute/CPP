# Tier-4 reasoning fragment — Patch 3003 (CONV-015 dispatch)

**Captured at-patch, producing window.**

The round's new machinery is the withheld-key discipline, live in this
arc for the first time, and the key-selection problem was more
interesting than expected. The precedent (KEY-E/KEY-F) requires
unprinted internals of committed deterministic scripts — values the
worker has not evaluated, so that possession of the key is proof of
execution rather than proof of reading the dispatch. My scripts print
every headline number, which initially looked like a design failure
for key purposes. The solution was noticing that np.polyfit returns
[slope, intercept] and both scripts print only the slope: the
intercepts are deterministic (seeded RNGs), computed on every run,
and never surfaced. KEY-G and KEY-H are those intercepts. I have
deliberately not evaluated them — writing this fragment is the last
act before commit, and the discipline's whole value is that the
adjudicating session reruns rather than remembers.

The disclosed-weak-points section took the most judgment, and W-5 and
W-6 are the entries worth explaining.

W-5 names the move I am least certain survives attack: the B1
derivation settles a dynamical fork (linear vs quadratic response) by
citing a ratified ONTOLOGY clause (AP-2's intensity-like language).
My own defense is registry consistency — the substrate dynamics must
realize the ontology the programme has ratified, so a dynamical
reading that contradicts a ratified clause is excluded — but I can
construct the counter myself: ratification fixed what the words MEAN,
not what the dynamics DOES, and using a definitional ruling to
discharge a physics question is exactly the kind of laundering a
hostile reviewer should test. Rather than pre-arguing it in the
package, the dispatch hands the panel the sharpest version of the
objection. If it sustains, the fork needs a dynamical closure (the
toy's phase-locking result is the seed of one: the rival reading
requires an arrival-phase discipline the substrate lacks — that
argument is dynamical, not definitional, and could carry the fork
alone if AP-2 is disallowed as a premise).

W-6 is the self-suspicion entry: both packages were produced,
verified, and self-graded within a day of the adjudication they
answer. Speed is genuinely a risk factor — adjudication-shaped
reasoning is a failure mode where the worker proves what is provable
NEAR the obligation rather than the obligation itself. W-1 is the
concrete instance to test: L-1's exactness claim is scoped to "the
transport class the shipped model uses," and whether that answers
D-4 or relocates it is precisely a question about whether I proved
what was asked. I think the case split answers it — the microscopic
question is then handled by L-2's bound and L-3's identification —
but the panel should reach that conclusion or refuse it without my
thumb on the scale.

Tree design notes: Q3 (the mutual-support prohibition) is decoupled
from the bar outcome on purpose — it is a citation-hygiene rule whose
discharge condition is the EXISTENCE of an independent derivation,
not the derivation's grade, so a WIDEN-PARTIAL round can still
discharge it. Q4 puts OPEN-QMRG-UNIQ's blocking status to the panel
rather than ruling it worker-side: CONV-014 registered the obligation
without classifying it, and the classification determines whether
full lift is even reachable this round, so it must be a panel
question, asked before outcomes are known. The FULL-LIFT conjunction
is deliberately demanding (four simultaneous majorities); the
realistic modal outcome is WIDEN-PARTIAL, and the tree makes that
path well-defined (conservative intersection again — the CONV-014
scope mechanism, which worked).
