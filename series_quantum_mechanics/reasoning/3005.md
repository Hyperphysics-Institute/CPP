# Tier-4 reasoning fragment — Patch 3005 (MULTILINK: the refresh-law exclusion)

**Captured at-patch, producing window.**

The adjudication called T1 "a registry-and-axioms analysis before it
is a computation," and the work confirmed that ordering in a way I
want to record: the entire resolution was found by GREP before any
mathematics was done. The question "does the committed refresh permit
multi-edge-correlated kernels" dissolves once the ratified clauses
are read side by side — the register is defined as a SUM (additive by
the meaning of the word), the messenger carries one edge's data and
nothing else (AP-2 minimality), and the per-Moment reset closes the
receiver-memory route. Multi-edge correlation is not a hard case
under the committed law; it is not IN the committed law. The theorem
is about the registry, and its premises are quotations.

The honest work was then twofold. First, the chirality audit — the
one place I genuinely expected trouble. The corpus carries a
primitive chirality with four of five manifestations closed, and
chirality is the natural home of cross products. If any committed
rule had (v_a x v_b) structure, the exclusion would have collapsed
into a bounding problem for a chiral channel. The registry answer
was better than I hoped: the committed dynamical entry point is F.1
Mechanism A, a per-edge scalar RATE asymmetry — which is not merely
single-edge but component-diagonal, so it sits in Channel A where
the 3001 exactness argument is weight-independent. The chirality of
the substrate rides on WHICH edges carry traffic faster, not on any
register arithmetic that could torque a vector. E-times-B and the
handedness manifestations live downstream, in derived observables
and pattern irreps. I registered W-MULTILINK-1 precisely because
this audit is only as durable as the committed mechanism set: a
future chirality patch with a new dynamical entry point must re-run
it before shipping.

Second, the moat — and here the negative control taught the lesson
of the patch. I built the excluded class (asymmetric-weight
cross-product pair kernel) expecting to measure a low suppression
exponent, slope 2 or so, to show the class is "less protected." The
measurement came back slope 0.00: out-of-plane fraction 0.70 at
EVERY wavelength. The excluded class does not leak weakly; it
destroys the U(1) reduction at order unity, wavelength be damned. My
first reaction was that the harness was broken; checking, it is
exactly right — a chiral pair kernel has no small parameter, because
the cross product manufactures the out-of-plane direction directly
from the shell geometry rather than from gradients. This inverted
the rhetorical structure of the record: the moat's job is not to
reassure that excluded things would have been small, but to show
they would have been CATASTROPHIC — which is what makes the three
ratified clauses load-bearing rather than decorative. The exclusion
theorem is not a technicality; it is the difference between quantum
mechanics and no quantum mechanics on this substrate.

The separable-kernel case also corrected me mid-patch: my first
separable example (isotropic moment factor times scalar stencil) came
back machine-EXACT rather than slope-4, and the initial assertion
failed on a fit over noise floor. The fix was to recognize the case
split I had missed — an isotropic factor (any moment of order <= 5,
by the 5-design) is a multiple of the identity and cannot leak at
all; only a direction-coupled factor inherits the (ks)^4 class. The
committed record now states both, and the failed first run is worth
this paragraph: the assertion caught a WRONG EXPECTATION, not a wrong
result, which is the assert-harness working as designed.

Per the KEY-DESIGN RULE from 3004, the script carries an unprinted
sentinel: after the printed checks, the RNG stream generates a fresh
configuration whose out-of-plane fraction is computed into a variable
and never printed. It is not on any printed curve and depends on the
post-checks RNG state, so it cannot be refit from committed data. I
have not evaluated it beyond the computation inside the run; its
value appears nowhere. The next dispatch can define KEY-I on it.

T2 (B1-CONST — the lattice turnover constant) is the remaining
