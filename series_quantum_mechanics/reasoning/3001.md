# Tier-4 reasoning fragment — Patch 3001 (R-4: the 5-design plane-stability lemma)

**Captured at-patch, producing window.**

R-4 looked like it needed a perturbative estimate; it turned out to
need a case split, and finding the right split was the session's real
work. My first framing — "bound the out-of-plane torque of the
tight-binding update" — stalled immediately on a trivial observation
that took embarrassingly long to trust: the shipped update applies ONE
scalar stencil to the site field. A scalar stencil is
component-diagonal. It cannot mix components, period, at any order in
Delta-s. For the transport class the papers actually use, plane
closure is not approximately true; it is exact by construction, and
D-4 as originally posed ("the Proposition does not verify...") is
answered for the shipped model by an argument of one sentence. The
reason this didn't end the work is that the panel's worry was really
about the MICROSCOPIC transport — does the physical per-edge transfer
stay component-diagonal? — so the honest lemma has to handle the
general single-edge kernel T(v) = alpha I + beta v v-transpose.

That is where the geometry delivered the result I did not expect to
be this clean. The beta-channel's stencil coefficients are directional
moments of the neighbor shell, and the 600-cell's 3D shell is the
icosahedron's 12 vertices — which are a spherical 5-design. I knew
the second-moment isotropy (the shipped Appendix A uses it); I did
not remember, until checking, that the design order is FIVE: the
fourth moment is also exactly isotropic, and the first anisotropy
sits at order six. The consequence is a four-power suppression of the
lattice-anisotropic channel, (k Delta-s)^4, which at sub-Planck
spacing against a Compton wavelength is ~10^-90 per refresh. The
numerical harness then did something better than confirm the slope:
the octahedron negative control (a 3-design) measured slope 2.00
against the icosahedron's 4.00 — the exponent tracks the design
order, which is the signature that the mechanism is the geometry and
not the harness. That control was worth more than the headline
number.

The subtle trap in the middle: isotropic tensors CAN mix components
(grad-div structure), so "moments through five are isotropic" does
not by itself keep vectors in a plane. What saves it is an argument I
had to state carefully: delta-built tensors cannot manufacture a
direction — their output lies in the span of the input vectors (the
field values and the wavevectors). So with in-plane fields varying
in-plane, isotropic terms are trapped in the plane, and the ONLY
escape route is the anisotropic order-six structure. With variation
along the normal, a (ks)^2 isotropic channel opens — and the right
response to that channel is not to bound it but to NAME it: it is the
longitudinal/transverse split, i.e., for the matter pattern, tilt
dynamics of the SSV_net direction. Tilting the plane is what spin
DOES. QM-3 already says the helix axis is the spin direction; QM-4's
pointer operator is the SSV projection. So D-4's feared leakage
channel, where it is physically real, appears to be the spin sector
wearing a worried expression. I registered that as an observation
(O-R4-1, the Hopf-fibration correspondence: tilt-S^2 times in-plane
U(1) = the spinor structure) and deliberately did NOT consume it in
any claim — it is the kind of beautiful-if-true statement that panels
exist to attack, and promoting it same-patch would repeat the
overclaim class this arc was born correcting.

Universality (L-4) dissolved rather than closed: the worry about
stationary states assumed "stationary" means "no periodic component,"
but under E = hbar nu_C the rotation rate is bounded below by the
rest-mass term — a Schrodinger-stationary state has a stationary
ENVELOPE and a pattern rotating at omega >= mc^2/hbar. The panel's
question was answered by a corpus identification already at Tier 1.

Honest limits registered: the bound covers single-edge kernels built
from the edge direction alone; multi-edge-correlated transport is
outside scope. And the whole package is CLOSURE CANDIDATE,
panel-pending — the bar scope moves only through the next
adjudication, where, for the first time in this arc, SCRIPT-EXECUTED
credit exists to be earned.
