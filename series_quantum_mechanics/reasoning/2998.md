# Tier-4 reasoning fragment — Patch 2998 (F-SW-6 steps 2–5: the lineage sweep)

**Captured at-patch, producing window.**

The sweep's execution risk was never physics — the 2995 record had
already inventoried every site with line citations — it was drift:
five files edited in one pass, where a missed site leaves the corpus
half-migrated and a hand-typed edit corrupts a proof. Two mechanical
decisions addressed this. First, every edit ran through an
assert-unique-then-replace harness that hard-fails on a missing or
ambiguous anchor, so nothing was edited "approximately." Second,
after the pass I re-grepped all five files for the retired vocabulary
and caught two sites the 2995 inventory had genuinely missed: QM-3's
§2 qubit definition line ("The two-component DI-bit state is") and a
"joint DI-bit states" in QM-6's abstract summary of Paper 4. Both
were naming-only, same class as their neighbors — but the lesson
stands: a line-cited inventory is an excellent map and still not the
territory; the post-edit residual grep is the step that makes the
sweep complete rather than merely faithful to its plan.

Judgment calls worth recording:

**QM-6's primitives list was restated, not minimally patched.** The
old primitive 2 was wrong twice (phase content; type
misclassification), and a word-level fix would have produced a
grammatically compliant sentence still organized around the wrong
ontology. The new list gives the three CP types as primitive 1 and
promotes the Grid-Point pattern state (the two registers) to
primitive 2 — because under the re-ground, that state IS the quantum
substrate, and a capstone's primitives list should name the thing the
series is actually about. The count of "four primitives" is
preserved, which matters for continuity with the abstract, PLS, and
the emergence-hierarchy figure.

**QM-2's anti-erasure note is longer than the others because its
equation is load-bearing.** For QM-3/4/5 the retired language was
naming; a one-line note suffices. QM-2's Eq. (phaseedge) is consumed
by the paper's predictions, so its note does three jobs: quotes the
retired reading, states the corrected bearer, and says explicitly
that the equation is unchanged and remains load-bearing — so no
reviewer reads the re-label as a retreat from the interference
results.

**QM-5 gained a B-QMRG-1 cross-reference on purpose.** Its mode
expansion is where the coherent-mode amplitude–count relation
actually lives; pointing the bridge at its natural derivation home
sets up the eventual hardening of B-QMRG-1 to happen in the right
paper.

**The glossary Born Rule entry was rewritten as a reconciliation,
not a correction.** F-SW-5 had flagged it as overstating the shipped
math; the 2997 bridge showed "amplitude²" is the count's coherent-mode
dual. The new entry says both readings and identifies them, with the
history note recording the full arc (flagged as overstatement →
resolved as dual). Erasing the amplitude² language entirely would
have discarded a formulation that turned out to be right.

**Version numbers diverge deliberately:** QM-6 → v2.0 (its normative
primitives section was rewritten — a substantive revision), QM-2..5 →
v1.2 (attribution language only; mathematics untouched). The same
stale "(Version 3.1)" title-marker drift found in QM-1 was present in
all five and reconciled in each, noted per-file in the CHANGELOG.
The QM-4 PLS Unicode superscript (10⁴², U+2074) was a pre-existing
compile failure on the standard toolchain, fixed as hygiene since the
file was open; flagged here so the ClearPC recompile of QM-4 isn't a
surprise diff.

All five two-pass compiles are clean except container-absent SVG
figure conversions (ClearPC's inkscape workflow); container PDFs
discarded per the Binary Artifact Workflow. With this patch, F-SW-6
steps 1–5 are complete: the entire QM lineage plus glossary now
states one ontology, the ratified one. What remains before the bar
can move is external: the panel dispatch package for the re-ground
arc (2988 audit → 2995 sweep → 2996 FI → 2997 QM-1 v2.0 → 2998
lineage), assembled next.
