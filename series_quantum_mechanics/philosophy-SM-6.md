## Key Philosophical Points

### 1. Topology vs Geometry: A Natural Distinction on the Lattice

The derivation rests on a clean separation between topological quantities (mode counts, graph invariants — Tr(A²), Tr(A³), z+1) and geometric quantities (metric corrections — l/R = 1/φ, the propagation efficiency). The denominator of the Weinberg angle formula is topological; the numerator correction is geometric. This distinction is forced by the lattice framework: on a graph, the number of closed walks is combinatorial (doesn't depend on edge lengths), while the physical weight of each walk depends on the metric.

This is why the φ correction is linear (not quadratic): it enters as a geometric efficiency on topologically-counted modes, not as a coupling ratio in a dynamic formula.

### 2. The Weinberg Angle as an Operational Quantity

CPP defines sin²θ_W as "the fraction of vacuum disturbances that propagate as photons." This is NOT the SM definition (g'²/(g²+g'²)), and the paper is explicit about this departure. The justification: in CPP, gauge couplings are emergent, not fundamental. The natural mixing variable is the mode fraction (a lattice observable), not the coupling ratio (a continuum-limit artifact).

This is a genuine conceptual contribution, not a dodge. It answers the question: "What does the Weinberg angle mean physically?" The CPP answer: it measures the geometric partitioning of the vacuum's propagation capacity between two classes of modes.
