## Measurement Without Collapse

QM-4's central philosophical contribution is an account of quantum measurement that requires neither wavefunction collapse nor many worlds. The DP Sea is a Markovian decoherence bath: when a quantum system interacts with the Sea, its off-diagonal density matrix elements decay at the Lindblad rate γ = (sea_strength)² × E_P/ħ. What appears as measurement outcome is the pointer state — the SSV eigenstate that survives decoherence. The Nexus maintains global unitarity throughout. There is no collapse of the wavefunction; there is progressive loss of phase coherence due to DP Sea scattering.

## The Measurement Problem Is Dissolved, Not Solved

The measurement problem asks: why does measurement produce a definite outcome when the Schrödinger equation predicts a superposition? CPP's answer is that no definite outcome is produced at the fundamental level — only apparent definiteness from decoherence. An observer (also a physical system interacting with the DP Sea) becomes entangled with the measurement apparatus through the same decoherence mechanism, making the superposition undetectable from inside the system. The measurement problem is dissolved by showing that "definite outcome" is the wrong description of what happens; "decohered superposition indistinguishable from definite outcome" is the correct one.

The quantitative content — γ from sea_strength — is what distinguishes CPP from interpretational accounts of QM that leave decoherence rates as phenomenological inputs.
