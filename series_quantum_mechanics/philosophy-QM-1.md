## The Central Claim

The Schrödinger equation is not a fundamental postulate of nature. It is the continuum-limit description of DI-bit hopping on the 600-cell lattice. The wavefunction ψ is not an abstract probability amplitude postulated to describe quantum systems — it is the complex DI-bit amplitude ψ = √ρ × e^{iφ} at each Grid Point, where ρ is the DI-bit number density and φ is the geometrically accumulated phase. The Born rule |ψ|² = ρ is built into the definition rather than postulated as an additional rule.

## What This Changes About the Wavefunction

Standard quantum mechanics faces the measurement problem: if the wavefunction is a real physical entity that evolves according to the Schrödinger equation, why does measurement produce a definite outcome rather than a superposition? If the wavefunction is merely a probability calculator, what is it describing physically? CPP answers this by identifying the wavefunction with a definite physical object — the DI-bit density field — that evolves deterministically. The appearance of probability arises from the DI-bit number density being what is observed at measurement, not from any irreducible randomness in the dynamics.

## The Role of ħ

The reduced Planck constant ħ enters QM-1 as the hopping amplitude T = ħ²/(4mΔs²) in the tight-binding model on the 600-cell graph. It is the physical scale that sets how strongly DI bits couple between nearest-neighbour Grid Points. In current CPP, ħ is derived from outside the QM-1 paper (SM-1 calibrates SSV₀ = m_e c²/2, and ħ enters through the Compton wavelength). The CPP programme's long-term goal is to derive ħ from the ZBW random walk statistics (OPEN-P-QM-new-1 in propositions.md). Until that derivation is complete, ħ is an input to the QM series rather than a derived output.
