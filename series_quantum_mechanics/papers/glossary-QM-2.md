# Glossary — QM-2: Superposition and Interference from DI-Bit Path Summation

**Paper:** QM-2
**Last updated:** 2 April 2026

*For the complete series-wide glossary covering all QM papers, see glossary-QM-1.md.*

---

## Multi-Path Propagation

The mechanism by which DI bits from a source propagate simultaneously along all available lattice geodesics to a detector. Each path accumulates a deterministic geometric phase. This is the CPP realisation of the Feynman path integral — not a formal device but a physical process on the lattice.

---

## Amplitude Summation

The total DI-bit amplitude at a detector d is ψ(d) = Σ_k A₀ e^{iφ_k}, the coherent sum over all paths. Constructive interference occurs where phases align; destructive where they cancel. The Born rule |ψ|² gives detection probability.

---

## Which-Path Tag

The SSV gradient left by a DI bit passing through a slit or along a path. If the tag is detectable (i.e., the SSV perturbation is resolvable), the path information decoheres the superposition and the interference pattern vanishes. This is the CPP mechanism for complementarity.

---

## Quantum Eraser

The recovery of interference fringes by erasing the which-path information after detection of the signal photon but before the idler is measured. In CPP, erasing the SSV tag restores phase coherence between paths, re-enabling amplitude summation.

---

## Geometric Phase (φ)

The phase accumulated by a DI bit propagating along a lattice geodesic: φ = (E/ħ)t − (p/ħ)x. This is deterministic — no randomness enters the phase. Quantum randomness arises from the detection event (which CP absorbs the DI-bit density), not from the phase evolution.
