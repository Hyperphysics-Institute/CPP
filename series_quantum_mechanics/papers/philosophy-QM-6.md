## The Reduction to Four Primitives

QM-6 makes the strongest philosophical claim of the series: all of quantum mechanics — the Schrödinger equation, the Born rule, entanglement, measurement, and QFT — follows from four primitives (CPs, DI bits, the 600-cell lattice, and the Nexus). These four primitives are themselves either CPP axioms (AXIM-1, AXIM-2, AXIM-6) or direct consequences of them. Standard quantum mechanics has twelve or more independent postulates depending on how they are counted. CPP has four, and all of QM emerges from them.

The philosophical gain is parsimony combined with mechanism. Standard QM is phenomenologically complete but mechanically silent — it accurately describes what happens but offers no account of why the Schrödinger equation holds or where the Born rule comes from. CPP answers both questions at the cost of introducing a specific discrete lattice structure at the Planck scale.

## The Nexus as Unity

The most important result in QM-6 is the Nexus roles table: at every level of QM (DI-bit dynamics, Schrödinger evolution, entanglement, measurement, QFT), the Nexus plays a different role but is always the same entity. It is not five different mechanisms — it is global DI-bit conservation manifesting at five different scales of description. This is the CPP programme's account of why quantum mechanics has the mathematical structure it does: the Schrödinger equation is linear, measurement has definite outcomes, entanglement is non-local, QFT has statistics — all because the Nexus enforces one conservation law that looks different at each scale.

## What CPP Does Not Yet Derive

QM-6 is honest that several key quantities are not yet derived from the four primitives. ħ is an input (OPEN-P-QM-new-1). The specific value of sea_strength enters from SS-1 geometry, not from the QM series itself. The decoherence rate γ uses sea_strength as input. The CPP QM series is complete as a logical structure; it is not yet complete as a first-principles derivation of every QM parameter from the four primitives alone.
