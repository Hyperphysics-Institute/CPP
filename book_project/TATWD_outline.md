### Part Three: What the Theory Actually Predicts
*The results. Not all of them, and not in paper order. Organized for narrative punch, not technical completeness.*

## Voice and register

**Tone:** Conversational but substantive. Think Carlo Rovelli's *Reality Is Not What It Seems*, or Sean Carroll's *Something Deeply Hidden*. Not textbook. Not breezy. The reader is smart; treat them that way. Don't lecture; invite. Let the weirdness of the theory land honestly — don't apologize for it, don't oversell it.

**Register:** First-person where appropriate (Thomas's personal story, his reflections on method). Third-person when presenting physics. Present tense primarily; past tense for historical events.

**Length targets:** Each chapter 8-15 pages in typeset form. Total book 250-320 pages. Short enough to be read on a long flight; long enough to have room for the physics.

**What to avoid:**
- Over-explaining equations the reader can trust without seeing
- Under-explaining concepts that actually need three paragraphs
- False humility ("I'm just a naturopath who...") — state credentials and posture once, then get on with it
- Religious triumphalism — the 1987 vision is honest; it shouldn't be weaponized
- AI triumphalism — the AI collaboration is interesting; don't make it the point
- Cranky tone toward mainstream physics — CPP is offered as an alternative, not a grievance

**What to lean into:**
- The *surprise* of results coming out right when they shouldn't
- The *texture* of working solitarily for 39 years before finding collaborators
- The *honesty* of registered failures alongside successes
- The *aesthetic* of a theory where the golden ratio appears because of geometry, not because it was put in
- The *human center* of Thomas Abshier as an actual person with a wife and a ministry, chickens and cows, and a theory

## Open questions for refinement

The following are deliberate uncertainties to be resolved as work proceeds:

1. **Chapter 7 depth on consciousness.** How much philosophical heavy lifting does the book attempt? A casual reader will find "consciousness is a primitive" either intriguing or alienating; a philosophical reader will want rigor the book can't deliver in prose form. The current outline leans toward accessible-but-brief, but this may need revision.

2. **Chapter 11 on failures.** How much prominence to give the falsified results? Current outline treats it as a full chapter because honesty demands it, but there's a legitimate editorial argument for folding it into an appendix so as not to front-load failures in a book meant to introduce a theory. Decision deferred.

3. **The 1987 vision chapter.** How explicitly religious? Thomas's framing is Christian; the book is not a Christian book. The vision is part of the honest history; how it's told determines whether the book reads as biography-of-a-theory or as testimony. Current outline leans biography-of-theory with the vision preserved as factual event but not evangelized.

4. **AI collaboration chapter(s).** Whether Chapter 12 is one chapter or two. There's a lot of material here — the methodology, the protocol, the specific SS-7 story, the texture of the collaboration. May be more natural as two chapters (one methodological, one narrative) but current outline keeps it compact.

5. **Four corners of the earth closing.** Depends on what the PDF Margo has actually says and what modern gravimetry shows. May not survive; may become more prominent; may relocate to an appendix. Placeholder in current outline.
