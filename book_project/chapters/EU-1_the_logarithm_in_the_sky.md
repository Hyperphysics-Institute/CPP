# The Logarithm in the Sky

*The story of EU-1: how the faint tilt in the oldest light turned out to be the fingerprint of anonymity, how a tenth axiom was written and then found unnecessary, and how the number that fixes the slope was already the number of things in the universe.*

---

## A number that is almost, but not quite, one

The oldest light in the universe is a little under three degrees above absolute zero, and it is almost perfectly smooth. When you map its temperature across the whole sky — the cosmic microwave background, released when the universe was a few hundred thousand years old and the first neutral atoms let light run free — you find the same temperature in every direction to one part in a hundred thousand. The map is the closest thing to featureless that nature produces at the largest scales.

But it is not quite featureless, and the way it departs from smoothness is the most useful number in cosmology. If you decompose the faint ripples in that map into ripples of different sizes, you find that small ripples and large ripples do not carry exactly equal power. There is a slight slope. Slightly more power on the large scales than on the small ones. Physicists summarize this slope with a single number, the scalar spectral index, written $n_s$. A perfectly equal distribution — the same power at every scale — would give $n_s = 1$. The measured value, from the Planck satellite's final analysis, is

$$n_s = 0.9649 \pm 0.0042.$$

It is almost one. It misses one by about three and a half percent, and that miss is measured well enough to exclude $n_s = 1$ by about eight standard deviations. The sky is tilted, faintly and definitely, toward the large scales.

That tilt is the cleanest single fact we have about the first fraction of a second of the universe. Everything that happened later — galaxies, clusters, the whole cosmic web — grew from the ripples whose tilt this number measures. If a theory of the early universe is going to be tested against anything, it will be tested against $0.9649$. This is the story of where that number comes from in Conscious Point Physics, and of a tenth axiom that was written down to get it and then turned out not to be needed.

## What inflation already explains

The standard account of the tilt is one of the genuine successes of twentieth-century cosmology, and it is worth stating fairly before saying what CPP adds, because CPP adds less than it might first appear and the honest version is the stronger one.

The number itself has a history. In 1970, before there was any data, Edward Harrison and Yakov Zel'dovich argued on general grounds that the primordial spectrum ought to be very close to scale-invariant — that $n_s$ should be almost exactly one — because any strong scale-dependence would have produced either too many black holes on small scales or too much large-scale structure on large ones. For two decades the Harrison–Zel'dovich value $n_s = 1$ was the natural expectation, the null hypothesis a theory would have to beat. The COBE satellite saw the ripples for the first time in 1992 but could not measure their tilt. WMAP, through the 2000s, first found hints that $n_s$ was slightly below one. Planck, with its final 2018 analysis, made the departure decisive: the spectrum is tilted, $n_s$ is not one, and the amount by which it misses is pinned to better than half a percent. The story of the measurement is the story of a null hypothesis slowly failing — the sky turning out to be tilted after everyone had reason to expect it flat.

In the inflationary picture, the very early universe underwent a brief epoch of accelerated expansion driven by a field — the inflaton — slowly rolling down a potential energy hill. While it rolled, quantum fluctuations in the field were stretched to enormous scales and frozen in, seeding the density ripples we see. The tilt of those ripples is set by how the expansion rate changed as the field rolled: a perfectly constant expansion would give $n_s = 1$ exactly, and the slight roll gives the slight tilt. The standard slow-roll relations even produce, for the simplest models, a tilt of roughly $n_s \approx 1 - 2/N_*$, where $N_*$ is the number of "e-folds" — the number of times the universe doubled in size — between when the observable scales left the horizon and when inflation ended. With $N_*$ somewhere around fifty to sixty, this lands near the measured value.

This is real, and it works. But it works at a price. The inflaton field, its potential, and the shape of that potential are inputs. They are chosen to fit, and a great many shapes can be made to give the right tilt. Inflation explains the tilt in the sense of providing a mechanism that produces a tilt of about the right size; it does not, in its standard form, tell you why the field exists, what sets the potential, or why $N_*$ has the value it has. The framework is a successful container for the answer rather than a derivation of it.

CPP's contribution is not to overturn any of this. The slow-roll relation $n_s - 1 = -2/N_*$ is mainstream; CPP does not claim it. What CPP claims is something narrower and, if it holds, more pointed: that in its picture of the early universe the inflaton, the potential, and even the value of $N_*$ are not free. They follow from what the substrate is made of and how many pieces it has.

## A universe that thins instead of stretching

Conscious Point Physics builds everything from a fixed lattice. Space is a scaffold of points — Grid Points — and the contents of space are Conscious Points stacked on that scaffold, bound into neutral pairs, the whole arrangement filling what the theory calls the Dipole Sea. In ordinary conditions the Sea is the vacuum; in the early universe it was something else. It was crowded. Every Grid Point was occupied not by one pair but by an enormous stack of them, the lattice loaded far past its resting density.

In this picture the universe does not expand by stretching the lattice. The Grid-Point spacing is fixed. The universe expands by *thinning* — the stacked occupation diluting outward across a scaffold that itself does not grow. Inflation, in CPP, is the early epoch when that thinning ran fast, and the question the theory inherits is whether the thinning produces a tilt, and whether that tilt is $0.9649$.

The first thing that happens when you set the problem up is that it collapses to a single unknown. The whole spectrum reduces to

$$n_s = 1 - \frac{p}{N_*},$$

where $N_*$ is fixed — not fitted — by the number of Conscious Points in the universe, and $p$ is a single number set by how the expansion rate depends on the occupation.

The way $N_*$ is fixed is worth dwelling on, because it is where the count enters. The number of e-folds — the number of times the universe doubled during inflation — is set in this picture by how far the lattice was loaded past its resting density, which is to say by the ratio of the total Conscious-Point count to the number of Grid Points. Take the logarithm of that ratio, divide by three for the three dimensions the dilution runs in, and you get the total number of e-folds: about sixty. This is not a fitted parameter; it is a count. The observable scales we measure in the microwave background did not leave the horizon at the very end of inflation but a few e-folds before, at the standard pivot near $N_* \approx 57$, and it is that pivot value that enters the tilt. I should be honest that the pivot placement — the few-e-fold offset between the total and the observable scale — is the standard cosmological convention rather than something the count fixes to four digits; what the count fixes is that the total is around sixty, and the robust prediction is a red tilt of size $2/N_*$. The enormousness of the count, roughly $10^{80}$, is what makes the tilt small: a small tilt is the signature of a very large universe.

With the pivot fixed, the entire problem becomes the determination of one integer. If $p = 2$, the tilt is $1 - 2/57 \approx 0.9649$, and the chapter is over. The work was finding out what sets $p$, and whether it is forced or chosen.

## The path to the exponent

The honest way to find $p$ is to ask how the expansion boost depends on the occupation $\bar n$ — the average number of Conscious Points stacked on a Grid Point — and to try the natural possibilities.

The natural possibilities are bad. If the boost goes as a power of the occupation, $H_{\text{eff}} \propto \bar n^{\,q}$, the tilt comes out as $n_s = 1 - 6q$. A linear dependence gives $n_s = -5$, which is not a small tilt away from one; it is a different universe. A packing-style dependence, $\bar n^{1/3}$, gives $n_s = -1$. A saturation law, where occupied sites simply switch on and off, drives the tilt to the opposite extreme and parks it exactly on $n_s = 1$, the featureless sky we do not observe. Every mechanical law — every law where the expansion responds to the occupation the way a pressure responds to a density — gives either an absurd tilt or the flat cliff.

For a while this looked like bad news accumulating. Each candidate for how the boost might depend on the occupation was a reasonable thing to try, and each one failed in the same direction: the tilt it produced was nowhere near the small, finite departure from one that the sky shows. A theory can survive one failed candidate; a string of them, all reasonable, all wrong by orders of magnitude, starts to feel like the question itself is malformed. What turned the accumulating failures into a result was noticing that they all failed for the *same* reason — they were all powers, and powers of a geometrically diluting quantity fall too fast or too slow in e-folds, never linearly. The thing that falls linearly in e-folds is the logarithm, because the occupation falls geometrically and the logarithm of a geometric fall is a straight line. The failures were not noise; they were the elimination of everything that was not a logarithm.

There is exactly one kind of dependence that gives a small, finite tilt near one: a *logarithmic* one. If the boost goes as the logarithm of the occupation, $H_{\text{eff}} \propto \ln \bar n$, then because the occupation dilutes geometrically as the universe expands — $\bar n$ falling as $e^{-3N}$, so that $\ln \bar n$ is just proportional to the number of e-folds remaining — the boost falls *linearly* in e-folds, and the tilt that follows is the small, finite $-2/N_*$ that the data wants.

The bridge from "the boost falls linearly" to "the spectrum is tilted" is a standard piece of inflationary machinery worth stating once, because the rest of the chapter leans on it. The size of the density ripple frozen in at a given scale is set by the expansion rate at the moment that scale left the horizon, and scales leave the horizon at different e-folds. So a scale-dependent ripple is just a boost that was slightly different at different e-folds, read off against the e-fold at which each scale froze. This is the separate-universe, or $\delta N$, picture: the curvature ripple is the fluctuation in the number of e-folds, and its power carries the *square* of the boost. A boost that is perfectly constant in e-folds gives equal power at every scale — the flat $n_s = 1$ — and a boost that drifts linearly gives a spectrum that drifts, with a tilt equal to twice the logarithmic slope of the boost. Two times $-1/N_{\text{rem}}$ is $-2/N_{\text{rem}}$. The factor of two in $p = 2$ is the square in the $\delta N$ relation; the rest is the logarithm.

I want to be careful here, because this is the place in the work where it would be easiest to claim too much. It is tempting to say the data *forces* the logarithm — that near-scale-invariance uniquely selects it. That is too strong, and the reviewers of this paper said so, correctly. What is true is narrower: among the natural laws by which an expanding substrate's boost could depend on its occupation, the logarithm is the only one that survives the demand for a small tilt. The power laws are excluded; the saturation law is excluded; the logarithm is what is left. That is a selection by elimination within a class of candidates, not a theorem that no other function could ever work. Stated at that strength it is still a strong result, because it means the shape of the answer is not a knob anyone turned. It is the only shape that fits, and the question becomes why the substrate would produce a logarithm at all.

## The tenth axiom

The answer to that question is the center of this paper, and it arrived as a small embarrassment.

A logarithm is a strange thing for a physical boost to follow. Forces do not naturally go as the logarithm of a density. So I went looking for what in the substrate would produce one, and when I could not immediately find it in the existing axioms, I did what one does: I wrote a new one. A candidate tenth axiom — the framework has nine — stating that the Zitterbewegung bath of the early substrate thermalizes its stacked occupations into a configuration whose pressure to disperse grows logarithmically. I gave it a name and a registry slot and started to build on it.

This was the right thing to be uneasy about. A theory that derives the periodic table's binding energies and the Weinberg angle and the neutrino mixing matrix from nine axioms, with no new postulate added across dozens of papers, does not get to quietly add a tenth the first time it reaches cosmology. The whole force of the programme is that the axiom count does not grow. A tenth axiom to get one cosmological number would have been a confession that the machinery did not reach this far.

So before building on it I took the candidate axiom apart to see what it was actually asserting, and it came apart into two halves. The first half was a claim about *dynamics* — that the bath actually reaches its most-probable configuration, and reaches it fast, before inflation ends. The second half was a claim about *what that configuration is* — that its dispersal pressure grows as the logarithm.

The second half is not an axiom. It is already in the framework, and it has been since the first axiom was written. Conscious Points of the same type carry no individual identity; what is physical about a stack of them is only how many there are, not which is which. This is the framework's first axiom, the indistinguishability of identical Conscious Points. And indistinguishability, in statistical mechanics, has a precise and famous consequence. When you count the configurations of $n$ identical, nameless objects, you divide by the number of ways of permuting them — the factor of $1/n!$ that Gibbs introduced to keep the entropy of a gas from depending on whether you imagined relabeling its molecules. That factor is what makes the chemical potential of a collection of indistinguishable things grow as the logarithm of how many there are:

$$\mu(\bar n) = kT \ln \bar n + \text{const}.$$

The logarithm is not a new physical law. It is the signature of anonymity. It is the same logarithm that appears in the Sackur–Tetrode entropy of an ideal gas, for the same reason — because the things being counted have no names. The dispersal pressure of an over-stacked Grid Point grows as the logarithm of its occupation because the Conscious Points on it are indistinguishable, and indistinguishability *is* the framework's axiom A1.

The first half of the candidate axiom — that the bath actually reaches this configuration — is not an axiom either, but for a different reason: it is provable. The minimal model of the substrate's Zitterbewegung bath is a process in which each Conscious Point hops between Grid Points at a common rate, with no site preferred — a structure that statistical physics calls a zero-range process. One can prove, for this process, that it relaxes to the indistinguishable configuration, and prove it the way such things are proven: by exhibiting a quantity that only ever decreases until equilibrium is reached, a Lyapunov function, here the relative entropy between the current occupation and the equilibrium one. The relaxation is fast — far faster than the expansion — so the bath is in its most-probable configuration throughout. This is a theorem about a stochastic process, not a postulate about the world. Grok, one of the three AI systems that reviewed the work, had independently built a Monte-Carlo simulation of exactly this bath while the derivation was being assembled, and its simulated occupations settled into the predicted distribution and equilibrated quickly; the theorem and the simulation agreed.

So the tenth axiom evaporated. Both of its halves were already present — one as the framework's oldest axiom, one as a provable property of the bath's dynamics. What had looked like a new postulate was a recognition that the existing machinery already did the work. The exponent $p$ is $2$ not because anyone chose it but because the dispersal pressure is logarithmic (from A1), because the occupation dilutes geometrically (from the expansion), and because the curvature ripples carry the square of the boost (the standard $\delta N$ relation). Run the chain:

$$H_{\text{eff}} \propto \ln \bar n \propto N_{\text{rem}}, \qquad n_s - 1 = 2\,\frac{d \ln H_{\text{eff}}}{dN} = -\frac{2}{N_{\text{rem}}},$$

and at the pivot $N_* \approx 57$,

$$n_s = 1 - \frac{2}{57} \approx 0.9649, \qquad \alpha_s = -\frac{2}{N_*^2} \approx -0.0006.$$

The first number is the Planck central value. The constants that went into the boost — the temperature, the coupling, the offset — all drop out of the logarithmic derivative, which is why the tilt carries no free parameter even though the boost itself has dimensional factors. The tilt is what the logarithm leaves behind when you differentiate it, and the logarithm is what anonymity leaves behind when you count.

## What had to hold for this to be clean

A result like this is only as good as the corners it does not cut, and there are two corners that could have ruined it.

The first is that the early substrate is not a gas of neutral things; it is a sea of charges, positive and negative Conscious Points. A charged plasma has a mean-field energy that grows with density, and a term that grows with density would have swamped the gentle logarithm completely — at the occupations in question, by some seventy orders of magnitude. The reason it does not is that the Dipole Sea is built of *bound* pairs, one positive and one negative, so the charge is exactly balanced at every occupation and the leading mean-field cancels. The neutrality is not arranged to save the result; it is the structure the Sea already had. After the cancellation the next residual is the Debye screening term, which grows only as the square root of the density and which, taken at face value, still looks alarming. But the Debye term is governed by how strongly the plasma is coupled, and a *hot* plasma — which the inflationary substrate is, at near-Planck temperature — screens itself so efficiently that the residual is about four parts in ten thousand, utterly below the logarithm's value of around a hundred and seventy. The square-root scare is real only for a cold, strongly coupled plasma, which is the opposite of the epoch in question. The corner is clean, but only because the epoch is hot.

Two further checks come for free, and both are the kind that would be hard to arrange by accident. The first is that the same logarithm, differentiated once more, predicts how the tilt itself changes with scale — the "running," $\alpha_s = -2/N_*^2 \approx -0.0006$. This is a second number, small and definite, that the framework does not get to adjust; it is the curvature of the same logarithm that produced the tilt. Current data constrain the running only weakly and are consistent with this value, so it stands as a falsifiable companion prediction rather than a confirmed one: a future measurement that found the running far from $-0.0006$ would break the picture. The second check is conceptual and sharper. The whole result hangs on the factor of $1/n!$, the anonymity of the Conscious Points. Take that away — imagine the stacked points were distinguishable, each carrying a history that made it a particular point — and the chemical potential stops being logarithmic, the boost goes flat in e-folds, and the tilt collapses back to exactly $n_s = 1$. The featureless Harrison–Zel'dovich sky is precisely the sky of a distinguishable substrate. The tilt is the difference between a universe whose constituents have names and one whose constituents do not, and the sky says they do not.

The second corner is honesty about what is still owed. The clean logarithm requires that the bath be the simple symmetric process described, with no large occupation-dependent complication hiding in the substrate's microphysics, and the symmetric bath in turn requires a homogeneous, isotropic background to live on. CPP does not yet derive that homogeneity from its primitive axioms — and neither does standard inflationary cosmology, which assumes a smooth background just as squarely. On this question the two pictures are at parity; CPP is not behind. But "at parity with an open assumption" is not "derived," and the paper says so in its headline rather than its footnotes. The result is leading-order and framework-conditional: conditional on the homogeneous background, on the neutrality of the Sea, and on the smallness of the substrate's residual corrections. Those corrections, estimated, shift the tilt by about five parts in ten thousand — roughly a tenth of the current measurement error. The four-digit agreement with $0.9649$ is at the standard pivot; the robust claim is a red tilt of magnitude $2/N_*$, which for any plausible pivot between fifty and sixty lands inside the measured band. Three independent reviewers — the systems the programme calls its swarm — recomputed the chain, ran the verification script, and agreed it should ship; none of them found a flaw that moved the verdict, and all three pressed exactly on the places where the claim had to be narrowed from "forced" to "selected," which is why it now reads the way it does.

## The fingerprint of anonymity

What EU-1 closes is narrow and, if it holds, strange. It does not derive inflation; it derives the *tilt* of the spectrum inflation produces, taking the inflating background as given. It adds no axiom. It leaves the deepest question — why the background is smooth — open and labeled. Within those bounds, it says that the faint slope in the oldest light is fixed by two things the framework already had: that identical Conscious Points have no names, and that there are about $10^{80}$ of them.

The first of those is the more interesting. The factor of $1/n!$ that produces the logarithm is the same factor Gibbs wrote down to fix the entropy of a box of gas, and the puzzle it resolved then was the same puzzle in miniature — that the entropy came out wrong if you pretended identical molecules could be told apart. The resolution, both times, is that they cannot. What EU-1 proposes is that this fact, the anonymity of identical things, does not stay in the box of gas. It is written into the sky. The slight tilt that seeds every galaxy is the differentiated trace of a logarithm, and the logarithm is there because the universe's earliest constituents could not be told apart from one another.

The number is $0.9649$ because counting nameless things gives a logarithm, and because the count is what it is. The geometers who first divided by $n!$ to make the entropy come out right were not thinking about the cosmic microwave background; it had not been discovered. But if this picture is correct, they had already written down the reason the sky is tilted. It was a statement about what happens when you count things that have no names, and the sky, it turns out, is counting.
