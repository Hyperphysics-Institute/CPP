# What Was Always There

*The story of Capotauro: how a substrate-vacuum chirality magnitude that had been written for eight versions of a paper as the outcome of a symmetry-breaking event got recognized — under cross-reviewer pressure at version 0.8 — as something the framework had simply always carried, what the recognition cost the paper, what closed at v1.0 SHIP once the substrate's first hand was treated as primitive, and what closed at v2.0 SHIP when the same primitive magnitude turned out to reach into three structurally distinct sectors through three different mechanisms producing the same number.*

---

## The Hand of the Universe

If you set a clock spinning forward on a table and look at it in a mirror, the reflected clock spins backward. If you fire a left-handed muon at a target and look at the reaction in a mirror, the reflected muon does not come out as a right-handed muon doing the mirror-image reaction. It comes out wrong. Weak interactions distinguish left from right; the universe has a hand.

This has been known since 1956. Wu's experiment with cobalt-60 settled it; the V−A structure of the weak current organizes the phenomenology. The Standard Model accommodates parity violation by writing the W boson's coupling with a definite chirality projection and then declaring that this is how nature happens to be. There is no derivation. There is a phenomenological fact and a Lagrangian that absorbs it.

This is the kind of place where a derivational framework like Conscious Point Physics is supposed to do work. The framework's substrate — a discrete lattice based on the 600-cell, four-dimensional, finitely many vertices, organized by axioms about how charge points exchange information bits — is supposed to have geometric content that the Standard Model fields do not. If parity violation is built into the substrate at the level of the geometry, the framework should say so, and should produce a number rather than a parameter.

The number turns out to be $\phi^{-3}/6 \approx 0.0394$. It is the chirality matrix element between two specific substrate states that organize charged leptons under weak interaction, and the leptogenesis chain — the standard back-derivation from the observed cosmological baryon asymmetry to the primary parity-violation asymmetry that fed it during early-universe sphaleron processes — gives an empirical anchor of approximately 0.04 with about ten percent framework-uncertainty in the back-derivation. The Capotauro paper, version 1.0, predicts $\phi^{-3}/6$ at zero free parameters. The match is to within two percent.

This chapter is about the closure that produced that number, and about a methodological correction along the way that changed how the paper said what it said without changing what the number is.

## A Bull Named Capotauro

The name comes from a paper that Thomas Abshier and the Grok AI assistant put together in December of 2025, before the framework had a programme-level architecture for closure campaigns. The paper proposed that the early universe contained, at a particular cosmological epoch around redshift $z \simeq 32$, an event in which the substrate's previously-degenerate chirality bifurcation collapsed onto a definite sign. The two enantiomorphs — left-handed and right-handed versions of the substrate's primitive chirality field — had been equally available before the event. After the event, the universe was committed to one of them. The matter-antimatter asymmetry that we observe today, with about one part in $10^{10}$ excess of matter over antimatter, traces back through the leptogenesis chain to that primordial selection.

The paper called the event the *capotauro* event. The Italian compound — *capo* for head, *tauro* for bull — frames it as a head-bull moment: a creature that had been symmetric in some pre-existing two-sided geometry suddenly committed to a single direction, the way a bull's head turns one way and not the other. The mythic resonance is more than ornamental. It captures the asymmetry: a bull is not a mirror-symmetric animal. Once you have one, you have a side.

The December paper proposed the event without deriving it from first principles. The cosmological framing was the proposal; the derivation was the problem. Two months later, when the programme architecture for closure campaigns had been set up and the registry had grown to track open problems systematically, the proposal entered the registry as OPEN-SM-4, the foundational open problem of the framework's substrate-chirality sector.

OPEN-SM-4 decomposed into three sub-claims that turned out to be substantially different in difficulty. Sub-claim (a) was the cosmological-event derivation: explain why the bifurcation collapsed when and how it did. Sub-claim (b) was the magnitude derivation: explain why the primitive chirality magnitude was $\phi^{-3}$ rather than some other dimensionless number. Sub-claim (c) was the observable-translation derivation: explain how the substrate's primitive chirality magnitude becomes the specific matrix element that controls parity violation in weak-interaction processes acting on charged leptons.

Sub-claim (c) was the one that closed at version 1.0 of the Capotauro paper. The other two remain open and are being worked on separately. The chapter that follows is mostly about sub-claim (c).

## The Three Sub-Claims, Briefly

It is worth pausing on the decomposition before describing the closure, because the decomposition itself is part of the result.

The honest framing, at the start of the campaign in February of 2026, was that the substrate's primitive chirality magnitude was a single foundational fact in three pieces. There was a *when*: the cosmological event in which the bifurcation collapsed. There was a *how-big*: the magnitude $\phi^{-3}$ that the bifurcation picked out. And there was a *how-this-becomes-observable*: the chain by which the primitive substrate-level chirality propagates into matrix elements between specific substrate states and finally into the observed parity-violation asymmetry.

Most frameworks would not split these. Either you have a derivation of the whole package — *when*, *how-big*, and *how-observable*, all from first principles — or you have a phenomenological fit in which one or more of these is a parameter and the others are computed from it. The Standard Model phenomenologically fits the V−A structure and computes parity-violation observables from it.

The decomposition into three sub-claims was a recognition that the three pieces have very different difficulties and very different dependencies. Sub-claim (c), the observable-translation, depends only on the substrate's primitive chirality magnitude and on the geometry of the 600-cell lattice — which the framework already has. Sub-claim (b), the magnitude, depends on substrate-vacuum dynamics that the framework has not yet developed. Sub-claim (a), the cosmological event, depends on sub-claim (b) — you can hardly explain when the bifurcation collapsed without knowing what set its magnitude.

The right campaign sequence was therefore (c) first, then (b), then (a). Closing (c) does not require closing (b) or (a); it requires treating the substrate's primitive chirality magnitude as a known foundational input — which is to say, taking $\phi^{-3}$ as given — and deriving the matrix element from there.

This sounds tautological until you do the work. It turns out that even with $\phi^{-3}$ taken as given, deriving the observed parity-violation matrix element requires substantial structure: a basis of substrate states organized as a particular doublet, an operator decomposition under the symmetry group that stabilizes that doublet, and an averaging integral over the geometric extension of the doublet states across the 600-cell's cage shells. The factor of $1/6$ in $\chi/6$ does not come for free. It is the work of sub-claim (c).

## The K3 Doublet and Its Stabilizer

The substrate states that organize charged leptons under weak interaction are not single states. They come in pairs — a doublet — with a specific group-theoretic character.

Three vertices of the 600-cell, chosen with appropriate symmetry, form a triangle. The graph on these three vertices — three vertices, three edges — is called K3 in graph theory, the complete graph on three vertices. The substrate vibrations that live on this triangle decompose into eigenmodes by the standard spectral theorem of K3: a bonding mode (symmetric under all three rotations) and an antibonding doublet (two degenerate modes that mix under the rotations). Physical charged-lepton states live in the antibonding doublet.

The antibonding doublet has a stabilizer group. A stabilizer is the set of symmetry operations of a geometric configuration that leave the configuration invariant. For the K3 triangle inside the 600-cell substrate, the stabilizer turns out to be a small finite group called $D_6$, which has order twelve and can be written as a direct product $D_6 = S_3 \times Z_2$. The $S_3$ part permutes the three K3 vertices among themselves. The $Z_2$ part is what physicists call a *cell-swap*: a discrete symmetry that flips between two interpenetrating tetrahedra inside the 600-cell that share the K3 triangle as a common face.

This is the level of detail at which the closure of sub-claim (c) operates. We have a doublet of substrate states, organized by a stabilizer group $D_6$ of order twelve. The chirality observable that produces the parity-violation matrix element is going to be a specific Hermitian operator on the doublet, and that operator's symmetry character under $D_6$ is going to determine, by classical representation theory, what the matrix element can be.

The chirality observable lives in a specific representation of $D_6$ called $B_2$. The $B_2$ representation is the product of the $A_2$ sign representation of $S_3$ — the one-dimensional representation in which transpositions of the K3 vertices act as multiplication by $-1$ — with the *odd* representation of the cell-swap $Z_2$. Both factors are forced by the chirality observable's physical content: chirality flips sign under K3-vertex transposition (because it is a pseudoscalar under the permutation group), and it flips sign under cell-swap (because the cell-swap is essentially a parity operation at the substrate level).

The classification of the chirality observable in $B_2$ is the entry point. Once the observable is in $B_2$, the Wigner-Eckart theorem — a piece of classical representation theory that goes back to the 1930s — gives the factorization of any matrix element of the observable into separate pieces, each computable independently.

## The Wigner-Eckart Factorization

The matrix element between two states of the doublet of an operator in $B_2$ factorizes into a product:

$$|M| = |M_{K3}| \cdot |M_\perp|$$

The first factor, $M_{K3}$, is the matrix element of the operator's $A_2$ component on the K3-amplitude basis of the doublet. The second factor, $M_\perp$, is the matrix element of the operator's cell-swap-odd component on the perpendicular-wavefunction basis of the doublet, which is the extension of the K3-amplitudes onto the 600-cell's first cage shell that surrounds each K3 vertex.

Each factor is computable independently. We compute them separately, then multiply.

The first factor is where the substrate's primitive chirality magnitude $\phi^{-3}$ enters. The $A_2$ generator on the K3-amplitudes is a specific antisymmetric 3×3 matrix that produces the cross product with the vector $(1,1,1)$ — the symmetric direction in the K3-amplitude space. The eigenvalues of this operator on the K3 amplitudes are $\{0, +b\sqrt{3}, -b\sqrt{3}\}$ for a parameter $b$ that the framework has not yet fixed.

Here is where the substrate input does its work. The chirality observable's eigenvalues on the substrate's primitive chirality eigenstates are $\pm\chi$ where $\chi = \phi^{-3}$. The two nonzero eigenvalues of the operator on the K3-amplitudes must match the substrate's primitive chirality eigenvalues. Setting $b\sqrt{3} = \chi$ gives $b = \chi/\sqrt{3}$, and the matrix element magnitude on the K3-amplitude basis is $|M_{K3}| = b\sqrt{3} = \chi$. The parameter $b$ is not chosen. It is fixed by the requirement that the operator's spectrum on the K3-amplitudes match the substrate's primitive spectrum. This is the principle that closes sub-claim (c)'s first half: matching the operator's eigenvalues to the substrate's chirality eigenvalues fixes the operator's normalization without introducing any free parameter.

The second factor is where the cage shell does its work. The perpendicular wavefunction of each K3-amplitude state extends onto the first cage shell of the 600-cell — the twelve-vertex icosahedral shell that surrounds each K3 vertex. The matrix element of the cell-swap-odd component of the chirality observable on these perpendicular wavefunctions is computed by Schur orthogonality on the icosahedral shell. The icosahedral shell carries the $D_6$ stabilizer symmetry, and the doublet's representation content is the two-dimensional $E$ representation of $D_6$. Schur orthogonality gives:

$$|M_\perp| = \frac{d_E}{V_{\text{cage}}} = \frac{2}{12} = \frac{1}{6}.$$

The numerator $d_E = 2$ is the dimension of the $E$ representation. The denominator $V_{\text{cage}} = 12$ is the number of vertices on the cage shell, which happens to equal the order of $D_6$ exactly. This structural identity — the cage shell's vertex count equals the stabilizer's order — is the geometric heart of the cage-shell averaging argument.

Multiplying:

$$|M| = |M_{K3}| \cdot |M_\perp| = \chi \cdot \frac{1}{6} = \frac{\phi^{-3}}{6} \approx 0.0394.$$

That is the matrix element. That is the prediction. That is the number that matches the empirical anchor within two percent. The work is in the foundational inputs that enter the derivation chain, and in the symmetric structure that makes the chain rigorous given those inputs.

## What Was Always There

The first version of the Capotauro paper had a section called §2 titled, in its earliest drafts, *Substrate Symmetry Breaking*. The framing was that the substrate vacuum had originally been chirally symmetric — a state with $\chi = 0$ and a $Z_2$ symmetry between left-handed and right-handed configurations — and that an event somewhere in the substrate's deep history had broken this symmetry spontaneously, leaving the vacuum with a definite chirality magnitude $\chi = \phi^{-3}$ and one of two sign-equivalent enantiomorphs realized.

This is how a physicist trained in continuum-field-theory traditions would naturally write the section. Spontaneous symmetry breaking is the standard framing for any situation in which a previously-symmetric system commits to one of several symmetry-related states. The Higgs mechanism is the canonical example; the substrate's primitive chirality framing in §2 of Capotauro v0.6, v0.7, and v0.8 followed the same pattern.

The problem with this framing did not surface immediately. Through the first six versions of the paper, the SSB framing was treated as a content question — *how* does the symmetry breaking happen, *what* drives the bifurcation collapse, *when* does it occur — and the answers in each case were deferred to a separate sub-claim or a separate paper. The framing itself was assumed to be fine.

At version 0.7 review, in May, ChatGPT flagged something different. The flag was not about content. It was about whether the SSB framing was *coherent with the framework's epistemic stance*. The framework's core methodological commitment — the one that distinguishes it from continuum-EFT traditions — is that mathematical descriptions are not physical mechanisms. SSB is a mathematical description: a Lagrangian pattern in which a potential's symmetric minimum is unstable and the system rolls into one of several degenerate asymmetric minima. The Lagrangian pattern can be re-derived from a more-primitive description in which the substrate had truly been chirally symmetric before the bifurcation and the bifurcation was a real dynamical event. But it can also be re-derived from a description in which the substrate had always carried its primitive chirality and no bifurcation event ever happened. Mathematically equivalent. Physically, not the same.

The framework commits to the latter description. Its substrate is what it is, not the outcome of a more-primitive substrate undergoing dynamics. The chirality magnitude $\phi^{-3}$ is a constitutive specification of the substrate, in the same sense that the existence of conscious points and the rules of their interaction are constitutive. They were always there. There is no event before which they did not exist.

ChatGPT's flag at v0.7 said this. The flag was repeated more emphatically at v0.8, and at that point — Session 119, the convergence round in which ChatGPT, CoPilot, and Grok each delivered independent SHIP-ready verdicts on v0.8 — the question became whether to ship the paper with the SSB framing in §2 and address the methodological concern in v2.0+, or to reframe §2 before shipping v1.0.

The Session 120 decision was to reframe. The mathematical content of §2 did not change. Every equation in the section is identical between the SSB-framed v0.8 paper and the primitive-feature-framed v0.9 paper. What changed was the prose: §2 in v0.9 introduces the substrate's primitive chirality magnitude as a foundational feature of the substrate, coeval with the substrate's other primitive specifications, and the SSB description is preserved in Remark 2.2 as a mathematically-equivalent alternative framing for readers from continuum-EFT traditions.

This is the kind of correction that surfaces in external review and that internal review tends to elide. The internal author writes naturally in the framing they were trained on. The external reviewer reads against the framework's stated commitments. The mismatch shows up as a flag. The flag is honored or it is not. In this case it was honored, and what shipped at v1.0 was a paper whose §2 said what it had always meant to say, instead of what its first eight drafts had inherited from a different physics tradition.

The reframe took one patch — 0413 — and added perhaps two pages of prose to the paper. It changed nothing about the prediction. But it changed everything about how the prediction reads to a reader who has internalized the framework's commitments.

## What the Reviewers Caught

A separate finding from the same v0.8 convergence round is worth recording because it teaches something about external review at scale.

The Capotauro paper at v0.8 cited, in its §6.1.1 paragraph on the empirical anchor for the parity-violation asymmetry, a paper labeled *Gandolfi 2025*. The citation was load-bearing for the leptogenesis back-derivation chain that connects the cosmological baryon asymmetry to the parity-violation asymmetry on the K3 doublet. The internal author had inserted the citation in good faith — the paper appeared in a bibliographic search at some point in early Capotauro drafting, and the citation entered the .bib file with what looked like reasonable provenance.

CoPilot's v0.8 review, delivered the same Session as ChatGPT's and Grok's, said: *I could not find this paper in arXiv, Google Scholar, or ADS.* The flag was clean and short. It was also load-bearing: the empirical anchor for the primary prediction depended on the back-derivation chain, and one of the citation links in the back-derivation chain failed external verification.

The Session 120 web-search round of verification confirmed CoPilot's finding. No paper with that author and year exists in any major database. The citation was removed, and the §2.4 empirical-anchor paragraph was rewritten to use only the chain whose individual links can each be externally verified — Planck 2018 for $\eta_B$, the sphaleron-equilibration framework from standard early-universe cosmology, the washout-factor literature, the lepton-flavor-mixing back-derivation matrix.

Internal review would not have caught this. Internal review verifies that the citation supports what the paper claims; external review at scale verifies that the citation exists at all. The difference is small in any individual case and substantial across a corpus. CoPilot's catch at v0.8 was unique among the three v0.8 reviewers — neither ChatGPT nor Grok had flagged the citation — and it was load-bearing. Citation veridicality is a different problem from citation soundness, and the second-round AI-review pattern that the framework has been developing handles both.

The two findings together — ChatGPT's methodological reframe and CoPilot's veridicality catch — are why v0.9 was a substantial polish round rather than a trivial cleanup before v1.0 SHIP. Each reviewer caught something the others did not, and each catch was load-bearing. The cross-reviewer convergence on SHIP-readiness was real; the cross-reviewer divergence on what specifically remained to fix was also real, and the v0.9 patches address each finding separately. The methodology generalizes: parallel multi-reviewer review at the SHIP-readiness round is more sensitive than serial single-reviewer review at finding the load-bearing issues that close a paper out.

## Shipping a Theorem Before its Paper

A small methodological footnote, before the Session 124 surprise, is worth attaching here.

The Composite Capotauro Wigner-Eckart Theorem — the formal statement that $|M| = \chi/6$ at zero free parameters under the paper's foundational inputs — was registered as Theorem 62 in the framework's programme-level theorem registry at Session 103, in March, almost three months before the Capotauro paper itself shipped at v1.0. This is the first instance in the framework's corpus of a theorem being registered in the programme registry ahead of its own flagship-paper publication.

The registration was conditional on four specific criteria. The theorem had to have a rigorous proof in a working-sketch document that other AI reviewers could read. It had to have end-to-end numerical verification at machine precision, which had been done at Session 102 with the matrix elements computed to seventeen decimal places and matching the $\chi/6$ form symbolically. It had to have a primary empirical prediction whose validation had been completed — the two-percent agreement with the leptogenesis back-derivation anchor was already in hand at Session 103. And it had to have honest scope-limitation framing for any re-scoped sub-problems, which in this case meant the explicit out-of-scope deferral of the $\sin^2\theta_{13}$ question to the SF-2 v2.0+ campaign in the electroweak sector.

The four-condition pattern, taken together, made the registration appropriate at Session 103 even though the paper itself was still in v0.6 internal development. The theorem was a programme-level fact. The paper was the venue in which that fact would be communicated to external readers. The two had different timelines, and the programme registry could honor the first timeline without waiting for the second.

The reason this matters is that the framework's swarm-validation methodology depends on the programme-level theorem count being accurate. Other papers in the framework can cite THEO-CAP-1 in their own theorem registrations, can rely on its conclusions in their own derivations, can build on its structural results in their own working sketches. Waiting until the paper shipped at v1.0 in May to register the theorem would have meant that papers SF-2, SM-9, and others that depend on substrate-chirality results would have had to either duplicate the derivation in their own appendices or punt on their own claims until Capotauro shipped. Registering at Session 103 let the rest of the framework continue.

The pattern was made explicit in the framework's operating manual after the registration, and it now applies to any flagship-paper-pending theorem that meets the four conditions. Capotauro was the precedent; subsequent registrations will inherit the convention.

## The Refutation That Strengthened the Trajectory

What happens after v1.0 SHIP is part of the chapter, because what happened after v1.0 SHIP was substantive.

The Capotauro paper at v1.0 ships sub-claim (c) at conditional theorem level. Sub-claims (a) and (b) remain open, and the working sketch for sub-claim (b) — registered at `Capotauro_chiral_mechanism_candidate.md` — proposes a candidate physical mechanism called *Reading C*. Reading C is the hypothesis that the substrate's primitive chirality magnitude $\phi^{-3}$ derives, rather than being postulated, from a primitive four-dimensional direction $\hat{n}$ embedded in the substrate's ambient four-space. The direction breaks the 600-cell's full $H_4$ symmetry to a smaller stabilizer subgroup, and the magnitude $\phi^{-3}$ falls out of a perturbative-distance-ratio calculation involving the broken-symmetry structure.

The first verification step of Reading C, called Q1, asks: what is the $H_4$-stabilizer of a primitive direction $\hat{n}$? The Capotauro paper's working sketch for Reading C said, at v1.0 SHIP, that the stabilizer is isomorphic to $I_4$, the rotational subgroup of $H_4$ of order 7,200. Q1 verification was registered as the first sub-step of sub-claim (b)'s Layer 3 closure trajectory, with an estimated cost of one to three sessions.

Two sessions after v1.0 SHIP, in the parallel working window that had been opened for forward physics work while the documentation suite for v1.0 was being completed, Q1 verification was attempted. The result was a refutation.

Elementary orbit-stabilizer counting — the same finite-group theorem from undergraduate algebra that says the size of an orbit times the size of its stabilizer equals the size of the group — does not allow $I_4$ to be the stabilizer of any direction in $\mathbb{R}^4$ under the $H_4$ action. The orbit of a generic direction under $H_4$ has order 14,400 over the stabilizer; the orbit of a special direction has smaller size and a correspondingly larger stabilizer; but no orbit has stabilizer of order 7,200. The $H_4 \to I_4$ assertion in the Reading C working sketch is geometrically impossible.

This sounds like a falsification, and in any other framework it would be the moment at which the candidate mechanism gets retired. In this case it was something stranger. The same orbit-stabilizer analysis, applied to all the natural choices of *what kind of primitive direction* $\hat{n}$ might be — vertex-direction, face-direction, edge-direction, cell-direction — produces a clean table of four possibilities, each with a different stabilizer, each elementarily computable by parabolic-subgroup theory:

A vertex-direction has stabilizer $H_3 \cong I_h$, the icosahedral group, of order 120.

A face-direction has stabilizer $Z_2 \times S_3 \cong D_6$, of order 12.

An edge-direction has stabilizer $D_5 \times Z_2$, of order 20.

A cell-direction has stabilizer $S_4$, of order 24.

The face-direction stabilizer is $D_6$. This is *exactly* the stabilizer of the K3 doublet that the Capotauro paper uses. The same group, with the same order, organized the same way as a direct product $S_3 \times Z_2$. The structural coincidence is striking. The Reading C candidate mechanism for sub-claim (b), the mechanism that produces the substrate's primitive chirality magnitude, points at the face-direction reading of $\hat{n}$; and the face-direction reading of $\hat{n}$ has, as its $H_4$-stabilizer, precisely the group that organizes the substrate states on which the Capotauro paper's mechanism for sub-claim (c) operates.

The refutation of the $H_4 \to I_4$ identification therefore did two things at once. It killed the working sketch's specific claim about which subgroup the direction $\hat{n}$ stabilizes. And it surfaced, in the same calculation, a structural-coincidence finding that the working sketch had not anticipated and that strengthens rather than weakens Reading C's plausibility. The face-direction reading is now the leading candidate; a new sub-question $Q_1'$ has been registered to determine which of the four direction-readings (vertex, face, edge, cell) is the right one; and the Reading C closure trajectory has been revised from ten-to-twenty sessions to eight-to-eighteen sessions on the basis of the cleaner direction-class structure.

The v1.0 paper itself does not depend on the resolution of $Q_1'$. The v1.0 paper takes the substrate's primitive chirality magnitude as a foundational input and derives the matrix element on that basis. Reading C is the candidate derivation of the foundational input itself; the candidate is now sharper than it was at v1.0 SHIP, not weaker.

Capotauro v2.0+ will need to wait until $Q_1'$ resolves at minimum before the paper's §2 can be reframed to reflect the direction-class structure. The trigger condition for v2.0+ is now: $Q_1+Q_2$ closed at Layer 3 (achieved at Session 124) and $Q_1'$ resolved (estimated 1-3 sessions). Sub-claim (b)'s full closure remains the eventual target; the eight-to-eighteen-session estimate stands.

## The Closure Trajectory After the Refutation

The Q1' resolution arrived two sessions later, in late May, at Session 126. The decisive input did not come from cross-sector geometric analysis; it came from re-reading the existing Capotauro paper's framing language. The paper's §10, in describing the cage-shell averaging argument, refers to "the icosahedral cage hosting the K3-doublet." Hosting requires a unique host vertex — the cage's central 600-cell vertex around which the icosahedral first shell wraps. Face-aligned Reading C, the working sketch's leading candidate immediately after Session 125, has two host vertices per face (every 600-cell face has exactly two 600-cell vertices for which the face sits in the host's first-shell icosahedron); vertex-aligned Reading C has one. The paper's existing internal commitment to a unique host was the disambiguating constraint.

Vertex-aligned Reading C settles the direction-class question at Session 126. The substrate primitive direction $\hat{n}$ aligns with a host 600-cell vertex; the substrate residual symmetry around the host is $H_3 = I_h$, the full icosahedral group; the K3-doublet's $D_6$ stabilizer is one of ten $D_{3d}$ sub-stabilizers of $H_3$, corresponding to the ten three-fold axes inside the icosahedron. The K3 base, geometrically realized as a triangular face of the host vertex's first-shell icosahedron, is itself a face of the 600-cell. Off-axis at cosine $\sqrt{3}/2$ from $\hat{n}$. The structural coincidence from Session 125 — the face-direction $D_6$ matching the K3-doublet $D_6$ — was real but pointed to the sub-stabilizer level rather than to the top-level direction-class. Both readings are visible inside the corrected picture; the vertex-aligned reading is the operational one.

What happened next was a longer arc. The Q3 question, "what is the relationship between the substrate edge-perturbation magnitude and the K3-amplitude chirality magnitude," opened at Session 128 with a Layer 2 closure that introduced a geometric prefactor between $\epsilon$ at the edge level and $\chi$ at the amplitude level. The next session, Session 129, surfaced a geometric error in the Layer 2 closure: the in-face projection construction had been carried out in three dimensions where the geometry is actually four-dimensional, and in four dimensions the K3-face edges are tangent to $\hat{n}$ entirely. Zero direct edge-length perturbation at first order. The local icosahedral symmetry around the host vertex is preserved as $I_h$-symmetric at first order in $\epsilon$, with only an isotropic radial scaling of the first-shell icosahedron. The Q3 closure becomes a direct identification: $\chi \equiv \epsilon$ at substrate level, with no geometric prefactor and no irrep-extraction factor. The v1.0 paper's $|M| = \chi/6$ derivation is preserved exactly, now grounded in a sharper substrate analysis. The Q4 question that the v0.5 Layer 2 sketch had named as the next sub-step dissolves — it was an artifact of the geometric error and does not appear in the corrected analysis.

What is striking about the §13 correction is what the same geometric analysis says about *other* substrate objects built from first-shell vertices around the host. The local-icosahedral-preservation theorem proved for the K3 base — three of the icosahedron's twelve vertices forming a triangular face — applies, by the same reasoning, to any subset of the first-shell icosahedron. The 6-vertex Petrie hexagons of the icosahedron, ten of them inscribed around any vertex, are sets of six first-shell vertices arranged as a hexagon. The local-icosahedral-preservation theorem applies to them too. Their first-shell-to-first-shell edges are tangent to $\hat{n}$ in four dimensions; their host-to-first-shell edges have the same uniform $\hat{e} \cdot \hat{n} = -1/(2\phi)$ component as the K3-base edges; both substrate objects are preserved at first order in $\epsilon$ under vertex-aligned Reading C.

The Petrie hexagons of the first-shell icosahedron, lifted into four dimensions at radius $\phi/2$ along the host vertex direction, are the W-bracelets of the electroweak SF-2 paper.

This was the cross-sector observation that opened the closure trajectory through 2026. The W-bracelet of SF-2 — six-vertex Petrie polygon, $D_6$ stabilizer, $120°/240°$ phase bias that mediates the V−A coupling at the electroweak scale — sits at the host vertex of the Capotauro paper's substrate. Both substrate objects, the 3-vertex K3 base for mass-mixing and the 6-vertex W-bracelet for electroweak, are first-shell-vertex configurations under vertex-aligned Reading C. Both have $D_6$ stabilizers as sub-groups of the same icosahedral residual symmetry $H_3$. Both inherit substrate-level chirality from the same identification $\chi \equiv \epsilon = \phi^{-3}$. The Substrate-Locality Theorem, registered at Patch 0425 in Session 130, makes this explicit: the substrate primitive direction feeds two structurally distinct sector mechanisms via the same identification at the same magnitude. Cross-sector unification at Layer 2.

## The Dynamical Engine That Is Not Yet There

## The Method Underneath

Three methodological observations from the closure campaign deserve recording, not because they are unique to Capotauro but because Capotauro is the cleanest reference case for each.

The first is that methodologically critical framings forced by SHIP-readiness convergence cannot be deferred. The §2 SSB-to-primitive-feature reframe was carried in the framework's review feedback for two rounds — ChatGPT's v0.7 round-2 and v0.8 round-3 — before being treated as a v1.0 SHIP gate. The lesson is that the SHIP-readiness round is precisely the round at which methodological framings become committed; what gets shipped to external readers is what the paper says it means, not what the author privately knows it means. Deferring methodological framings to v2.0+ amounts to publishing the wrong framing first and correcting it later, which is the wrong order. The Capotauro reframe is the precedent for the framework's standard practice now: any methodologically critical framing flagged in the SHIP-readiness round closes before SHIP, not after.

The second is the four-condition pattern for theorem-registry registration ahead of paper publication. Rigorous proof, end-to-end numerical verification at machine precision, primary empirical prediction validated, and honest scope-limitation framing for re-scoped sub-problems — these four together make a theorem programme-level even before its flagship-paper venue is shipped. The pattern lets the framework's swarm-validation methodology continue uninterrupted by the paper-production timeline. THEO-CAP-1 was the first instance; future flagship-paper-pending theorems will use the same convention.

The third is what the framework's operating manual now calls the *discipline-tightening-after-precedent* principle. The full ten-patch documentation suite that accompanied Capotauro's v1.0 SHIP — six standalone companions, four section-E four-tier-discipline files, plus the registry-drift catch-up patch that landed first — is the framework's first complete documentation suite shipped synchronously with a paper's v1.0 SHIP. Earlier flagships (SS-9, SF-4, SF-2) shipped their papers at v1.0 with documentation backlogs of various sizes; Capotauro shipped at v1.0 with no documentation backlog. The precedent makes the discipline credible. Doing one full example end-to-end before codifying it as a programme-wide rule lets the codification reference an actual instance rather than an abstract template. The codification is now queued as a separate patch that will modify the framework's paper-completion-checklist gate language to require synchronous documentation-suite completion for v1.0 SHIP going forward; the OPEN-WORKFLOW-DOCS-CATCHUP registration will at the same time call in the documentation backlogs from the earlier flagships.

A fourth observation, registered against the v2.0 ship rather than the v1.0 ship, concerns the reviewer-cycle methodology pattern that emerged across two diagnostic-framing exchanges during the Session 135 reviewer cycle. Both exchanges produced the same lesson: when a reviewer delivers a response whose form does not match the requested critique format — a synthesis where critique was requested, or an acknowledgment that omits substantive figure or section content — the productive next move is a diagnostic-framing follow-up that respects the reviewer's epistemic autonomy and asks specifically for what was originally requested. The follow-up does not accuse the reviewer of inattention; it does not reframe the request to make the original response retroactively acceptable; it does not fabricate critique content where the reviewer did not produce it. In one of the two exchanges, the diagnostic frame surfaced what turned out to be a missing-figure-rendering context: a master architecture figure had been added to the paper as TikZ source, and the reviewer's processing of the source without TikZ compilation had missed the figure entirely. Re-submitting with the figure attached as a rendered image produced the strongest upward verdict revision in the cycle. The pattern is registered as a workable protocol for handling LLM reviewer synthesis-level gaps where the missing acknowledgment correlates with missing-figure-rendering context.

The methodological observations are not specific to Capotauro. They are claims about how a framework that uses AI assistants for closure work can develop methodological discipline through repeated reference cases.
