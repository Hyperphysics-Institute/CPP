## The mechanism

A charged unpaired CP (e.g. an electron) sitting in the DP Sea **radially polarizes** the surrounding
DPs: it pulls the opposite pole of each DP toward it and pushes the like pole away. That radial pole
displacement **is the electric field** — E is the radial polarization of the DP Sea.

Now let the charge **move**. As it passes a given DP, the attracted (opposite) pole stays close,
trailing the charge, while the repelled (like) pole is pushed around — so the DP **rotates about an
axis** as the charge sweeps by. That azimuthal/rotational motion of the DP **is the magnetic field**.

> **There is no separate "B field." B is the rotation of the same DPs whose radial displacement is E.**

## Why this matters (beyond being a hoot)

It makes the magnetic field *physical* rather than an abstract mathematical object, and it explains —
mechanically, not just formally — several things the math otherwise just asserts:

- **E and B are locked**, because they are two motions (radial vs rotational) of *one* DP responding to
  *one* charge. This is the physical content of "B = curl of the propagating SSV pattern" (c06).
- **B reverses when the velocity reverses** (the rotation sense flips with the direction of passage).
- **μ and ε are not independent knobs** — they are two faces of one DP-Sea response, governed by one DP
  stiffness. *This is exactly the physical reason behind the Patch 0740 result:* a single DP stiffness
  driving both the radial (ε) and rotational (μ) response is why the impedance Z₀ = √(μ/ε) is a fixed
  lattice-geometric ratio (so an SSV change moves only the product μ₀ε₀ = 1/c², i.e. c varies =
  gravity, while α stays fixed). The cartoon supplies the mechanical "why" under
  `series_relativity/development/dp_sea_mu_eps_symmetry.md`.
