## The Higgs Without a Vacuum Expectation Value

EW-4 presents the most philosophically novel claim of the EW series: there is no Higgs mechanism in CPP. The SM electroweak symmetry breaking — SU(2)_L × U(1)_Y → U(1)_EM — is driven by the Higgs VEV. This VEV is a property of the vacuum: the ground state of the Higgs field is not at Φ = 0 but at |Φ| = v ≈ 246 GeV. CPP's vacuum is the DP Sea at maximum entropy, with all CPP fields at their minimum energy configuration. There is no VEV. The bosons are massive because their composite structures store confinement energy, not because they couple to a non-zero vacuum field.

The philosophical implication is that electroweak symmetry breaking, as described in the SM, is an effective description of something that is not actually symmetry breaking at the fundamental level. The W, Z, and Higgs have always had their current masses; there was no high-energy phase in which they were massless. The SM "symmetry breaking" is the effective-field-theory encoding of the energy gap between the closed composite structures (massive W, Z, H) and the open-path modes (massless photon, gluons). The gap has always been there; the SM description of a phase transition is a phenomenological convenience.

## Spin from Symmetry Without a Spin Field

The J = 0 derivation from A₅ is philosophically significant in a different way: spin is usually associated with a quantum field's transformation properties under the Poincaré group. In CPP, the Higgs spin follows from the spatial symmetry of the composite structure — the A₅ symmetry of the dodecahedron forces no preferred axis. Spin emerges from geometry, not from field theory.
