## The Z as Least Reactive Ground State

The Z boson occupies the ground state of the 600-cell spectrum. The philosophical significance is that the least reactive, most stable, neutral-current-only electroweak boson corresponds to the most symmetric, lowest-energy lattice configuration. Stability and symmetry are linked: the icosahedron is the most symmetric of the three EW subgraphs (full icosahedral symmetry), and it is the least reactive (no entry points, no charge transfer). The W's reactivity and the Z's inertness are not separate features of the two bosons — they are opposite consequences of the same topological asymmetry between an open ring and a closed polyhedron.

## Topology as the Source of Physical Role

EW-3 makes a strong philosophical claim: the physical role of a gauge boson in SM interactions is determined by its topology, not by its quantum numbers. The SM assigns roles through group theory: W is an SU(2)_L gauge boson that couples chirally; Z is a neutral current boson with both L and R couplings. These roles are assigned by the gauge group structure. CPP assigns the same roles through topology: W has an open interior (→ charge transfer), Z is completely closed (→ no charge transfer). The coupling structures follow from closure, not from postulated group representations.
