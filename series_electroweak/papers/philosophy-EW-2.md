## The Emergent Charge of the W Boson

The most philosophically significant feature of EW-2 is the claim that the W± charge is not an intrinsic property of the boson but a relational property borrowed from the quark or lepton interaction. In standard physics a particle's charge is one of its intrinsic quantum numbers — an electron always has charge −e regardless of context. The W⁰ in CPP is topologically neutral (net Q = 0) and becomes W± only when it acquires charge from a collision.

This raises a question about the nature of charge in CPP: is charge ever truly intrinsic, or is it always relational? For the electron, the charge −e is carried by the central −eCP, which is a permanent property of that CP. For the W, the charge is carried by a CP deposited from an external particle — a transient property of the configuration. The philosophical distinction is between the electron as a carrier of its own charge and the W as a temporary carrier of someone else's charge. The Nexus conservation law governs both: charge is conserved at every Absolute Moment in both cases, but the physical locus of charge storage is different.

The CPP claim that W charge is relational and emergent is not merely philosophical — it makes a specific physical prediction: the W⁰ neutral bracelet exists as a distinct vacuum configuration at STP. If the W charge were intrinsic, there would be no neutral precursor; if it is emergent, the precursor must exist.
