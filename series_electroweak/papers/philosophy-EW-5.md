## Deriving Gauge Symmetry vs Postulating It

The most important philosophical distinction in EW-5 is between deriving gauge symmetry (THEO-EW-6, SU(2)_L from phase interference; THEO-EW-7, Nexus invariance) and postulating it (SM approach). In the SM, SU(2)_L × U(1)_Y is imposed as the starting principle from which everything else follows. In CPP, gauge symmetry is a derived consequence of two more primitive facts: the binary icosahedral group structure of the 600-cell (which forces the [I^a, I^b] = iε^{abc}I^c algebra), and the Nexus DI-bit conservation law (which forces local phase invariance).

The epistemological gain is one level of explanation: instead of answering "why is the electroweak force described by SU(2)_L × U(1)_Y?" with "that is the postulated gauge group," CPP answers "because the 600-cell lattice has H₄ symmetry and the Nexus conserves DI-bits." Whether this constitutes a deeper explanation depends on whether H₄ and the Nexus are themselves explainable — and CPP's answer is that they are postulated (AXIM-1, AXIM-2, AXIM-6). The explanatory regress stops at the CPP axioms, not at gauge symmetry.

## Unification as Shared Geometry

EW-5's eigenvalue bridge — the same six 600-cell eigenvalues selecting both three fermion generations (QM series) and three EW bosons (EW series) — is a form of geometric unification that is philosophically distinct from gauge-theoretic unification (GUT, string theory). Gauge-theoretic unification embeds SU(3) × SU(2) × U(1) in a larger group and shows they are subgroups. CPP geometric unification shows that both matter (fermion generations) and force (EW bosons) arise from the same geometric object. The unification is not about symmetry groups but about geometric structure.
