### Q1. "The SM Higgs mechanism is highly successful and experimentally confirmed. Why replace it with a composite hDP structure?"

The SM Higgs mechanism is phenomenologically successful — it correctly predicts the existence and mass range of the Higgs boson, which was discovered in 2012. CPP does not claim the SM Higgs mechanism is wrong; it claims the SM Higgs mechanism is an effective description of something more fundamental. In CPP, the 125 GeV resonance is a composite hDP structure on the dodecahedral subgraph. The SM Higgs field is the effective field theory description of this composite's quantum mechanics at long wavelengths. The VEV is the effective encoding of the dodecahedral shell's rest energy in the Lagrangian formalism. Neither is "more correct" — they are at different levels of description. CPP's goal is not to falsify the SM Higgs mechanism but to explain where it comes from.

### Q2. "A₅ symmetry forcing J = 0 is an elegant argument. But how do you know the dodecahedral shell actually has full A₅ symmetry in the CPP lattice?"

The A₅ symmetry of the dodecahedral subgraph is a consequence of the H₄ group symmetry of the 600-cell. H₄ (order 14,400) acts transitively on the 120 vertices of the 600-cell. The 20 vertices of the dodecahedral shell form a specific orbit under a subgroup of H₄ that includes A₅ as its rotation subgroup. The even distribution of CP types (5 each of +eCP, −eCP, +qCP, −qCP) is enforced by the Nexus rule to minimise SSV gradients — this rule is the physical content of A₅ acting as the symmetry of the shell. As long as the Nexus minimisation produces a symmetric distribution, A₅ holds exactly and J = 0 follows.

### Q3. "The Higgs couples to all massive particles with coupling proportional to mass (Yukawa couplings). Does CPP explain this?"

Partially. In CPP, mass is organisational energy (AXIM-6). The dodecahedral shell couples to all CP aggregates via SSV compression — the more organised (massive) the particle, the stronger the coupling to the shell's confinement field. This gives a coupling proportional to mass, consistent with the observed Yukawa pattern. What CPP does not yet derive are the absolute magnitudes of the Yukawa couplings (the top quark Yukawa ≈ 1; the electron Yukawa ≈ 3 × 10⁻⁶). The pattern (proportional to mass) is derived; the scale (Yukawa ≈ m/v where v = 246 GeV is the Higgs VEV) depends on OPEN-P-EW-1 (the η derivation) since v is itself a Planck-to-weak-scale quantity.
