## §1 Setup: the mechanism question

### §1.1 What we are asking

The Capotauro paper v0.9 derives the parity-violation matrix element $|M| = \chi/6 = \phi^{-3}/6 \approx 0.0394$ from three structural inputs: the substrate's primitive chirality magnitude $|\chi| = \phi^{-3}$ (FI-C-9), the cage-shell averaging factor $1/6 = d_E/V_{\text{cage}}$ (FI-C-10), and the substrate-physics parameter $b = \chi/\sqrt{3}$ (chirality-eigenvalue matching). Of these three, FI-C-10 is the load-bearing technical postulate at v1.0 with first-principles closure registered as open work (OPEN-FI-C-10-FP). FI-C-9, by contrast, is now registered as a primitive feature of the substrate per the v0.9 framing reframe.

**The mechanism question.** What physical primitive in the substrate produces the chirality magnitude $|\chi| = \phi^{-3}$? The CPP programme's core methodological principle is that mathematical descriptions are not themselves physical mechanisms; declaring the chirality as a primitive feature is an honest stance at v1.0 ("we register this as a foundational input because we do not have a derivation"), but it is not the final stance ("we will derive this from a mechanism eventually"). This sketch is the working development of that derivation.

### §1.2 Why "spontaneous symmetry breaking" doesn't answer it

Earlier versions of the Capotauro paper (v0.4 through v0.8) used spontaneous-symmetry-breaking (SSB) language to describe the chirality: $\chi$ as the "order parameter" of a "broken-symmetry phase" of an $\Hfour \to \Ifour$ "chirality $\mathbb{Z}_2$". The vocabulary is correct as a mathematical description of the symmetry-reduction structure, but it does not answer the mechanism question:

- SSB framing posits a prior $\Hfour$-symmetric phase. The substrate has no observable prior state; if the substrate carries its chirality as a feature of its existence-state, there is no prior phase from which it transitions.
- SSB framing posits dynamics that drive the symmetry breaking. The CPP framework does not currently specify what those dynamics are; calling them "spontaneous" is a label, not a mechanism.
- SSB framing leaves the sign of $\chi$ (which of the two enantiomorphs is selected) as a "random initial condition." For CPP this is the wrong answer at the foundational level: either the substrate has a primitive directional feature that picks the sign (the case developed here), or the universe is structured with a primitive handedness as a feature of its existence-state.

The v0.9 paper's primitive-feature framing acknowledges this and registers the chirality as a foundational input rather than as the outcome of unspecified dynamics. The mechanism question becomes: what is the physical primitive that produces the chirality?

### §1.3 Three candidate readings considered

In Session 120 (conversation), three readings of "slight modification of the Golden Ratio so the substrate's geometry is non-equilateral" were considered:

**Reading A — Substrate-level deviation in $\phi$-power distance ratios.** The 600-cell as a mathematical object has all edges of equal length, with vertex shells at distances related by exact powers of $\phi$. The *physical substrate* doesn't quite satisfy this: edge lengths along one 4D direction differ slightly from edges in the orthogonal 3D subspace, with the difference correlated with handedness. The substrate is geometrically chiral at the lattice level, not via emergence from a symmetric prior state.

**Reading B — Two competing scales $\phi$ and $\phi'$.** The substrate has two near-Golden-Ratio scales whose interplay defines the chirality. The chirality bias is the difference between them. This introduces a second parameter, which is a cost: structural determination of $\phi'$ would be required to avoid breaking the zero-free-parameters discipline.

**Reading C — Primitive 4D direction breaking $\Hfour$ to $\Ifour$.** A specific 4D direction $\hat{n}$ is "preferred" in the substrate. Edges aligned with vs orthogonal to this direction have different effective lengths. The $\Hfour$ symmetry of the idealized 600-cell is broken down to the subgroup that preserves the chirality axis, which is approximately $\Ifour$. The chirality magnitude $|\chi| \sim \phi^{-3}$ is fixed by how aggressively the preferred direction breaks the full symmetry under the substrate-structure-preservation constraint.

Reading C is the cleanest version of Reading A: it identifies a specific physical primitive (the preferred 4D direction) that produces the geometric chirality. Reading C is developed in detail in §2 below. Reading A and Reading B remain candidate alternatives if Reading C fails at sketch level.

## §2 Reading C developed: the preferred 4D direction $\hat{n}$

### §2.1 The primitive

**Postulate (provisional, sketch-level).** The substrate has a primitive 4D direction $\hat{n}$ in the 4D ambient space where the 600-cell sits. This direction is a foundational feature of the substrate's existence-state, on the same epistemic footing as the existence of Conscious Points and Grid Points (A1, A2) and the rules governing their interactions (A3 through A11).

The primitive $\hat{n}$ is a unit vector in $\mathbb{R}^4$. Its specific orientation in 4D space is not a derivable quantity within the framework (any rotation of the entire substrate is unobservable from inside the substrate); what matters is that *one* direction is preferred over all others. The orientation of $\hat{n}$ defines the "axis" of the substrate's chirality; the sign of $\hat{n}$ (i.e., $\hat{n}$ vs $-\hat{n}$) determines which of the two enantiomorphs of the 600-cell is the actual substrate.

This is exactly the role that "the rule, the process, the physicality, the mechanism that produces the chiral bias" plays in Thomas Abshier's original monologue: a primitive feature of substrate structure, in the same epistemic category as CPs/GPs and rules of relationships, that picks out one of two otherwise-equivalent enantiomorphs.

### §2.2 Geometric consequences: how $\hat{n}$ breaks $\Hfour$ to $\Ifour$

The idealized 600-cell has full symmetry group $\Hfour$ of order 14,400. Among the 14,400 symmetry operations are:
- 7,200 rotational operations (the chiral subgroup $\Ifour = H_4^+$)
- 7,200 reflection-type operations (the coset $\Hfour \setminus \Ifour$)

A primitive direction $\hat{n}$ in 4D space is preserved (up to overall sign) by some symmetry operations and reversed by others:
- **Rotations that fix $\hat{n}$ pointwise**: form a 3D rotation subgroup acting on the 3D hyperplane orthogonal to $\hat{n}$. This is approximately the chiral icosahedral group $I$ (3D, order 60) embedded in $\Ifour$.
- **Rotations that map $\hat{n}$ to $-\hat{n}$**: these reverse the orientation of the chirality axis. Under the postulate that $\hat{n}$ is primitive (with a definite sign, not a direction modulo sign), these rotations are *not* symmetries of the substrate.
- **Reflections**: the standard reflection generators of $\Hfour$ that act on $\hat{n}$ to send it to $-\hat{n}$ are broken; the reflections that fix $\hat{n}$ (act within the orthogonal hyperplane) are preserved.

**Claim (sketch-level, to be verified by group-theoretic computation).** The subgroup of $\Hfour$ that preserves the primitive direction $\hat{n}$ (with sign) is isomorphic to $\Ifour$, the rotational subgroup. The substrate's actual symmetry is therefore $\Ifour$, not $\Hfour$. The $\Hfour \to \Ifour$ reduction in the v0.9 paper is the structural consequence of $\hat{n}$ being primitive, not the outcome of a dynamical event.

This is the key structural connection: under Reading C, the algebraic structure "$\Hfour \to \Ifour$ reduction" carries the same mathematical content as the SSB framing, but its physical interpretation is opposite. The idealized geometric description treats all 4D directions as equivalent (full $\Hfour$); the substrate's actual existence has $\hat{n}$ as a primitive feature, breaking the symmetry down to $\Ifour$ at the structural level. The breaking is "structural" in the sense that it requires no dynamics — it is simply that the substrate has $\hat{n}$ as a feature.

### §2.3 Edge-length variation from $\hat{n}$

Each edge of the 600-cell connects two vertices $v_i, v_j \in \mathbb{R}^4$. The edge vector is $\vec{e}_{ij} = v_j - v_i$. Under the idealized $\Hfour$-symmetric 600-cell, all 720 edges have equal length $|\vec{e}_{ij}|_{\text{ideal}} = 1/\phi$ in vertex-distance units (with the second vertex shell at distance 1).

Under the preferred direction $\hat{n}$, each edge acquires a chirality-correlated length perturbation. The simplest perturbation that respects $\Ifour$ symmetry (i.e., respects the residual symmetry of the substrate after $\hat{n}$ is fixed) and produces a chirality bias is:

$$|\vec{e}_{ij}|_{\text{actual}} = |\vec{e}_{ij}|_{\text{ideal}} \cdot \left(1 + \epsilon \cdot (\hat{e}_{ij} \cdot \hat{n})\right)$$

where $\hat{e}_{ij}$ is the unit vector along the edge, $\hat{n}$ is the primitive direction, and $\epsilon$ is the perturbation magnitude.

**Physical reading.** Edges that are oriented in 4D space such that $\hat{e}_{ij} \cdot \hat{n} > 0$ (i.e., aligned with the preferred direction) are slightly longer than the idealized length; edges with $\hat{e}_{ij} \cdot \hat{n} < 0$ (anti-aligned) are slightly shorter. Edges orthogonal to $\hat{n}$ retain the idealized length. The asymmetry between aligned and anti-aligned edges is the substrate's structural chirality at the lattice level.

This is the "non-equilateral isosceles/equilateral triangle" intuition: the substrate's triangular faces have slightly unequal edge lengths, with the inequality correlated with the orientation in 4D space relative to $\hat{n}$. The universe has a chiral twist built into the edge ratios — exactly Thomas Abshier's original geometric intuition.

### §2.4 Why $\epsilon \sim \phi^{-3}$: the perturbative-distance-ratio constraint

The v0.9 paper's §2.3 perturbative-distance-ratio argument selects $\phi^{-3}$ as the first natural scale at which a chirality bias is small enough not to disturb the 600-cell's geometric structure at zeroth order. Under SSB framing, this argument was stated as: "the order parameter must be perturbative." Under Reading C, the same argument is restated as: "the primitive-direction perturbation $\epsilon$ must be perturbative, and the natural distance-ratio scales $\phi^{-n}$ provide a discrete set of candidate magnitudes; $\phi^{-3}$ is the first viable scale."

The argument structure:

1. **Constraint**: $\epsilon$ must be small enough that the substrate-as-structure remains approximately the idealized $\Hfour$-symmetric 600-cell. If $\epsilon$ is comparable to the natural geometric scales of the polytope, the substrate's geometry no longer "looks like" the 600-cell at all — vertices would be at wildly different distances, edges would have macroscopically different lengths, and the algebraic machinery (irrep decomposition, $\Dsix$ stabilizer at the K3 location, cage-shell structure) would not apply.

2. **Candidate scales**: The natural distance-ratio scales available in the 600-cell are powers of $\phi$. The smallest geometric scale is the edge length $1/\phi$ in vertex-distance units. Candidate magnitudes for $\epsilon$, in descending order: $\phi^{-1} \approx 0.618$, $\phi^{-2} \approx 0.382$, $\phi^{-3} \approx 0.236$, $\phi^{-4} \approx 0.146$, ...

3. **Reductio at $n = 1, 2$**:
   - $\epsilon = \phi^{-1}$ is at the edge-length scale: aligned vs anti-aligned edges would differ by 100%, destroying the polytope structure.
   - $\epsilon = \phi^{-2} \approx 0.38$ is ~60% of the edge length: still too large to be perturbative.

4. **First viable scale at $n = 3$**: $\epsilon = \phi^{-3} \approx 0.24$ is the third-order distance-ratio scale, the first at which the perturbation drops below ~25% of the edge length. At this scale, the substrate's lattice retains its $\Hfour$-symmetric idealization at leading order, with the $\hat{n}$-induced asymmetry as a perturbative correction.

5. **Forward consequence**: $\phi^{-3}$ is the first viable scale; higher $n$ are mathematically allowed but produce predicted Capotauro matrix elements far below the empirical anchor (v0.9 paper §2.3.4 quantifies: $\phi^{-4}$ undershoot ~39%; $\phi^{-5}$ undershoot ~63%).

Under Reading C, this argument is **a structural derivation, not a postulate**: $\epsilon = \chi = \phi^{-3}$ is the unique consequence of the perturbative-distance-ratio constraint combined with the primitive-direction postulate. The substrate's chirality magnitude is not a free parameter — once $\hat{n}$ is postulated as primitive, the magnitude $\phi^{-3}$ is forced by the geometry of the 600-cell.

### §2.5 What this derivation accomplishes (and what it does not)

**What it accomplishes.** If the primitive-direction postulate ($\hat{n}$ as a foundational substrate feature) is accepted, the chirality magnitude $|\chi| = \phi^{-3}$ is derived rather than postulated. The argument structure is parallel to the v0.9 paper's §2.3 perturbative-distance-ratio argument, but with one additional ingredient: a physical primitive ($\hat{n}$) that justifies the perturbation in the first place. The chirality bias is no longer described mathematically as an "order parameter" without specifying what is doing the ordering; it is described as the direction-correlated edge-length perturbation produced by the primitive 4D direction.

**What it does not accomplish.** The mechanism question is not fully closed — Reading C replaces "what produces the chirality?" with "what produces the primitive 4D direction?" The new primitive ($\hat{n}$) is registered as a foundational feature of the substrate's existence-state, on the same epistemic footing as the existence of CPs and GPs. The question of why the substrate has any preferred direction at all is then deferred to the same epistemic category as "why does the substrate exist?" — a primitive feature question, not a derivable-from-deeper-physics question.

This trade is the honest mechanism candidate: we cannot derive everything; we can choose what we declare as primitive. Reading C chooses to declare a preferred 4D direction as primitive, in exchange for deriving the magnitude $\phi^{-3}$. The chirality is then a structural consequence of the primitive direction, not an additional postulate.

## §3 Predicted fractional chirality retention across observable classes

A distinctive feature of Reading C: the primitive direction $\hat{n}$ produces a chirality bias of magnitude $\chi = \phi^{-3}$ at the substrate level, but the bias appears in observable quantities only to the extent that the observable retains the chirality content of its operator. This generates a structured prediction across observable classes.

### §3.1 The mechanism of fractional retention

The chirality bias at the substrate level is $\chi = \phi^{-3} \approx 0.236$. At the observable level, the bias is filtered through:

1. The operator's irrep content under the cage's symmetry group ($\Dsix$ for K3-doublet observables).
2. The cage-shell averaging factor $d_\Gamma / V_{\text{cage}}$ where $d_\Gamma$ is the irrep dimension.
3. The observable's specific measurement context (which states are probed, what averaging the measurement performs).

The Wigner-Eckart machinery of the Capotauro paper computes this filtering for the chirality observable $\Cchi$ (B$_2$ irrep, the chirality-content irrep): the result is $|M_\perp| = 2/12 = 1/6$, so the observable retains $1/6$ of the substrate's chirality. The full prediction is $|M| = \chi \cdot (1/6) = \chi/6 \approx 0.0394$.

For other irrep contents, the cage-shell factor changes:

| Operator irrep | Dimension $d_\Gamma$ | Cage-shell factor $d_\Gamma / V_{\text{cage}}$ | Chirality retention |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| $A_1$ (trivial) | 1 | $1/12$ | $\chi/12 \approx 0.020$ |
| $A_2$ (sign) | 1 | $1/12$ | $\chi/12 \approx 0.020$ |
| $B_1$ | 1 | $1/12$ | $\chi/12 \approx 0.020$ |
| **$B_2$ (chirality)** | **1** | **$1/12$, but × 2 for K3-doublet** | **$\chi \cdot 2/12 = \chi/6 \approx 0.039$** |
| $E$ | 2 | $2/12 = 1/6$ | $\chi/6 \approx 0.039$ |

(The K3-doublet states transform in the $E$-irrep of $\Sthree$, so the cage-shell factor effectively gains a factor of 2 from the doublet dimension. This is what produces $|M_\perp| = 2/12 = 1/6$ for the chirality observable specifically.)

### §3.2 The prediction: different observable classes carry different fractions

For a substrate-level observable $\mathcal{O}$ that couples to the chirality primitive $\hat{n}$ through cage-shell averaging at K3-doublet locations, the predicted observable magnitude is:

$$\langle \mathcal{O} \rangle = (\text{substrate chirality content of } \mathcal{O}) \cdot (\text{cage-shell averaging factor}) \cdot \chi$$

where the cage-shell averaging factor is $d_\Gamma / V_{\text{cage}}$ for the operator's $\Gamma$-irrep on the cage of volume $V_{\text{cage}}$.

**Specific predictions across cage geometries**:
- Tetrahedral cage ($V = 4$, stabilizer order $|T_d \text{-related}| = 24$): retention $\sim 2/24 = 1/12$, observable $\sim \chi/12 \approx 0.020$
- **Icosahedral cage** ($V = 12$, $|\Dsix| = 12$): retention $1/6$, observable $\chi/6 \approx 0.039$ ← **the Capotauro case**
- Dodecahedral cage ($V = 20$, stabilizer order 60): retention $\sim 2/60 = 1/30$, observable $\sim \chi/30 \approx 0.008$
- Icosidodecahedral cage ($V = 30$, stabilizer order 120): retention $\sim 2/120 = 1/60$, observable $\sim \chi/60 \approx 0.004$

**Specific predictions across operator irreps** (on the icosahedral cage, for K3-doublet states):
- Mass operator ($A_1$ content at leading order): chirality retention zero at leading order; surviving corrections are $\mathcal{O}(\chi^2) \sim 0.056^2 \approx 5.6 \times 10^{-3}$
- Charge operator (mixed irrep content): partial chirality retention
- Chirality operator ($B_2$ content): full chirality retention $\chi/6$

Dr. Abshier's observation: the CPP programme consistently produces empirical matches at the 1-2% level across multiple sectors:

| Sector | Observable | Match precision |

Under Reading C, this pattern admits two readings:

**Strong reading (Dr. Abshier's hypothesis)**: The 1-2% residual is the chirality bias leaking through into observables that should otherwise match precisely. This would require the chirality content of the observable's operator to manifest as a structural noise at the percent level across the corpus. Under this reading, the recurring 2% is a *signature* of the substrate's primitive chirality.

**Weak reading (preferred at sketch level)**: The 1-2% residual is dominated by measurement uncertainty in the empirical anchors (which are typically known at the 1-10% level), and the chirality bias appears at predicted magnitudes where it should:

The weak reading is more conservative and more easily defensible. The strong reading is a stronger claim that, if true, would constitute a programme-level signature of the substrate's primitive chirality — but it requires more structural argument to defend than is currently available at sketch level.

**A third reading (the one Reading C actually supports)**: Different observables retain different fractions of the chirality content depending on the operator's irrep structure on the cage. The 1-2% pattern is not a noise signature; it is the predicted appearance of chirality content in observables that retain it. Mass observables retain very little ($\mathcal{O}(\chi^2)$); chirality observables retain the full $\chi/6$. Other operators (charge, electroweak coupling, etc.) retain intermediate fractions. This is a *prediction* of Reading C that can be tested by computing the cage-shell averaging factor for each observable in the corpus and comparing to the observed empirical match precision.

## §4 Testable consequences and falsification routes

### §4.1 Direct falsifiers of Reading C

**Falsifier 1: $|\chi| \neq \phi^{-3}$ at the substrate level.** If a future direct measurement of the substrate chirality (e.g., via a CPP-native experimental signature for $\Delta p_{LR}$ at the K3-doublet level at the 1% precision level) yields $|\chi|$ inconsistent with $\phi^{-3}$ at the ±2% level, Reading C is falsified along with FI-C-9 itself.

The K3-doublet states' $\Dsix$ stabilizer structure (FI-C-4 inherited from SF-2 v1.0) is a downstream consequence of $\Ifour$ being the substrate's actual symmetry; structural inconsistency at this level would propagate.

(This probe would require access to substrate-level geometric structure, which is not currently experimentally available; this falsifier is over the long horizon.)

**Falsifier 4: Fractional chirality retention prediction violated.** If different observable classes do not exhibit the predicted fractional chirality retention pattern across the corpus, Reading C is falsified. Specifically, if mass observables (predicted $\mathcal{O}(\chi^2)$ retention) exhibit chirality content at $\mathcal{O}(\chi)$ or higher, or if chirality observables in different cages do not match the predicted $\chi/12$, $\chi/30$, $\chi/60$ retention factors, the prediction fails.

### §5.2 The perturbative-distance-ratio argument under Reading C

**Question 4**: Higher-order terms in the $\epsilon$-perturbation expansion. The leading-order $\epsilon \cdot (\hat{e}_{ij} \cdot \hat{n})$ correction in §2.3 produces a chirality bias linear in $\epsilon$. What about second-order corrections $\epsilon^2 \cdot (\hat{e}_{ij} \cdot \hat{n})^2$? Detailed computation required to verify the predicted retention factors.

### §5.3 Cross-sector consistency

The W bracelet is defined on a specific subspace of the substrate; its orientation relative to $\hat{n}$ matters for the chirality coupling. Cross-sector consistency check required.

**Question 6**: SM-2 charge asymmetry mechanism. The qDP/eDP linear ZBW screening asymmetry (FI-C-7 from SM-2) is registered as inheriting the substrate-level chirality. Under Reading C, this means SM-2's asymmetry must be derivable from the same primitive direction $\hat{n}$. Specifically, the qDP-eDP distinction must correlate with edges oriented along vs orthogonal to $\hat{n}$. Detailed computation required.

### §5.4 Cosmological-timing connection

**Question 7**: Sub-claim (a) vs sub-claim (b) interaction. Sub-claim (a) (\S\ref{sec:open_subclaim_a} of the v0.9 paper) is the cosmological nucleation event that locks in the sign of $\chi$ universe-wide.
