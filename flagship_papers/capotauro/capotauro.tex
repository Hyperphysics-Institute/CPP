\subsection{The Capotauro problem in CPP}\label{sec:cap_problem}

\subsection{Wigner-Eckart on $\Dsix$}\label{sec:wigner_eckart_d6}

The Wigner-Eckart theorem on $\Dsix$ states that the matrix element of an irrep-$\Gamma$ operator $\hat{O}^{\Gamma}$ between states transforming in irreps $\Gamma_1$ and $\Gamma_2$ factorizes as
\[
\langle \Gamma_2, m_2 | \hat{O}^{\Gamma} | \Gamma_1, m_1 \rangle = \langle \Gamma_2 || \hat{O}^{\Gamma} || \Gamma_1 \rangle \cdot C^{\Gamma_2, m_2}_{\Gamma, \Gamma_1; m_1}
\]
where $\langle \Gamma_2 || \hat{O}^{\Gamma} || \Gamma_1 \rangle$ is the reduced matrix element (irrep-dependent only) and $C^{\Gamma_2, m_2}_{\Gamma, \Gamma_1; m_1}$ is the Clebsch-Gordan coefficient. For the present matrix element, $\Gamma = B_2$, $\Gamma_1 = \Gamma_2 = E_2$, $m_1$ and $m_2$ index the K3-doublet states.

Because $\Dsix$ factorizes as $\Sthree \times \mathbb{Z}_2$, the Wigner-Eckart matrix element factorizes correspondingly into K3-amplitude and perpendicular-wavefunction factors:

\end{equation}
The K3-amplitude factor depends only on the $\Sthree$ irrep structure of the K3-doublet states and the $A_2$-irrep generator of the operator. Tested across 22 candidate scalings combining linear and quadratic terms; none reproduces the observed $\sin^2\theta_{13}$ within $5\sigma$ without ad hoc parameter tuning.
    \item \textbf{Linear-amplitude reading of $\sin^2\theta_{13}$}: the hypothesis that $\sin^2\theta_{13}$ should be read as a linear-in-amplitude quantity rather than modulus-squared.
