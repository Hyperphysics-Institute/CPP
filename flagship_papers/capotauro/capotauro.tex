\subsection{The $\Hfour \to H_3 = I_h$ vertex-aligned reduction}\label{sec:h4_i4}

The 600-cell is the regular four-dimensional polytope with 120 vertices, 720 edges, 1200 faces, and 600 tetrahedral cells, with full symmetry group $\Hfour$ (the $H_4$ Coxeter group) of order 14{,}400. The rotational subgroup $\Ifour = H_4^+$ has order 7{,}200, an index-2 normal subgroup with quotient $\Hfour / \Ifour = \mathbb{Z}_2$. The two enantiomorphic forms $[\text{600-cell}]_L$ and $[\text{600-cell}]_R$ are related by a reflection element of $\Hfour$ that is not contained in $\Ifour$~\cite{coxeter_1973}. Reflection of $\hat{n} \to -\hat{n}$ produces the enantiomorphic substrate; this is the structural origin of substrate chirality under Reading C.

\subsection{The 4D inner-product pattern at the host vertex}\label{sec:host_vertex_inner_products}

Fix a host vertex $v_{\text{host}}$ on the unit 600-cell with $\hat{n} = v_{\text{host}}$ (vertex-aligned Reading C; FI-C-RC-2). The 600-cell's edge structure around $v_{\text{host}}$ has two edge classes:

\begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{0.2em}
    \item \textbf{Host-to-first-shell edges} (12 of them): edges connecting $v_{\text{host}}$ to each of its 12 first-shell vertices $v_i$ at vertex-to-vertex distance $1/\phig$, with $v_i \cdot v_{\text{host}} = \phig/2$ (the 600-cell edge cosine on $S^3$).
    \item \textbf{First-shell-to-first-shell edges} (30 of them, among which lie all K3-base edges): edges connecting pairs $v_a, v_b$ of first-shell vertices that are themselves 600-cell edge-neighbors of each other (each first-shell icosahedron has 30 such edges).
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Host-to-first-shell inner product.} For any host-to-first-shell edge $\hat{e}_{(\text{host},i)} = (v_i - v_{\text{host}})/|v_i - v_{\text{host}}|$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:host_to_first_shell_inner_product}
\hat{e}_{(\text{host},i)} \cdot \hat{n} \;=\; \frac{(v_i - v_{\text{host}}) \cdot v_{\text{host}}}{|v_i - v_{\text{host}}|} \;=\; \frac{v_i \cdot v_{\text{host}} - 1}{|v_i - v_{\text{host}}|} \;=\; \frac{\phig/2 - 1}{1/\phig} \;=\; -\frac{1}{2\phig} \;\approx\; -0.309,
\end{equation}
where the third equality uses $|v_i - v_{\text{host}}|^2 = 2(1 - v_i \cdot v_{\text{host}}) = 2(1 - \phig/2) = 2 - \phig = \phig^{-2}$ giving $|v_i - v_{\text{host}}| = 1/\phig$, and $(\phig/2 - 1) \cdot \phig = \phig^2/2 - \phig = (\phig + 1)/2 - \phig = (1 - \phig)/2 = -1/(2\phig)$ using $\phig^2 = \phig + 1$. The value $-1/(2\phig)$ is identical across all 12 host-to-first-shell edges (the inner product depends only on $v_i \cdot v_{\text{host}}$, common to all 12).

\textbf{First-shell-to-first-shell inner product.} For any first-shell-to-first-shell edge $\hat{e}_{ab} = (v_b - v_a)/|v_b - v_a|$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:first_shell_to_first_shell_inner_product}
\hat{e}_{ab} \cdot \hat{n} \;=\; \frac{(v_b - v_a) \cdot v_{\text{host}}}{|v_b - v_a|} \;=\; \frac{v_b \cdot v_{\text{host}} - v_a \cdot v_{\text{host}}}{|v_b - v_a|} \;=\; \frac{\phig/2 - \phig/2}{|v_b - v_a|} \;=\; 0,
\end{equation}
identically in 4D. This is the key 4D geometric fact: \emph{first-shell-to-first-shell 600-cell edges are tangent to $\hat{n}$}; their direct edge-length perturbations under Eq.~\eqref{eq:edge_perturbation} vanish at $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)$.

\textbf{Numerical verification.} A direct computational check confirms Eqs.~\eqref{eq:host_to_first_shell_inner_product}--\eqref{eq:first_shell_to_first_shell_inner_product} at machine precision~\cite{abshier_capotauro_chiral_mechanism_sketch}.\footnote{Standalone NumPy verification at \texttt{flagship\_papers/capotauro/code/q1prime\_w\_bracelet\_geometry.py} (referenced in the working sketch \S13.1, Patch 0424): all 12 host-to-first-shell edges produce $\hat{e} \cdot \hat{n} = -1/(2\phig)$ uniformly to machine precision; all 30 first-shell-to-first-shell edges produce $|\hat{e} \cdot \hat{n}| < 10^{-9}$.} The 4D inner-product pattern is therefore: uniform $-1/(2\phig)$ for host-to-first-shell, identically zero for first-shell-to-first-shell.

\subsection{The Local-$I_h$-Preservation Theorem}\label{sec:local_ih_preservation_theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Local $I_h$ Preservation at the Host Vertex]\label{thm:local_ih_preservation}
Under vertex-aligned Reading C with $\hat{n} = v_{\text{host}}$ a 600-cell vertex direction (FI-C-RC-1 + FI-C-RC-2, \S\ref{sec:h4_i4}), the local geometric structure at $v_{\text{host}}$ --- the first-shell icosahedron of 12 nearest-neighbor 600-cell vertices and the 20 triangular faces of this icosahedron, including all candidate $\Kthree$-base placements --- is preserved as $I_h$-symmetric at first order in the substrate-perturbation amplitude $\epsilon$, with only an isotropic radial scaling of the first-shell icosahedron by factor $(1 - \epsilon/(2\phig))$. Equivalently: at $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)$, all 12 first-shell vertices remain at identical distance from $v_{\text{host}}$ (modified from $1/\phig$ to $(1/\phig)(1 - \epsilon/(2\phig))$) and at identical pairwise distances among themselves (unchanged from the ideal 600-cell first-shell distance structure).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The $\Hfour$-stabilizer of $v_{\text{host}}$ is $\Stab_{\Hfour}(v_{\text{host}}) = H_3 = I_h$ of order 120 (\S\ref{sec:h4_i4}); $I_h$ acts transitively on the 12 first-shell vertices and on the 30 first-shell-to-first-shell edges. Under the edge-perturbation formula Eq.~\eqref{eq:edge_perturbation}, the perturbed distance between any two 600-cell vertices is determined by the unperturbed distance and the unit edge direction's inner product with $\hat{n}$. From Eqs.~\eqref{eq:host_to_first_shell_inner_product}--\eqref{eq:first_shell_to_first_shell_inner_product}:

\textbf{Host-to-first-shell distances.} For each $i \in \{1, \ldots, 12\}$:
\[
|v_i - v_{\text{host}}|_{\text{actual}} \;=\; \frac{1}{\phig}\Bigl(1 + \epsilon \cdot \bigl(-\tfrac{1}{2\phig}\bigr)\Bigr) \;=\; \frac{1}{\phig}\Bigl(1 - \frac{\epsilon}{2\phig}\Bigr).
\]
The factor $(1 - \epsilon/(2\phig))$ is identical across all 12 first-shell vertices --- an isotropic radial scaling of the first-shell icosahedron toward $v_{\text{host}}$.

\textbf{First-shell-to-first-shell distances.} For any 600-cell-edge-connected pair $v_a, v_b$ in the first shell:
\[
|v_b - v_a|_{\text{actual}} \;=\; \frac{1}{\phig}\bigl(1 + \epsilon \cdot 0\bigr) \;=\; \frac{1}{\phig},
\]
unchanged from the ideal 600-cell value. The 30 first-shell-to-first-shell 600-cell edges therefore retain their ideal length at $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)$.

\textbf{$I_h$ preservation.} The 12 first-shell vertices, viewed as points on the 2-sphere $\{x \in S^3 : x \cdot v_{\text{host}} = \phig/2 \cdot (1 - \epsilon/(2\phig))\}$ (the perturbed first-shell hyperplane), are at identical distances from $v_{\text{host}}$ (modified radius) and at identical pairwise 600-cell-edge distances among themselves (unchanged). The angular configuration of the 12 first-shell vertices on this 2-sphere is therefore unchanged from the ideal icosahedron; the perturbed first-shell vertices form an icosahedron differing from the ideal only by uniform radial scaling. The $I_h$ symmetry of the first-shell icosahedron is preserved.

The 20 triangular faces of the first-shell icosahedron, each formed by 3 mutually-600-cell-edge-connected first-shell vertices, retain their equilateral structure (all three edges of any such triangle are first-shell-to-first-shell at unperturbed length $1/\phig$). The K3-base, geometrically realized as one of these 20 triangular faces (sketch \S11.2; Finding C-W37)~\cite{abshier_capotauro_chiral_mechanism_sketch}, inherits the preserved equilateral structure.
$\square$
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Substrate-actual symmetry under Theorem~\ref{thm:local_ih_preservation}]\label{rem:substrate_actual_symmetry}
Theorem~\ref{thm:local_ih_preservation} sharpens the substrate-actual symmetry statement of \S\ref{sec:h4_i4}: the $\Hfour \to H_3 = I_h$ reduction (Operation B) is realized geometrically as the preservation of the first-shell icosahedron at $v_{\text{host}}$. \end{remark}

\subsection{The chirality observable $\Cchi$}\label{sec:chirality_observable}

\textbf{Physical meaning.} The chirality observable $\Cchi$ is the substrate-level operator whose expectation value measures the substrate's preference for one enantiomorph over the other at a specific $\Kthree$ location. A racemic substrate ($|\chi| = 0$) would have $\langle \Cchi \rangle = 0$; the actual substrate has $\langle \Cchi \rangle \neq 0$ because its $\Ifour$ symmetry (not $\Hfour$) breaks the parity that would otherwise force the difference to vanish. The operator's matrix element on K3-doublet basis states is the substrate-level handle on parity-violating processes that descends, through SF-2's W bracelet $\Dsix$ stabilizer (FI-C-4) and the cage-shell averaging factor 1/6 (FI-C-10), to the observable parity-violation asymmetry $\DeltapLR$ (\S\ref{sec:dplr}).

\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.2em}

The composite irreducible representation of $\Cchi$ within $\Dsix = \Sthree \times \mathbb{Z}_2$ on the K3-amplitude $\times$ perpendicular-wavefunction tensor structure is $B_2$ (the irrep that is $\sigma_1$-ODD and $\zeta$-ODD). \subsection{The $\Kthree$ stabilizer $\Dsix = \Sthree \times \mathbb{Z}_2$}\label{sec:k3_stabilizer}

The $\Kthree$ triangle's automorphism group as a labelled graph is $\Sthree$ (the symmetric group on three elements, order 6), acting by permutations of the three vertices $\{V_1, V_2, V_3\}$. The Finding C-W5 ($\Dsix = \Sthree \times \mathbb{Z}_2$ structure) and Finding C-W6 (the perpendicular $\zeta$-parity component generates the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ factor) of the working sketch~\cite{abshier_capotauro_subclaim_c_sketch} together establish the stabilizer's product structure. The $S_3' \subset \Dsix$ identification is the chirality-preserving subgroup in the sense that the chirality observable $\Cchi$ is invariant under $S_3'$ action: $\sigma \cdot \Cchi \cdot \sigma^{-1} = \Cchi$ for all $\sigma \in S_3'$. The complement $\Dsix \setminus S_3'$ contains the chirality-violating elements that flip the sign of $\Cchi$.

\subsection{Irreducible representations of $\Dsix$}\label{sec:d6_irreps}

The irreducible representations of $\Dsix = \Sthree \times \mathbb{Z}_2$ are products of the irreducible representations of $\Sthree$ and $\mathbb{Z}_2$:

    \item $\Sthree$ irreps: $A_1$ (trivial), $A_2$ (sign), $E$ (2-dimensional).
    \item $\mathbb{Z}_2$ irreps: trivial ($\zeta$-EVEN), sign ($\zeta$-ODD).
\end{itemize}

The product irreps of $\Dsix$ are then $A_1^{\pm}$, $A_2^{\pm}$, $E^{\pm}$, with the $\pm$ superscript denoting $\zeta$-parity. In conventional notation:
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
& \zeta\text{-EVEN} & \zeta\text{-ODD} \\
\hline
A_1 & A_1 & A_1' \\
A_2 & A_2 & B_2 \\
E & E_1 & E_2
\end{array}
\]
where the second column corresponds to the $\zeta$-ODD versions. The chirality observable $\Cchi$ transforms in the irrep $B_2 = A_2 \otimes (\zeta\text{-ODD})$: it is $\sigma_1$-ODD in K3-amplitudes (\S\ref{sec:chirality_observable}: the $A_2$ generator $T_{A_2}(b) = i \cdot b \cdot S$ is the unique $\Sthree$ $A_2$ representative) and $\zeta$-ODD in the perpendicular wavefunction (Finding C-W11)~\cite{abshier_capotauro_subclaim_c_sketch}.

The K3-doublet states transform in irrep $E_2 = E \otimes (\zeta\text{-ODD})$ as a tensor product of the $\Sthree$ $E$-doublet on K3-amplitudes and the $\zeta$-ODD perpendicular wavefunction (\S\ref{sec:perpendicular_wavefunction}, Eq.~\ref{eq:full_basis}). The matrix element $|M|$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:composite_cap_we} is therefore the K3-doublet $E_2$ expectation value of the $B_2$ operator $\Cchi$.

\subsection{Wigner-Eckart on $\Dsix$}\label{sec:wigner_eckart_d6}

The Wigner-Eckart theorem on $\Dsix$ states that the matrix element of an irrep-$\Gamma$ operator $\hat{O}^{\Gamma}$ between states transforming in irreps $\Gamma_1$ and $\Gamma_2$ factorizes as
\[
\langle \Gamma_2, m_2 | \hat{O}^{\Gamma} | \Gamma_1, m_1 \rangle = \langle \Gamma_2 || \hat{O}^{\Gamma} || \Gamma_1 \rangle \cdot C^{\Gamma_2, m_2}_{\Gamma, \Gamma_1; m_1}
\]
where $\langle \Gamma_2 || \hat{O}^{\Gamma} || \Gamma_1 \rangle$ is the reduced matrix element (irrep-dependent only) and $C^{\Gamma_2, m_2}_{\Gamma, \Gamma_1; m_1}$ is the Clebsch-Gordan coefficient. For the present matrix element, $\Gamma = B_2$, $\Gamma_1 = \Gamma_2 = E_2$, $m_1$ and $m_2$ index the K3-doublet states.

Because $\Dsix$ factorizes as $\Sthree \times \mathbb{Z}_2$, the Wigner-Eckart matrix element factorizes correspondingly into K3-amplitude and perpendicular-wavefunction factors:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:we_factorization}
\langle \Phi_-^{(i)} | \Cchi | \Phi_-^{(j)} \rangle = \underbrace{\langle \phi_-^{(i)} | T_{A_2}(b) | \phi_-^{(j)} \rangle}_{\text{K3-amplitude factor } M_{K_3}^{(ij)}} \cdot \underbrace{\langle \chi_i | T_\perp | \chi_j \rangle}_{\text{cage-shell factor } M_\perp^{(ij)}}.
\end{equation}
The K3-amplitude factor depends only on the $\Sthree$ irrep structure of the K3-doublet states and the $A_2$-irrep generator of the operator. The cage-shell factor depends on the perpendicular-direction $\mathbb{Z}_2$ structure and is computed via Schur orthogonality on the icosahedral cage hosting the charged-lepton substrate states (\S\ref{sec:composite_we}).

\subsection{The $\sigma_1$-ODD operator parameterization correction}\label{sec:sigma1_odd_correction}

A substantive correction to the working-sketch §13 framework emerged during the closure trajectory~\cite{abshier_capotauro_subclaim_c_sketch}.\footnote{Correction introduced at Session 95 Patch 0389.} The naive parameterization of $\sigma_1$-ODD operators on the K3-amplitude space uses two parameters $(a, b)$ giving $M_{K_3} = (b - 2a)/\sqrt{3}$. However, this parameterization captures the general $\sigma_1$-ODD operator subspace, which includes \emph{both} the $A_2$-irrep and the $E$-irrep components of $\Sthree$. For $\Cchi$ to be in $B_2$ of $\Dsix$ specifically, the K3-amplitude part must be in $A_2$ of $\Sthree$ (the unique irrep that is $\sigma_1$-ODD \emph{and} $r$-invariant, where $r$ is the cyclic K3-vertex rotation generator).

The unique $A_2$-irrep generator on the $\Sthree$ K3-amplitude space is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:t_a2_generator}
T_{A_2}(b) = i \cdot b \cdot S, \qquad S = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 0 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
where $S$ is the real antisymmetric matrix with cross-product-with-$(1,1,1)$ structure: for any K3-amplitude vector $v \in \mathbb{R}^3$, $S v = (1,1,1) \times v$ (Finding C-W21)~\cite{abshier_capotauro_subclaim_c_sketch}. The parameter $b$ is the substrate-physics parameter to be determined in \S\ref{sec:composite_we} via the chirality-eigenvalue matching principle.

\begin{lemma}[Spectral radius of $S$]\label{lem:spectral_S}
The matrix $S$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:t_a2_generator} has eigenvalues $\{0, +i\sqrt{3}, -i\sqrt{3}\}$ with spectral radius $\sqrt{3}$. The zero eigenvalue corresponds to the eigenvector $(1,1,1)/\sqrt{3}$ (the bonding K3-amplitude $|\phi_+\rangle$); the non-zero imaginary eigenvalues $\pm i\sqrt{3}$ correspond to the K3-doublet antibonding eigenvectors. Consequently, $T_{A_2}(b) = i \cdot b \cdot S$ has eigenvalues $\{0, \pm b\sqrt{3}\}$ on the K3-amplitude space, with the non-zero eigenvalues $\pm b\sqrt{3}$ residing entirely on the K3-doublet subspace $\{\phiminusOne, \phiminusTwo\}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Direct computation: the characteristic polynomial of $S$ is $\lambda^3 + 3\lambda = \lambda(\lambda^2 + 3) = 0$, giving $\lambda \in \{0, \pm i\sqrt{3}\}$. The null vector is found by inspection: $S(1,1,1)^T = (1-1, -1+1, 1-1)^T = (0,0,0)^T$. The cross-product structure $S v = (1,1,1) \times v$ confirms the null direction is parallel to $(1,1,1)$, hence orthogonal to the K3-doublet which is the orthogonal complement of $|\phi_+\rangle = (1,1,1)/\sqrt{3}$. The non-zero eigenvalues live on the K3-doublet subspace.

For $T_{A_2}(b) = i \cdot b \cdot S$, the eigenvalues are $\{i b \cdot 0, i b \cdot (+i\sqrt{3}), i b \cdot (-i\sqrt{3})\} = \{0, -b\sqrt{3}, +b\sqrt{3}\}$ --- real and centered at zero on the K3-doublet, as required for $T_{A_2}(b)$ to be a self-adjoint chirality-like operator on the antibonding doublet.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:spectral_S} is the load-bearing structural input for the chirality-eigenvalue matching principle in \S\ref{sec:k3_amplitude_factor}: identifying the K3-doublet's $T_{A_2}(b)$ eigenvalues $\pm b\sqrt{3}$ with the physical chirality eigenvalues $\pm \chi$ of $\Cchi$ at substrate level determines $b = \chi/\sqrt{3}$ uniquely, fixing the substrate-physics parameter.

\section{The Composite Capotauro Wigner-Eckart Theorem}\label{sec:composite_we}

\subsection{Theorem statement}\label{sec:theorem_statement}

\begin{theorem}[Composite Capotauro Wigner-Eckart Theorem]\label{thm:composite_cap_we}
Under foundational inputs FI-C-1 through FI-C-10 (enumerated in \S\ref{sec:fi_summary}) and CPP axioms A1 (DI-bit exchange substrate primitive), A3 (substrate orientation field), A4 (substrate isotropy at vertex level), and A7 (substrate-stress framework), the chirality matrix element of the chirality observable $\Cchi$ on the tribimaximal-aligned $\Kthree$-doublet basis states $\PhiminusOne$ and $\PhiminusTwo$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:full_basis}) is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main_result}
\boxed{\;|M| = |\langle \Phi_-^{(1)} | \Cchi | \Phi_-^{(2)} \rangle| = \frac{\chi}{6} = \frac{\phig^{-3}}{6} \approx 0.0394.\;}
\end{equation}
The matrix element decomposes as the product of two structural factors:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:decomposition}
|M| = |M_{\Kthree}| \cdot |M_\perp| = \chi \cdot \frac{1}{6}
\end{equation}
where $|M_{\Kthree}| = \chi$ is the K3-amplitude factor (Lemma~\ref{lem:k3_factor}, chirality-eigenvalue matching principle, \S\ref{sec:k3_amplitude_factor}) and $|M_\perp| = 1/6$ is the cage-shell averaging factor (Lemma~\ref{lem:cage_shell}, cage-shell averaging principle, \S\ref{sec:cage_shell_factor}).
\end{theorem}

The theorem is the publication-grade statement of the eight-step closure trajectory documented in the sub-sketch~\cite{abshier_capotauro_subclaim_c_sketch} across Sessions 87-102. The proof proceeds in eight steps, structurally factoring the matrix element via the Wigner-Eckart machinery of \S\ref{sec:wigner_eckart_d6} and applying two derivation principles (chirality-eigenvalue matching and cage-shell averaging) to determine the two structural factors.

The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:composite_cap_we} proceeds in eight steps:

\begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{0.2em}
    \item[\textbf{S1}] \textbf{Irrep identification}: $\Cchi \in B_2$ of $\Dsix$ via $\sigma_1$-ODD K3-amplitude content (unique $A_2$ generator $T_{A_2}(b) = i \cdot b \cdot S$, \S\ref{sec:sigma1_odd_correction}) and $\zeta$-ODD perpendicular-wavefunction content (Finding C-W11, \S\ref{sec:chirality_observable}).

    \item[\textbf{S3}] \textbf{K3-amplitude part identification}: the K3-amplitude factor depends only on the $A_2$-irrep generator $T_{A_2}(b)$ on the K3-doublet subspace (irrep-uniqueness from \S\ref{sec:sigma1_odd_correction}).

\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The K3-amplitude factor: chirality-eigenvalue matching principle}\label{sec:k3_amplitude_factor}

\begin{lemma}[K3-amplitude factor]\label{lem:k3_factor}
Under FI-C-9 (substrate-vacuum chirality magnitude $|\chi| = \phig^{-3}$), the K3-amplitude factor in the Wigner-Eckart factorization of the chirality matrix element on the TBM-aligned $\Kthree$-doublet is
\[
|M_{\Kthree}| = |\langle \phi_-^{(1)} | T_{A_2}(b) | \phi_-^{(2)} \rangle|_{b = \chi/\sqrt{3}} = \chi.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The unique $A_2$-irrep generator on the K3-amplitude space is $T_{A_2}(b) = i \cdot b \cdot S$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:t_a2_generator}). Direct symbolic computation on the TBM-aligned basis $\phiminusOne = (2,-1,-1)/\sqrt{6}$, $\phiminusTwo = (0,-1,1)/\sqrt{2}$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:m_k3_symbolic}
M_{\Kthree} = \langle \phi_-^{(1)} | T_{A_2}(b) | \phi_-^{(2)} \rangle = -i \cdot b \cdot \sqrt{3}.
\end{equation}
The imaginary phase $-i$ is intrinsic to the $A_2$-irrep generator (it reflects the antisymmetric structure of $S$); the magnitude $b\sqrt{3}$ tracks the spectral radius of $S$ on the K3-amplitude subspace (Lemma~\ref{lem:spectral_S}).

To determine the substrate-physics parameter $b$, we apply the \emph{chirality-eigenvalue matching principle} (Finding C-W23)~\cite{abshier_capotauro_subclaim_c_sketch}. The K3-doublet eigenvalues of $T_{A_2}(b)$ are $\pm b\sqrt{3}$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:spectral_S}). The physical chirality eigenvalues of $\Cchi$ at substrate level are $\pm \chi$ (the substrate primitive chirality magnitudes, FI-C-9 \S\ref{sec:order_parameter}: the eigenvalues of the chirality observable are $\pm |\chi|$ for the two enantiomorphs distinguished by the substrate's primitive chirality). Identifying:
\[
b\sqrt{3} = \chi \;\Longrightarrow\; b = \frac{\chi}{\sqrt{3}}.
\]
Substituting into Eq.~\eqref{eq:m_k3_symbolic}:
\[
M_{\Kthree}\big|_{b = \chi/\sqrt{3}} = -i \cdot \frac{\chi}{\sqrt{3}} \cdot \sqrt{3} = -i\chi.
\]
The magnitude is $|M_{\Kthree}| = \chi$.
\end{proof}

The chirality-eigenvalue matching principle (Finding C-W23) is the load-bearing structural step that determines the substrate-physics parameter $b$ uniquely from the substrate primitive chirality $\chi$. Without this identification, the parameter $b$ would remain free; with it, the matrix element acquires its $\chi$-dependence at theorem level.

\subsection{The cage-shell averaging factor: cage-shell averaging principle}\label{sec:cage_shell_factor}

The cage-shell averaging factor $|M_\perp| = 1/6$ is the second structural factor in the Wigner-Eckart decomposition. Before formalizing this in Lemma~\ref{lem:cage_shell}, this subsection establishes the physical motivation for why Schur orthogonality on the icosahedral cage is the correct averaging mechanism for chirality observables --- not just a mathematically suggestive coincidence. The motivation rests on two CPP axioms (A4 substrate isotropy at vertex level, A3 substrate orientation field / DI-bit propagation) and shows that the formal Schur-orthogonality result emerges as the structural consequence of these axioms applied to the cage geometry.

\subsubsection{Physical motivation: substrate isotropy + DI-bit propagation}\label{sec:cage_shell_factor_motivation}

The cage-shell extension to chirality observables (FI-C-10) is the load-bearing claim that the cage-shell coupling mechanism for chirality observables follows the same averaging structure as for mass observables (FI-C-6). At v1.0, FI-C-10 is registered as a foundational postulate (\S\ref{sec:fi_summary}); first-principles derivation from CPP axioms is registered as open work (\S\ref{sec:open_fi_c10}). The v1.0 paper does not derive FI-C-10 first-principles, but establishes physical plausibility through three complementary arguments.

\textbf{Intuition for the $1/6$ factor.} Before the formal arguments, the geometric picture: the K3-doublet has $d_E = 2$ basis states (the two members of the doublet); the icosahedral cage hosts $V_\text{cage} = 12$ symmetry-equivalent vertices. Under substrate isotropy, the chirality bias distributes its contribution uniformly across the cage --- two doublet degrees of freedom averaged over twelve cage positions yields the averaging factor $2/12 = 1/6$. The three formal arguments below justify why this geometric picture is the correct one structurally, and not an ansatz.

\textbf{Argument 1: Substrate-isotropy + irrep averaging (A4-based).}
CPP axiom A4 states the substrate is isotropic at the vertex level: no preferred direction emerges from substrate dynamics at individual vertices. The substrate's primitive chirality bias $\chi$ is a global property (\S\ref{sec:substrate_vacuum}), not a vertex-local property. Schur orthogonality is the mathematical expression of ``no preferred direction within an irreducible representation'': for an irrep $\Gamma$ of dimension $d_\Gamma$, summing $\Gamma$-projected quantities over the irrep basis produces a group-invariant average with factor $d_\Gamma / |G|$.

For $\Cchi$ acting on $\Kthree$-doublet $E$-irrep states extended over the icosahedral cage: the K3-doublet is $E$-irrep with $d_E = 2$; the cage hosts $V_\text{cage} = 12$ vertices, invariant under $\Dsix = \Sthree \times \mathbb{Z}_2$ (\S\ref{sec:k3_stabilizer}); A4 isotropy at the cage-vertex level prevents preferred-direction projections; the cage-level chirality observable is therefore the symmetric irrep-average over the $E$-doublet projections with factor $d_E/|\Dsix| = 2/12 = 1/6$. This is the Schur-orthogonality coefficient for the $E$-irrep on a group of order 12.

\textbf{Argument 2: DI-bit propagation paths + path-averaging (A3-based).}
CPP axiom A3 governs the substrate orientation field through DI-bit (Direction Information bit) exchanges between vertices. The chirality observable's value at a cage location is determined by DI-bit propagation paths from the substrate-vacuum reference frame to that location. For the icosahedral cage: propagation paths to any cage vertex traverse the cage's $\Dsix$ symmetry; the total cage-level $\Cchi$ is the symmetric sum over all $|\Dsix| = 12$ symmetry-equivalent paths with the chirality bias entering as the $E$-irrep projection at each endpoint; the $E$-irrep-relevant channels number $d_E = 2$; the averaged contribution per path is $d_E/|\Dsix| = 1/6$. \subsection{The Sessions 99--101 trajectory and candidate $\gamma$ observation}\label{sec:gamma_observation}

\subsection{The linear-vs-quadratic scaling tension}\label{sec:scaling_tension}

Sessions 99--100 tested the candidate $\gamma$ observation against standard PMNS perturbation theory and found a fundamental tension. The standard reading is that $\sin^2\theta_{13}$ at the PMNS level is the modulus-squared of a perturbation-amplitude:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pmns_quadratic}
\sin^2\theta_{13} \propto |M|^2 \cdot \frac{2}{3} \approx (0.0394)^2 \cdot \frac{2}{3} \approx 0.00103.
\end{equation}
The factor $2/3$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:pmns_quadratic} is the standard PMNS-perturbation coefficient on the K3-doublet-to-third-eigenmode coupling channel under the SM-5 inheritance framework. The result $\approx 0.001$ is off from observation by factor $\sim 21$.

Three resolution attempts were tested in Sessions 99--101 and ruled out:

\begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{0.2em}
    \item \textbf{Wavefunction-level coupling}: the hypothesis that $\sin^2\theta_{13}$ inherits from the wavefunction-level coupling $|\langle \nu_3 | \text{Capotauro perturbation} | \nu_1 \rangle|^2$ rather than the PMNS-level off-diagonal entry. Direct computation gives quadratic scaling at the wavefunction level too: $\sin^2\theta_{13} \propto |M|^2 / 4 \approx 0.000388$, off by factor $\sim 64$. Ruled out~\cite{abshier_capotauro_subclaim_c_sketch}.\footnote{Direct computation performed at Session 101 Patch 0395.}
    \item \textbf{Multiple-channel coherent sum}: the hypothesis that $\sin^2\theta_{13}$ aggregates over multiple coherent channels with destructive interference enhancing the leading-order amplitude. Tested across 22 candidate scalings combining linear and quadratic terms; none reproduces the observed $\sin^2\theta_{13}$ within $5\sigma$ without ad hoc parameter tuning.
    \item \textbf{Linear-amplitude reading of $\sin^2\theta_{13}$}: the hypothesis that $\sin^2\theta_{13}$ should be read as a linear-in-amplitude quantity rather than modulus-squared. This requires a CPP-specific perturbation framework in which the leading-order PMNS deviation from TBM is linear in the substrate-physics parameters; no such framework is available within Capotauro mechanism's direct scope.
\end{enumerate}

The 22 candidate scalings exhausted the parameter space accessible within the Capotauro mechanism alone, given the foundational input stack FI-C-1 through FI-C-10. None produces $\sin^2\theta_{13}$ at observed magnitude with rigorous derivation.
