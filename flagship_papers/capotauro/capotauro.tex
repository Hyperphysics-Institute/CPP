\subsection{The $\Hfour \to \Ifour$ chirality $\mathbb{Z}_2$}\label{sec:h4_i4}

The 600-cell is the regular four-dimensional polytope with 120 vertices, 720 edges, 1200 faces, and 600 tetrahedral cells, with full symmetry group $\Hfour$ (the $H_4$ Coxeter group) of order 14{,}400. The rotational subgroup is the chiral icosahedral group $\Ifour = H_4^+$ of order 7{,}200, an index-2 normal subgroup with quotient $\Hfour / \Ifour = \mathbb{Z}_2$. The two enantiomorphic forms $[\text{600-cell}]_L$ and $[\text{600-cell}]_R$ are related by a reflection element of $\Hfour$ that is not contained in $\Ifour$~\cite{coxeter_1973}.

The CPP substrate as a \emph{geometric structure} respects the full $\Hfour$: the polytope itself is racemic with no inherent left/right bias. This is verified at the level of the 600-cell's edge structure --- all 720 edges have a single length $1/\phig$ relative to the vertex-to-vertex unit distance, and the $\Hfour$ action is transitive on both vertices and edges. The substrate-as-structure carries no chirality.

The substrate-vacuum \emph{state}, by contrast, is in one specific chirality. The broken-symmetry phase of the $\Hfour \to \Ifour$ symmetry breaking selects one of the two enantiomorphs; the broken-$\mathbb{Z}_2$ reflection element is no longer respected at the dynamical level. The distinction between substrate-as-structure (racemic, $\Hfour$-symmetric) and substrate-vacuum-state (chiral, $\Ifour$-symmetric) is critical: the geometric symmetry-respecting framework is preserved, while the vacuum state carries the broken-symmetry information.

where $\langle \Gamma_2 || \hat{O}^{\Gamma} || \Gamma_1 \rangle$ is the reduced matrix element (irrep-dependent only) and $C^{\Gamma_2, m_2}_{\Gamma, \Gamma_1; m_1}$ is the Clebsch-Gordan coefficient. \begin{lemma}[Spectral radius of $S$]\label{lem:spectral_S}
The matrix $S$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:t_a2_generator} has eigenvalues $\{0, +i\sqrt{3}, -i\sqrt{3}\}$ with spectral radius $\sqrt{3}$. \end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Direct computation: the characteristic polynomial of $S$ is $\lambda^3 + 3\lambda = \lambda(\lambda^2 + 3) = 0$, giving $\lambda \in \{0, \pm i\sqrt{3}\}$. The null vector is found by inspection: $S(1,1,1)^T = (1-1, -1+1, 1-1)^T = (0,0,0)^T$. The cross-product structure $S v = (1,1,1) \times v$ confirms the null direction is parallel to $(1,1,1)$, hence orthogonal to the K3-doublet which is the orthogonal complement of $|\phi_+\rangle = (1,1,1)/\sqrt{3}$. The non-zero eigenvalues live on the K3-doublet subspace.

For $T_{A_2}(b) = i \cdot b \cdot S$, the eigenvalues are $\{i b \cdot 0, i b \cdot (+i\sqrt{3}), i b \cdot (-i\sqrt{3})\} = \{0, -b\sqrt{3}, +b\sqrt{3}\}$ --- real and centered at zero on the K3-doublet, as required for $T_{A_2}(b)$ to be a self-adjoint chirality-like operator on the antibonding doublet.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:spectral_S} is the load-bearing structural input for the chirality-eigenvalue matching principle in \S\ref{sec:k3_amplitude_factor}: identifying the K3-doublet's $T_{A_2}(b)$ eigenvalues $\pm b\sqrt{3}$ with the physical chirality eigenvalues $\pm \chi$ of $\Cchi$ at substrate level determines $b = \chi/\sqrt{3}$ uniquely, fixing the substrate-physics parameter.

\subsection{The K3-amplitude factor: chirality-eigenvalue matching principle}\label{sec:k3_amplitude_factor}

\begin{lemma}[K3-amplitude factor]\label{lem:k3_factor}
Under FI-C-9 (substrate-vacuum chirality magnitude $|\chi| = \phig^{-3}$), the K3-amplitude factor in the Wigner-Eckart factorization of the chirality matrix element on the TBM-aligned $\Kthree$-doublet is
\[
|M_{\Kthree}| = |\langle \phi_-^{(1)} | T_{A_2}(b) | \phi_-^{(2)} \rangle|_{b = \chi/\sqrt{3}} = \chi.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The unique $A_2$-irrep generator on the K3-amplitude space is $T_{A_2}(b) = i \cdot b \cdot S$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:t_a2_generator}). Direct symbolic computation on the TBM-aligned basis $\phiminusOne = (2,-1,-1)/\sqrt{6}$, $\phiminusTwo = (0,-1,1)/\sqrt{2}$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:m_k3_symbolic}
M_{\Kthree} = \langle \phi_-^{(1)} | T_{A_2}(b) | \phi_-^{(2)} \rangle = -i \cdot b \cdot \sqrt{3}.
\end{equation}
The imaginary phase $-i$ is intrinsic to the $A_2$-irrep generator (it reflects the antisymmetric structure of $S$); the magnitude $b\sqrt{3}$ tracks the spectral radius of $S$ on the K3-amplitude subspace (Lemma~\ref{lem:spectral_S}).

To determine the substrate-physics parameter $b$, we apply the \emph{chirality-eigenvalue matching principle} (Finding C-W23)~\cite{abshier_capotauro_subclaim_c_sketch}. The K3-doublet eigenvalues of $T_{A_2}(b)$ are $\pm b\sqrt{3}$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:spectral_S}). The physical chirality eigenvalues of $\Cchi$ at substrate level are $\pm \chi$ (the substrate-vacuum order parameter magnitudes, FI-C-9 \S\ref{sec:order_parameter}: the eigenvalues of the chirality observable are the magnitudes of the broken-symmetry order parameter, $\pm$ for the two enantiomorphs). Identifying:
\[
b\sqrt{3} = \chi \;\Longrightarrow\; b = \frac{\chi}{\sqrt{3}}.
\]
Substituting into Eq.~\eqref{eq:m_k3_symbolic}:
\[
M_{\Kthree}\big|_{b = \chi/\sqrt{3}} = -i \cdot \frac{\chi}{\sqrt{3}} \cdot \sqrt{3} = -i\chi.
\]
The magnitude is $|M_{\Kthree}| = \chi$.
\end{proof}

The chirality-eigenvalue matching principle (Finding C-W23) is the load-bearing structural step that determines the substrate-physics parameter $b$ uniquely from the substrate-vacuum order parameter $\chi$. Without this identification, the parameter $b$ would remain free; with it, the matrix element acquires its $\chi$-dependence at theorem level.

\subsection{The cage-shell averaging factor: cage-shell averaging principle}\label{sec:cage_shell_factor}

\begin{lemma}[Cage-shell averaging factor]\label{lem:cage_shell}
Under FI-C-10 (cage-shell extension to chirality observables, \S\ref{sec:fi_summary}), the perpendicular-wavefunction factor in the Wigner-Eckart factorization of the chirality matrix element on the icosahedral cage hosting the charged-lepton K3-doublet states is
\[
|M_\perp| = |\langle \chi_+ | T_\perp | \chi_- \rangle| = \frac{d_E}{V_\text{cage}} = \frac{2}{12} = \frac{1}{6}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The K3-doublet states $\{\PhiminusOne, \PhiminusTwo\}$ are extended over the $V_\text{cage} = 12$ icosahedral cage via FI-C-6 (cage-shell coupling for mass observable). FI-C-10 extends this coupling structure from mass observables to the broader class of $D_6$-equivariant observables on K3-doublet states, in particular to the chirality observable $\Cchi$ (\S\ref{sec:fi_summary}, Patch 0391 Session 97~\cite{abshier_capotauro_subclaim_c_sketch}).

Under this extension, the perpendicular-wavefunction matrix element on the $V_\text{cage} = 12$ icosahedral cage acquires a Schur-orthogonality averaging factor for the $E$-irrep doublet observable:
\[
|M_\perp| = \frac{d_E}{V_\text{cage}}
\]
where $d_E = 2$ is the dimension of the $E$-irrep of $\Dsix$ on the K3-doublet space and $V_\text{cage} = 12$ is the number of vertices in the icosahedral cage. Direct computation:
\[
|M_\perp| = \frac{2}{12} = \frac{1}{6}.
\]
Equivalently via the structural identity $V_\text{cage} = |\Dsix| = 12$:
\[
|M_\perp| = \frac{d_E}{|\Dsix|} = \frac{2}{12} = \frac{1}{6},
\]
which is the standard Schur orthogonality factor for $E$-irrep doublet observables on a group of order 12.
\end{proof}

The cage-shell averaging principle (Findings C-W25, C-W26)~\cite{abshier_capotauro_subclaim_c_sketch} is the second load-bearing structural step. It converts the icosahedral cage's vertex count $V_\text{cage} = 12$ into the perpendicular-wavefunction factor $1/6$. The structural identity $V_\text{cage} = |\Dsix| = 12$ is a non-trivial coincidence: the cage hosting the charged-lepton K3-doublet states has exactly the order of the K3 stabilizer, allowing the cage-shell averaging factor to be expressed equivalently via vertex-count or group-order formulas.
