## §1 Setup: the F.1 sub-question

### §1.1 What we are asking

The substrate primitive 4D direction $\hat{n}$ is established at Layer 3 rigor via Capotauro v2.0 Reading C trajectory: under vertex-aligned $\hat{n} = v_{\text{host}}$, the substrate's $H_4$ symmetry breaks to $H_3 = I_h$ residual symmetry at the host vertex, with the substrate primitive's magnitude $|\chi| = \varphi^{-3}$ derived from the 600-cell's perturbative-distance-ratio constraint (Patch 0424 Finding C-W39 substrate-locality theorem + Patch 0419 Finding C-W37 vertex-aligned resolution). The substrate primitive's content includes:

- A specific 4D direction in the substrate's ambient $\mathbb{R}^4$ space, aligned with one of the 120 vertex directions of the 600-cell.
- A magnitude $|\chi| = \varphi^{-3} \approx 0.236$ controlling the substrate's edge-length perturbation: edges aligned with $\hat{n}$ have effective length $(1 + \varepsilon \cdot \hat{e} \cdot \hat{n})$ relative to the $H_4$-idealized substrate (Capotauro Reading C §2.3).
- A residual symmetry structure $H_3 = I_h$ at the host vertex, with the K3-doublet's $D_{3d}$ stabilizer + the W-bracelet's Petrie-polygon $D_6$ + the qDP/eDP sector's $D_{5d}$ all sitting as sub-stabilizers of $H_3$ (Capotauro v2.0 §6 + chirality continuum §3.2).

What is **not** established at the framework's current state: the relationship between $\hat{n}$ (a spatial directional primitive in the substrate's ambient 4D space) and $\vec{\omega}_{PCD}$ (the PCD-cycle-orientation pseudovector — the temporal-direction pseudovector representing which way the Polarize → Capture → Depolarize cycle progresses at each CP at each Absolute Moment).

**The sub-question, precisely formulated.** Let $\vec{\omega}_{PCD}(v)$ be the PCD-cycle-orientation pseudovector at substrate vertex $v$, defined as the axial-vector representing the temporal-direction of the PCD cycle at the CP at $v$. The sub-question asks whether, under CPP primitive axioms A1–A11 + Reading C ($\hat{n}$ as substrate primitive), the constraint

$$\vec{\omega}_{PCD}(v_{\text{host}}) = \sigma \cdot \hat{n}, \quad \sigma \in \{+1, -1\}$$

(PCD-cycle-orientation pseudovector at the host vertex aligned with the substrate primitive direction; sign $\sigma$ is a global convention) is **derived** from a substrate-mechanism, or is registered as an **independent primitive** parallel to $\hat{n}$.

### §1.2 Why this is the load-bearing F.1 sub-question

The F.2/F.3 viability decision gate established that the substrate-Wigner-Eckart datum for manifestation (iv) thermodynamic causal arrow is structurally constructible at the group-theoretic level (§2 of decision gate) via direct analog to the qDP/eDP sector closure (Capotauro v2.0 §20 / Finding C-W46). The datum specifies $\mathcal{S}^{thermo} = \vec{\omega}_{PCD}(v_{\text{host}})$ aligned along $\hat{n}$, with the rest of the tuple ($\Gamma^{thermo}, \zeta^{thermo}, \hat{C}^{thermo}$, matter-doublet, cage-shell factor $1/6$) inheriting structurally from the qDP/eDP precedent.

The decision gate identified that this construction is *physically grounded* only if the link $\hat{n} \mapsto \vec{\omega}_{PCD}$ has substrate-mechanism support; without it, the substrate handle magnitude $\chi/6$ for manifestation (iv) does not satisfy Definition 3.4's exclusion clause for "dynamical degrees of freedom evolving in time" (chirality_continuum.tex §3.5) and the topological-projection argument (METH-CHIR-CONT-4) does not generalize from spatial-static-object manifestations (i)+(ii)+(iii) to dynamical-object manifestations (iv)+(v).

**Therefore**: closure of the $\hat{n} \mapsto \vec{\omega}_{PCD}$ link sub-question is the load-bearing prerequisite. With Scenario A closure (link derived via substrate-mechanism), the §2 datum construction of the decision gate becomes physically grounded, and F.2 + F.3 proceed via the chirality continuum architectural template (estimated 4–8 sessions for F.2 single-sector or 6–14 sessions for joint F.2+F.3 post-F.1). With Scenario B closure (PCD-cycle-orientation is independent primitive), the umbrella's scope refines to three-way and F.2 + F.3 require alternative architecture.

### §1.3 Three candidate mechanism categories considered

A scan of the candidate mechanism space at the opening of this scoping work identifies three structurally distinct categories for the link:

**Mechanism A — DI-bit propagation rate asymmetry between $+\hat{n}$ and $-\hat{n}$ directions.** The substrate's DI-bit (Dimensional Increment bit, the discrete unit of information propagation between CPs per CPP axiom A1) acquires a small direction-correlated propagation-rate asymmetry under the substrate primitive $\hat{n}$. DI-bits propagating in the $+\hat{n}$ direction propagate at slightly different rates than those in $-\hat{n}$. The PCD cycle's Polarize → Capture → Depolarize sequence couples to the asymmetric DI-bit flow, inducing a definite cycle-orientation $\vec{\omega}_{PCD}$ aligned with $\hat{n}$. *Leading candidate by structural analogy to Reading C edge-length perturbation* (Capotauro sketch §2.3): both involve a direction-correlated propagation-asymmetry of substrate quantities.

**Mechanism B — Substrate-orientation field with gauge-like coupling to PCD-cycle.** The substrate carries an orientation field $\hat{n}(v)$ at each lattice vertex, with Reading C vertex-aligned $\hat{n}(v_{\text{host}}) = v_{\text{host}}/|v_{\text{host}}|$. The PCD cycle at each CP couples to this field via a gauge-like minimum-energy-locking interaction: the cycle-orientation $\vec{\omega}_{PCD}$ aligns with $\hat{n}(v)$ at each vertex by energy-minimization, analogous to how a magnetic moment aligns with a magnetic field. *Alternative candidate*; requires positing a substrate-orientation field and a coupling rule, both of which are framework extensions.

**Mechanism C — Position-dependent clock-skew correlated with $\hat{n}$.** The local PCD-cycle clock-rate at each CP is uniform at the $H_4$-idealized substrate level. Under the substrate primitive $\hat{n}$, the clock-rate at each CP acquires a small direction-correlated skew, with CPs at positions $v$ with $\hat{v} \cdot \hat{n} > 0$ ticking slightly faster (or slower) than $\hat{v} \cdot \hat{n} < 0$. *Registered for completeness but unmotivated*: the skew produces a spatial-rate asymmetry, not directly a cycle-orientation. Mechanism C requires an additional step coupling rate-asymmetry to cycle-orientation that is unmotivated by existing CPP corpus.

Mechanism A is the leading candidate. Mechanisms B and C are registered as alternatives. Each is developed at Layer 1 / Layer 2 below; Layer 3 closure work would test whether the leading candidate (Mechanism A) closes under CPP primitive axioms A1–A11 or whether one of the alternatives is needed.

## §2 Mechanism A: DI-bit propagation rate asymmetry

### §2.1 The primitive

Under CPP axiom A1 (DI-bit propagation), each CP at substrate vertex $v$ broadcasts DI-bits to its 12 first-shell neighbors at each Absolute Moment (one tick of the cosmic clock). The DI-bits carry the CP's SSV-content (Substrate State Vector, axiom A3 + Capotauro v2.0 mechanism statements) at the propagation event, with the receiving CP's PCD cycle's Polarize phase coupling to the incoming DI-bit field. The idealized $H_4$-symmetric substrate has uniform DI-bit propagation rate $r_0$ in all directions at the host vertex.

**The Mechanism A primitive.** Under the substrate primitive $\hat{n}$, DI-bit propagation rates acquire a direction-correlated perturbation:

$$r(\hat{e}_{ab}) = r_0 \cdot \left(1 + \delta \cdot (\hat{e}_{ab} \cdot \hat{n})\right)$$

where $\hat{e}_{ab}$ is the unit vector along the propagation edge from CP $a$ to CP $b$, $\hat{n}$ is the substrate primitive direction, and $\delta$ is the propagation-rate-asymmetry magnitude. The structural analog of Reading C edge-length perturbation: aligned propagation ($\hat{e}_{ab} \cdot \hat{n} > 0$) is slightly faster (or slower), anti-aligned ($\hat{e}_{ab} \cdot \hat{n} < 0$) is slightly slower (or faster), orthogonal propagation is at $r_0$.

### §2.2 Coupling to PCD-cycle phases

The PCD cycle at each CP has three phases: Polarize (P), Capture (C), Depolarize (D). At each Absolute Moment, the cycle executes once: Polarize accepts incoming DI-bits, Capture processes the SSV-content, Depolarize broadcasts outgoing DI-bits. The cycle's temporal direction is P → C → D.

The cycle's orientation $\vec{\omega}_{PCD}$ as a pseudovector represents the cyclic direction in an abstract orientation space. To couple $\vec{\omega}_{PCD}$ to spatial $\hat{n}$, we need a substrate-mechanism that produces an axial-vector content from the cycle's phase structure.

**Coupling proposal (Mechanism A working hypothesis).** The DI-bit propagation asymmetry of §2.1 produces a *net DI-bit current* in the $\pm\hat{n}$ direction at each lattice vertex. At the host vertex $v_{\text{host}}$ under Reading C, the net current is:

$$\vec{j}_{DI}(v_{\text{host}}) = \sum_{i=1}^{12} r(\hat{e}_{(\text{host},i)}) \cdot \hat{e}_{(\text{host},i)} \neq 0$$

where the sum is over the 12 first-shell neighbors and the propagation-rate perturbation breaks the $H_3 = I_h$ first-shell isotropy (preserved at zeroth order per Capotauro v2.0 Finding C-W39) at first order in $\delta$, producing a net current along $\hat{n}$.

The PCD-cycle couples to this net current via the Polarize phase's preferential accumulation from $-\hat{n}$ and Depolarize phase's preferential emission toward $+\hat{n}$ (or reverse; sign convention). The cycle-orientation pseudovector $\vec{\omega}_{PCD}$ aligns with $\hat{n}$ as a structural consequence:

$$\vec{\omega}_{PCD}(v_{\text{host}}) = \sigma_{\delta} \cdot \hat{n}$$

where $\sigma_{\delta}$ is determined by the sign of $\delta$ (the substrate's chirality-bias sign, a primitive content of the substrate per founders' voice 005 point 1).

### §2.3 Structural connection to Reading C edge-length perturbation

Mechanism A is structurally parallel to Reading C's edge-length perturbation (Capotauro sketch §2.3): both are direction-correlated perturbations of substrate quantities at first order in a small magnitude, with the magnitude tied to the substrate primitive $\hat{n}$. The structural difference:

- **Reading C (spatial)**: edge-LENGTH perturbation $|\vec{e}|_{actual} = |\vec{e}|_{ideal} \cdot (1 + \varepsilon \cdot \hat{e} \cdot \hat{n})$. Produces spatial chirality content at the substrate-geometry level. Affects static spatial substrate-objects (K3-doublet, W-bracelet, qDP/eDP). Magnitude $\varepsilon \equiv \chi = \varphi^{-3}$ at Layer 3 (Patch 0424 Finding C-W39).
- **Mechanism A (temporal)**: DI-bit propagation-RATE perturbation $r(\hat{e}) = r_0 \cdot (1 + \delta \cdot \hat{e} \cdot \hat{n})$. Produces temporal chirality content at the substrate-dynamics level. Affects dynamical substrate-objects (PCD-cycle-orientation, cosmological-vacuum nucleation timing). Magnitude $\delta = ?$ — open question at this scoping sketch.

**The magnitude question.** If Mechanism A is the right closure, the natural question is: does $\delta = \varepsilon = \chi = \varphi^{-3}$ (the same magnitude as the spatial perturbation), or is $\delta$ an independent perturbation magnitude with its own derivation? Two sub-cases:

- **A.1**: $\delta = \chi = \varphi^{-3}$ via a unification argument (single substrate primitive $\hat{n}$ controlling both spatial and temporal perturbations at the same magnitude). This gives the OPEN-SD-CHIR-PRIMITIVE umbrella's full unification claim at the magnitude level.
- **A.2**: $\delta \neq \chi$ — the substrate primitive controls direction (the alignment of perturbation with $\hat{n}$) but not magnitude. The magnitude $\delta$ is set by a separate substrate-physics derivation, possibly via an analog of the perturbative-distance-ratio constraint applied to substrate-dynamics rather than substrate-geometry.

Case A.1 is structurally cleaner and matches the umbrella's unification framing; Case A.2 is the fall-back if A.1 fails to close at Layer 3.

### §2.4 What closure looks like at Layer 3

Layer 3 closure of Mechanism A requires:

- **(i) Derive the DI-bit propagation-rate asymmetry from CPP axioms.** Under Reading C with $\hat{n} = v_{\text{host}}$, demonstrate that the substrate's broken symmetry $H_4 \to H_3 = I_h$ at the host vertex induces a propagation-rate asymmetry at first order in $\varepsilon$ via the substrate-locality framework (Capotauro v2.0 §13 / Finding C-W39). - **(ii) Derive the coupling rule from PCD-phase structure.** Demonstrate that the Polarize → Capture → Depolarize sequence's coupling to net DI-bit current produces a cycle-orientation pseudovector $\vec{\omega}_{PCD}$ as a structural consequence. Requires articulating the PCD-cycle's substrate-dynamics at sufficient detail to support a coupling-rule derivation.

- **(iii) Derive the magnitude $\delta$.** Case A.1: derive $\delta = \chi$ via unification argument. Case A.2: derive $\delta$ independently via substrate-dynamics-analog of the perturbative-distance-ratio constraint.

- **(iv) Verify against empirical anchors.** The leptogenesis CP-asymmetry $\Delta p_{LR}^{\text{obs}} \sim 0.04$ at sub-percent precision is the strongest empirical anchor across the umbrella's manifestations (chirality continuum §7.2 Threshold (C)). Estimated Layer 3 closure trajectory for Mechanism A: 4–8 sessions of substrate-physics derivation work, assuming the host-to-first-shell DI-bit current calculation is structurally tractable (analog of the §13 substrate-locality theorem) and the PCD-coupling rule derivation does not require introducing new substrate-physics primitives. If Mechanism A fails Layer 3 closure (sub-step (i) produces zero net current at the host vertex due to symmetry-cancellation at the $I_h$-residual level, or sub-step (ii) requires a new PCD-phase substrate-physics primitive that has no axiomatic basis), the trajectory pivots to Mechanism B or C.

## §3 Mechanism B: Substrate-orientation field with gauge-like coupling

### §3.1 The primitive

Mechanism B posits a substrate-orientation field $\hat{n}(v)$ at each lattice vertex, with the field's value at $v_{\text{host}}$ identified with the substrate primitive direction $\hat{n}(v_{\text{host}}) = v_{\text{host}}/|v_{\text{host}}|$ under Reading C. Away from $v_{\text{host}}$, $\hat{n}(v)$ is determined by the substrate's residual symmetry $H_3 = I_h$ at each substrate location plus a propagation rule that extends the field consistently across the 600-cell lattice.

**Distinction from Mechanism A.** Mechanism A does not require a substrate-orientation field; the substrate primitive $\hat{n}$ is a single 4D direction in ambient $\mathbb{R}^4$ space, and DI-bit propagation asymmetry is a structural consequence of this primitive at the propagation-rate level. Mechanism B is a framework extension: the substrate primitive is generalized from a single ambient-space direction to a vertex-field $\hat{n}(v)$ structured across the lattice.

### §3.2 Coupling to PCD-cycle via energy minimization

The PCD cycle at each CP couples to the local substrate-orientation field $\hat{n}(v)$ via a gauge-like minimum-energy interaction:

$$E_{\text{coupling}}(v) = -J \cdot \vec{\omega}_{PCD}(v) \cdot \hat{n}(v)$$

where $J$ is a coupling constant. The minimum-energy state has $\vec{\omega}_{PCD}(v) = \sigma \cdot \hat{n}(v)$ with $\sigma = +1$ (or $-1$; sign convention). The substrate's ground state therefore has PCD-cycle-orientation aligned with the substrate-orientation field at every lattice vertex.

The mechanism is analogous to spin-orbit coupling in atomic physics: a vector content (spin) aligns with another vector content (orbital angular momentum) by energy-minimization. Here a pseudovector content (PCD-cycle-orientation) aligns with a vector field content (substrate-orientation field) by the same principle.

### §3.3 What closure looks like at Layer 3

Layer 3 closure of Mechanism B requires:

- **(i) Derive the substrate-orientation field as a framework primitive or consequence.** Is $\hat{n}(v)$ an independent substrate-physics primitive that requires new axiomatic content, or is it derivable from existing CPP axioms A1–A11 (e.g., as a structural consequence of the $I_h$ residual symmetry at each host vertex)?

- **(ii) Derive the coupling constant $J$.** The coupling $J$ controls the energy scale of the PCD-orientation alignment. Is $J$ tied to the substrate primitive magnitude $|\chi| = \varphi^{-3}$ (giving the umbrella's unification at the coupling level), or is $J$ an independent substrate-physics parameter?

- **(iii) Demonstrate ground-state alignment.** At sufficient temperatures (substrate-thermal-equilibrium), do PCD cycles align with $\hat{n}(v)$ at every vertex, or do thermal fluctuations break alignment? At leptogenesis-era temperatures $T \sim 10^{10}$–$10^{12}$ GeV (chirality continuum §7.2 Threshold (C) primary observable), is alignment preserved?

Estimated Layer 3 closure trajectory for Mechanism B: 5–10 sessions, structurally longer than Mechanism A because of the framework-extension overhead (substrate-orientation field is a new primitive concept). Mechanism B is the fall-back if Mechanism A fails Layer 3 closure at sub-step (i).

## §4 Mechanism C: Position-dependent clock-skew

### §4.1 The primitive

Mechanism C posits a position-dependent PCD-cycle clock-rate at each CP, with the rate's perturbation correlated with $\hat{n}$:

$$r_{\text{clock}}(v) = r_0 \cdot \left(1 + \eta \cdot (\hat{v} \cdot \hat{n})\right)$$

where $\hat{v}$ is the unit vector from the substrate origin to $v$ and $\eta$ is the clock-skew magnitude. Under the perturbation, CPs at positions with $\hat{v} \cdot \hat{n} > 0$ tick slightly faster than CPs with $\hat{v} \cdot \hat{n} < 0$.

### §4.2 Difficulties at the framework level

Mechanism C produces a spatial-rate asymmetry, not directly a cycle-orientation asymmetry. To couple position-dependent clock-rate to cycle-orientation, an additional substrate-physics primitive is required: a rule by which clock-rate differences between neighboring CPs produce a cycle-orientation asymmetry. No such rule exists in the current CPP corpus, and the natural candidate (clock-rate gradient inducing a net rotation in cycle-phase space) is unmotivated from CPP primitive axioms.

Additionally, Mechanism C conflicts with CPP's commitment to an Absolute Moment as the universal tick of the cosmic clock (`master_glossary.md` Absolute Moment entry: "Time in CPP is discrete and absolute (not relative), but relativistic time dilation is reproduced through the PSR mechanism"). Position-dependent clock-skew at the substrate level would break the absolute character of the cosmic clock; relativistic time dilation is supposed to emerge through the PSR (Phase-Shift Rate) mechanism, not through direct substrate clock-skew.

Mechanism C is registered for completeness but is *not pursued as a leading candidate* at this scoping sketch. If Mechanisms A and B both fail Layer 3 closure, Mechanism C could be revisited via a refinement that addresses both the cycle-orientation coupling and the Absolute Moment compatibility, but the burden of proof on such a refinement is high.
