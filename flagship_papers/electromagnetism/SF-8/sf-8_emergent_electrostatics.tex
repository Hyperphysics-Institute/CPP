\begin{abstract}
Conscious Point Physics (CPP) posits a discrete substrate in which
Conscious Points occupy Grid Points of a 600-cell lattice and exchange
Displacement-Increment bits (DI-bits) once per Absolute Moment. The
substrate rules contain no field equation, no force law, and no inverse
power of distance. This paper reports that Coulomb's law is nonetheless
\emph{measured out of} those rules, under two independent relay
implementations, in a cellular-automaton realisation of the substrate.
Against an Ewald reference independently validated to $0.856\%$ of free
space, the idealized-shell relay reproduces the shape of the exact
periodic Coulomb solution to $\pm 2.9\%$ pointwise with a fitted-exponent
gap $\Delta p \le 0.022$ across a $2\times$ range of shell radii, and the
lattice-native origin-directed twelve-neighbour hop relay reproduces it to
$\pm 0.4\%$ pointwise at hop count $R = 4$ with $\Delta p = 0.010$, the
agreement tightening monotonically with hop count. Pre-registered pass
bands were $\pm 10\%$ pointwise and $\Delta p \le 0.15$; both
implementations land well inside them. Separately, the same substrate
exhibits a \emph{bonded} zero-point-oscillation sea: partner persistence
of $100.0\%$ at every lag out to 4000 Moments against a $2.0\%$ random
null across $52/52$ dedicated pairs, with separation cycling through
superposition at lattice-quantised turning radii. This supplies the direct
empirical content of the dedicated-bond rule C26.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{What this paper does not establish.} The result is
confined to the \emph{scalar} sector. CPP's magnetic sector is
\emph{derived but not measured}: the curl construction and its
normalisation are analytic results, the corresponding validation item
(OPEN-C23-MAGNETIC-SECTOR-VALIDATION) is open, and no measurement in this
paper bears on it. Nor is any coupling constant predicted here --- the
relay's amplitude unit is conventional and only the radial \emph{shape}
is measured. SF-6 advances the unification claim for electromagnetism as a
whole; SF-8 supplies the substrate measurement for one half of it and
names the other half as open.
\end{abstract}

\bigskip\noindent
\textbf{Plain Language Summary:}
The rules of this model say nothing about electric forces. They say only
that each point of space passes small messages to its neighbours once per
tick of a universal clock, and that each point then shifts by an amount
set by how much those messages agree in direction. When we ran those rules
on a computer and measured how strongly a charge pushes on its
surroundings at different distances, we recovered the familiar
electrostatic law --- not because it was put in, but because it fell out.
Two quite different ways of passing the messages gave the same answer, and
the more carefully we passed them the closer the answer became. In the same
model, the pairs of particles filling empty space turned out to lock into
dedicated partnerships that survived unbroken for thousands of ticks,
rather than swapping partners at random. We are careful to say what is
\emph{not} shown: this covers the electric side only, and the magnetic
side remains calculated but unmeasured.

\section{Introduction}
% ============================================================

The substrate question in electromagnetism is old and usually deferred.
Maxwell's equations describe how the electromagnetic field behaves; they do
not say what the field is made of. Special relativity constrains how the
field transforms; quantum electrodynamics quantises its excitations. At
each step the constitutive question --- what \emph{is} this thing whose
divergence and curl we are computing? --- was set aside as either
metaphysical or premature.

Conscious Point Physics answers it structurally: the electromagnetic field
is a bookkeeping summary of Displacement-Increment bits in transit across a
discrete lattice. SF-6 develops that claim into a unification, treating
classical Maxwell theory, special-relativistic photon kinematics, and QED
as three limits of a single substrate mechanism. But SF-6 is a synthesis
paper. It assembles shipped results and argues that they cohere; it does
not measure the substrate.

This paper measures it, for the scalar sector, and reports the result
that the review panel unanimously recommended leading with.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The claim.} The substrate rules of CPP, implemented
faithfully as a cellular automaton, contain no field equation, no force
law, and no inverse power of separation anywhere. Coulomb's law is not
among the inputs. Nevertheless, when a single pinned charge is placed in
the automaton against a neutralising background and the resulting radial
profile is measured, that profile agrees with the exact Coulomb solution
for the same geometry --- and it does so under two structurally
independent implementations of the message relay, with the agreement
improving as the relay is made more faithful.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Why two implementations matter.} A single automaton
reproducing Coulomb's law is suggestive but weakly so: any lattice
construction has geometric accidents, and a result that depends on one
relay's particular shell structure is a result about that shell structure.
The two relays here differ in exactly the respect that matters. The first
integrates over an idealised sphere of influence; the second hops
DI-bits along the twelve nearest-neighbour directions of the lattice
itself, which is the founder's own specification and is not a sphere at
all. That both recover the same law, and that the lattice-native version
recovers it \emph{better}, is the evidence that the law is a property of
the relay rule rather than of a convenient idealisation.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{What is deliberately absent.} This paper makes no
magnetic claim. In CPP the magnetic field is identified with the
rotational component of the same dipole displacement whose radial
component is the electric field, and that identification has been carried
through analytically, including a normalisation verified at several
geometries. It has not been \emph{measured}. The corresponding validation
item is open, and nothing reported below touches it. Section~\ref{sec:scope}
states the boundary precisely, because the gap between ``we measured the
electric sector out of the substrate'' and ``we derived electromagnetism
from the substrate'' is exactly the gap a reader is most likely to close
without noticing.

\section{The Substrate Rules, and What They Do Not Contain}
\label{sec:rules}
% ============================================================

This section states the rules in full. Its purpose is adversarial: a
reader should finish it able to confirm that no electrostatic content has
been smuggled in, because the paper's entire claim rests on that absence.

\subsection{Ontology}

Space is not a container. The lattice \emph{is} space: a 600-cell
polytope structure whose vertices are Grid Points, each occupied by a
Conscious Point. The 600-cell has $V = 120$ vertices, $E = 720$ edges,
$F = 1200$ faces, $C = 600$ cells, and vertex coordination $z = 12$; the
coordination number is the only one of these that does load-bearing work
below.

Time is discrete and absolute. One \emph{Absolute Moment} ($\AM$) is one
tick of a universal clock, during which every Conscious Point executes one
Perceive--Compute--Displace cycle. There is no continuous time and no
per-observer time coordinate at this level.

\subsection{The three quantities}

\begin{definition}[Space Stress Vector]
The \emph{Space Stress Vector} $\SSV$ at a Grid Point is the running
vector bookkeeping of DI-bit arrivals at that point. Two reductions of it
are used:
\[
\SSVnet \;=\; \sum_i \vec{s}_i ,
\qquad
\SSVabs \;=\; \sum_i \lVert \vec{s}_i \rVert ,
\]
the vector sum and the scalar magnitude-sum of the contributions $\vec{s}_i$
arriving at that point. $\SSVnet$ is directional and subject to
cancellation; $\SSVabs$ is not.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Planck Sphere Radius]
The \emph{Planck Sphere Radius} ($\PSR$) is the maximum displacement a
Conscious Point can achieve in one Absolute Moment --- a per-Moment reach
ceiling, equal in the rest frame to the Planck length.
\end{definition}

\subsection{The relay rule}

Once per Absolute Moment, every Conscious Point emits DI-bits to its
neighbours and receives those emitted toward it. The emission is
\emph{synchronous}: all points emit on the same tick and all arrivals are
tallied before any displacement occurs.

\begin{remark}[Bit content closure --- rule C21, reconciled to AP-4 (v0.2, Patch 3039)]
As originally ratified (C21), a DI-bit carried \textbf{charge, polarity,
and origin, and nothing else}. Under the ratified payload
respecification AP-4a (founder-ratified Patch 3032, superseding the
AP-2/C21 enumeration) the content is \{origin address, electric vector
$\mathbf{E}$, strong vector $\mathbf{S}$\} --- a static snapshot of the
\emph{origin} GP's computed registers, with species identified by slot
rather than flag. The closure's load-bearing point SURVIVES intact: the
payload still carries no receiver-side distance, no field strength at
the receiver, no force, no potential, and no per-messenger phase or
orientational dynamics --- $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{S}$ are frozen
images of the \emph{source} registers, and Coulomb structure still
emerges only from receiver-side tallying of arrivals under geometric
dilution. A bit that carried the receiver's field strength would be
carrying Coulomb's law; the AP-4 payload does not. The measured results
of this paper consumed only count-like magnitude and origin-directed
steering, both preserved verbatim by AP-4a; they stand unchanged under
the respecification.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The displacement law}

Having tallied arrivals, each Conscious Point displaces by
\begin{equation}
d \;=\; \frac{\lVert \SSVnet \rVert}{\SSVabs}\,\PSR .
\label{eq:displacement}
\end{equation}
The prefactor is a \emph{directional coherence fraction} lying in
$[0,1]$: it is unity when every arriving bit points the same way and zero
when arrivals cancel exactly. The displacement is that fraction of the
per-Moment reach ceiling.

Equation~\eqref{eq:displacement} is the whole dynamics. It is worth
stating plainly what it is not:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item It contains \textbf{no separation variable}. Distance appears
      nowhere on the right-hand side.
\item It contains \textbf{no exponent}. There is no $r^{-2}$, no
      $r^{-1}$, and no power law of any kind to be tuned or fitted.
\item It contains \textbf{no coupling constant}. No $\epsilon_0$, no
      elementary charge, no fine-structure constant.
\item It is \textbf{not a force law}. Nothing is accelerated by anything;
      a point is displaced by a fraction of its own reach.
\end{itemize}

Whatever radial dependence the automaton exhibits must therefore come
from the \emph{geometry of the relay} --- how many bits arrive from where,
after how many hops --- and not from the displacement law. That is the
sense in which the measurement below is a measurement.

\section{The Measurement}
\label{sec:measurement}
% ============================================================

\subsection{The reference must come first}
\label{sec:reference}

A percentage agreement against an unvalidated reference measures nothing.
Before either relay result is quoted, the comparator itself must be
established.

The reference is a real-space Ewald summation for the periodic
(three-torus) geometry the automaton runs on. Validated against free space,
it reproduces the free-space result to a worst deviation of
$\mathbf{0.856\%}$ over $r \in [3,6]$ in three directions at $M = 48$,
inside its pre-declared $2\%$ band.

\begin{remark}[A geometry effect that must not be mis-stated]
\label{rem:p2291}
On the periodic geometry with a neutralising background, the
\emph{exact} Coulomb solution does not have local logarithmic slope $2$.
Over the original diagnostic window it has $p = 2.291$. This is a property
of the torus and the background, not of the automaton.

Two consequences follow, and both matter for how this paper may be read.
First, an early gate band of $[1.8, 2.2]$ on the fitted exponent was
\emph{unsatisfiable by any Coulombic field whatsoever on this geometry};
the resulting failure was correctly reclassified as a gate-design defect
rather than a defect of the substrate implementation. Second, and more
important here: \textbf{this paper does not claim to have measured an
exponent of $2$, and no such claim should be read into it.} The quantity
tested is agreement with the exact Coulomb solution \emph{for the geometry
simulated}, which is the correct comparison and a strictly more demanding
one than fitting a power law in free space would be.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[The two figures of merit]
For each configuration, with $\rho(r)$ the ratio of the automaton's
measured radial profile to the Ewald reference, normalised by its own
window mean:
\begin{align}
\text{(shape)} \quad &\rho(r) \in [0.90,\, 1.10] \text{ at every window point,}\\
\text{(slope)} \quad &\Delta p \;\equiv\; \lvert p_{\text{auto}} - p_{\text{Ewald}} \rvert \;\le\; 0.15 ,
\end{align}
both required at no fewer than two of three hop counts. These bands were
\textbf{frozen before any gate run}.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Why $\rho$ is normalised by its window mean]
The relay's amplitude unit is conventional. The automaton is not asked to
predict the strength of the electrostatic interaction and does not do so;
it is asked to predict its \emph{radial shape}. No coupling constant is
derived anywhere in this paper.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Configuration}

Single positive charge pinned at the origin; uniform neutralising
background injected at $-1/M^3$ per Grid Point per Moment; $M = 48$;
equilibration $4M$ Moments; averaging over a further $2M$ Moments; profiles
read along $\pm x$, $\pm y$, $\pm z$; window $r \in [2R+2,\, 16]$ for hop
count $R$.

\begin{remark}[Blinding, stated for the record]
At $R = 3$ the axis profile was already of record when the gate was
frozen and is therefore reported as \textbf{confirmatory-disclosed}. The
$R = 2$ and $R = 4$ configurations were \textbf{blind}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Relay B --- the lattice-native twelve-neighbour hop}
\label{sec:relayB}

The second implementation abandons the shell. DI-bits hop along the
twelve nearest-neighbour directions of the lattice --- the coordination
number $z = 12$ of Section~\ref{sec:rules} --- directed by origin, for $R$
hops. This is the founder's own specification of the relay and is
geometrically nothing like a sphere.

\midrule
$R = 2$ & $[0.986,\ 1.044]$ & $0.052$ \\
$R = 3$ & $[0.991,\ 1.004]$ & $0.011$ \\
$R = 4$ & $[0.996,\ 1.002]$ & $\mathbf{0.010}$ \\

\caption{Relay B (lattice-native origin-directed twelve-neighbour hop).

\begin{remark}[The trend is the result, not the endpoint]
The three rows above should be read as a sequence. From $R = 2$ to
$R = 4$ the shape window narrows from $[0.986, 1.044]$ to
$[0.996, 1.002]$ and the slope gap falls from $0.052$ to $0.010$ ---
\textbf{the agreement tightens monotonically as the relay is given more
hops to work with}. A fixed offset that happened to be small would be a
coincidence; a discrepancy that shrinks under refinement of the mechanism
is the signature of a limit being approached. Quoting only the $R = 4$
figure discards the evidence.
\end{remark}

\subsection{What the two relays jointly establish, and what they do not}
\label{sec:scope}

\noindent\textbf{Established.} The radial shape of electrostatics is a
consequence of synchronous DI-bit relay under
Equation~\eqref{eq:displacement}, and not an input. It survives
replacement of the relay's geometry by a structurally different one, and
improves under refinement of the more faithful of the two.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Not established, itemised.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Any coupling constant.} Amplitude is conventional
      (Section~\ref{sec:reference}); only shape is measured.
\item \textbf{An exponent of $2$.} See Remark~\ref{rem:p2291}. The
      comparison is against the exact solution on the simulated geometry.
\item \textbf{The magnetic sector.} Derived, not measured. The curl
      construction and its normalisation are analytic; the validation item
      is open. \textbf{No measurement in this paper bears on the magnetic
      sector, and the scalar result must not be read as covering it.}
\item \textbf{Any property of the physical dipole-particle sea's density
      or geometry.} The automaton's occupancy figures are regime
      artifacts and are forbidden as physical. This paper reports that the
      sea is \emph{bonded and dedicated} and characterises it no further
      --- not as dilute, not as dense, not as geometrically anything.
\item \textbf{Inertia, or any dynamical law beyond
      Equation~\eqref{eq:displacement}.} The substrate rules implemented
      here carry no rotational or orientational state, which is precisely
      what a treatment of arc-stored inertia would require.
\end{itemize}
