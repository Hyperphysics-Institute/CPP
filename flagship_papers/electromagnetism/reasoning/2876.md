# Reasoning fragment — Patch 2876 (SF-6 pin adjudication)

**STATUS: contemporaneous, captured at patch. Session of 2026-07-30.**

Two of the returns were identical and I nearly scored them as two seats.
I caught it on the opening sentence — the same slightly odd phrase,
"broadly appropriate, but it should stop short of exoneration," arriving
twice under different labels — and then the same LaTeX artifacts and the
same closing table settled it.

Then I went looking for whether this had happened before, which is the
part I should have done first, and it had. Patch 2849 hit it, established
that identical returns count as one position, declined to assign blame,
and asked for confirmation before the next round. The rule was already
written. I did not consult it, and worse, the occurrence between 2849 and
now went straight past me: at the placeholder attempt S4 and S5 returned
identical declines and I counted them as two independent refusals and
then built a capability table on the count. I told Thomas that three
seats cannot fetch raw GitHub. I know that about one seat. The other two
are undetermined and one of them may not have returned at all.

That is the fourth time this session the answer was sitting in the
repository. The pattern is not that I fail to check sources when I know a
question is contested; it is that I do not recognise a question as
contested when the surface looks like bookkeeping. Counting returns feels
like counting, not like adjudicating, so I counted.

CONV-008 exists because per-round flagging demonstrably does not work.
2849 flagged it in a header and requested confirmation, and the
recurrence happened anyway, twice. A flag is a note to a reader; a
convention is a step in a procedure. The distinguishing feature of this
failure is that it is silent and it inflates apparent consensus, which is
the single thing I have least ability to detect from the inside — an
inflated roster looks exactly like agreement.

On the substance, the round cost the programme something real and I want
to record that I think the panel was right.

A2 came back unanimous for demotion. All four positions said the pin's
F = kappa a is a scalar-toy result until someone shows that F_self
corresponds to anything in CPP. S5 put it most sharply: the coefficient
is statics-pinned and survives, but the dynamical reading depends on what
F *is*, and if F_self maps to nothing then the law does not travel even
though the number does. That is correct and I had not separated those two
things. I had been treating "kappa is pinned by statics" as though it
insulated the whole result, when it only insulates the coefficient.

S1's A1 qualification is the most useful sentence in the round and it is
one I should have written myself. Showing the fit window is legitimate
establishes that tau was extracted in a procedurally defensible way. It
does not establish that the toy's late-time behaviour is the right
physical analogue. I had been sliding between those without noticing —
my 2875 note reads as though justifying the window vindicated the
readings, and it does not; it vindicates the estimator. The readings are
still readings of a scalar toy whose correspondence to CPP is now
formally open. Registered as a standing caveat.

A1 went my way, four to nothing, on the one question that asked the panel
to check me. I do not think that is worth much on its own and the
discipline says a favourable answer earns less confidence, not more. What
earns confidence is that the same seats attached an unfavourable
qualification and unanimously demoted my headline result in the next
question. A panel that was rubber-stamping would not have done that.

A3 I resolved against the majority on the action while adopting them on
the validity, and I want the reasoning on record because splitting a
question the dispatch had conflated is the kind of move that can be used
to get whatever answer one wants. Three seats said "authorised in part."
S5 alone said withdraw, on the ground that a half-replacement misleads.
S5 is right about that and the majority did not address it. SF-6 v1.0
currently says "by tuning the model parameters" — a disclosed weakness.
Swapping in half an argument would replace a disclosed weakness with an
undisclosed gap. And since the paper needs no re-ship, waiting is free.
When the cautious option costs nothing I take it; that is the same rule
that saved me on the sixth convergence yesterday.

B1 is now mine. Nobody on the panel can execute, the typing worked in the
sense that nobody pretended to, and the consequence is that the linear
stability analysis will not arrive from outside. Two rounds of typed
questions have produced zero votes on arithmetic, against four out of
five before typing, so the discipline holds. But its success has a
corollary I should state plainly: typing a question as derivation means
the panel will decline it, and if I only ever type the hard questions as
derivation I will get honest silence on everything that matters and
opinions on everything that does not. The panel is not a compute
resource. B1 goes on my list, not theirs.
