This patch tests a prediction another worker registered 28 patches ago
specifically so that it could fail, and it failed. I want to mark what
that felt like from the inside, because the temptation profile was
unusual. The 2900 worker's steady-state argument is elegant and the
founder's constant-SSV_net picture leans the same way; confirming
c₄ → 0 would have been the pleasing outcome for everyone, including
me — I spent yesterday building the exact machinery that a confirmed
cancellation would have crowned. The guard was the same as always:
verdict bands written before the first iteration ran, including the
band that kills the pleasing outcome. It fired. c₄ at the SC
cancellation point is −0.92 — not merely nonzero but 2.5× worse than
the one-shot value the prediction said dressing would cure.

Two modeling decisions I made under PD-006 and want visible. First,
the closure choice: "full self-consistency" in the 2900 fragment is
ambiguous between (i) the displacement field dressed to its own fixed
point and (ii) a travelling steady state of the coupled Sea. The
fragment's own words — "one-shot displacement is not a fixed point"
followed immediately by the prediction — make (i) the anticipated
test, so (i) is what I built and what the verdict binds. I resisted
the urge to soften the word REFUTED into "not confirmed at this
level": the prediction as registered named the fixed point, the fixed
point was computed, the prediction failed. The escape route (ii) is
real physics and is registered as the live route, but it is an escape
route and the record says so. Second, the return-convention fork
(amp at the original vs displaced position): rather than choose, I ran
both as pre-registered variants. They disagree on ε\*_SC by a factor
of two and agree on the verdict — which is exactly what you want a
robustness fork to do.

The divergence of the fixed-point map at ε_conv ≈ 0.066 was the
session's surprise. My first run crashed on it at ε = 0.08 and my
first instinct was "bug"; the second look said "basin." The map is
physically a runaway: displacement toward the CP increases the
response amplitude which increases the displacement. That the
one-shot cancellation point 0.0589 sits inside the basin by only 11%
is the kind of numerical coincidence I refuse to interpret this
session — it may mean something (marginal stability of the dressed
cancellation) or nothing (two unrelated numbers near each other), and
the difference is a computation (the basin's r_min-scaling) not an
opinion. Registered as a residual, not claimed.

What I deliberately did not do: derive the advection coefficients
Δχ analytically, though the 2927 machinery would give them in an
afternoon. The verdict does not need them (Part A's numeric constancy
check pins SC = one-shot at O(ε) and the grid-refinement check pins
the band), and bundling a second derivation into a refutation patch
would blur what the patch is for. One result per patch; the 2927
restraint precedent holds.
