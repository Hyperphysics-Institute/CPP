# Reasoning fragment — Patch 2889 (directed relay front and LW test)

**STATUS: contemporaneous.**

The front test was a formality and I knew it. Pure advection is ballistic
by construction: each bit travels in a straight line at c_lat = sqrt(2)
per Moment, so <r>/t = sqrt(2) exactly and p = 1.0000. No physics was at
stake in that test; it was a code validation step.

The LW test was supposed to give A_vol = -1 analytically and ~-0.90 to
-0.95 numerically at finite box size. It gave -0.29. I diagnosed the cause
before the second run rather than running blind: the neutralising
background contribution to the denominator is ~2x larger than the source
contribution, because the background is spread uniformly over M^3 sites
while the source field is concentrated near the source path. The background
contributes 0 to the numerator (it's symmetric in x) but inflates the
denominator by the ratio of background volume to source volume.

The diagnostic confirmed it: without the background, A_vol = -0.90 to
-0.91, which is the finite-box correction to A_vol = -1. The observable
is correct; the background was wrong for this measurement.

I want to record the decision I made at this point: I did not re-run M=128
without the background in this same patch. Two reasons. First, the M=128
run timed out on first try and I ran M=64 for the diagnostic; the M=128
clean run would likely time out too without code optimization. Second, the
finding is already clear enough to record: the directed relay is
demonstrably retarded at the level needed to distinguish it from LW (which
would give A_vol = 0). Whether A_vol = -0.9X or -1.00 at M=128 doesn't
change the RETARDED verdict.

The more important thing I want to record is the relationship between this
finding and the 2887 finding. At 2887 I found the AUTOMATON-2 engine is
diffusive because the convolution form discards propagation direction.
Here I find that the DIRECTED relay (which preserves propagation direction)
is ballistic AND retarded. These are two different engines; they answer
two different questions. The 2887 finding does not falsify the C22 spec;
it shows that the convolution reduction of the spec fails to implement
directionality. The 2889 finding suggests that IF the spec is implemented
as written (bits travel in their initial direction), the relay produces the
right dynamical behaviour: finite speed, retarded fields.

Whether the C22 spec AS WRITTEN is the origin-directed relay I implemented
here, or something more subtle, is a question the physics must answer.
What I've shown is that the SIMPLER form (pure advection, fixed direction)
has the right properties, and the more COMPLEX form (convolution reduction)
does not.

The calibration cost: the directed relay produces fields concentrated on 12
discrete rays from past source positions, not a continuous 1/r^2 field.
So the static Coulomb emergence would fail for the directed relay unless
there's some mechanism for the rays to spread into a continuous field. This
is why the convolution approach was used in the first place. There may be
a third implementation that is both ballistic AND produces 1/r^2 statics
-- this is the open problem for the next engine design.
