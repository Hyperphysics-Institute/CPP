# Reasoning fragment — Patch 2891 (2890 static slope withdrawn)

**STATUS: contemporaneous.**

I set out to test the lambda-window claim from 2890 section 4 -- the claim
I had myself flagged as unestablished -- and could not, because the
statics estimator I built at 2890 is broken.

The catch came from cross-checking rather than from suspicion. The new run
reported slope -0.259 at sigma=1.0 where 2890 reported -0.970 for what I
believed was the same construction. A factor of four between two of my own
numbers. I stopped and diagnosed rather than picking whichever supported
the narrative, and the diagnosis is that the per-voxel log-log fit is
simply the wrong estimator for a radial profile: voxel count grows as r^2
so the outer shells swamp the fit, and within-shell scatter from ray
structure biases log-space slopes. Shell-mean is the right method and
gives -1.56 over [3,6] and -2.07 over [3,10] against the -0.97 I published.

The uncomfortable part is WHY -0.97 got through. It was close to -1, and
1/r was what I expected the diffusive limit to produce, so I accepted it
without interrogating the estimator. That is the same failure as the
c_lat misreading at 2887 -- I measured a quantity correctly and then
assigned it a meaning it did not have -- and the same as the no-light-cone
overclaim -- I had evidence for half a proposition and asserted the whole.
Three instances in one arc, all with the same shape: A NUMBER THAT LANDS
NEAR A PHYSICALLY EXPECTED VALUE GETS ACCEPTED WITHOUT CHECKING WHAT
PRODUCED IT. The robustness test makes this vivid: dropping the
neutralising background moves the shell-mean slope by under half a
percent and moves the per-voxel slope from -0.97 to -2.34. A number that
sensitive to an irrelevant change was never measuring the profile.

I also built a second broken observable in the same run and caught it by
inspection rather than by cross-check. The anisotropy CV came back exactly
0.000 at r = sqrt2 and 2 sqrt2 for EVERY sigma including sigma=0, which is
pure rays and maximally anisotropic. Those shells contain only
symmetry-equivalent lattice sites, so the CV is identically zero by
construction. Exact zeros in a noisy measurement are a gift -- they are
almost always a symmetry or a bug rather than physics, and here they were
both.

What survives 2890 matters and I have said so explicitly rather than
letting the correction taint the whole patch. The light-cone invariance is
a direct maximum-extent measurement with no fitting anywhere in it, and it
was the load-bearing claim; it stands. The bulk exponent trend stands with
the offset caveat already flagged. What falls is the specific claim that
sigma=1 reproduces 1/r statics, and with it the lambda-window test, which
I could not run and have not run.

G1 is unaffected and I checked rather than assuming: it was measured on
the real engine against an Ewald reference pointwise to 0.4 percent, not
by a power-law fit. The error is confined to my own sigma-family
exploration.
