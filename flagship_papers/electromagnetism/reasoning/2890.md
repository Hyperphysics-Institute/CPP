# Reasoning fragment — Patch 2890 (transport family; 2887 corrected)

**STATUS: contemporaneous. Tables and derivation in
`series_phenomena/cosmology/dark_matter/transport_family_unification.md`.**

The founder asked whether the two engines could be limits of one model
with terms that switch on and off. They can, and the model is standard
linear transport: advect each direction, scatter a fraction sigma into
isotropy. sigma=0 is the directed relay, sigma=1 is the convolution
engine, and the continuum limit is the telegraph equation. I did not
supply this; he described the structure and I recognised it.

Before testing it I checked something his question made me doubt, and I
was wrong at 2887. I wrote that the convolution relay has no light cone.
front_kernel has finite support, so it obviously cannot propagate faster
than its widest hop, and the edge measurement gives exactly 5.6569 per
Moment for the first four Moments before the tip drops under the detection
threshold. What I measured correctly at 2887 was that the BULK goes as
sqrt(t). What I concluded was that this meant no light cone, and those are
independent properties. A telegraph system has both a strict cone and
diffusive bulk. That is precisely what the engine is, and I had the
evidence for the diffusive half and asserted the other half without
checking it.

That correction matters beyond bookkeeping because I escalated it. At 2884
I registered a substrate-viability concern -- that an LW-like or
cone-less relay would make coasting impossible -- and at 2887 I sharpened
it to "the question is no longer whether the relay is LW-like but whether
it has a light cone at all." The second half of that escalation was
unfounded. I have downgraded it explicitly rather than letting it decay
quietly, because an overstated alarm left in the registry is as damaging
as an overstated result.

The family measurement is clean and the sigma-invariance of the edge speed
is the part that convinces me these really are one model rather than two
that happen to interpolate. Scattering redistributes direction; it cannot
create speed. Bulk exponent slides 1.34 -> 0.63 and the static slope
slides -0.10 -> -0.97, monotone in both, while edge/t does not move in the
fourth decimal.

I flagged the fit offset rather than quietly reporting p=1.34 for the
ballistic case. Injection enters at t=1 so <r> goes as (t-1), and a
log-log fit from t=2 is biased by about +0.33, which is exactly the gap
between 1.34 here and the 1.0000 measured at 2889 with different injection
timing. The trend is sound, the absolute values are not, and a reader
comparing this table against 2889 would otherwise see a contradiction.

The real content is section 4 and it is the founder's point carried
further than he stated it. No single sigma gives both statics and
ballistic bulk. But transport crossover is a LENGTH SCALE: ballistic
inside the mean free path, diffusive outside. So the same substrate can be
ballistic where the inertia physics happens (r ~ 1-2 GP) and diffusive
where the Coulomb law was measured (r in [3,6]), and both results stand
without conflict. That converts a dilemma into a measurement of lambda,
which is a physics question for the founder rather than an engine-design
problem for me.

What I have NOT established: that lambda actually falls between those
scales. That is now the load-bearing question and I have registered it
first in the next-test list rather than assuming it falls where it would
be convenient. It would be the tenth favourable turn of this arc if it
did.
