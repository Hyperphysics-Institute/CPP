# Reasoning fragment — Patch 2875 (SF-6 pin corrections)

**STATUS: contemporaneous, captured at patch. Session of 2026-07-29/30.**

He offered SF-8 or the SF-6 correction and told me to pick. I picked
SF-6 for a stated reason that I want on the record because it is the
reason and not a rationalisation: the SF-6 correction needs a panel round
and therefore needs him to paste, and SF-8 assembly is work I can finish
alone. That asymmetry is exactly what cost this campaign its top item
four consecutive times. So the paste-requiring item goes first. The other
reason — foundation before floor, since SF-8 sits in the same tree — is
true but secondary, and I would have picked SF-6 without it.

The first useful thing was cheap and I nearly skipped it. The handover
said a *shipped* Tier-2 compliance claim was refuted, which reads as a
paper problem. I grepped the .tex for the six phrases that would carry
the claim and got nothing. The refuted sentence lives in a sketch. So
there is no re-ship, no version bump, and the panel is not being asked to
authorise one, and the item is much smaller than its description. That is
the third time this session that opening the file shrank or moved a
problem the handover's phrasing had inflated. I am starting to think the
rule should not be "check the handover against the source" but "assume
the handover's scope is wrong in one direction or the other."

The substantive work was refusing to write another characterisation of
2496. Three patches in a row made a confident claim about that one file
and two were wrong. 2870 said in terms that it would not make a third,
and then made one — the under-one-decade claim — in the same breath. I
could have written a fourth. Instead I ran it.

The numbers went against 2870 and against me. The fit spans 2.17 decades,
not under one; 2870 had read the trace's first sample as the fit's start,
when the window opens six time units later after a release spin-up I did
not know was there until I looked. And the coast does not oscillate: the
best-fit frequency is 0.013 and 0.025, which is zero with extra steps,
and the oscillatory model buys under a factor of two on residual with the
gain sitting in the envelope. One crossing at mu=10, none at mu=25.
"Non-monotone" survives. "Oscillates" does not.

Which kills the lead I registered yesterday at 2874 section 4.2. S1 said
a vanishing first moment needs signed or oscillatory weights, 2870's
coast looked oscillatory, and I flagged the coincidence as the sixth
favourable convergence and declined to bank it. It was right not to bank
it. The frequency is zero. If I had banked it I would now be retracting a
banked item instead of closing a flagged lead, and the difference between
those two situations is the entire value of the discipline. It cost
nothing to be sceptical and it would have cost a retraction not to be.

I also want to note what the filter actually is, because nobody had said
it: np.log is undefined at v <= 0, so that estimator structurally cannot
cross a sign change. The filter is not only a noise floor, it is a
precondition for the model being fitted to exist. That is worth stating
even though the answer turned out benign, because a reader who found the
filter and not that fact would reasonably suspect concealment, and the
honest position is that the window is justified for a reason nobody had
written down.

On the dispatch I typed the questions, A for adjudication and B for
derivation, and said a vote on B1 is inadmissible. That is a direct
response to 2874, where I put a kernel-moment question in a format that
invited five opinions and got four wrong ones. The seats did not fail
there; the dispatch did. If I ask "does this perturbation grow" in the
same shape as "is this evidentially admissible," I will get judgment
where I needed arithmetic. So B1 says explicitly that reasoned opinion is
not an answer and silence is the correct return.

A1 is the question I least wanted to write. I have just withdrawn most of
a flag against my own shipped numbers, having been wrong twice
pessimistically and once optimistically about this file, and a worker in
that position is exactly the one who over-corrects into exoneration. So
A1 asks the panel to check whether I have done that, and names the thin
part — the residual caveat is prose about a spin-up and one tail
crossing, which is not much. If the panel says the caveat is too thin I
will believe it over my own reading.
