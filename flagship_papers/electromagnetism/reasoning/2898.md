# Reasoning fragment — Patch 2898 (B1 meets the SR sector)

**STATUS: contemporaneous.**

The founder asked what to change and whether to check relativity. I had
one specific idea and it was wrong, and then checking relativity made
things worse rather than better. Both are worth recording exactly.

The idea was that SSV_abs might carry the same beta^2 curvature as the
drive, since both are computed from the same arriving contributions --
vector sum versus magnitude sum. If so, mu = PSR/SSV_abs would inherit the
required growth for free and Newton I would hold with no new physics. It
was a clean idea and it fails by four orders of magnitude: c_S = 0.00003
against 0.201 required. In hindsight obvious, which is why I should have
predicted it rather than measured it -- summing magnitudes over a
symmetric sphere, what the fore loses the aft gains, and the cancellation
is nearly exact.

Then the SR sector. c05 gives PSR_eff = l_P/(1 + k Delta SSV) and states
the contraction under inertial motion is identical to that under
gravitational free fall, by the equivalence principle. c04 says PSR
contracts severely at relativistic velocity. B1 requires PSR to GROW. So
this is a sign conflict, not a coefficient mismatch, and the SR sector
deepens the drag rather than relieving it.

The structural point in section 3 is the thing I most want on record, and
I did not see it until the sign came out wrong. CPP's primitive is
velocity-proportional: d is set by SSV_net, so SSV_net = 0 gives d = 0 and
the CP stops. Free motion has to be actively sustained every Moment. In
Newtonian mechanics F = 0 gives a = 0 and constant velocity for free --
Newton I costs nothing and survives any velocity-dependence in the
coefficients, because those only affect the response to forces. Here
Newton I requires the drive to be exactly linear AND the coefficient
exactly constant, and both fail, and both fail in the same direction.

That is a much sharper statement of the problem than "the mechanism might
have a sign issue", and it is not a statement about the conjecture. It is
a statement about the primitive. I have registered it as a conflict
between three things that cannot all be right rather than as a refutation
of any one of them, because I do not think the evidence picks out which.

I also want to flag the tension I noticed while writing section 5B and
nearly left out because it cuts against the founder's own ruling. His 7
July no-carried-velocity ruling says velocity is held holographically in
the Sea and not carried by the particle. That is what makes the primitive
velocity-proportional, and it is exactly what makes Newton I fragile here.
If displacement were part of the CP's carried state, Newton I would be
automatic. So the ruling and the difficulty are the same fact seen from
two sides. He should know that, because it means the resolution may cost
him a commitment he has held since July rather than a coefficient.

On sequencing: option A in section 5 -- that my own drive model is wrong
because it holds the Sea static in the absolute frame -- is the cheapest
to test and I recommended it first. I want to note that I am recommending
the option that would let my own measured c = 0.201 be an artifact, which
is the right order to try things in, but I should watch that I do not
pursue it harder than the others for that reason.
