# Reasoning fragment — Patch 2887 (relay is diffusive)

**STATUS: contemporaneous. Derivation and tables in
`series_phenomena/cosmology/dark_matter/automaton_relay_is_diffusive.md`.**

I went looking for a better-instrumented LW measurement and found that the
question was ill-posed. That is the most useful thing that happened today
and it came from following a systematic residual instead of averaging over
it.

The re-run fixed what I said it would fix -- the wrap contamination was
real, and with M=128 and no lapping the per-beta band criterion passed at
every beta, fore and aft. Failure 2 from 2886 is genuinely resolved. But
linearity still failed at 0.556, and the residual was not scatter: |A| rose
monotonically with r at every beta and fell monotonically with beta at
every r. Clean structure is not noise, and a discriminant that varies
smoothly with the thing it is supposed to be independent of is measuring
something other than what I thought.

The r-dependence was the clue. A ~ r is what you get if the lag goes as
r^2 rather than as r, and lag ~ r^2 is diffusion. So I stopped
instrumenting the LW test and measured the propagation class directly,
which took one impulse and twelve Moments. p = 0.478. The front goes as
sqrt(t). There is no light cone.

I should have run that test first. Asking whether a relay reproduces
Lienard-Wiechert structure presupposes it has a propagation speed, and I
never checked. Worse, I measured c_lat = 4.0384 from the kernel and used
it to define beta throughout both runs, and that number is the width of a
one-hop kernel, not a speed -- after t Moments the front is at 3.78 sqrt(t)
and not 4.04 t. I flagged in 2886 that assuming c_lat would have been an
error; I measured it correctly and then misinterpreted what I had
measured, which is the subtler version of the same mistake.

The diagnosis in section 2 is the part I am most confident about because
it is legible in the code rather than inferred from the output. The
engine's own docstring says the field rule is a translation-invariance
reduction of the C22 origin-directed hop relay. A convolution cannot be
origin-directed -- a translation-invariant kernel has no way to know where
the origin was -- so every point re-radiates outward from itself and the
direction a contribution was already travelling is discarded each Moment.
Repeated convolution with a spreading kernel is a random walk. The
diffusion is not a tuning artifact, it is what that update form is.

The uncomfortable part is section 3, and I want it stated plainly rather
than buried. A diffusive relay under continuous injection relaxes to
Poisson, whose solution is 1/r. So the emergent-Coulomb result would come
out the same whether the relay is diffusive or ballistic. G1 passing at
0.4% is a correct measurement of a steady state and it is not evidence
about dynamics. The programme -- me included, three days ago, in the SF-8
inventory -- has been treating that gate as though it established the
relay. It establishes the relay's fixed point.

What I am NOT claiming: that C22 is wrong. The spec is origin-directed and
may be perfectly ballistic when implemented as specified. What is in
question is whether this engine implements it, and the answer for dynamics
appears to be no. That distinction matters because a reader could take
this as a strike against the founder's specification when it is a strike
against a reduction step in the code.

Condition B is not settleable here and I have said so rather than
producing a third inconclusive run with better error bars. The next move
is an engine that carries hop direction forward, then a front-class test,
then the LW question -- in that order, which is the order I should have
used.
