B1 has been open since the SF-6 pin arc, went to the panel twice, came
back 5-0 REASONED-UNVERIFIED both times because no seat can execute, and I
have deferred it repeatedly in favour of whatever was in front of me. It

is worth recording as a scheduling lesson: the item nobody else can do is
the item that should go first, not last.

The analytic core is four lines and I should have written them weeks ago.
The coasting map is v -> mu D(v), the eigenvalue is mu D'(v*), and
neutrality is Newton I. If D is exactly linear the fixed-point condition
forces mu k = 1 and then lambda = 1 identically -- the whole coasting
family is neutral. If D has curvature, lambda = 3 - 2 mu k and neutrality
forces v* = 0. So Newton I requires EXACT linearity. That converts a vague
stability question into a single sharp test.

Then the test. At 2884 I saw drive/beta drift by 0.2% and did not
determine whether it was real. It is real: the fractional spread is
0.00803 at 160x240, at 320x480 and at 640x960, identical to five decimals,
while the absolute magnitude converges properly with halving deltas. A
numerical artifact would shrink under refinement. This does not move at
all.

The robustness check is the part I am most confident in and I nearly did
not run it. c = 0.20129 across six model variants -- three different
falloff exponents, four different integration domains -- while the
amplitude k varies by a factor of six. A coefficient that survives that
while its prefactor moves sixfold is a property of the retardation
geometry, specifically of the (c^2 - v^2) denominator in the doubly
retarded solve, not of my modelling choices. I have been wrong three times
this session by accepting a number that looked right; this one I tried to
break and could not.

The consequence with constant mu is severe and I want it stated without
softening: Delta v = -c v^3 per Moment gives a decay time around 1e-37
seconds at beta = 1e-3. That is not "Newton I fails approximately". It is
"nothing moves". So the velocity dependence of mu is not an escape hatch I
reached for when the result went badly -- it is forced by 37 orders of
magnitude.

What B1 delivers is therefore not a verdict but a condition: mu(v) =
1/(k(1 - 0.201 beta^2)). That is checkable against the SR sector, which
already derives how substrate quantities behave under motion, and it is a
zero-parameter cross-sector test if that sector's mu(v) is derived
independently. I noted the relativistic gamma^2 form corresponds to c = 1
against the required 0.201 -- same sign, same shape, five times weaker --
and explicitly did not claim a gamma^2 origin, because the temptation to
announce a relativistic connection on a factor-of-five mismatch is exactly
the kind of thing I have had to withdraw three times already.

The caveat that matters: this is all inside the continuum retardation
model of 2884, not the substrate. That model earns some trust by
reproducing the Lienard-Wiechert null exactly, which is a nontrivial
check, but c = 0.201 is a property of the model and whether the automaton
reproduces it is untested. Registered as such rather than as a substrate
prediction.
