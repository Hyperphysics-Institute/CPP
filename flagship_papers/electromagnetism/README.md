## Scope

SF-6 covers the **electromagnetism programme** as a unified cross-domain flagship, demonstrating that classical Maxwell electromagnetism, special-relativistic photon kinematics, and quantum-electrodynamic phenomena are three views of one underlying CPP mechanism: the polarization of the eDP (electron-DP) sea by non-conservative/radiative impulses of motion.

The photon in CPP is **not** a cage-bound particle (and therefore not in SF-2's scope). It is a quantum (volume) of polarized eDPs — a coherent polarization wave in the dipole sea propagating at the substrate's null trajectory speed. This single mechanism produces:

- **Classical electromagnetism:** Maxwell's equations as the macroscopic-limit description of eDP-sea polarization dynamics, with the speed of light $c$ as the substrate's null-trajectory speed and the impedance of free space arising from substrate primitives.
- **Special-relativistic photon kinematics:** the photon as a null-trajectory excitation of the eDP sea; SR-1 derives time dilation and length contraction from substrate-state-vector behavior, and the photon's massless null trajectory falls out of the same construction.
- **Quantum electrodynamics:** photoelectric effect, Compton scattering, and double-slit interference all emerging from quantized eDP-sea polarization interactions with bound-mode fermion structures (charged leptons and quarks).

The headline argument: classical EM, SR, and QED are typically taught as separate pillars of physics. CPP unifies them via the eDP-sea polarization mechanism, with each pillar emerging in a specific limit (macroscopic / relativistic / quantum). The flagship ambition is to make this unification visible as a single coherent derivation rather than a sequence of separate phenomena.

## The cross-domain unification narrative

The flagship-class strength of SF-6 is the cross-domain narrative. Three pillars of physics — classical electromagnetism (Maxwell, $c.$1865), special relativity (Einstein, 1905), and quantum electrodynamics (Tomonaga/Schwinger/Feynman, 1940s) — have never been derived from a common substrate primitive in any other framework. Each was discovered separately, formalized separately, and is taught separately. The reason for the sequence in physics history is that each pillar required new mathematical machinery (vector calculus / tensor analysis / functional integrals), and the substrate question (what *is* the EM field made of?) was deferred at each step.

CPP answers the substrate question: the EM field is the eDP-sea polarization state. From that single answer, the three pillars emerge in three limits. SF-6 makes the three-pillar unification visible.

## Audience

**Extraordinarily broad.** Classical EM is in every undergraduate physics curriculum. Special relativity is in every modernized undergraduate physics curriculum and most secondary-physics curricula. QED is in every graduate-physics curriculum. The cross-domain unification reaches all three audiences simultaneously and provides the bridge each audience already wants to see (EM ↔ SR is taught implicitly through the relativistic-EM tensor formulation; SR ↔ QM is taught implicitly through the relativistic Schrödinger / Dirac equation; the *unification* is the missing piece).

This makes SF-6 potentially the highest-readership paper in the SF-line. The strong-sector flagship (SF-5) reaches strong-physics specialists; the fermion-mass flagships (SF-1, SF-3, SF-4) reach respective family specialists. SF-6 reaches the entire physics community, and even reaches non-physicist audiences with sufficient mathematical preparation.
