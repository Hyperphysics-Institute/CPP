## §3 The substrate-mechanism gap — the load-bearing question

The premise of the topological-projection argument (METH-CHIR-CONT-4, chirality_continuum.tex §3.5 Definition 3.4) is that the substrate handle magnitude $\chi/6$ is a **topological substrate quantity** in the sense of Definition 3.4:

> A topological substrate quantity is a dimensionless substrate-level quantity whose value is determined entirely by:
> - The combinatorial-geometric structure of the substrate polytope (vertex counts, edge-length ratios, irrep dimensions, stabilizer-subgroup orders);
> - Primitive feature identifications (e.g., $\hat{n} = v_{\text{host}}$);
>
> without dependence on:
> - Substrate-field-theoretic dynamics (no Lagrangian, no Hamiltonian, no action principle);
> - RG-flow scale parameters (no running coupling, no anomalous dimension);
> - Dynamical degrees of freedom evolving in time.

For manifestation (iv), the substrate handle magnitude $\chi/6$ that §2 recovered at the structural level depends on the substrate object $\mathcal{S}^{thermo}$ having a definite physical content — specifically, $\vec{\omega}_{PCD}$ aligned along $+\hat{n}$ (not $-\hat{n}$) with magnitude inherited from $\chi = \varphi^{-3}$. Whether this physical content satisfies Definition 3.4's exclusion of "dynamical degrees of freedom evolving in time" depends on whether the link $\hat{n} \mapsto \vec{\omega}_{PCD}$ is established as a topological feature of the substrate or as a substrate-dynamical consequence.

**The load-bearing question (Q1$_{\text{core}}$):**

> *Is the link from $\hat{n}$ to $\vec{\omega}_{PCD}$ — that is, the rule "PCD-cycle-orientation aligns with the substrate primitive direction" — polytope-geometrically determined, or is it substrate-dynamics-dependent?*

The answer determines the viability verdict.
