## Version archaeology — methodological observations

### Single-session compression of multi-session planned budget

The chirality continuum closure trajectory was originally estimated at 10–16 sessions for Venue (b) fallback (two separate single-sector papers) or 6–10 sessions for Venue (a) joint paper. Actual: **1 extended session (28 patches)**. This compression was enabled by:

1. **Pre-existing substrate-level closures** (Capotauro v2.0 v1.0 SHIPPED 19 May Patch 0479 — one session before the chirality continuum trajectory began)
2. **Joint-paper format** saving 4–11 sessions vs Venue (b) fallback
3. **Real-time reviewer-cycle methodology** (three reviewers + four rounds compressed into one session window)
4. **Established methodological scaffolding** (four-condition test pattern, conditional-closure framework, THEO-CHIR-CONT-N convention, methods catalogue infrastructure)

### Three-reviewer convergence at first reviewer round

Previous flagship papers (SF-4, SF-2, Capotauro v1.0, Capotauro v2.0) required multi-round reviewer cycles to achieve three-reviewer convergence. Chirality continuum is the **first paper to achieve three-reviewer convergence at first reviewer round each** (ChatGPT R2 + Grok R1 + CoPilot R1 at v0.6 SHIPPED).

### Theorem-registration cadence

Three programme-level theorems registered across 11 substantive patches (0485–0495). Pre-registration cadence at this rate (~3.6 patches per theorem) is comparable to Capotauro v2.0 v0.8.1 DRAFT (13 patches for 2 theorems = 6.5 patches per theorem) and faster than SF-4 v4.0 (6 patches for 5 theorems = 1.2 patches per theorem; SF-4 was unusually dense).
