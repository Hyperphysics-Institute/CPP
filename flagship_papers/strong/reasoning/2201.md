## The instruction, and what "appropriate gradient" means

Thomas asked for the *appropriate gradient*. In this arc that is unambiguous: the **ponderomotive (Kapitza)
treatment**, in which the bond's restoring stiffness is the curvature of the **squared-field** potential |E|²
(E = −∇V), not the static curvature of V. This is not a stylistic choice — **1834 forces it.** 1834 showed the
static edge-bond configuration is **Earnshaw-unstable**: V'' is non-restoring, so the static curvature I used in
2200 is the *wrong operator* for a bond that physically does not exist statically. The bond is held by the qCP
**ZBW jitter**: a fast oscillation about the slow geometric coordinate gives an effective potential
U_eff(q) = V(q) + C·|E(q)|², with C = a_ZBW²/4. The |E|² term is **positive-definite** — it supplies restoring
stiffness *even where V''<0* — which is exactly how ponderomotive trapping defeats Earnshaw. Since the static
part is non-restoring, the bond stiffness **is** the ponderomotive term, κ(mode) = C·∂²_q|E|².

## Why the raw number (g~0.001) was rejected — anti-priority guardrail

The first naive ponderomotive pass gave g_pond ≈ 0.001 — a "too clean" vindication. I did **not** ship it. Its
own static-comparison column disagreed with 2200 by orders of magnitude, the tell of a **counting artifact**: a
dense full-width bend grid has far more cores and far more coherent field addition than a sparse point-crossing
scissor, so raw κ_bend is inflated by sheer count — the *same* apples-to-oranges error I flagged in 2200 for the
corpus's 0.27/0.71 comparison. The honest unit is **per participating core** (per unit contact). Under per-core
normalization the result is the conditional verdict above — viable in the screened regime, tense in the shallow
one — not an unconditional g~0.001. Manufacturing the clean number would have violated the handover's hard
anti-priority; the per-core normalization plus the explicit p≲2 escape hatch is what keeps this honest.
