## Where the barrier is NOT (two origins ruled out with numbers)

**(A) Not strain (verify §A).** Forming a cross-junction bends the soft scissor hinge (κ_scissor = g·κ_bend ≈ 0.02·E_qq
≈ 1.3 MeV/rad²). A 90° bend costs E_strain = ½κ_scissor(π/2)² ≈ 1.6 MeV ≈ **0.3–1.2 kT_form** — far below 6.5 kT.
The *same* angular softness (g ≈ 0.02) that made the velocity floor viable (G1a) makes junction strain negligible.
Consistent, and it means strain cannot be the nucleation barrier.

**(B) Not bond energy (verify §B).** E_qq/kT_form ≈ 12–51 ≫ 1: every axial bond is deeply favorable. A generic sticky
(isodesmic) rod with E_bond ≫ kT nucleates **barrierlessly** (ΔG* ≈ 0 ⇒ ε_nuc ~ O(1) ⇒ **N_form ~ 1, under-cored**).
So a bare strong-bond rod *fails* the dwarf branch. The barrier must come from elsewhere — and (A)+(B) leave exactly
one place.

## Where the barrier IS — configurational selectivity of the coat-buffered ordered seed (verify §C)

The element is a color core **buffered by an eCP coat** (1842); E_qq bonding is accessible only on the exposed spine
face — a fraction f of incoming orientations. The relic is the *ordered* Cross-Rod (amorphous polymerization is ruled
out — 1842), so forming a correctly-**registered** seed rather than misregistered/amorphous product costs an
orientational-entropy penalty ~ |ln f| kT per registered bond. For a critical nucleus of n* elements (n*−1 bonds):

  **ΔG*_nuc ≈ (n*−1)·|ln f|·kT_form.**

This is exactly the physics that separates a *cooperative* (nucleated) polymer from an isodesmic one: the seed pays a
registration penalty that the elongating rod, growing on an already-registered template, does not. Numerically, for
the natural range f ~ 0.05–0.30 (coat blocks 70–95% of orientations) and n* ~ 2–4:

| f | \|ln f\| | ΔG*/kT (n*=2) | (n*=3) | (n*=4) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.30 | 1.20 | 1.2 | 2.4 | 3.6 |
| 0.20 | 1.61 | 1.6 | 3.2 | 4.8 |
| 0.12 | 2.12 | 2.1 | 4.2 | **6.4** |
| 0.08 | 2.53 | 2.5 | 5.1 | 7.6 |
| 0.05 | 3.00 | 3.0 | **6.0** | 9.0 |

The natural range **~1–9 kT brackets the dwarf window 6.0–6.9 kT** — and the most natural reading, **n* ~ 4** (the
4-wide cross closing its first full cross-section) with **f ~ 0.11** (axial-spine acceptance ~1/9 of orientations;
a ±30° cone about the two spine ends gives f ≈ 0.13), lands **squarely in-window** (verify §D). The target is not a
coincidence at the edge of the range; it is near its center.
