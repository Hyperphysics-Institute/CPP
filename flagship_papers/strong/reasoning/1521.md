## Which calibration items I took, and one I weighed

All five integrated items are editorial — no physics, no numbers, no verdicts moved. Four were obvious polish (Grok's abstract cross-ref; Copilot's three: the qualitative-β echo, the AME-2020 RMS scope, the §4→§8 parenthetical). 

The one I weighed is ChatGPT's §13.1 probability-language softening. The original sentence ("the probability of reproducing them jointly by accident or hidden tuning scales as (residual band/parameter space)^N") is inherited verbatim from SF-3, which shipped with it through four rounds plus a hostile pass. ChatGPT's point is nonetheless correct: the word "probability… scales as" implies a formal statistical model that SF-5 does not supply. Honesty wins over consistency-with-SF-3 here — I softened it to "heuristic likelihood… falls off roughly as… a scaling estimate, not a formal statistical model." This is the more defensible framing and matches the SF-line strict posture. It does create a small inconsistency with SF-3's identical un-softened sentence; that is SF-3's own D3-style residual, not this window's to fix, and I am flagging it for the SF-3 maintainer / SF-7 assembly rather than reaching into another paper's .tex from this window.
