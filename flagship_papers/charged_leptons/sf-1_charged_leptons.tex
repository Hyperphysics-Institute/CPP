\section{Introduction: The Charged-Lepton Mass Problem}

The charged leptons present one of the sharpest unexplained regularities in particle physics. Their masses span more than three orders of magnitude,
\[
m_e = 0.5110~\text{MeV}, \qquad m_\mu = 105.66~\text{MeV}, \qquad m_\tau = 1776.9~\text{MeV},
\]
with ratios $m_\mu/m_e \approx 207$ and $m_\tau/m_\mu \approx 17$ that the Standard Model accommodates through three independently fitted Yukawa couplings and explains from no deeper principle. Yet the three masses are not free of one another. In 1981 Koide observed~\citep{koide1983} that
\begin{equation}
K \;\equiv\; \frac{m_e + m_\mu + m_\tau}{\left(\sqrt{m_e} + \sqrt{m_\mu} + \sqrt{m_\tau}\,\right)^2} \;=\; \frac{2}{3}
\label{eq:koide-def}
\end{equation}
to a precision that, with current Particle Data Group values~\citep{pdg2024}, holds at roughly 11 parts per million. Equation~\eqref{eq:koide-def} is dimensionless, basis-independent, and has survived four decades of improving measurement; it sits at the boundary $K \in [1/3, 1]$ exactly halfway, the value an isotropic three-state superposition would produce. The Standard Model has no mechanism that requires it.

A second, equivalent statement of the same content is parametric. Writing the square-root masses as a three-phase modulation,
\begin{equation}
\sqrt{m_i} \;=\; A\left(1 + \sqrt{2}\,\cos\!\Big(\theta + \tfrac{2\pi i}{3}\Big)\right), \qquad i \in \{0,1,2\},
\label{eq:koide-param}
\end{equation}
the relation $K=2/3$ is reproduced for \emph{any} phase $\theta$ provided the modulation depth is exactly $\rho = \sqrt2$; the masses themselves then depend on the single phase $\theta$, whose empirical value is $\theta \approx 132.73^\circ$. The Standard Model offers no reason for either the depth $\sqrt2$ or the phase $132.73^\circ$.

This paper derives both. In Conscious Point Physics (CPP) the charged leptons are geometric cage structures on the 600-cell substrate~\citep{abshier2026sm1}, and the three-fold structure underlying Eqs.~\eqref{eq:koide-def}--\eqref{eq:koide-param} is the complete graph $\Kthree$ on the three base vertices of the lepton cage. We show that the modulation depth $\rho=\sqrt2$ is fixed by the $\Kthree$ adjacency spectrum (Section~\ref{sec:k3}), that the phase $\theta$ is fixed by an electroweak correction built from the lattice's own Weinberg angle (Sections~\ref{sec:weinberg}--\ref{sec:phase}), and that the full spectrum then follows from a single calibration (Section~\ref{sec:spectrum}). No content in Sections~\ref{sec:k3}--\ref{sec:spectrum} is new derivation: each result is inherited from shipped CPP papers SM-1, SM-3, SM-4, and SM-6. SF-1's contribution is to assemble them into the cleanest single-calibration sector in the corpus and to expose the two cross-sector threads it carries into the programme's unification synthesis.

\label{sec:weinberg}

The Koide phase (Section~\ref{sec:phase}) requires an electroweak ingredient, and that ingredient is the lattice's own Weinberg angle. \section{Falsifiers}

\end{enumerate}
A fifth, structural falsifier is the scope boundary itself: the framework \emph{requires} the Koide relation to break for quarks (Section~\ref{sec:ledger}). A quark sector found to satisfy Koide at lepton-grade precision would contradict the colour-contribution account.
